2026-10-22

On 22 October 2026 Ukraine's military intelligence agency HUR published an interactive 3D model, an assembly breakdown and an electronic-component database of a new Russian cruise missile, the Filin ("eagle owl"), on its War&Sanctions portal. According to HUR, Russia began firing the missile at Ukraine in late summer 2026. It is developed by AO NPO Molniya, part of the state-owned Tactical Missiles Corporation, and HUR describes it as the latest step in a Russian trend towards cheap, jet-powered, ground-launched strike weapons. HUR did not say how many Filins have been launched, what they hit or how the examined parts were obtained. Per HUR's data, the Filin has a carbon-fibre and fibreglass composite fuselage 3.5 m long with a 3 m wingspan; Defence Blog cites a maximum launch weight of 210 kg. Some parts are 3D-printed. It is fired from a ground launcher with a solid-fuel booster, after which a Russian R500 turbojet made by OOO Reynolds takes over (the same engine HUR previously found in the S-71K Kover and S-71M Monokhrom cruise missiles). Russia claims a top speed of 800 km/h; HUR's specification gives a range of 600–1,200 km depending on configuration, flight from ultra-low altitude up to 6 km, and versions with 50 kg or 100 kg warheads. Defence Blog reports that HUR lists high-explosive fragmentation, penetrating and thermobaric warhead types. These performance figures are Russian claims relayed by HUR and have not been independently verified. The onboard equipment is made by PLAZ LLC, which also produces equipment for the Garpiya ("Harpy") drone, a Geran-2 derivative built by the Izhevsk Electromechanical Plant Kupol; HUR says Filin and Garpiya units share a similar layout and markings. Guidance combines a Chinese-made inertial navigation system with a 16-channel Kometa anti-jamming satellite-navigation module (CRPA) from Russia's VNIIR-Progress. Of 81 identified components, 35 are American and 22 Chinese, a larger Chinese share than in earlier Russian missiles and drones. Named suppliers include Texas Instruments, Analog Devices, NXP and STMicroelectronics plus the Chinese firms WinChipHead and Mornsun; some parts carry 2021–2022 production dates, including an Ethernet switch chip made in May 2022. Euromaidan Press notes that Ukrainian adviser Serhii Beskrestnov warned in September that cheap Russian cruise missiles such as the Banderol, Dan-T, Dan-M and Gerbera-5 would soon outpace Ukraine's jet-powered interceptor drones.

2026-10-20

Two new tools let Ukrainians see roughly where Russian attack drones are flying, Euromaidan Press reports. A Ukrainian air-threat project called monitor announced a free, ad-free beta map on X on 13 October. It does not use radar: it aggregates posts from regional Telegram channels that report drones and missiles over their oblasts, cross-checks them algorithmically to estimate each target's type, approximate position and heading, and overlays official air-raid alerts from alerts.in.ua (areas shown red or yellow). A target log lists each drone's district and course, the map refreshes about every 20 seconds while targets are airborne, and it works in a browser or on Telegram on a weak connection. On 17 October Ukraine's Defence Ministry said its Air Alert app now shows the approximate position and heading of some targets, namely cruise missiles, Banderol drone-launched missiles and Shahed-type drones, using data from the Air Force over secure channels. For security reasons it shows no exact coordinates and no true real-time tracking and may lag; ballistic missiles and anything faster than 2,000 km/h are not shown. Both the ministry and the monitor developers tell users to go to shelter first and check the map from there. The tools respond to the changing Russian strike campaign: nightly raids of over a hundred drones, jet-powered Shaheds that can cut their engines and glide in, and the fact that since September drone-only (yellow) alerts in some oblasts no longer shut everything down, so many people stay at work during drone alerts. Publishing approximate tracks is a trade-off between public safety and giving Russia feedback on its routes, which explains the deliberate delays and coarseness in the official version.
