Ramstein (37th UDCG): Zelensky proposes swapping allied Patriot and IRIS-T stocks for future Ukrainian-ordered missiles; UK pledges £240m SHORAD package, Germany $300m for Patriot interceptors; Sweden, UK and Ukraine sign Gripen weapons letter of intent

2026-11-17

At the 37th meeting of the Ukraine Defence Contact Group (Ramstein format) at NATO headquarters in Brussels on 17 November, President Volodymyr Zelensky, addressing online, proposed an exchange of interceptors, Ukrainska Pravda reports. Allies would give Ukraine Patriot and IRIS-T missiles from their existing stockpiles now, and receive newer missiles that Ukraine has already ordered once they are produced. "This is not a donation – it's an exchange," he said, arguing that waiting lists for new interceptors are long and that the missiles Ukraine needs already sit in US, European and other partners' warehouses. Defence Minister Yevhenii Khmara had floated the same idea earlier. In his evening address Zelensky added that the US could help destroy Russian and North Korean ballistic missile launchers near Ukraine's borders by opening up satellite communications, but that the speed limit on Starshield in occupied territories "still remains in place"; he said Ukraine is discussing it with partners and the US side. The Starshield claim is Zelensky's own and was not independently confirmed. On the sidelines, Defence Blog reports, UK Defence Secretary Wes Streeting, co-chairing with German Defence Minister Boris Pistorius, announced a £240 million ($316 million) package of more than 1,000 Thales-built short-range air defence missiles and launchers (RapidRanger, firing Starstreak and Martlet/LMM) plus more than a dozen radars, with deliveries from mid-December on top of 400 surface-to-air missiles already sent. Pistorius said Germany adds $300 million to a fifth PURL package, funded together with Norway, focused on PAC-2 and PAC-3 interceptors; Defence Blog cites Ukraine's Defence Ministry saying about 95% of the Patriot interceptors Ukraine now receives come through PURL. Germany is also working on integrating IRIS-T missiles into Norwegian-supplied NASAMS launchers, no timeline given, and will send more radars, F-16 ammunition and drones. Sweden, the UK and Ukraine signed a letter of intent on supplying ammunition, spare parts, weapons integration and training for Ukraine's Gripen fleet, covering the 16 donated Gripen C/Ds (arriving from early 2027), the 16 Gripen E/Fs bought in June (deliveries 2029-2030) and future orders; the UK has pledged €300 million toward Gripen deliveries. These are announcements and a letter of intent, not deliveries, and delivery timelines come from the reporting outlets and the Swedish government.

«russia Demonstrates Ground Launch of Dan-T Missile for the First Time: Why Does It Pose a Greater Threat to Ukraine?» (Defense Express: 'Russia demonstrates ground launch of Dan-T missile for the first time: why it poses a greater threat to Ukraine') — Ukrainian assessment that ground-launched Dan-T/Banderol strikes would be harder to detect and easier to mass

2026-11-17SINGLE SOURCE

Defense Express reports that during the Tsentr-2026 (Center-2026) exercise Russian forces launched the Dan-T small cruise missile, which the outlet treats as the same weapon as the S8000 Banderol, from a ground launcher, and released video of it for the first time. Until now the only public launch footage showed the missile fired from Mi-28NM attack helicopters. Defense Express notes that the ground capability itself was not new: the launcher was first seen at a military exhibition attended by Putin in September 2025 and has recently reappeared at exhibitions. The images show a fairly long solid-fuel booster under the missile, which accelerates it to the required speed and brings it to operating altitude. Because the missile starts from a standstill on the ground without the initial altitude of an air launch, it burns more fuel and its maximum range is somewhat lower. The article gives no figures for range, speed or warhead. The outlet nevertheless judges the ground-launched version more dangerous for Ukraine. Air launch needs a Mi-28 helicopter or an Orion UAV at considerable altitude and a safe distance from the front, and these carriers can often be seen before launch, giving Ukrainian air defence time to prepare. A ground launch is said to be almost impossible to detect in advance and can take place closer to the target. Ground launchers are also much cheaper than helicopters or drones, which Russia has in limited numbers, so they could be built in quantity and allow larger salvos. Defense Express stresses that there are no confirmed reports so far of ground launchers being used against Ukraine, though they may already be in service or close to it. Topwar reported the same first ground-launch video on 13 November. Single source: this is a claim by Defense Express, a Ukrainian defence-media outlet, and has not been independently verified.

According to Beskrestnov, the missiles therefore carry optical homing heads or more expensive radar-guided ones: as the warhead descends, a radar scans the terrain for a few seconds and a correction is fed into the missile's guidance system. Beskrestnov pointed to War&Sanctions, the database of Ukraine's Defence Intelligence (HUR/DIU), which lists both seekers for the 9M723 Iskander-M: the 9E436 optical correlation homing head, made by Russia's TsNIIAG with the NII Televideniya institute involved, and the 9B918 active radar homing head, made by NPP Radar MMS.
