Largely intact new Russian small cruise missile, provisionally a Geran-5 derivative ('Geran-5 K5-K'), found in a field in Odesa region

2026-10-27

Photos circulating on 27 October show a Russian small cruise missile that came down in an open field in Ukraine's Odesa region during an attack and stayed relatively intact. Militarnyi says the photos were posted by the local Telegram channel ODESSA INFO LIVE and that the light damage lets Ukrainian specialists study the design in detail. Defence Blog identifies the weapon as the upgraded Geran-5 'K5-K' and says the serial number ANS-3031 is visible on the airframe. Militarnyi headlines it as a missile 'of unknown type'. It is not known whether the missile was shot down, jammed by electronic warfare or failed on its own, and Ukrainian officials have not commented. The photos show a compact cruise missile with a box-shaped fuselage (rectangular cross-section, rounded edges, flat sides), swept wings, a V-shaped tail with offset fins, and a small turbojet whose nozzle sits in the tail and whose air intakes are on the fuselage sides. Militarnyi says the dark composite skin and flat surfaces may be meant to reduce radar cross-section and simplify production. Serhiy 'Flash' Beskrestnov, adviser to Ukraine's president on defence technology, published the first photos of this new missile type on 22 October and called it the Geran-5 K5-K. He named optical navigation and greater range, speed and flight altitude as its distinctive features. Militarnyi points out that its exterior differs significantly from the Geran-5 K5-K variant already known. That variant, used sporadically since late September, has an internal engine, smaller swept or triangular wings and a tail without wingtips. The new airframe also has a diamond-shaped wing and a V-tail. The type designation is therefore still open: either a new K5-K configuration or a separate Geran-5 derivative. According to Militarnyi's Ukrainian military sources and Flash, the new Geran-5 variants use autonomous optical navigation that matches images of the ground to stored data. This lets them correct course without satellite navigation and makes jamming largely ineffective. Developers reportedly aim for about 900 km/h, which would put them largely beyond the reach of interceptor drones. No measured data exist yet. For comparison, Ukrainian intelligence (HUR) gives the baseline Geran-5, first used in January 2026, as about 6.5 m long with a 3.2 m wingspan, up to 850 kg take-off weight, a roughly 90 kg warhead, a Chinese Telefly turbojet, 450–600 km/h cruising speed and a range of about 950–1,000 km. HUR also says around 3,000 Geran-4/5 are produced monthly at Alabuga. These are Ukrainian state and intelligence claims; the identification of the Odesa missile is not independently verified.

Since 14 October, Starlink and its military version Starshield stopped working on Ukrainian drones and other unmanned systems moving faster than about 160 km/h (100 mph). These figures come from Ukrainian officials and have not been independently verified. Rubio and Hegseth denied administration involvement, Vice President Vance hinted at coordination with Musk, and Senator Richard Blumenthal has asked the Pentagon who ordered the restrictions.
