«Тысяча порезов» (Rybar: 'A thousand cuts') — new Geran variants with metal- and cable-cutting munitions hit Dnipro bridges but cause only limited damage

2026-10-30SINGLE SOURCEMILBLOGGER

Rybar says new modifications of the Geran one-way attack drone reached Russian drone units in October 2026. According to the channel, they carry munitions designed to cut through metal surfaces and cables, which made them look like the answer to Ukraine's cable-stayed bridges over the Dnipro. In practice, Rybar writes, the results have been limited. Over the past month the new drones struck Kyiv's cable-stayed Southern and Northern bridges many times. This caused partial traffic collapse in the city but only limited structural damage. The channel says even a direct Iskander hit failed to bring down a section of the Southern Bridge. Rybar describes a similar pattern elsewhere. Several attacks with the modified Gerans hit the cable-stayed bridge in Zaporizhzhia, which stayed in use. FAB glide bombs and cruise missiles aimed at crossings in Zaporizhzhia and Dnipropetrovsk had a similarly limited effect, it says. The channel puts this down less to weak munitions than to how robust such structures are. In its view, bringing down girder bridges, and even more so cable-stayed ones, requires systematic strikes on structural weak points to unbalance how the load is distributed. Rybar concludes that disrupting Ukraine's transport system does not require destroying every crossing, because even local damage restricts traffic and forces repairs. It argues that the priority is to keep up the tempo of strikes and steadily weaken the bridges so they become harder to repair. The existence and effect of the cutting-munition Geran variants are not independently confirmed. US approves possible $2.1bn sale of 500 Patriot PAC-3 MSE interceptors to Ukraine, paid for by Canada, Germany and Norway, as NATO's top commander says PURL deliveries face no delays

2026-10-30

On 30 October the US State Department approved a possible sale to Ukraine of 500 Patriot PAC-3 Missile Segment Enhancement (MSE) interceptors, with spare parts and logistical support, at an estimated $2.1 billion, the Kyiv Independent, Meduza and the Associated Press report. Canada, Germany and Norway would finance the purchase through the US JUMPSTART programme and no US government funds are involved; additional costs would be met from Ukrainian funds backed by the EU's Ukraine Support Loan. The approval is a step towards a sale, not a signed contract: Congress normally has 30 days to review it and the State Department gave no delivery timeline. Zelensky welcomed the decision but warned that deliveries could take several years, while Ukraine needs the missiles this winter to protect its energy infrastructure; he urged the US and partners to supply interceptors from existing stocks now and replenish them later through the new contracts. The same day NATO's Supreme Allied Commander Europe, US Gen. Alexus Grynkewich, told reporters in Riga that US deliveries to Ukraine were ongoing, with one in the past week, that no items in European-funded PURL packages had been delayed and that every third-party transfer requested of the US government had been approved, according to Bloomberg as cited by Ukrainska Pravda and the Kyiv Independent. He declined to give numbers or missile types. That account conflicts with Kyiv's: Zelensky says Ukraine has received an average of only 12 Patriot interceptors a month from the US in 2026 and had waited nearly a month for Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth to sign off on allied-funded Patriot and NASAMS transfers (see the earlier entry on that hold). Secretary of State Marco Rubio said on 20 October that nothing was 'being deliberately denied to Ukraine'. Euromaidan Press, citing a NATO source quoted by Radio Svoboda on 29 October, reports that the US is holding back PAC-3 interceptors, the Patriot variant built mainly against ballistic missiles, while older PAC-2s, used chiefly against aircraft and cruise missiles, keep arriving via PURL; the source said the matter is being discussed at a high political level. Zelensky said on 27 October that Russia had fired more than 100 ballistic missiles at Kyiv and Kyiv Oblast in two weeks and that Ukraine above all lacks Patriot missiles, and on 29 October that intelligence points to new ballistic attacks being prepared. The PAC-3 hold claim rests on a single anonymous source and is not independently verified; the delivery figures come from Ukrainian officials, a party to the war, and are contradicted by Grynkewich.

Ukraine has completed its share of Freyja, the multinational anti-ballistic system it is building with European partners, President Zelenskyy said on 17 October, Defense News reports. According to Defence Blog, the Navy called it a "Long Neptune" strike, referring to the extended-range land-attack version of the missile, and President Zelensky later confirmed the result. Ukraine is thus repeatedly using its domestic cruise missile against the glide-bomb carrier fleet at bases hundreds of kilometres behind the front.

Largely intact new Russian small cruise missile, provisionally a Geran-5 derivative ('Geran-5 K5-K'), found in a field in Odesa region

2026-10-27

Photos circulating on 27 October show a Russian small cruise missile that came down in an open field in Ukraine's Odesa region during an attack and stayed relatively intact. Militarnyi says the photos were posted by the local Telegram channel ODESSA INFO LIVE and that the light damage lets Ukrainian specialists study the design in detail. Defence Blog identifies the weapon as the upgraded Geran-5 'K5-K' and says the serial number ANS-3031 is visible on the airframe. Militarnyi headlines it as a missile 'of unknown type'. It is not known whether the missile was shot down, jammed by electronic warfare or failed on its own, and Ukrainian officials have not commented. The photos show a compact cruise missile with a box-shaped fuselage (rectangular cross-section, rounded edges, flat sides), swept wings, a V-shaped tail with offset fins, and a small turbojet whose nozzle sits in the tail and whose air intakes are on the fuselage sides. Militarnyi says the dark composite skin and flat surfaces may be meant to reduce radar cross-section and simplify production. Serhiy 'Flash' Beskrestnov, adviser to Ukraine's president on defence technology, published the first photos of this new missile type on 22 October and called it the Geran-5 K5-K. He named optical navigation and greater range, speed and flight altitude as its distinctive features. Militarnyi points out that its exterior differs significantly from the Geran-5 K5-K variant already known. That variant, used sporadically since late September, has an internal engine, smaller swept or triangular wings and a tail without wingtips. The new airframe also has a diamond-shaped wing and a V-tail. The type designation is therefore still open: either a new K5-K configuration or a separate Geran-5 derivative. According to Militarnyi's Ukrainian military sources and Flash, the new Geran-5 variants use autonomous optical navigation that matches images of the ground to stored data. This lets them correct course without satellite navigation and makes jamming largely ineffective. On the night of 24–25 October Russia launched 153 long-range strike assets at Ukraine, according to the Ukrainian Air Force as reported by Ukrainska Pravda and the Kyiv Independent. The mix included Shahed-type attack drones, Gerbera and decoy drones, and S8000 Banderol low-cost cruise missiles, which Ukrainska Pravda describes as both drone- and air-launched. Of the Shaheds, 92 were jet-powered. By 08:00 Ukrainian air defence had shot down or jammed 119 targets, including 64 of the jet-powered Shaheds; nine drones were still in Ukrainian airspace. Hits were recorded at 15 locations and falling debris at nine more. By simple arithmetic on these figures, roughly 70% of the jet-powered drones were stopped against about 90% of the remaining types, suggesting the faster jet variants are harder to intercept. These are Ukrainian military figures and have not been independently verified. The strikes hit residential areas in several cities. In Vinnytsia a drone hit a five-storey apartment building, destroying four flats and injuring six people; the regional administration said the wider attack on the oblast also damaged critical and civilian infrastructure and an elderly care home. In Kharkiv's Novobavarskyi district Mayor Ihor Terekhov said a jet-powered drone killed one person and injured four on the evening of 24 October. In Synelnykove (Dnipropetrovsk Oblast) a drone strike on 24 October set a multi-storey building on fire, killing three people and injuring at least 12, including an 11-year-old girl; the oblast administration reported more than 60 drone and artillery attacks on six districts that day. At short range, Russian forces struck a passenger bus in Kherson's Tsentralnyi district with a drone at about 15:11 on 25 October, with passengers on board; six people suffered blast injuries and a 67-year-old man later died, according to the city military administration. The source does not specify the drone type. Across Ukraine, local authorities reported 8 people killed and 51 injured over the day, per the Kyiv Independent; Zaporizhzhia Oblast alone reported 832 strikes on 52 settlements using drones, artillery, aircraft and MLRS. Casualty and strike figures come from Ukrainian regional authorities and have not been independently confirmed.

«New russian Filin Missile Consists Half of American, Chinese Components: Ukraine's Defense Intelligence Reveals Details» (Defense Express)

2026-10-24SINGLE SOURCE

Defense Express reviews the component analysis of Russia's new Filin cruise missile that Ukraine's Defence Intelligence (HUR) published on its War&Sanctions portal, including a 3D model, an assembly breakdown and a list of electronic parts. The outlet stresses HUR's finding that the missile's electronics resemble those of other Russian missiles and UAVs, with two differences: a larger share of Chinese parts and standardised designs across its modules and boards. Defense Express gives the split as "35% American, 22% Chinese". HUR's own figures are counts, however: 35 American and 22 Chinese parts out of 81 identified components, so Defense Express appears to have turned counts into percentages. The headline's claim that the missile is "half" American and Chinese parts is the outlet's own framing. According to HUR as cited, the onboard equipment is made by PLAZ LLC, the firm also linked to the Garpiya (Harpy) drone, a Geran-2 modification. The two weapons have similar layouts and markings. Guidance uses a Chinese-made inertial navigation system and a 16-channel Kometa antenna. The engine is the Russian R500 turbojet from Reynolds LLC, which HUR earlier found in the S-71K Kover and S-71M Monokhrom cruise missiles. Defense Express repeats the claimed specifications: a carbon-fibre and fibreglass fuselage 3.5 m long, a 3 m wingspan, some 3D-printed parts, up to 800 km/h, up to 1,200 km range, flight from extremely low altitude up to 6 km, 50 kg and 100 kg warhead versions, and a ground launch using a solid-propellant booster. Developer NPO Molniya, part of the Tactical Missiles Corporation, describes the missile as part of a trend towards cheap, ground-launched, jet-powered strike weapons. The outlet notes that HUR earlier published a similar breakdown of the Kh-47M2 Kinzhal. This is a single source: Defense Express, a Ukrainian defence outlet, relaying findings from Ukraine's military intelligence (HUR), a Ukrainian state body; the claims have not been independently verified.

NYT: North Korea upgrades ballistic missiles and mass-produces attack drones with Russian help

2026-10-24

According to a New York Times report (23 October 2026), relayed by Ukrainska Pravda and SFG Media, North Korea is continuing to upgrade its ballistic missiles and has begun mass-producing attack drones, largely thanks to its military cooperation with Russia. Russia is said to share its own developments with Pyongyang in exchange for soldiers, workers and weapons. A key element is access to Russian data and feedback on how North Korean weapons perform in combat: Anton Ponomarenko of the Pacific Forum says this analysis of battlefield performance has helped improve warhead accuracy and missile manoeuvrability, making the threat to South Korea "more substantial". The reports do not name specific missile types such as the KN-23/KN-24 (Hwasong-11 family); this could not be checked in the original NYT article, which was not accessible. On missiles, the report says Pyongyang is working on more powerful warheads, missiles that can penetrate modern missile defences, shorter launch preparation, harder-to-detect launchers (including tunnel-concealed mobile launchers), submarine-based systems and a shift to solid fuel; some missiles can carry either a conventional or a nuclear warhead. In early October North Korea tested a tactical ballistic missile it claims is nuclear-capable: South Korea and Japan estimated a range of about 700 km, Pyongyang claimed 1,000 km. Five days later South Korea test-fired its own hypersonic ballistic missile for the first time, observed by President Lee Jae-myung, which the NYT reads as growing unease in Seoul. On drones, Ukrainian officials claim Russia has recruited 12,000 North Korean workers to build Geran-2 drones at facilities in Tatarstan, and Russian officers have reportedly been sent to North Korea to train drone operators. Russia has also helped North Korea develop and mass-produce its own unmanned systems; Kim Jong Un visited a drone factory last month and photos suggest series production has started. In a September drill a drone swarm attacked a target island while cruise and ballistic missiles struck simultaneously from different directions — a tactic that, according to the NYT and Hong Min (KINU), could force South Korea and its allies into very costly air-defence upgrades. Background: in a May 2026 Lowy Institute analysis, Ponomarenko noted that North Korean troops fought in the Kursk region and remain active in Kursk Oblast, that Russian Defence Minister Belousov decorated them for the Kursk operations and announced a 2027–31 cooperation plan, and estimated North Korea's earnings from arms deals with Russia at about US$14.4 billion, alongside technology transfers in drones, loitering munitions, ballistic and air-defence systems. The individual claims about Russian assistance rest largely on analysts and Ukrainian/South Korean officials and have not been independently verified.

Zelensky: Ukraine received only 24 Patriot interceptors from the US in the past 90 days

2026-10-24

In an interview with ABC News, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said the United States had supplied Ukraine with only 24 interceptor missiles for the Patriot air defence system over the past 90 days, Militarnyi reported on 24 October, linking to the interview. He compared that with the opening of the Middle East war, when he said countries fired about 2,000 interceptors in the first 48 hours. Ukraine got 24 from its "strategic partner" in three months. Zelensky also confirmed earlier reports that Washington is holding up the paperwork for interceptors that European countries have already paid for. A Ukrainian official, cited by The Kyiv Independent, said US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth has the Patriot contracts on his desk and is not signing them. Officials in Kyiv see the delay as political pressure that puts civilians at risk. The US government has called similar claims untrue. Patriot is Ukraine's main system against Russian ballistic missiles such as the Iskander-M and the Kinzhal. The figure of 24 missiles comes from the Ukrainian head of state and has not been independently confirmed; the US has published no delivery numbers.

Zelensky sanctions Russian missile makers and military-industry suppliers, including RUSAL group companies

2026-10-24

President Volodymyr Zelensky has enacted two decisions of Ukraine's National Security and Defence Council. They impose sanctions on Russian companies and individuals involved in producing ballistic and cruise missiles, drones, military aircraft and other weapons, Ukrainska Pravda reports, citing the President's Office. The first list covers 51 individuals and 44 legal entities involved in producing and supplying components for the Iskander, Kalibr, Kh-101 and Kinzhal missiles. Named entities include the G.S. Petrov Institute of Plastics, the Kometa Corporation for Special-Purpose Space Systems, VitaReaktiv and Promenergo. The second package targets 32 Russian nationals and 35 Russian companies that supply high-precision industrial equipment, electronics, raw materials and components used to build missiles, drones, armoured vehicles and military aircraft. Companies of the RUSAL aluminium group were also sanctioned; according to the President's Office, their products are used in Russian weapons production. The report names no non-Russian suppliers. The President's Office says it will work with international partners to synchronise the measures with their sanctions regimes.
