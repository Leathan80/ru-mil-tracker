2026-10-26

According to Defense News, the company says Contra can down fast attack drones for under $100,000 per shot; it is still in testing, is expected to be market-ready in the second half of 2027, and one contract has already been signed. CEO and co-founder Gundbert Scherf framed the design as a direct response to Russia's Geran drones: the early, propeller-driven types were intercepted at rates of about 90%, but Russia now fields thousands of Geran-4 and Geran-5 drones flying at 400–700 km/h, and according to Scherf almost half of them now get through. Helsing says the seeker is built from widely available components, free of Chinese supply-chain or US export-control dependencies, using two independent European supply chains. The companion AD-1 'Inferno' is jet-powered, flies at about 700 km/h with a 20 km range and is meant as the standard countermeasure. Defense Express describes Shershen, a Ukrainian modular short-range surface-to-air missile system intended to engage aircraft, helicopters, cruise missiles, UAVs and precision-guided munitions, including under heavy electronic warfare. According to the developers, its radar detects targets out to 85 km and tracks up to 24 at once; depending on the missile, the engagement envelope is 1.5–25 km in range and 20 m–10 km in altitude, against targets up to 1,000 m/s. It can engage six targets simultaneously with a single-shot kill probability of 0.75. Its open architecture accepts Soviet-era R-27 and R-73 and Western AIM-9 and AIM-132 air-to-air missiles, launched from the Boryey launcher. Two new missiles resemble the R-27 family: the R-287T with the Ukrainian GS-203T imaging infrared seeker, said to lock onto a typical target at least 30 km head-on and over 100 km from behind, and the R-287 with a Ukrainian active radar seeker that, unlike the semi-active R-27R/ER, needs no continuous target illumination (comparable in concept to NASAMS with AMRAAM). The article links this to other Ukrainian SAM work such as the 'S-300 Clone' and GAM-67 under the US-Ukrainian FrankenSAM initiative, but stresses that none of these has entered serial production and technical challenges remain. Shershen has completed testing and its components are codified, yet no procurement decision has been taken. On cost, the article compares Norway's 2023 NASAMS purchase for Ukraine ($860 million for four batteries, about $215 million each) and a current NASAMS III price of €410 million with the developers' preliminary export estimate for Shershen of €150–170 million; the price of the active-radar missiles is unknown. Developers claim Shershen could be built much faster than the wait for new NASAMS or IRIS-T SLM.
