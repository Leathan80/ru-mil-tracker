«Переводять із тилових посад у піхоту»: на Донеччині росіяни компенсують втрати штурмовиків тиловиками (ArmyInform: ''They are moving people from rear posts into the infantry': in Donetsk region the Russians are replacing assault-troop losses with rear-echelon personnel') — Azov group intelligence officer says Russia lacks operational reserves after record October losses and leans on attached units such as the 76th Air Assault Division

2026-11-11SINGLE SOURCE

According to an intelligence officer of Ukraine's 'Azov' group of forces, speaking exclusively to ArmyInform, Russian forces in Donetsk region are trying to sustain their combat power by shuffling units between sectors and transferring servicemen from rear posts into assault infantry. Ukraine claims Russia lost 13,624 personnel killed and wounded in the Azov group's zone in October, a quarter of Russia's losses along the whole front that month, and a record 54,400 across the front overall. ArmyInform presents the personnel transfers as a consequence of these losses and of Russia's limited available reserves. The officer said that the effect of the losses differs by sector, with fighting intensity falling on some axes and rising on others. In Donetsk region, he said, Russia has no operational reserves on his axis: it moves the same unit from axis to axis, reassigns personnel from support units and other rear posts to the infantry, and cuts the establishment of artillery and tank units because of their low effectiveness. People are usually sent to the infantry or, 'if they are lucky', trained as UAV operators. Despite this redistribution, he said, the manpower shortage is increasingly visible, so Russia places units that are not part of its 'Center' group of forces under that group's operational control; he named the 76th Air Assault Division as one such unit, whose replenishment partly falls to its parent command. Single source: a report by the Ukrainian MoD outlet ArmyInform, based on an unnamed Ukrainian intelligence officer; the loss figures are Ukrainian claims and none of this is independently confirmed.

Sources: ArmyInform

«Рідкісний звір: дронарі «Рейду» уразили ворожу важку вогнеметну систему «Сонцепьок»» (ArmyInform: 'A rare beast: «Reid» drone operators hit an enemy TOS-1A «Solntsepyok» heavy flamethrower system') — Ukraine claims the TOS-1A belonged to the CBRN defence company of Russia's 76th Air Assault Division.

2026-11-09SINGLE SOURCE

Drone operators of the 4th battalion of the 413th «Reid» regiment of Ukraine's Unmanned Systems Forces say they located and struck a shelter in occupied Donetsk region in which a Russian TOS-1A «Solntsepyok» heavy flamethrower system was housed. The unit published a video of the strike and its aftermath on social media; the article does not state the extent of the damage beyond describing the system as hit. According to the article, the vehicle belonged to the radiological, chemical and biological defence (RKhBZ) company of the Russian 76th Air Assault Division. The article states that Russia uses such systems against positions and fortifications and that destroying them weakens the enemy's firepower. Single source: Ukrainian MoD media platform ArmyInform, based on a video published by the Ukrainian unit itself. The strike result and the attribution of the vehicle to the 76th Air Assault Division have not been independently confirmed.

«ВСУ нарушили режим прекращения огня в Алешках» (WarGonzo: 'Ukrainian forces violated the ceasefire regime in Oleshky') — Russian milbloggers on the Oleshky humanitarian mission: VDV sapper killed, Ukrainian drone overwatch and claimed drone strikes on civilian cars

2026-10-30SINGLE SOURCEMILBLOGGER

Russian milbloggers covered the humanitarian mission to occupied Oleshky (Alyoshki) in Kherson Oblast on 30–31 October from the Russian side. WarGonzo, citing the Russian MoD, reported on 30 October that Guards Sergeant Andrei Vedernikov, a sapper of the 98th Guards Airborne (VDV) Division, was killed while clearing mines from the roads into Oleshky to open the way for the humanitarian convoy; the channel repeated Vladimir Putin's claim that Ukrainian forces had mined the approaches to the town and so brought it close to humanitarian catastrophe. WarGonzo founder Semyon Pegov travelled into Oleshky with the convoy on 30 October. The channel claims that since Ukrainian forces returned to Kherson in 2022 the town has faced daily shelling and, as FPV drones spread, constant air strikes, mined supply roads and attacks on civilian vehicles bringing in food, while Ukraine 'cynically' blamed Russia for blockading it. Dva Mayora reported that members of the team of fellow milblogger 'Arkhangel Spetsnaza' were part of the group covering the mission and showed foreign journalists what the channel calls the weapons Ukraine uses against civilians, including a US-made 'Hornet' drone with a Starlink terminal on its back. The channel also highlighted that the Ukrainian side kept reconnaissance drones, launched from across the Dnipro, over the town throughout the mission. A post by war correspondent Aleksandr Kots, republished by WarGonzo on 31 October, dismisses counts of how many evacuees chose buses to Ukraine rather than Russia as manipulative: he says logistics were planned from the outset so that people could go to Ukraine, that about 22,000 people lived in Oleshky when Russia left Kherson in 2022 against up to 2,000 at the time of the evacuation, and that the number that matters is those who refused to leave and for whom food, medicine and fuel were brought in, which he says exceeds the number who left in either direction. He wrote that 'the cameras have left' and the truce expires the next day, and predicted that Ukrainian drones would again mine the road and strike ambulances, hospitals and residents. On 31 October WarGonzo posted that Ukrainian forces had violated the ceasefire in Oleshky, with a Ukrainian drone striking a civilian car (the full post could not be retrieved; this rests on its headline). Fighterbomber, citing contacts in Oleshky, wrote the same day that Ukrainians had begun attacking cars carrying civilians with drones and mining roads from drones, that the cases were still isolated but increasing, and that the ceasefire would probably be over by evening, which he called expected. Single source: WarGonzo, Dva Mayora and Fighterbomber are personal/editorial pro-war Telegram channels, not a Russian MoD position; milbloggers can contradict each other and the MoD. The claimed ceasefire violations are not independently verified.

2026-10-29SINGLE SOURCEMILBLOGGER

In two posts, Dva Mayora says front-line soldiers keep sending it images of downed Ukrainian drones fitted with Starlink antennas. One such drone, the channel says, was brought down by the mobile fire group (MOG) of the 108th Guards Air Assault Regiment. In the second post, whose title puns on 'MOG', the channel says Russian forces shot down an FPV drone carrying Starlink. It argues that putting satellite equipment on a disposable short-range drone shows the Ukrainian armed forces have Starlink terminals in abundance. The channel puts this in a political frame. It mentions Western media reports that Elon Musk has moved from generous support for Ukraine to open criticism of Kyiv and wonders how that will affect aid. It also links the Starlink sightings to the negotiation track with the United States, which it accuses of pressuring Russia militarily while tempting Russian business with the lifting of sanctions. The posts give no technical details on the drones or how the Starlink terminals were integrated, and the claims are not independently verified. Meduza: Russia’s main push in northern Donbas closes on Kramatorsk as forces cut the Dobropillia–Kramatorsk road and Moscow claims Dobropillia; Ukraine forms two Azov-led groupings to hold Kramatorsk and Sloviansk

2026-10-22

A front-line analysis by Meduza’s explainer team describes Russia’s current offensive towards the Kramatorsk–Sloviansk urban area as the decisive push in the battle for Donbas. In Meduza’s account, the most acute crisis is between Dobropillia and Kramatorsk, possibly worse than the August 2025 breakthrough in the same area. Russian troops have cut the Dobropillia–Kramatorsk road and are storming Dobropillia. The Russian Defence Ministry claims full capture of the town and released videos as proof; Meduza says some clips show clear signs of AI processing, while others appear genuine. Meduza reports that Russian command moved additional forces from eastern Zaporizhzhia Oblast and repeated, on a larger scale, last year’s failed manoeuvre east of Dobropillia. Russian forces retook Kucheriv Yar and part of Zolotyi Kolodiaz, though control of the latter is contested, and have got behind its defenders near Petrivka. Further Russian groupings are pushing towards Kramatorsk’s south-eastern outskirts via Novoandriivka, past Kostiantynivka towards Bilokuzminovka and the airfield on the city’s southern edge, and along the Bakhmut–Sloviansk highway. Meduza says Ukrainian command is unsure of Moscow’s plan. Kyiv previously assessed that Russia would envelop the urban area from the west (towards Barvinkove) and east (towards Izyum) to force a withdrawal without urban fighting. Russia may now instead be converging on Kramatorsk, with a pre-war population of 150,000, from several directions to storm it, with Sloviansk next. To hold the area, Ukraine created two operational groupings led by Azov veterans. The “Center” grouping under Andriy Biletsky, former commander of the 3rd Army Corps, defends Sloviansk. The “Azov” grouping under Denys Prokopenko, former commander of the 12th National Guard Brigade, covers Kramatorsk. According to Meduza, Biletsky’s forces pushed Russia’s 20th Combined Arms Army back west of the Chornyi Zherebets river, where it is now trying to hold a line between Torske and Zarichne. Reinforced with two brigades from Zaporizhzhia, they counterattacked Russia’s “South” group at Rai-Oleksandrivka, east of Sloviansk. Russian infiltration between the Siverskyi Donets–Donbas canal and the Bakhmut–Sloviansk road continues, and the 25th Combined Arms Army is pressing into Lyman. In the south, Meduza notes that Ukraine is dismantling its strike grouping at the junction of the Zaporizhzhia, Donetsk and Dnipropetrovsk regions to feed the Donbas defence. Russia’s “Dnipro” group is attempting to storm Orikhiv. Airborne (VDV) forces committed from the reserve advanced south-west of the town; Ukraine reportedly may have pushed them out of Novopavlivka, without visual confirmation. Russia’s “East” group has resumed a broad-front offensive towards Orikhiv and the Orikhiv–Novomykolaivka supply road, infiltrating Omelnyk. All positional claims come from Meduza’s reading of published video and statements from both sides and could not be independently verified.

This is the personal, editorial voice of Dva Majors (Два майора), a Russian pro-war Telegram channel, not a position of the Russian Ministry of Defence. Milbloggers can contradict each other and the MoD; the content has not been independently verified. These are fundraising posts by the channel's own charity foundation (BF Dva Majors). The posts illustrate that Russian frontline units continue to obtain basic tactical equipment (commercial reconnaissance drones, batteries, relays, radios, generators and anti-thermal camouflage) through public crowdfunding rather than solely through state supply. Головна загроза прийде разом із туманами: «Перун» про плани росіян на Запоріжжі

2026-09-26SINGLE SOURCE

Dmytro 'Perun' Filatov, commander of Ukraine's 1st Separate Assault Regiment, told Suspilne that Russian forces on the Zaporizhzhia axis have reduced the number of assaults, which he attributes to Russia choosing another direction for its main effort, apparently Dobropillia. Infiltration attempts still happen daily, mostly by groups of two or three infantrymen, countered by strike-and-search actions along Ukrainian supply routes. According to Filatov, Russian forces fire little artillery at his unit because Ukrainian counter-battery fire quickly destroys guns after they fire; there is therefore no classic artillery preparation before infantry actions, and Russian fire is concentrated on drones. Russian crews hide their guns with revetments, camouflage nets and dug-in positions, and limit their firing arc so they do not have to cut trees in tree lines. Filatov named the former Kakhovka reservoir bed as a particular threat: dense vegetation, high humidity and long autumn and winter fogs make it hard to observe and control. He says Russian troops used these conditions last year to enter the village of Prymorske, which Ukrainian forces now fully hold again, and expects the area to become very hard to hold with current forces as the weather worsens. He does not expect a full-scale offensive on Zaporizhzhia, but the same infiltration tactics under more favourable conditions, requiring no extra units. He identified the Russian force in the sector as the 'Dnepr' grouping, which includes airborne (VDV) troops. Caveat: this is single-source reporting from ArmyInform, the news agency of the Ukrainian Ministry of Defence. The claims about Russian plans have not been independently verified.
