Russian government bill would let bankrupt debtors keep up to 10 million rubles in assets if they sign a contract to fight in Ukraine, with creditors taking the loss

2026-11-20

The Russian government has submitted a bill to the State Duma that would let people in bankruptcy proceedings keep property worth up to 10 million rubles if they sign a contract with the Defense Ministry to fight in Ukraine, Meduza reports. Under the draft, creditors' total claims must not exceed 10 million rubles, bankruptcy proceedings must have begun before 1 November 2026, and the debtor must sign the contract after the law takes effect. People filing for bankruptcy on or after 1 November 2026 would not qualify for now, although Meduza notes that the authorities have shifted similar cut-off dates before. The bill has not yet been passed. Creditors would bear the loss, and the state would not compensate them. According to Meduza, banks and other organisations could classify the written-off loans as bad debts to reduce their corporate income tax. Some claims would not count toward the cap and could still be pursued after bankruptcy: compensation for harm caused by a crime, for death or injury, unpaid wages, and child support or alimony. Debts above the cap would not be relieved, and asset sales or payments completed before the bankruptcy petition would not be reopened. The bill builds on a December 2024 provision that allowed up to 10 million rubles of loan debt to be written off for people who signed a contract. A spring amendment moved its dates to 1 May 2026, so people who signed between December 2024 and April 2026 lost that right. The Association of Russian Banks called this a technical error and asked the Central Bank and the Duma to fix it. The bill's explanatory note gives no estimate of how many recruits it could bring in. Russian courts declared more than 425,000 people bankrupt in 2024 and more than 561,000 in 2025, and Meduza suggests the scheme could involve tens of thousands of people. The author, Denis Dmitriev, argues that the government wants new recruits at almost any cost while those already serving get less help.
