Russia hopes its 80-missile cruiser Admiral Nakhimov will return to service after nearly 30 years of repairs

2026-08-30SINGLE SOURCE

SINGLE SOURCE — Ukrainian defence media (Defense Express), not independently verified. The report says Russian state media are circulating claims that the heavy nuclear-powered battlecruiser Admiral Nakhimov (Project 1144 Orlan, NATO reporting name Kirov-class) will return to the Russian Navy's combat composition after almost three decades under repair. The cruiser is said to be completing factory and sea trials, with state trials expected by the end of 2026, after which it could rejoin the fleet. Defense Express frames this timeline as uncertain. The ship was officially placed under repair in August 1999 (in fact transferred to the shipyard in July 1997), just nine years after entering service in 1988. Repair and modernisation costs are now put at over 200 billion rubles (about $2.32 billion), while earlier sources cited figures as high as $5 billion. After modernisation the Nakhimov is said to carry 80 launch cells for Kalibr, Onyx and Zircon missiles, which the article says would make it the most heavily armed ship in the Russian fleet. The source does not break down the count by missile type. Defense Express questions concentrating so much firepower in a single hull, noting that weapons such as naval drones have emerged during the long repair period. There is no independent confirmation of the operational status, armament fit or timeline.

О способности БПЛА «отменить» надводные корабли

2026-08-30SINGLE SOURCE

An opinion piece on the Russian military portal Topwar (by Andrey Kolobov) argues that unmanned systems — both aerial UAVs and uncrewed surface vessels (USVs) — will not make the crewed surface fleet obsolete. English gloss of the title: 'On the ability of UAVs to "cancel" surface ships.' The author concedes that Ukrainian USVs scored notable successes against Russia's Black Sea Fleet and that the cost asymmetry is real, but concludes that drones 'have not become, and will not become, the ultimate weapon of naval warfare.' He rests the case on two core limitations. First, speed: cheap strike drones of the Shahed-136 class cruise at only 150-180 km/h and take nearly two hours to cover 350 km, versus minutes for missiles, so a large drone salvo gives the enemy long warning. Second, guidance: affordable drones use GNSS plus inertial navigation and cannot track a moving ship; doing so requires expensive satellite-internet infrastructure (Starlink/OneWeb) and real-time targeting. Citing Soviet Cold War experience tracking US carrier strike groups, he argues the hard problem is, and remains, finding a moving squadron at all. He frames the change in naval warfare as evolutionary rather than revolutionary. Fixed caveat: this is a single-source Russian opinion/analysis article (Topwar), not an official statement and not independently verified. It reflects an ongoing debate in Russian military circles about the implications of the Black Sea Fleet losses, but the arguments and figures are the author's own.

Sources: topwar

Russian milbloggers claim Ukrainian drones are increasingly hitting Turkish merchant ships in the Black Sea

2026-08-26SINGLE SOURCEMILBLOGGER

Russian milbloggers (Два майора / Two Majors and WarGonzo) continue to claim that Ukrainian drones are increasingly striking Turkish merchant ships in the Black Sea. Earlier posts (WarGonzo 26 Aug, Two Majors 27 Aug, topwar 27 Aug) described claimed strikes on ro-ro and cargo vessels such as the Lider Bordo Mavi near Tuapse and on grain ships queued at the Sulina anchorage. The thrust is that Kyiv is deliberately waging a campaign against shipping that calls at Russian ports, and that Ankara is doing too little to stop it. In a 29 August post, Two Majors relayed a reader message about the container ship RMS Team of the Turkish carrier Akkon Lines. The vessel was struck by drones in the Black Sea on 21 August while sailing from Gemlik toward Novorossiysk. A fire spread from the accommodation and engine room to the containers; the crew evacuated, with one seafarer missing. According to the post, Akkon declared the ship and cargo a total loss and suspended its container service to Novorossiysk indefinitely as of 27 August. Unlike the earlier vessel claims, the RMS Team strike and its commercial fallout are corroborated by Western reporting (including Bloomberg, Kyiv Independent, CGTN and the maritime trade press), which also notes that MSC suspended Novorossiysk bookings after the attack. Even so, the milblogger posts are personal, editorial voices and not the Russian Ministry of Defence; milbloggers can contradict each other and the MoD, and their wider portrayal of a systematic Ukrainian campaign against Turkish shipping is not independently verified.

Russia's Drone Defenses Are Getting Absurd: 'Barbecue' Cages Now Cover Ship Guns

2026-08-19SINGLE SOURCE

SINGLE SOURCE — Ukrainian publication (Defense Express), not independently corroborated. Based on photographs published by Flag Rodiny (the Black Sea Fleet's own newspaper), Defense Express analyzes a vessel it assesses as a Project 12700 Alexandrit-class minesweeper (likely one of three: Georgy Kurbatov, Ivan Antonov, or Vladimir Yemelyanov). Both the forward gun — an AK-306, a 30mm six-barrel Gatling-type automatic cannon standard to this ship class — and an aft-mounted gun, assessed as a 30mm twin-barrel AK-230 (an addition not originally fitted to this class), appear enclosed under improvised 'barbecue' cages: metal lattice and rebar structures covered with netting, meant to trigger premature detonation of incoming kamikaze drones before they reach the hull or the guns themselves. Elsewhere on the ship, netting is stretched across other sections and sandbags are placed in front of guns and machine guns. The notable drawback: the cages around the gun mounts likely restrict the guns' elevation and traverse angles, potentially undermining the very weapons meant to engage incoming aerial threats — a gun tasked with shooting down drones ends up caged in itself. This fits a broader pattern Defense Express has repeatedly flagged this year: similar improvised cages and grille structures have also been observed on Bulava-carrying nuclear submarines, on the missile ship Serpukhov, and on 'shadow fleet' vessels, spanning the Black Sea, Baltic, and Pacific fleets. The assessment remains indicative — it rests on photo analysis without official Russian confirmation of the ship's identity or weapon configuration.

Sources: Defense Express

Russian Corvette Soobrazitelny Encounters Danish USV Near Bornholm During Shadow Fleet Escort

2026-07-23SINGLE SOURCE

On 21 July 2026, the Russian Project 20380 corvette Soobrazitelny reportedly closed to within about 200 meters of the Danish uncrewed surface vessel (USV) Stormborn X-Wave roughly 30 km southeast of Bornholm in the Baltic Sea, according to Ukrainian outlet Defense Express, citing Danish broadcaster TV 2 and the Stormborn organization. The X-Wave was conducting a routine test of its radar and hydroacoustic sensor suite when the corvette approached with its AIS transponder switched off, then launched a reconnaissance drone that circled the USV at close range to inspect it before the ship continued on its way. According to the report, the USV's onboard AI managed to identify the corvette's hull number (P531), crew visible on deck, an embarked Ka-27 helicopter, and tracked the Russian drone by radar, though it reportedly sometimes misclassified the drone as the corvette itself. The collected data was said to be passed to Danish naval surveillance authorities, who declined to comment publicly. Days after the encounter, the same corvette was reportedly observed escorting Russian "shadow fleet" tankers along the European coast, a role Defense Express links to protecting the tankers against possible Ukrainian drone strikes — illustrating a dual maritime role attributed to Russian escort vessels in the Baltic: shadowing NATO-linked surveillance assets while safeguarding sanctioned oil shipments. SINGLE SOURCE — this item is based on one source, the Ukrainian defense outlet Defense Express, itself citing Danish broadcaster TV 2 and the Stormborn organization, and has not been independently corroborated by official Danish, NATO, or Russian statements.

Two Unmanned Boats of Ukrainian Armed Forces Destroyed in the Black Sea

2026-07-20SINGLE SOURCE

SINGLE SOURCE — Russian state media (TASS). The Russian Ministry of Defense claims that the Black Sea Fleet destroyed two unmanned (drone) boats belonging to the Ukrainian Armed Forces in the Black Sea. TASS reports this based on the ministry's daily loss summary, without further detail on the exact location, timing, or method by which the boats were allegedly neutralized. The claim is presented as part of a broader daily rundown in which the ministry also states it intercepted two Neptune-MD long-range missiles, eight guided aerial bombs, a US-made HIMARS munition, and 678 fixed-wing UAVs. This report fits a recurring pattern of Russian claims about intercepting Ukrainian naval drones, which have long been used against Russian naval vessels and, more recently, against targets linked to Russia's "shadow fleet." The Defense Ministry typically publishes such destruction claims daily as part of cumulative loss figures, and TASS, as a state outlet, relays these figures without independent verification. There is no Ukrainian confirmation and no independent verification of this incident available. Both Russia and Ukraine routinely publish mutual destruction and interception claims in this conflict that are rarely independently verifiable; this entry should therefore be read strictly as a representation of what the Russian Ministry of Defense states, not as a verified fact.
