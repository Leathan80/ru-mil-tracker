Russia launches Project 20380 corvette Grozny for the Pacific Fleet

2026-10-25

The Amur Shipbuilding Plant in Komsomolsk-on-Amur, in Russia's Far East, has launched the multipurpose corvette Grozny, a Project 20380 (Steregushchiy-class) ship for the Russian Navy's Pacific Fleet, the fleet's press service said (via Interfax). Defence Blog dates the ceremony to 23 October; Militarnyi gives 24 October. The ship was laid down on 23 August 2021, so the launch came more than five years later. Grozny was launched only partially complete: according to Militarnyi its crew began training on 5 October, and the ship is to be commissioned "in the coming years". The launch report comes from the Russian Pacific Fleet, an official Russian source. Grozny is the fifth Project 20380 corvette built at the Amur plant and, according to Militarnyi, the ninth of the project overall. The Pacific Fleet already operates four ships of the class: Sovershenny, Gromkiy, Aldar Tsydenzhapov and Rezkiy. The lead ship, Steregushchiy, was laid down in late 2001 and went to the Baltic Fleet in early 2008. Militarnyi lists these specifications: a displacement of 2,200 tonnes, a length of 104.5 m, a top speed of 27 knots and a crew of about 100. Armament is one 100 mm A-190 gun, two 30 mm AK-630M close-in weapon systems, eight Kh-35 anti-ship missiles, twelve 9M96 surface-to-air missiles (Redut system) and eight 324 mm torpedoes. The ship can also carry a Ka-27PL helicopter and an Orlan-10 UAV. Militarnyi places the launch against wider losses and adaptations in the class. The Baltic Fleet corvette Boikiy was badly damaged by a Ukrainian drone attack on 3 June 2026 while in dry dock in Kronstadt. Since late July, ships of the class have been fitted with extra machine-gun mounts against drones: the Baltic Fleet's Stoikiy was seen with 12.7 mm Kord and 7.62 mm PKM machine guns.

Drone strike sinks Comoros-flagged cargo ship Khoryv off Romania, killing one Ukrainian sailor; Romania and EU blame Russia

2026-10-23

The Comoros-flagged cargo ship Khoryv sank in the Black Sea on 23 October 2026 after being struck by a drone. According to the Romanian navy, the vessel sent a distress signal at around 10:00 local time roughly 13 nautical miles (24 km) east of the port of Constanța, just outside Romania's territorial waters. Defence Blog reports it was sailing from Ereğli (Türkiye) to the Romanian port of Sulina carrying ferrous metals. The ship caught fire and capsized. Most sources put the crew at 13 (10 Ukrainians, 3 Azerbaijanis); Ukraine's Foreign Ministry cited 14. Nine sailors were rescued and one body recovered; three remain missing, and per Ukraine's MFA the missing are Ukrainian. The dead sailor was Ukrainian, and four Ukrainian survivors, including the captain, were hospitalised. Search efforts continued in rough seas. The drone type has not been reported. Romanian President Nicușor Dan said the Khoryv was hit by a drone and called it "part of Russia's escalation process in its war in Ukraine", accusing Moscow of stepping up attacks on NATO's eastern flank. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said on X she was deeply alarmed by "yet another Russian escalation in the Black Sea" and wrote that "these reckless attacks target innocent lives and openly defy international law". Ukraine's MFA initially said only that a drone strike "may have been" the cause. No Russian response to this specific incident was reported. Moscow has previously described its strikes on vessels as retaliation for Ukrainian attacks on Russian or Russia-linked ships. The sinking is part of a series of drone attacks on merchant shipping off Romania and Bulgaria. Citing Reuters, the sources count at least seven ships hit in two weeks, with around 30 sailors dead or missing. Romanian broadcaster Digi24 counts 11 vessels in recent weeks, seven of them near Romania, mostly around the Sulina Channel, now the main route for Ukrainian grain and vegetable-oil exports. Earlier this month the Turkish-owned Royad Mammadov sank after a drone strike, killing two crew. Romania's Supreme Council of National Defence met on 22 October. On 20 October NATO announced additional maritime patrol aircraft, radars and drones for the Black Sea. Ukraine, Romania and Bulgaria are pressing the alliance to do more to protect commercial shipping, and the Croatian Seafarers' Union has asked for the waters off Romania and Bulgaria to be declared a combat zone.

UK parliamentary committee finds a 'serious anti-submarine warfare gap' in the Royal Navy that weakens its ability to counter Russian submarines and undersea sabotage in the High North

2026-10-22

The UK House of Commons Defence Committee, in an inquiry report on defence in the High North published on 22 October, concludes that the Royal Navy has a 'serious anti-submarine warfare gap' that weakens Britain's ability to respond to Russia's submarine and underwater-sabotage threat, Defense News reports. Only five Type 23 anti-submarine frigates are in service against a stated requirement of eight, and the number with a towed array sonar could stay as low as five for the next three years. These frigates are described as critical for locating Russian submarines in the High North and in home waters, including to protect the UK's ballistic-missile submarines leaving and entering Faslane. The committee recommends that no further towed-sonar Type 23s leave service until replaced one-for-one by the eight Type 26 frigates ordered from BAE Systems, the first due in 2028. If two UK Type 26s were diverted to Norway, which ordered five in 2025, the Royal Navy would not reach eight towed-array frigates until 2037. The report also notes the lack of a credible under-ice capability for the Astute-class attack submarines, and warns that uncrewed systems of the Hybrid Navy programme must be proven in Arctic conditions, including resistance to jamming, before they replace crewed capability. According to the committee, melting ice is exposing previously inaccessible Russian bases, and Russia seeks both to defend them and to project force into the Atlantic and UK waters, threatening transatlantic supply lines, undersea infrastructure and the UK nuclear deterrent. It asked the Ministry of Defence for a classified assessment of capability gaps in a 2030 High North conflict fought without US support. The report reflects a British parliamentary assessment of UK readiness rather than new information on Russian naval activity.

2026-10-15

A US Navy Boeing P-8A Poseidon maritime patrol aircraft has flown a low-altitude patrol off Bulgaria's Black Sea coast near the ports of Varna and Burgas, Militarnyi and Defence Blog report, citing the flight-tracking outlet ItaMilRadar (report of 19 October) and Flightradar24 data. The aircraft crossed Bulgaria from the west near Shumen, passed over the Varna area, made several turns over the western Black Sea east of Varna and then circled off Burgas, near the maritime border with Türkiye, at times at about 2,740 m. According to ItaMilRadar it was the first such low-level US Navy P-8A flight over this part of the Black Sea in several months. The P-8A, based on the Boeing 737, is built for anti-submarine warfare and surface surveillance: its radar can pick out small boats, it carries electro-optical/infrared sensors and sonobuoys, and flying low makes it easier to identify small objects such as uncrewed surface vessels. Militarnyi notes that the long low-level profile points to maritime patrol rather than an electronic-intelligence mission. No official statement on the purpose of the flight has been published. The flight follows Bulgarian Prime Minister Rumen Radev's request to allies to help identify where the drones came from and to strengthen air and sea surveillance off the coast. What struck the Chelsea 3 on 13 October is still disputed: the crew spoke of three drones and Bulgarian reports of aerial drones, while Russian Telegram channels published images of hull damage they attributed to a drone boat. Drones have struck at least eight merchant vessels near Romania and Bulgaria since 5 October, according to Euromaidan Press; Reuters counts at least five cargo ships hit in ten days, with 23 sailors dead or missing. The tanker Yara J (Comoros flag) was hit inside Romanian territorial waters off Sulina late on 14 October and towed to Constanța. The San Marino-flagged chemical tanker Wine Trader was struck early on 15 October about a mile outside Romanian waters, and seven of its 12 crew were still missing on 16 October. Other ships hit include the Sea Destiny, inside Romanian waters according to NATO, and the Dania J at the Ukrainian Danube port of Izmail. President Nicușor Dan called the strikes obvious Russian attempts to destabilise Romania and Bulgaria, but also said it was too early to say definitively that Russia launched the drones; Radev said the attacks serve Russian interests but that Sofia "cannot prove it". At the same time, Geran-4 jet drones hit two more cargo ships in Ukrainian ports, near Mykolaiv and at Chornomorsk. In an analysis for Euromaidan Press, David Axe frames the campaign as Russia's answer to Ukraine's drone war on Russian Black Sea shipping, which has cut Russian Black Sea wheat exports by about 75% year on year, and says Russia now also targets Ukraine's Danube grain ports of Izmail, Reni and Kiliia. The P-8A sortie is the first visible US surveillance step off Bulgaria since the strikes began; its sensor mix fits a search for both aerial and surface drones and their launch points. Who launched the drones off Bulgaria has still not been officially established.

The US is weighing a partial easing of sanctions on 'shadow fleet' tankers to carry non-sanctioned oil, days after licensing trade in Russian diesel, according to Reuters.

2026-10-15

The Trump administration is discussing a partial easing of sanctions on so-called shadow fleet tankers that have carried oil from Russia, Iran and Venezuela, Reuters reported citing a US official on 14 October; Ukrainska Pravda, Meduza and Euromaidan Press relayed the report. The talks centre on letting "legal oil" - crude or products not sanctioned by Washington, so not Russian or Iranian oil - move on previously blacklisted vessels. The stated aim is to bring down record freight rates and get more oil and products out of the Persian Gulf. The White House did not respond to Reuters; Reuters noted that high fuel prices are a political risk for Trump ahead of the 3 November midterms. The move follows a 9 October Treasury licence allowing trade in Russian-origin diesel until 7 April 2027, which Zelensky reportedly called weak and Democratic senators Shaheen, Schumer and Warren called a betrayal of Ukraine; Republican senator Thom Tillis questioned its legality. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov called the diesel deal mutually beneficial. According to Euromaidan Press, UK minister John Healey urged at IMF meetings on 15 October that tough sanctions on Russia, including on shipping services sustaining the shadow fleet, be maintained.
