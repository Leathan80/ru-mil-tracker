Not Just More Drones: How Russia Is Changing Its Air Attacks With Jet-Powered Shaheds

2026-09-21SINGLE SOURCE

Russia has launched roughly 7,700 jet-powered ("reactive") Shahed/Geran drones since introducing the variant, Ukrainian Air Force spokesperson Colonel Yuriy Ihnat said. In September 2026 Russia fired an estimated 1,800-1,900 jet-powered units, down from about 2,800 in July, but drones are now airborne nearly around the clock. Ihnat stressed the change is not just about growing volume but about employment: strike packages increasingly alternate or combine jet-powered Shaheds with conventional propeller-driven ones and other weapons rather than relying on a single type. In early September, Russia reportedly launched several waves of large numbers of classic (propeller) Shaheds toward Ukraine's western regions via northern routes, preceded by heavy waves of jet-powered drones — a sequenced combination rather than simultaneous mixed use. Weather reportedly influences which drone variant Russia deploys on a given night, since conditions affect Ukrainian detection and engagement. Ukrainian forces report intercepting roughly 60% of jet-powered UAVs at various flight stages, with aviation playing a significant role, but Ihnat cautioned that not all current Ukrainian air-defense systems can effectively engage the faster jet-powered variant, and available interceptor missiles remain insufficient in quantity. Ihnat said Ukraine needs manufacturers to deliver cheaper, more effective interception systems at scale, and faster warning from Western partners on missile launches to improve response time. This is a single-source report from a Ukrainian military-affiliated outlet, not independently corroborated, and the Ukrainian side has an interest in emphasizing the evolving and growing nature of the threat it is defending against.

Kalashnikov Unveils 'Zveroboy' Drone-Interceptor Air Defense Complex

2026-09-21SINGLE SOURCE

Kalashnikov Concern has publicly unveiled "Zveroboy" ("Beast-Hunter"), a dedicated counter-drone interception complex, at the Fourth All-Russian Weapons Forum in Moscow on 15 September 2026. Unlike passive armor add-ons, Zveroboy is an active air-defense system built specifically to intercept small reconnaissance/attack drones: it comes in stationary, trailer-mounted, and vehicle-mounted configurations, built around a remote-operated combat module. Detection and tracking use an electro-optical suite (TV, infrared, laser rangefinder) plus a retractable radar mast, feeding a remote operator console. Rather than missiles, guns, EW, or nets, Zveroboy intercepts targets kinetically using small interceptor drones called "Kraken" — VTOL quadcopter-tailsitters carried in launch containers (reported as three containers of three Kraken each, nine per unit). A baseline camera-equipped variant and a thermal-imaging "Kraken-N" for night use are described. The system is pitched as specialized against slow reconnaissance drones (cited example: Ukraine's "Leleka," ~70-80 km/h) at short range, and reportedly less effective against faster attack drones above roughly 150 km/h. Status is prototype/testing per the source. Norway's Nammo to develop engine for IRIS-T SLX air-defense missile

2026-09-20

IRIS-T SLX is the long-range variant of the German Diehl Defence IRIS-T SLM air-defense system already fielded by Ukraine. It roughly doubles the intercept range to about 80 km, targeting fighter jets, bombers, and long-endurance drones/support aircraft, and can be fired from the same launcher as SLM missiles for mixed loadouts, reusing existing SLM radar, tactical operations center, and software infrastructure. Norwegian firm Nammo, at its Raufoss plant, has begun developing the SLX's new rocket motor — a "dual-pulse" design that splits the motor into two sections with a tailored thrust profile to extend range, described by Nammo as an essentially new development effort with little carryover from earlier motors. Diehl is targeting the first Safety Integrity Level (SIL) development firing test in 2027, with serial production planned to start around 2029. Ukraine relevance: IRIS-T SLS/SLM systems already in Ukrainian service have reportedly destroyed over 1,000 Russian targets since 2022, with a claimed 95% hit rate against cruise missiles and drones. The longer-ranged SLX, once fielded, would extend that intercept envelope and could reduce Ukraine's/Europe's dependence on U.S. Patriot interceptors amid ongoing shortages — though fielding is still several years away. What are the "S-300 Clone" and GAM-67 missiles in the US's new $2.68B Ukraine aid package?

The US State Department approved a $2.68 billion aid package for Ukraine on September 18, 2026, financed through European partners and unused US military aid funding, per Defense Express. Two line items in the paperwork caught analysts' attention for their unusual names: "S-300 Clone" missiles and "GAM-67." The package also covers sustainment items — spare parts, consumables, software, repair/return support, and engineering, technical, and logistical services. Experts cited by Defense Express and Defense Mirror argue the naming is misleading: despite the "clone" label, the missile is not a replica of the Soviet 5V55 used in legacy S-300 systems, but a new US development with enhanced characteristics and a more modern guidance principle, intended to be fired from Ukraine's existing Soviet-era S-300 launchers (which have plenty of launchers but a dwindling missile supply, since Russia will not sell more). GAM-67 likewise reuses the designation of an unrealized 1950s American anti-radar missile project, but is described as a new development intended for medium-range systems. Notably, the US government itself did not disclose the index, technical characteristics, or manufacturer of either missile in its official statement, so these identifications are analyst inference, not official confirmation. The article links this to a September 2024 statement by then-Defense Secretary Lloyd Austin about "designing and building a substitute" for the Soviet S-300 and the R-27 air-to-air missile. For Ukraine's air-defense posture, this matters because it would extend the operational life of Soviet-legacy S-300 batteries by feeding them Western-made replacement rounds. Given the lack of official technical disclosure, this should be treated as informed speculation about weapon identity rather than a confirmed capability announcement.

US Approves $2.68 Billion Air Defense Sale to Ukraine, Including S-300 Clone and GAM-67 Missile Systems

2026-09-19

The US Department of State on 18 September 2026 approved a possible Foreign Military Sale to Ukraine worth an estimated $2.68 billion, covering what the State Department calls 'Air Defence Development Upgrades and related equipment and services.' Congress was formally notified late Friday, the standard step required before such a sale can proceed, giving lawmakers a statutory window to object. The package includes S-300 Clone missiles, GAM-67 missiles, Range-Extended Air Defence Laser Guided Rocket (ADALGR) missile systems, Improvised Transporter Erector Launcher (ITEL) 1.5 units with mobile launching system modification kits, and RPS 202 counter-drone radars, alongside spare parts, technical assistance and contractor logistics support. Financing would combine European contributions with US Foreign Military Financing (FMF) funds appropriated under the Biden administration; the FMF portion would count as a US capital contribution to the Ukraine Reconstruction Investment Fund on a 1:1 reimbursement basis — the fund created under the 2025 US-Ukraine minerals deal. The sale underscores Washington's continued reliance on Ukraine-funded, rather than US-gifted, arms transfers and on the reconstruction fund as a financing vehicle, as Ukraine braces for another winter of Russian strikes on its power grid and air defence network.

Ukraine Receives Australian Slinger 30mm Anti-Drone Systems

2026-09-17

Ukraine's armed forces have received Slinger anti-drone modules from the Australian company Electro Optic Systems (EOS), based in Canberra. The system is a remotely operated weapon station built around the 30 mm Bushmaster M230LF cannon, purpose-built to detect and destroy low-flying drones ranging from FPV kamikaze drones to reconnaissance UAVs. Slinger pairs an Echodyne 4D radar with active electronic beamsteering to a 4-axis optoelectronic sighting unit that tracks independently of the gun in both elevation and azimuth, aided by an integrated wind sensor for precision. The fully stabilised mount, weighing under 400 kg, can track and engage moving drones beyond 800 metres using specialised 30x113mm ammunition fitted with radio-frequency proximity fuzes, allowing effective kills against small, fast-moving targets without requiring a direct hit. In Ukraine the modules are being mounted on pickup trucks to form mobile counter-drone fire teams, and the system is also designed for integration onto light armoured vehicles and armoured personnel carriers. The delivery is part of a broader 2023 agreement covering 160 units, with 110 intended for integration onto M113 APCs and 50 ordered by the company Praktika. The systems add a mobile, precision-guided layer to Ukraine's counter-UAS defenses against the ongoing Russian drone and Shahed threat.

Ukraine Fields Italian-Swiss Skyguard/Aspide Air Defense System, Built Around a Cold War Sparrow-Derived Missile

2026-09-16

Ukraine's 38th Anti-Aircraft Missile Regiment has released the first combat footage of a Skyguard/Aspide air-defense system in operational use, showing crews loading missiles and the system standing watch. The Skyguard/Aspide combines a Swiss-made fire-control unit — originally from Oerlikon Contraves, now part of Rheinmetall Air Defence — with Italian-built Aspide missiles. The system can engage targets at ranges of roughly 12 to 25 km and altitudes from 15 meters up to 6,000 meters, and is designed against aircraft, helicopters, cruise missiles and drones. The Aspide missile itself traces its lineage to the American AIM-7 Sparrow, a radar-guided weapon whose development the US Navy began in the late 1940s for Cold War-era bomber interception; Aspide is essentially an Italian evolution of that mid-20th-century design, adapted for today's threats. Ukraine received its Skyguard/Aspide systems as Western aid: Spain began deliveries in November 2022, after training 19 Ukrainian personnel a month earlier at its Zaragoza base, and Italy pledged further units in February 2023. Italian-supplied variants reportedly differ from the Spanish ones, carrying target-illumination radar and TV-tracking equipment mounted directly on the launcher. The deployment comes as Ukraine faces a widening shortage of higher-end interceptors: Patriot missile deliveries are reported to have fallen from 675 units in 2023 to a projected 264 in 2026. Short-range systems such as Skyguard/Aspide are being used against drones and cruise missiles, letting Ukraine conserve its scarce Patriot interceptors for ballistic-missile threats — a trade-off made more pressing as Ukraine enters its fifth consecutive winter of Russian strikes on energy and other infrastructure.

Suspected Russian Shahed-type drone violates Moldovan and Romanian airspace, crashes in Moldova

On 8 September 2026 an uncrewed aerial vehicle violated the airspace of both Moldova and NATO member Romania before crashing on Moldovan soil.
