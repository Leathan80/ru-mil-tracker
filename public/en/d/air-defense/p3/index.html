Russian MoD claims 443 Ukrainian drones downed over 17+ regions overnight 22-23 October; all four Moscow airports shut again in daytime

2026-10-23

Russia's Defence Ministry claims its air defences intercepted and destroyed 443 Ukrainian fixed-wing drones overnight from 22 to 23 October, one night after the attack in which Moscow mayor Sergei Sobyanin claimed 630 drones were downed. According to the ministry, drones were shot down over the Smolensk, Nizhny Novgorod, Belgorod, Bryansk, Kursk, Kaluga, Tula, Oryol, Lipetsk, Tambov, Voronezh, Ryazan, Vladimir, Rostov, Volgograd, Perm and Krasnodar regions, as well as the Moscow region, Tatarstan, Udmurtia, Crimea, the Black Sea and the Sea of Azov. Sobyanin said more than 150 drones (155, according to AeroTime's reading of his statements) flew towards the Moscow region; he said most were destroyed at long range and five were intercepted on approach to the capital. Regional governors gave their own figures: 20 over Smolensk, 19 over Nizhny Novgorod, 12 over Tula, plus interceptions over four districts of Rostov region. In the Gorodetsky district of Nizhny Novgorod region, falling debris injured three people, who were taken to hospital, according to preliminary information from governor Gleb Nikitin. Several residential buildings, an educational facility and infrastructure were damaged. Schools in the district switched to remote learning, and traffic over the dam of the Nizhny Novgorod hydroelectric power station at Zavolzhye was temporarily halted because of the drone threat. According to Nikitin, MoD units and the volunteer unit Bars-NN took part in the air defence. The pressure on Moscow went on into the daytime. According to AeroTime, which cites notices from the aviation authority Rosaviatsiya, Vnukovo and Domodedovo were restricted to cleared flights from about 12:55-13:00 Moscow time, and at 14:05 all four Moscow airports (Sheremetyevo, Domodedovo, Vnukovo and Zhukovsky) were closed entirely, citing flight safety. It was the second full closure in two days. Sobyanin reported nine drones destroyed by 13:39. Shortly before 15:00, Sheremetyevo declared an air raid alert and moved passengers, including those on aircraft parked on the apron, into shelters. A claim on social media that drone debris fell on the runway was not verified. Vnukovo and Domodedovo resumed flights under the clearance regime at 16:43.

Romanian F-16s shoot down two jet-powered drones believed to be Russia's new Geran-4; analyst warns European NATO is not ready for swarms of such low-cost cruise missiles

2026-10-22

Romanian F-16 fighters shot down two jet-powered Geran-family drones over Romania's territorial waters in the Black Sea on 17 October 2026, the Romanian Defence Ministry said. Interim Defence Minister Radu Miruță said the drones flew at about 450 km/h and were likely Geran-4s, which would make them the first of that type Romania has downed. The identification rests on the Romanian assessment; no wreckage analysis has been published. According to Ukraine's military intelligence (HUR), Russia began using the Geran-4 in May 2026 as a response to effective Ukrainian interceptor drones, and the type reaches up to 500 km/h. By June, interceptors were downing about half of the slower Shahed-type drones, according to an estimate by defence adviser Serhii Beskrestnov. In a 20 October op-ed for CEPA, Fabian Hoffmann of the Norwegian Institute for Defence Studies traces the shift to the Geran-3, recovered intact by Ukraine in 2025, whose Chinese JT80 turbojet roughly doubled speed from 185 to 350 km/h. He puts newer models, including the Geran-4, at 600 km/h or more, higher than HUR's figure, and estimates their cost at $150,000–250,000 each against about $50,000 for a propeller-driven long-range drone; in his view Russia buys a significantly higher chance of penetration for that price. Hoffmann, who calls these weapons low-cost cruise missiles, argues that in a direct confrontation European NATO states would be even more vulnerable to swarms of them than Ukraine. Defences must match their speed, for example with jet-powered interceptor drones, but jet engines are scarce because too few European and US suppliers can meet demand, while gun systems and higher-end missile defences are too scarce or too costly. Ukraine already fields Bars jet interceptors; President Zelenskyy said on 4 October they hit about 30% of targets, the highest rate among Ukrainian interceptors, and Defence Minister Yevhenii Khmara called aviation one of the most effective tools against jet drones.
