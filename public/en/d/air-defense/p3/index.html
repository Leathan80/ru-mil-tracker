Why Does russia Need DF-3 Gadyuka Jet-Powered Interceptor Drone With 800 km Range, How Could It Actually Be Used? (Defense Express)

2026-10-11SINGLE SOURCE

The DF-3 Viper (Russian: Gadyuka) is a concept for a jet-powered interceptor drone with a stated range of 800 km and a total mass of 240 kg, presented by the Russian company DOFA-Tek alongside its other jet-powered drones. These include the DF-1 Roscherk interceptor (250 km range, up to 500 km/h, 15 kg total mass including a 1.5 kg warhead), which according to the company has entered factory testing with a next round of tests scheduled for next month, and the DF-5 Iskra strike drone (1,200 km range, up to 850 km/h, 20 kg warhead, 120 kg total mass). On DOFA-Tek's website both the DF-3 and the DF-5 are labelled "technical proposals", i.e. at an early stage of development; the company has not specified the DF-3's payload capacity or type, and the only concept image published so far resembles the Italian Aeritalia G.91 light fighter-bomber. Defense Express argues that an 800 km range makes little sense for a conventional interceptor: more endurance means more fuel, a larger airframe and higher cost, which could make intercepting cheap UAVs economically unviable. The 240 kg mass, double that of the longer-ranged DF-5, points to a large payload, yet a warhead of tens of kilograms would be excessive for a normal interceptor drone. The outlet therefore hypothesises that the DF-3 may be intended as a reusable 'unmanned fighter' carrying smaller interceptor drones, electronic warfare equipment or even machine guns, engaging several targets across a whole region in a single sortie before returning to its launch point, which would explain the range. It stresses that these are unconfirmed hypotheses until more details emerge. Single source: this is a Ukrainian military-affairs outlet (Defense Express) report that has not been independently verified.

Ukraine's SBU Alpha unit releases footage of a drone destroying a Russian Pantsir air-defence system mounted on an elevated 'Flakturm' tower.

2026-10-10

A Ukrainian drone has destroyed a Russian Pantsir air-defence system on top of a so-called Flakturm, one of the purpose-built towers Russia has been erecting to counter Ukrainian drones, according to footage released by the Alpha special operations unit of the Security Service of Ukraine (SBU) and reported by Defence Blog. The SBU did not say where or when the strike took place, which drone was used, which Pantsir variant was hit, or whether the tower itself was also destroyed. The claim has not been independently verified. The towers are nicknamed after the concrete anti-aircraft towers Nazi Germany built in Berlin, Hamburg and Vienna. Russia's use of them became public in September 2023, when state channel Russia-24 showed Pantsirs on metal towers around Moscow. According to Defence Blog, lifting the system several metres gives its radar a clearer view over trees and terrain, so it can spot small, low-flying drones earlier and at longer range. The tradeoff is that a tower cannot move: once its location is known, drone units can plan a precision strike against a target that will still be there when the drone arrives. The Pantsir is a truck-mounted short-range system combining a radar, surface-to-air missiles and twin 30 mm cannons, and is one of Russia's main defences against drones and cruise missiles. SBU head Vasyl Malyuk said in late 2025 that Ukraine had destroyed 48% of Russia's Pantsir systems that year, while Russia builds about 30 per year. Defence Blog places the strike in a series of October attacks on Russian air defences: Ukraine's General Staff reported a hit on a Pantsir-S1 near Kudepsta (Krasnodar region) on 8-9 October, and footage showed FP-2 strike drones destroying a Pantsir-S2 earlier in the month.
