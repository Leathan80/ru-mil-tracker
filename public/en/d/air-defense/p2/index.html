Three Russian Kh-101 cruise missiles crash in eastern Poland during mass strike on Ukraine; NATO-cleared Swedish Gripen could not identify a missile in bad weather and held fire

2026-11-20

During Russia's massive missile and drone attack on Ukraine on 20 November 2026, three Russian Kh-101 cruise missiles came down in eastern Poland. Initial reports by Defence Minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz spoke of two cruise missiles and a jet-powered Shahed-type drone, but on 22 November the Lublin District Prosecutor's Office said Kh-101 debris had been found at all three sites: Nowy Lipowiec (Biłgoraj County, Lublin Voivodeship), where photos show a crater, Rudnik nad Sanem (Nisko County, Podkarpackie) and Mołodycz (Jarosław County), the site earlier attributed to the drone. According to the prosecutors' early findings, all three missiles were manufactured in the fourth quarter of 2026 (the Kyiv Independent: "in recent weeks") at plants in Dubna, Moscow Oblast; with the fourth quarter starting on 1 October, Euromaidan Press notes that none would have been more than about 50 days old if the dating holds. The single Kh-101 that crashed in Lublin Voivodeship in July had been dated by prosecutors to the second quarter of 2026, so all four were apparently made this year. Work at Nowy Lipowiec and Rudnik nad Sanem is complete; the Mołodycz site stayed under guard overnight with work resuming on 23 November, and the debris will undergo further expert examination. Ukrainian monitoring channels had tracked a Kh-101 from Lviv Oblast towards Poland around 06:28 local time, and Polish radars detected an object at about 06:30, followed by warnings and sirens in Lublin. A farm camera in Kopki recorded a flash that officials said matched radar data. Nobody was hurt, and the Nowy Lipowiec object burned in a field several hundred metres from buildings. Lublin prosecutors, military police and military counterintelligence are investigating. Ukrainian PM Serhii Koretskyi was at Rzeszów-Jasionka Airport at the time, according to TVN24 and European Pravda. None of the missiles was shot down. Lt. Gen. Ireneusz Nowak, Poland's operational commander, told Rzeczpospolita that a pair of Swedish Gripens flying under NATO's Combined Air Operations Centre in Uedem, Germany, had a condition-based order letting the pilots decide themselves to fire once identification requirements were met. One pilot moved on an object preliminarily identified as a Kh-101, but "extremely difficult weather conditions" prevented him from identifying it visually or with the jet's electro-optical sensors, so he did not fire despite the NATO authorisation. According to Nowak, who estimated that Russia fired 72 Kh-101s at Ukraine that night, Poland tracked 11 potentially threatening objects, three of which crossed the border; Radio ZET, citing unnamed sources, reported that more than three may have entered Poland and that the military was considering whether at least two were heading towards Nowa Dęba. Two Polish F-16s and the two Gripens were airborne, supported by a Saab 340 early-warning aircraft, a Dutch MRTT tanker and ground-based air defences; according to Euromaidan Press, the alert rose from yellow at 01:40 to red at 05:49 and held until noon. Russia first sent drones, including jet-powered ones, then Kalibr and Kh-101 missiles; Nowak said Poland knew launch sites, numbers and flight paths, helped by data from Ukraine's Virazh air-surveillance system, and had no doubt who was behind the attack. Euromaidan Press, citing Espreso, adds that Poland introduced procedures on 1 October allowing shootdowns without visual confirmation, and that Ukrainian military expert Serhii Zhurets believes Poland has likely returned to peacetime protocols; this assessment is not confirmed by Polish officials. Kosiniak-Kamysz called the incident "another provocation by Russia", timed to coincide with a Coalition of the Willing exercise with British and French troops at Nowa Dęba (Podkarpackie), and Prime Minister Donald Tusk said it did not appear accidental, while cautioning that the link to the exercise remains a hypothesis. On 23 November the Lublin prosecutors said they would try to establish whether the missiles had been deliberately directed at Poland. Poland asked NATO for more support under Operation Eastern Sentry, specifically AWACS aircraft and allied F-35s. NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte called the incident dangerous and, visiting Warsaw on 21 November, said "NATO will defend every inch of Polish territory"; according to Reuters, the North Atlantic Council is to discuss the violations in Brussels on 23 November. French President Emmanuel Macron said Russia was making an "irresponsible gamble". None of the sources report an Article 4 request or a Russian reaction. In a Fox News interview that Kyiv Independent dates to 19 November, US President Donald Trump said Putin had been "landing drones in Poland and a couple of little missiles, by mistake"; given that date, the remark may refer to earlier incursions. A Kh-101 had previously flown nearly 100 km into Poland and crashed in Lublin Voivodeship on 30 July 2026.

Ukrainian 'Blysk' laser shoots down a jet-powered Geran drone, in what a presidential adviser calls a world first, but serial production is still 'too early'

2026-11-19

Serhii "Flash" Beskrestnov, a radio-technology expert and adviser to Ukraine's president on defence technology, said on Telegram on 25 November 2026 that a Blysk laser air-defence system built by the Ukrainian company Carmine Sky had shot down a Russian jet-powered Geran drone while protecting a critical infrastructure facility. He called it the first confirmed downing of a jet-powered Geran by a combat laser and a world first, and published a video showing the drone being hit by the beam. The date, location and facility were not disclosed. Beskrestnov added that several Ukrainian companies are developing air-defence lasers, that the technology is complex and expensive, and that it is "too early" to talk about mass production: trials continue, with the lasers guarding critical infrastructure while they are tested, within the state Private Air Defence programme and with support from the Brave1 defence-tech cluster. Beskrestnov explained that the laser does not slice off wings as in films: it holds its spot on one fixed point of the drone until the internal components fail from overheating, and its power determines both range and dwell time. According to the manufacturer, Blysk has an output of 4 kW and a range of up to one kilometre against aerial targets; it takes a fraction of a second to burn through a small FPV drone and several seconds to penetrate a Shahed fuselage. Carmine Sky put the system on combat duty in Kyiv in early November, and in its first days it engaged no targets, which the company blamed on cloud and fog. Carmine Sky says Blysk is unrelated to Tryzub, the laser project of Celebra Tech. The claimed kill matters because jet-powered Gerans are too fast for most of the machine-gun crews Ukraine relies on against slower drones, and a laser's cost per shot is essentially electricity. According to Euromaidan Press, Ukraine's layered defences had raised interception rates against slower drones to 80–93%, pushing Russia towards the jet-powered Geran. All performance figures are manufacturer claims with no independent testing reported, and the kill is a claim by a Ukrainian government adviser, a party to the conflict, that has not been independently confirmed.
