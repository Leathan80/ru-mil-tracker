Russian Mi-8 lost over Voronezh region, three crew killed; reportedly shot down by friendly fire during Ukrainian drone raid

ArmyInform reports, based on a video and statements from the Ukrainian Air Force's Air Command 'Centre', how short-range air defence is currently organised in Chernihiv Oblast, across which thousands of Russian attack drones have flown in the past six months. According to the military, a single means is no longer sufficient against modern aerial targets; machine guns, anti-aircraft guns, interceptor drones and automated turrets must complement each other. One system still in use is the Tunguska gun-missile vehicle: a commander showed 25 kill markings for downed Shaheds on its hull (with one more not yet applied), and the crew has also shot down jet-powered attack drones. Automated anti-aircraft systems are another layer; one such system is credited with 41 kills, including two cruise missiles, with the military citing speed (a shorter time from detection to opening fire) as the main advantage of automation. Remotely operated turrets were also shown: according to an operator, one turret downed up to six Shaheds in a single night during a massed raid, needing roughly 25-30 rounds per target, and it can also engage a jet drone that enters its engagement zone. Short-range air defence is therefore being built as a multi-layered system (Tunguska, mobile fire groups, interceptor drones, automated systems and remote turrets), and Air Command stresses that when the enemy changes tactics or introduces a new type of target, the task is to find a countermeasure quickly and scale what works. Soldiers of a mobile fire group (MOG) tasked with regional air defence, call signs 'Leshiy' and 'Modny', showed machine-gun mounts and a new MANPADS: a transport case holding two missile tubes (transport-launch containers), a detachable reusable gripstock and four small canisters of undisclosed purpose, plus the weapon in ready-to-fire state. A soldier stated a launch range of up to 4 km; Ryabov stresses this is the only stated figure, no live firings were shown, and the seeker type (presumably infrared), its bands and jamming resistance are unknown. He notes the name fits the Russian 'plant' naming tradition (Strela, Igla, Verba), which weakly suggests a Russian design but proves nothing, and that one TV report cannot show whether Iva is a serial product, prototype, upgrade or closed-programme system. The article addresses claims that circulated after the broadcast that Iva is practically identical to Chinese QianWei-family MANPADS such as the QW-18 or QW-19, or to Iran's Misagh-2/Misagh-3, which are themselves derived from the Chinese designs - the same identification debate as in the earlier tracker entry on the unmarked shoulder-fired SAM (possibly QW-18A/QW-19) seen in this Rossiya 1 footage. Ryabov warns that external similarity does not prove origin and lists possible explanations: an independent Russian design, use of foreign technical solutions, a localised or upgraded foreign product, direct import of ready-made systems, or similarity limited to the case and packing layout. Direct Chinese delivery and Chinese design roots should not be conflated, he argues: a Chinese-lineage design could have reached Russia through a third country. In an editorial assessment, Topwar calls a single design lineage going back to the Chinese QianWei family the most economical explanation, making the 'Chinese' and 'Iranian' versions the same thing at different distance from the original. It judges an Iranian channel most likely, because open data show Iran delivering ready-made weapons to Russia while there are no direct reports of Chinese arms deliveries, with the matching packing layout as only a weak supporting argument; it explicitly does not present this as fact. Possible motives for an import - replenishing stocks amid heavy expenditure against drones, production rates, component availability - are mentioned, but the author says Iva's appearance alone does not show that Russian industry cannot meet demand. Conclusion: one Russian unit has the Iva with a claimed 4 km range; everything else is hypothesis.
