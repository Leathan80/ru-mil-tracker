2026-10-25

On the afternoon of 25 October, missiles struck the Energiya plant in Yelets, Lipetsk Oblast, about 250 km from the Ukrainian border. The Ukrainian OSINT community Exilenova+ said the strike most likely involved UK-supplied Storm Shadow cruise missiles. Lipetsk Governor Igor Artamonov confirmed a strike on Yelets's industrial zone and said one person was injured, without naming the facility. On 26 October Ukraine's General Staff confirmed a 'successful strike' on the plant's workshop producing power-supply elements for Russian weapons, carried out on 25 October or in the early hours of 26 October. It said a fire broke out on the premises and that the strike complicates Russia's production of power sources for missiles, guided aerial bombs and other armaments. New photos published by Exilenova+ on 26 October show at least seven hits on the plant's premises; after examining them, the Dnipro OSINT analyst Harbuz concluded that both buildings that may be critical to production were hit, one physically destroyed and the other gutted by fire from the inside. Energiya PJSC develops and manufactures electrochemical power sources: accumulators, power cells and batteries. According to the General Staff it is the sole Russian manufacturer of batteries for the glide and correction modules fitted to aerial bombs, and it also produces batteries for Iskander-M missile systems and sea-launched cruise missiles. Citing the War&Sanctions project of Ukraine's Defence Intelligence (HUR), Ukrainska Pravda earlier listed Iskander-M, Zircon, RM-48U, the 95Ya6 missile of the Pantsir air-defence system and the Kh-47M2 Kinzhal as users; Dnipro OSINT adds Iskander-K and power sources for UMPB D-30SN guided bombs. According to the Kyiv Independent, Energiya also makes UAV batteries and is under sanctions from the US, EU, UK, Canada, Switzerland, Japan, Australia and Ukraine. The plant has been hit before: in May and July 2025 and in January 2026, according to the Kyiv Independent (Dnipro OSINT counts at least four earlier attacks). In the same statement the General Staff reported damage from the 23 October strike on Proton-Electrotex in Oryol (main microchip workshop destroyed, server building damaged) and the 24 October strike on Tolyattikauchuk in Tolyatti (a process pipeline, two butyl rubber units and a fractionation column damaged), both covered earlier. The damage assessment and the 'sole producer' claim come from the Ukrainian General Staff and Ukrainian OSINT channels; the weapon type has not been officially confirmed, and none of this has been independently verified.

Lithuania unveils X-12 jet-powered interceptor drone aimed at Russia's Geran-5, reportedly combat-tested in Ukraine

2026-10-25

Lithuania has presented the X-12, a turbojet-powered interceptor drone built to destroy fast aerial targets, according to Ukrainian outlet Militarnyi, which cites the Lithuanian news site Delfi. The primary target named is Russia's jet-powered Geran-5 attack drone. The project is linked to the Strategic Defense Alliance (SDA), an association of Ukrainian, Lithuanian and Latvian defence-technology companies. No single manufacturer is named. Its engines were assembled in Lithuania, and some components come from Latvia and Ukraine. An SDA representative says the drone contains no Chinese-made parts. The developers give a maximum speed of up to 700 km/h, a cruising speed of about 350 km/h, a range of up to 126 km, a maximum operating altitude of 10 km and an endurance of more than 12 minutes, depending on configuration and payload. According to Delfi, the X-12 has already been used in combat in Ukraine, and the Ukrainian personnel who tested it gave it a positive assessment. Lithuania itself lacks the conditions to test such systems. These performance and combat-test claims come from the developers and have not been independently confirmed. The X-12 is expected to be supplied to the Ukrainian armed forces, and Lithuania is considering adding it to its own air defence. SDA's Richardas Savickas claimed the system currently has no equivalent. Tadas Ruzhele, who coordinates Lithuanian-Ukrainian defence-industry cooperation at Lithuania's Ministry of National Defence, called such interceptors a critical part of layered air defence and stressed the need for trained operators and for lessons from Ukraine's combat experience. Russia's shift to jet-powered Geran variants is pushing up the speed that interceptors need, and Ukraine is pursuing its own jet interceptors as well, including one based on the Bars missile-drone that is planned for joint production with Germany.

Russia's campaign against Kyiv's 'power ring': the capital's own CHP generation is out and strikes on 330/750 kV substations raise the prospect of 8-20 hour daily blackouts this winter

2026-10-24

In an analysis for Ukrainska Pravda (24 October 2026, translated from Ekonomichna Pravda), energy journalist Mykola Topalov describes how Russia is systematically attacking Kyiv and Kyiv Oblast with two aims: to destroy the city's combined heat and power (CHP) plants and to break the so-called 'power ring'. That ring is the system of high-voltage lines and 330 kV substations linking the capital to the national grid from several directions, with the 750 kV Kyivska substation as a key node through which power from the Rivne and Khmelnytskyi nuclear plants reaches the city. Kyiv normally uses 1-1.2 GW (12-15% of national demand), rising to 1.8-2 GW in winter. Before the large-scale winter attacks of 2025, local generation from CHP-5, CHP-6 and Darnytsia CHP (CHP-4) supplied up to about 1.3 GW, according to an energy-company source. Those plants can no longer generate electricity, yet all three were hit again on the night of 13-14 October. The article explains the tactical logic. Russia does not need to destroy the whole ring: damaging a few critical nodes that carry a large share of the flow is enough. Power may then exist in the system but cannot be delivered to Kyiv in sufficient volume, forcing dispatchers into emergency or scheduled cuts. The ring's key components have been under constant attack for more than a month.
