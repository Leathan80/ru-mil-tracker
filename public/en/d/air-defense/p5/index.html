The specific assertion that a Lancet — rather than a Geran/Shahed one-way attack drone, a longer-range UAV or a cruise missile — achieved this range is the core of what remains unverified. The U.S. Navy is pursuing a next-generation variant of the Evolved Sea Sparrow Missile (ESSM/RIM-162), which itself evolved from the RIM-7 Sea Sparrow — the missile Ukraine fires from its FrankenSAM air defense system. According to Defense Express, the Navy issued a "Sources Sought" notice to gauge industry interest in developing and producing a new "Guided Missile Assembly," with a proposal deadline of October 19, 2026. The effort would be releasable to all navies in the NATO SeaSparrow consortium (the U.S., Australia, Belgium, Canada, Denmark, Germany, Greece, the Netherlands, Norway, Portugal, Spain and Turkey). The stated requirements call for a missile able to engage targets across a range of speeds and altitudes — from sea-skimming threats to high-diving aircraft — and to counter highly maneuverable, high-G targets in contested RF, electro-optical and infrared environments, while discriminating between closely-spaced targets and resisting countermeasures. This points to a significant guidance and target-discrimination upgrade. Physically, the new variant is expected to retain the current 10-inch (25.4 cm) diameter to remain compatible with Mk41 vertical launch cells (four missiles per cell), broadly matching today's ESSM (RIM-162): roughly 280 kg, 3.66 m long, a 39 kg warhead, range over 50 km and speeds above Mach 4. The push appears driven by the need to replace aging missile stocks and improve guidance against modern threats. For Ukraine, the FrankenSAM link is notable: since 2024 Ukraine has fired RIM-7 Sea Sparrow missiles from converted Soviet-era Buk-M1 launchers, pairing Western missiles with Soviet air-defense platforms to offset a shortage of original Buk munitions. It remains unclear, however, whether or when a future ESSM successor would become relevant to Ukraine's actual inventory: the current announcement is only an exploratory market-research notice with a proposal deadline in October 2026, missile development and production programs typically take years, and there is no indication this specific effort will alter Ukraine's existing FrankenSAM systems in the near term. The operational impact on Ukrainian air defense therefore remains speculative for now.
