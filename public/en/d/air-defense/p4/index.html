Ethiopia announces delivery of 12 Russian Su-35 fighters and plans to acquire four S-400 batteries

2026-10-28

Ethiopian Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed announced on X on 28 October 2026 that Ethiopia has received 12 Russian-built Su-35 multirole fighters. TASS also reported the announcement, which came during Abiy's visit to Moscow for the third Russia-Africa Summit (28-29 October). Abiy said the aircraft arrived a year ahead of schedule and credited President Vladimir Putin with speeding up delivery. Photos show him with Ethiopian and Russian officials next to two of the jets; some outlets say the handover took place at Zhukovsky. According to Janes, the Ethiopian Su-35 order had not been confirmed before. The War Zone notes it has only Abiy's posts to go on and has not independently verified the delivery. Abiy said the package also covers four S-400 surface-to-air missile batteries, without giving a delivery date. The financial terms, where the jets will be based and whether more aircraft will follow are all undisclosed. Ethiopia currently flies around 10 to 15 second-hand Su-27s and two Su-30Ks delivered in 2024, which were originally built for India and later refurbished in Belarus. The War Zone speculates, without confirmation, that the Su-35s may come from the batch of 24 built for Egypt; that deal collapsed under the threat of US CAATSA sanctions, and some of those aircraft are believed to have gone to Algeria. For Russia, this is a rare export success for the Su-35 and the S-400 while it is under Western sanctions. Analysts question whether Moscow can provide training, maintenance and spare parts while it also has to sustain its own Su-35S fleet in the war against Ukraine. US sanctions against Ethiopia under CAATSA would require a separate decision by Washington.

Ukraine's General Staff says it shot down nearly 60% of Russian jet-powered Geran-4 drones over the past three months

2026-10-27

In an update on its Telegram channel on 27 October 2026, Ukraine's General Staff said the shoot-down rate of Russia's jet-powered Geran drones is steadily rising. Over the past three months Ukrainian air defence has intercepted nearly 60% of the Geran-4 jet drones launched, and in some individual attacks the rate reached 80%. Russia has increasingly been using these faster jet-powered variants alongside the propeller-driven Shahed/Geran-2 type. Their speed makes them harder to counter. The General Staff gave no interception rate for the more advanced Geran-5 jet drone. According to the General Staff, several means are proving effective. Tactical aviation (F-16s, French Mirage fighters and MiG-29s) is used to hunt the jet drones. Short-range air defence is also being strengthened: Gepard and Shilka anti-aircraft guns, the newer Skynex, Skyranger and Terrahawk Paladin systems, interceptor drones, and mobile fire groups drawn from several branches of the Defence Forces. These mobile fire groups carry machine guns and also MANPADS, which the General Staff says troops have used successfully against jet drones. Ukraine is testing cheaper interceptor-drone models, some of which have reportedly already downed dozens of jet-powered Gerans. No models or exact figures were given. The stated aim is to raise interception rates while cutting the cost of defence, because Ukraine is short of guided surface-to-air missiles and wants to keep them for targets that require them. Caveat: all figures come from Ukraine's General Staff, a party to the conflict. They have not been independently verified, and the absolute number of Geran-4 launches behind the percentages was not disclosed.
