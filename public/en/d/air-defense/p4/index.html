Ukraine turns its jet-powered Bars strike drone into a Shahed hunter

2026-10-19

Video shared by the Ukrainian military shows an interceptor version of Ukraine's jet-powered Bars drone, launched from a ground launcher, destroying a Russian Geran-5 — the first footage of the Ukrainian missile-drone being used against Russia's own jet-powered attack drones, Defence Blog reports. The interceptor is based on the Bars strike drone, a small turbojet-powered weapon Ukraine has used for long-range attacks on Russian targets, including oil refineries. Upgraded strike versions of Bars can reportedly fly at up to 700 km/h and travel up to 1,000 km, carrying warheads of 22, 60 or 105 kg. In July 2026 Germany and Ukraine signed an agreement on joint production of Bars drones. The Geran-5 looks and flies more like a small cruise missile than the propeller-driven Shahed-type drones; it can fly at up to about 600 km/h and at higher altitudes than older models, which makes it hard to catch for the small quadcopter interceptors Ukraine relies on against slower Shaheds. Russia launched 3,177 jet-powered drones at Ukraine in September alone, and President Zelenskyy said that month that Ukrainian forces were shooting down only 57% of them. Ukrainian manufacturers have rushed faster interceptors into service: Wild Hornets' Sting S, SkyFall's P1-SUN JetKiller and a jet-powered interceptor supplied by the Serhiy Prytula Charity Foundation have all been shown destroying Geran-5s since late September, and former Defence Minister Mykhailo Fedorov said on 12 October that a new interceptor developed by his team had downed two Geran-5s heading for Kyiv. Many of those interceptors were hit by a speed cap SpaceX placed on Starshield terminals overnight on 13-14 October, cutting connections on drones moving faster than about 160 km/h; a larger, faster ground-launched jet interceptor such as Bars could give Ukraine another option for reaching Geran-5s before they reach cities. Slovenia's Valhalla Turrets announces new Skythunder counter-drone air-defence variant with 20 mm M197 three-barrel Gatling and 70 mm guided rockets

2026-10-19

Slovenian company Valhalla Turrets has announced on X a new version of its Skythunder air-defence system, intended to engage UAVs and other low-flying targets and to protect critical infrastructure against mass attacks, Militarnyi reports. The announcement is another example of the European shift towards cheap gun-plus-guided-rocket short-range air defence built to counter mass Shahed/Geran-type drone attacks rather than relying on expensive interceptor missiles.

«Зенитная артиллерия возвращается: справятся ли пушки с реактивными беспилотниками?» (Topwar: 'Anti-aircraft artillery is back: can guns cope with jet-powered drones?')

2026-10-19SINGLE SOURCE

In an analysis for Topwar (Voennoe Obozrenie), author Anatoly Blinov examines whether gun-based air defence can counter jet-powered one-way attack drones such as Russia's Geran-5. Citing Ukrainian military intelligence (HUR), he puts the Geran-5's cruise speed at 450–600 km/h (versus roughly 170–180 km/h for the piston-engined Geran-2), with a range of 950 km and a ceiling of 6 km; combat sightings were at 200 m to 3 km. At 600 km/h a drone covers about 167 m per second and crosses 3 km in some 18 seconds, which leaves gunners a very short window to acquire, track and engage. The article's systems are mostly Western ones in Ukrainian service or in development: the Rheinmetall Skynex with 35 mm Revolver Gun Mk3 and programmable AHEAD ammunition (152 tungsten sub-projectiles per round), the Gepard, the Swedish TRIDON Mk2 40 mm with 3P programmable rounds (purchased for Ukraine by Sweden and Denmark in February 2026), the Italian 76 mm DART guided round, and BAE Systems' 155 mm Hypervelocity Projectile for the US MDACS gun programme. The only Russian system discussed in detail is Rostec's 30 mm ZAK-30 Tsitadel, presented in May 2026 with radar, electro-optical sensors and airburst rounds and reportedly combat-tested. Blinov contrasts claimed successes with failures. A Stern report citing an internal Ukrainian document claimed that during a 1 April 2026 attack in western Ukraine, two Skynex units (8 guns) suffered hydraulic, loading and tracking failures and let one drone through; Rheinmetall disputed this. Ukraine's Western Air Command, by contrast, claimed a Skynex battery downed 34 Shaheds and two Kh-101 cruise missiles. He also cites Ukrainian Air Force spokesman Yuriy Ihnat on robotic machine-gun turrets with machine vision against Geran-5 (limited mainly by range), and an 8 October engagement in Rivne Oblast in which machine guns fired first and an Igla MANPADS finished off a jet Geran. For the US MDACS he notes a slipped schedule (assessment now in 2030) and a historical cost reference of about $85,000 per guided round. The author concludes that automated 30–40 mm guns are viable for point defence of individual sites against jet drones, and that programmable and guided rounds can extend reach, but only with precise sensors and expensive ammunition. Magazine depth, reload time and saturation by mass raids remain constraints, and guns must be weighed against EW, interceptor drones and decoys: artillery will hold the 'close sky' over key objects, not the whole airspace.
