Putin says Ukraine has 'no antidote' to jet-powered Gerans as Russia fields Geran-3/4/5; 1020th AA Regiment downs a Geran-5 at 425 km/h

2026-09-29

Speaking at the Cultural Forum, Vladimir Putin claimed Ukraine was seeking an 'air ceasefire' because it cannot cope with Russia's new jet-powered Geran drones: 'At least for now, they have no way to counter them — no antidote.' Meduza sets out the family behind the claim. The Geran-3 (first jet variant; Meduza dates it to early 2025, TWZ to early 2024) roughly doubled speed to 300–350 km/h with a Chinese Telefly JT80 turbojet, at the cost of range and payload. The delta-wing Geran-4, which TWZ says appeared in May 2026, uses a Telefly TF-TJ2000A with about 2.5 times the JT80's thrust; TWZ gives 450–500 km/h, altitudes up to 8 km and a documented flight of over 700 km (Crimea to Lutsk). The conventionally laid-out Geran-5 uses the same engine, can be launched from aircraft and helicopters as well as from the ground, and costs an estimated $100,000+, more than twice a Geran-2 (Meduza). HUR data cited by Euromaidan Press give the Geran-5 a 90 kg warhead, 450–600 km/h cruise speed, 950 km range and 6 km ceiling. Ukrainian military intelligence estimates Russian output at about 3,000 Geran-4/5 a month. TWZ describes how guidance has evolved. Most drones still fly autonomously to preset coordinates, but some carry GSM modems with SIM cards (Ukrainian, Russian, Belarusian and EU) and Chinese mesh modems (about 300 km range) with tail-mounted relay antennas, so that, per RFE/RL reporting, an operator in Bryansk, Oryol or Crimea can steer the drone and receive video for real-time battle damage assessment; a Geran-5 variant reportedly carries a DSMAC-style terrain-image navigation module (Polkovnyk GSh Telegram channel). Wreckage has included Texas Instruments circuit boards. Tactics include high-altitude flight to stay above cheap interceptors and, per one open-source analyst, a Geran-4 that cuts its engine and glides slowly before diving on its target. In the 28 September night attack, according to the Ukrainian Air Force, 79 of 165 drones launched were jet-powered; 112 were downed or suppressed and hits were recorded at 14 locations. Ukraine is already shooting them down, but at a price. A crew from the 1020th Anti-Aircraft Missile and Artillery Regiment (callsign Yastrub) downed a Geran-5 over Chernihiv Oblast at 425 km/h and above 3 km with a newly delivered interceptor, according to the Serhiy Prytula Foundation, which published the footage; TWZ attributes the 1020th's Geran-5 kill to Wild Hornets' STING S, whose maker claims about a dozen Geran-4s and several Geran-5s in recent days (not independently verified). Zelensky said at the UN General Assembly that four new jet-powered interceptor drones had been successfully tested, and on 26 September that Ukraine intercepts about 55% of jet drones (versus over 90% of older models). Meduza notes that the more than ten electric interceptor models (under $10,000, about four per kill) are nearly powerless against the jets; the alternatives are $70,000–100,000+ missiles or APKWS II rockets (up to $20,000) fired only by F-16s and fewer than 20 Vampire systems. Scaling up faster interceptors could add hundreds of millions or even billions of dollars a year to a budget that, per Zelensky, already has a $27 billion gap in 2026.

ISW assesses Russia's mass drone strikes aim to force Ukraine's capitulation by spring 2027

2026-09-28

The Institute for the Study of War (ISW) assesses that Russia is running a large-scale long-range strike campaign, built on jet-powered drones and ballistic missiles, with the aim of forcing Ukraine to capitulate by spring 2027, as reported by Euromaidan Press. ISW calls this Putin's theory of victory: launching cheap Geran (Shahed-type) jet-powered drones in such numbers that enough of them get through Ukrainian defences. The figures cited: Russian use of jet-powered drones rose eightfold between June and August; about 7,700 have been used since their first launch; more than 2,730 Shahed drones were launched in September alone, of which Ukraine intercepted or suppressed over 1,500. On 26 September Zelensky put the interception rate for jet-powered Shaheds at 55%. ISW says the approach relies on Ukraine's shortage of Patriot interceptors against ballistic missiles, the degradation of the power grid and Ukraine's limited air defence resources. On 25 September Putin said Ukraine 'must feel' Russia's 'retaliatory' strikes, which ISW reads as a sign of intensification over the winter. Possible disruptions: Presidential Office head Kyrylo Budanov indicated that mass deployment of Ukrainian interceptor drones could begin within months, though it is uncertain whether it comes in time for the winter escalation. ISW also assesses that Putin may postpone large-scale forced mobilisation until the results of the drone campaign are clear, despite groundwork already being laid. Note that the capitulation timeline is an ISW assessment, not a stated Russian aim, and the figures on launches and interceptions come from Ukrainian and ISW reporting.

Russia reportedly fields Geran-5 'K5' variant with autonomous optical (scene-matching) navigation and redesigned fuselage

2026-09-27

Russian forces have reportedly begun using a new modification of the jet-powered Geran-5 strike UAV with an autonomous optical navigation system and a modified fuselage. The report comes from the monitoring Telegram channel Colonel GSh ("Polkovnik GSh"), which calls the variant the Geran-5 K5 and published a photo comparing the old and new airframes. According to the channel, the drone has an electro-optical correction system that navigates by matching images of the ground below. It works on the same principle as DSMAC-type (Russian: OEKS) scene-matching systems, which let a weapon correct its course without satellite navigation. The channel also says the K5 is faster than the standard Geran-5 and may therefore be misidentified in flight as other jet-powered strike weapons such as the Dan-T. Militarnyi stresses that Ukrainian sources have not officially confirmed either the K5 designation or the presence of a DSMAC-class system. Russian sources partly corroborate the claim. Russian military correspondent Andrei Filatov (via Gazeta.ru) said that Geran-series drones already use an autonomous optical navigation system called "Susanin", which navigates by terrain imagery and lets the UAV continue on its route without radio beacons. He did not say which Geran modification carries it or describe it as DSMAC. Rossiyskaya Gazeta (27 September), citing military Telegram channels, reports that Susanin compares terrain imagery with a map loaded before flight, similar to the "Otblesk-U" optical correction option of the Kh-101 strategic cruise missile. The Russian channel Voyenny Osvedomitel said the jet Geran is "increasingly becoming a full-fledged cruise missile". For comparison, Ukraine's Defence Intelligence (HUR) gives the baseline Geran-5 as 6.5 m long, with a 3.2 m wingspan, a maximum take-off weight of up to 850 kg, a warhead of about 90 kg, a cruising speed of 450–600 km/h, a range of about 950 km and a ceiling of 6 km. HUR's teardown of a baseline Geran-5 found a Kometa-M12 jam-resistant satellite navigation receiver, an inertial navigation system, a tracker with cellular modems, a mesh modem and a Chinese TELEFLY TF-TJ2000A turbojet. The public HUR component list includes no separate DSMAC system. Russia had already moved the turbojet from an external nacelle into the fuselage to cut drag, which may add speed and range. If confirmed, terrain-image navigation would give the drone a way to find its route that does not depend on GNSS or radio links. GNSS jamming and spoofing, which the baseline already partly counters with the Kometa-M12 receiver, would then do less against the final route correction.
