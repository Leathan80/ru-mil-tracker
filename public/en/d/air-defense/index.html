Russia's 6–7 November combined strike on Kyiv used 36 ballistic missiles, including North Korean KN-23s, plus 172 drones; Zelensky says only four missiles were downed amid a Patriot interceptor shortage

2026-11-07

On the night of 6–7 November Russia launched a combined strike on Ukraine with 172 drones (Shahed, Gerbera, Parodiya and other types) and missiles of the Iskander-M, Zircon, KN-23 and S-400 types, with Kyiv and Kyiv Oblast as the main target, according to the Ukrainian Air Force figures reported by Ukrainska Pravda. Zelensky said six ballistic missiles were fired at Kyiv's Pivnichnyi (Northern) Bridge over the Dnipro, four of which struck it; the bridge is closed in both directions and contingency transport plans are being prepared. The sources give no number of KN-23s used and no indication of a new variant. Zelensky used the finding to call for tougher sanctions against suppliers of such weapons and suggested the strike was meant to pressure Ukraine ahead of planned trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi.

Ukraine's Patriot interceptor shortage deepens: Berlin says no more Patriots for now, Kyiv wants Russia's missiles stopped at the factory through sanctions, Romania reopens SkyCeptor talks with Rafael, Spain pledges an unspecified €437 million package, and Finland urges the US to release already-paid missiles

2026-11-06

German Defence Minister Boris Pistorius told the Berliner Morgenpost that Germany has, for now, "exhausted its capabilities" to supply Patriot systems. Germany has already delivered several systems and missiles, and anything further would not be replaced for the time being and would leave gaps in German defence that he could not justify. "We cannot do more right now," he said, calling on other countries to take on more of the burden at the Ramstein-format Ukraine Defence Contact Group in mid-November. On 4 November he discussed with Zelenskyy in Berlin how to speed up a two-year contract for German-made PAC-2 missiles. According to Zelenskyy, Ukraine downed only four of the 36 missiles on ballistic trajectories fired on the night of 7 November, part of a strike that Euromaidan Press says hit energy facilities in 13 regions with 172 drones as well; preliminary findings point to North Korean-made KN-23 missiles at one key energy site. Euromaidan Press reports that Ukraine intercepted only about 7% of Russian ballistic missiles in October. Zelenskyy said Russia launched 115 missiles, nearly 1,900 guided aerial bombs and more than 3,000 attack drones in the past week, and that a significant share of the missiles were built this year. If anti-ballistic defences fall short, he said, "the missiles must be stopped on the assembly lines". Kyiv is pressing partners to synchronise sanctions on the firms that build Russia's ballistic missiles and is counting on a strong 22nd EU sanctions package. New supply remains distant. Romania's Defence Ministry told journalists it has recently resumed talks with Israel's Rafael to revive production of SkyCeptor, a low-cost Patriot-compatible derivative of the Stunner interceptor from David's Sling, at Electromecanica Ploiești, Militarnyi reports citing DefenseRomania. Memoranda with Raytheon (now RTX) in 2022 and August 2023 did not get production started, and the report mentions no new contract or timetable. The reported production cost of a Stunner is about $1 million, against $4–5 million for a Patriot missile. Aviation expert Kostiantyn Kryvolap, speaking to 24 Channel, said the newly approved US sale of 500 PAC-3 missiles for $2.1 billion does not solve the core problem, because deliveries only start after 2030. He estimates Russia fires about 100–120 ballistic missiles a month at Ukraine, while Raytheon and Lockheed Martin can produce at most 750 PAC-3 missiles this year, which he called "absolutely too few". He also doubted that swap deals, such as taking PAC-2s from Spanish stocks in return for post-2030 deliveries, would make much difference. Finnish Defence Minister Antti Häkkänen told Euronews that the US is holding back Patriot interceptors that European countries have already paid for and needs to show "political willingness" to deliver; he would not say whether US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth was personally responsible. According to Zelenskyy, Ukraine has waited almost a month for Hegseth to approve missiles for Patriot and NASAMS and has averaged only about 12 Patriot missiles a month in 2026. A NATO source told Radio Svoboda that the US is delaying PAC-3 missiles while older PAC-2s keep arriving; European countries can pass on their own only with US approval. Euronews quotes a NATO source as saying SACEUR is urging seven member states to release missiles from their stocks, but they want guarantees of US replacement. On 26 October Ukrainian Defence Minister Yevhenii Khmara asked the E5 (Germany, the UK, France, Poland and Italy) to transfer 10% of their Patriot and IRIS-T interceptors, to be repaid from deliveries Ukraine has already contracted. On 5 November in Madrid, Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez promised Zelenskyy a €437 million military package, according to the Ukrainian presidency. Neither government has said what it contains, Spain's own readout does not mention it, and it is unclear whether it falls under Spain's earlier €1 billion pledge for 2026. Spain has previously given older PAC-2 interceptors, including five pledged in March, but like Greece refuses to give up its own Patriot systems.
