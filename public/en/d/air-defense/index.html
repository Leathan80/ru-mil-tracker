According to Kongsberg and the Norwegian side, the system combines Ukrainian-made missiles and launchers with a Norwegian fire-control centre, and complements the NASAMS systems Ukraine has operated since 2022. Militarnyi, citing NRK, reports Sandvik as saying that Norway bought and delivered several systems in recent months and is working to expand the project to cover more of Ukraine. Sandvik stressed that the Ukrainian missiles are cheaper than traditional interceptors and can be produced in large batches, at a time of global shortage of air defence missiles. Khmara said DESNA has proven effective against Russian drones, including jet-powered ones, and that both sides discussed scaling it up. Key technical details remain unconfirmed. No official has named the missile. Militarnyi links it to the 130-mm RK-2PZ, which Ukroboronprom presented in September at the MSPO fair in Poland for DESNA and upgraded Strela-10 systems, and which is derived from the Barrier anti-tank missile. Euromaidan Press notes that this link has not been officially confirmed and that the Ukrainian industrial partner has not been named. No figures have been published on systems delivered, drones downed or cost per missile, and the effectiveness claims come from the Ukrainian minister, not from independent data. The ministers discussed integrating other missile types into Ukraine's NASAMS, additional combat aircraft and ammunition, joint production and the exchange of combat experience. Its counter-drone effectiveness is therefore unproven, and the vehicles have not yet been seen in combat. Rheinmetall does make a dedicated air-defence variant, the Skyranger 30, with its own radar and short-range missiles, but that is not the version Ukraine received. The 21st Brigade was also the first Ukrainian unit to operate Swedish CV90s, in 2023.
