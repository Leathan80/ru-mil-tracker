Ukrainian drones hit St. Petersburg area and Tula Oblast; rubber plant linked to Iskander fuel set ablaze

2026-11-27

Overnight on 26–27 November 2026, Ukrainian drones struck the Yefremov Synthetic Rubber Plant in Tula Oblast, about 295 km from the Ukrainian border. Ukraine's General Staff confirmed the strike, saying it was carried out together with the Black Spark (Chornaya Iskra) resistance movement inside Russia, that the plant produces a key component of solid rocket fuel, including for Iskander ballistic missiles, and that damage was still being assessed. According to Astra, the plant makes hydroxylated polybutadiene; Astra's analysis of footage also showed an explosion at the Yefremov thermal power plant, which supplies heat and electricity to the town and to the rubber plant. Locals reported more than 20 explosions, a large fire and loss of heating. It is at least the fourth reported strike on the plant in under a year (24 December 2025, 25 September and 26 October 2026). Tula Governor Dmitry Milyayev said air defence downed four drones and debris broke windows in three private houses, with no infrastructure damage according to preliminary information; he did not mention the explosions at either facility. In St. Petersburg, residents reported explosions in at least eight districts (including Primorsky, Vyborgsky, Kalininsky and Nevsky, per the outlet Bumaga). An upper floor of the Shuvalovsky residential complex was damaged; Militarnyi attributed damage to residential buildings to Russian air defence, and a video circulated by a Ukrainian channel was said to show a hit on an electrical substation followed by a power cut. Pulkovo airport halted all arrivals and departures, and the Ladoga Bridge was closed while, according to Leningrad Oblast Governor Aleksandr Drozdenko, mobile fire teams operated from it; he claimed 45 drones were shot down over the region. Latvia and Estonia reported that drones briefly entered or threatened their airspace; NATO jets were scrambled and Latvia launched an interceptor drone. Estonia's military could not confirm the drones' origin. Elsewhere, a drone attack injured three people in Novorossiysk (Krasnodar Krai), according to the regional emergency headquarters. Ukraine's General Staff also reported a strike on the Novoshakhtinsk refinery in Rostov Oblast (20 horizontal storage tanks destroyed, further tanks and a pipeline section damaged), Neptune missile strikes on Marinovka air base (Su-34s) in Volgograd Oblast and a hit on a Transneft pumping station in Bashkortostan; Ukrainian military intelligence claimed hits on the Slavyansk Eco refinery. The Ukrainian strike claims and the reported damage in Russia have not been independently verified; the Kyiv Independent stated it could not verify the reports.

Russian senators submit bill to the State Duma criminalising the dissemination of information on the aftermath of drone and missile strikes, with up to five years in prison; pro-war bloggers unanimously criticise it

2026-11-26

A group of Russian senators has submitted a bill to the State Duma that would make it a criminal offence to "unlawfully" disseminate information about the aftermath of attacks on sites in Russia, Meduza reported on 26 November 2026; the bill has been published in the Duma's legislative database and would add a new article to the Criminal Code. Covered would be information about the "aftermath of the enemy's use" of drones and missiles, about the locations of military equipment and weapons storage sites, and about the aftermath of terrorist attacks, sabotage and assassination attempts against government and public figures. The first part of the article, with a maximum of three years in prison, applies to people who already have an administrative violation on record for a similar offence; the second applies to anyone, but only if the dissemination led to serious consequences such as accidental death, harm to health or property, or mass violations of public order, and carries a maximum of five years. According to the explanatory note, the aim is to protect information whose unauthorised dissemination "could endanger the state's defense capability", the safety of the population, and strategically important and military sites. Meduza does not name the senators, and the bill does not define what counts as "lawful" dissemination. The bill would turn a patchwork of regional rules into federal criminal law. By May 2026, when Moscow introduced its own ban on publishing photos and videos of the aftermath of terrorist attacks and drone strikes, more than 30 regions already had similar bans; in Moscow, violations are punished only with fines (3,000-5,000 rubles for individuals, 30,000-50,000 for officials and 50,000-200,000 for legal entities). In military terms, the measure targets the open-source imagery that both the public and the attacking side use to assess where drones and missiles hit and how well Russian air defence performed. The bill is at the submission stage; it has not been debated or adopted. The Kremlin defended it, saying, as quoted by Meduza, that "there is a war going on" and that wartime decisions "must be made to ensure security". In a follow-up on 27 November, Meduza reported that authors of pro-war Z-channels and "military correspondents" reacted with unanimous criticism. Meduza published their views as a single combined, lightly edited text, without attributing points to individual named bloggers. In it, they argue that almost anything could count as unlawful dissemination, from forwarding a video to one's mother to a screenshot from a channel registered with the media regulator Roskomnadzor, and that merely saying the word "strike" could land someone in court: "No reports, no strikes." They say nobody should be jailed over poorly organised regional air defence, warn that the result will be "total censorship" pushing Russians towards Ukrainian social media, and propose prompt official reporting on strikes right after they happen instead of a week later. In their view the law is meant to "shut up" critics and eliminate "unnecessary questions" about how the state allows the attacks to happen, and is "either legally incompetent, or deliberately written so that anyone could be imprisoned".
