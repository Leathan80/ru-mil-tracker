2026-11-10

Russia's Defence Ministry claimed for the first time that its air defences shot down AGM-188A "Rusty Dagger" cruise missiles, the US-made weapon better known as ERAM (Extended Range Attack Munition), Defence Blog reports. The statement was later deleted from the ministry's official accounts without explanation. The ministry gave no details on where the missiles were intercepted or how many; Ukraine has not commented, and neither side's claims could be independently verified. If accurate, it would be the first official Russian acknowledgment that the missiles are being fired at targets inside Russia; the deletion leaves open whether it was published in error, was inaccurate, or was withdrawn for another reason. ERAM is a low-cost air-launched cruise missile the US Air Force developed on a fast timeline to give Ukraine long-range strike weapons in large numbers; Zone 5 Technologies produces the AGM-188A version. Public data give a range of about 450 km and a 230 kg (500 lb) warhead. The US State Department approved the sale of up to 3,350 ERAMs to Ukraine in August 2025 (about $825 million), to be paid by Denmark, the Netherlands, Norway and US Foreign Military Financing, with Ukraine's F-16s expected as the main launch platform. The US Air Force also wants 1,000 more ERAMs within 15 months. Russian military channels reported suspected first uses in June and August, which could not be confirmed; Russian sources later said they recovered parts of one missile, and Moscow also said Ukraine used AGM-188s in a strike on Voronezh. The interception claim is a Russian MoD claim, since withdrawn, and is unverified.

Ukraine's Pluton detection system upgraded to detect the mesh-network modems of Russian Geran, Gerbera and Molniya drones

2026-11-10

The Ukrainian company INFOZAHYST told Militarnyi that its Pluton drone-detection ESM system has been upgraded to detect the mesh modems used in Russian Geran-, Gerbera- and Molniya-type drones. Monitoring covers the main known frequencies of 1.5 GHz and 3.4 GHz, and the developer says the upgrade allows timely detection of threats at distances of up to 70 km. A new software version also allows remote monitoring and partial control of any number of Pluton systems over a dedicated VPN. Mesh modems are one of the most notable recent upgrades to Russian drones: each drone acts as both receiver and relay, which extends control range, lets operators correct a drone's flight path almost until impact, and makes jamming much harder because the network reroutes when a node drops out. Euromaidan Press notes that a jam-resistant link still emits and can therefore be detected. It also recalls earlier reporting that the mesh modem found in downed Russian drones is an openly sold Chinese part (Frontelligence Insight, August 2026), and that Ukraine's HUR in December 2025 described a Geran-2 variant relaying video and commands via a Chinese Xingkay Tech XK-F358 modem. Pluton scans 500 to 6,000 MHz (extendable to 24 GHz according to Militarnyi), already detects FPV, Supercam, Zala, Lancet and Orlan drones, estimates distance and time of arrival at a protected site, and can work with the Plastun-RP-3000M direction finder. Digital FPV video detection and control of counter-UAS assets are planned for the next update. All performance figures are developer claims; it is not stated whether the 70 km range applies specifically to mesh modems, and there is no independent test data or information on deployment numbers.

«Противник заметил ложные цели-МОГи» (Dva Mayora: 'The enemy has spotted the decoy mobile fire groups') — Russian milblogger Dva Mayora responds to the decoy reports with guidance on making decoys convincing to thermal cameras

2026-11-09SINGLE SOURCEMILBLOGGER

The Russian milblogger channel Dva Mayora (Two Majors) acknowledges that "the enemy has spotted" Russian decoy mobile fire groups (MOG), noting that photos of such decoys are circulating online. Rather than deny it, the channel gives practical advice on building decoys, especially for night use. Because Ukrainian drones carry thermal cameras, the operator can tell a black wooden mop handle from a machine-gun barrel, so dummy weapons should be made of metal. Mannequins and the bonnet or other warm areas of dummy vehicles must be warm: with power banks and electric heating mats, chemical hand warmers or other improvised material, troops should imitate a "cooling" engine and the warmth of faces and hands. Tracks also matter: if the rut to a fake MOG is overgrown with grass or covered in snow, the enemy will fly an extra reconnaissance pass. Positions must be plausible: a fake observation post (ПВН) should be at least minimally prepared, for example with sandbags, and a fake MOG should stand where a real one would logically be placed. The channel suggests commanders use a Mavic with a thermal camera, or just a thermal imager, to compare a properly made decoy with a badly made one that is merely painted and fitted with cold LED lamps that a thermal camera ignores. It also calls for fake "radars" made from underfloor heating, plus fake MOGs and observation posts. Commanders are asked to reward personnel whose decoy position was struck by a real Ukrainian drone, which the channel says costs the enemy tens of thousands of dollars, adding that a drone spent on a decoy will not hit real people. In a follow-up post the channel shares what it calls an interesting decoy example "from the last century" and quotes a "comrade Rogozin", who says he once proposed such decoys to former defence leadership and was ridiculed. In the quote he argues that decoys would now need chemical warmers against drone thermal cameras and emitters to fool electronic reconnaissance, which front-line workshops could make, while highly accurate replicas of real equipment can only be produced in the rear. This is a single source: a Russian milblogger channel. It is a personal or editorial voice, not a position of the Russian Ministry of Defence, and milbloggers can contradict each other and the MoD.
