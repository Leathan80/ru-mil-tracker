2026-11-13

Ukrainian drone attacks have paralysed Russian airports, Meduza writes in a feature published on 13 November 2026. Russian regions impose a "carpet protocol" — the suspension of all flights in an area — almost every day, causing cancellations and delays whose length neither passengers nor airport staff can predict. According to Meduza, passengers get little guidance, airlines try to save on compensation, and airports were not designed for passengers from dozens of flights to be stranded at once. The piece is built around one reader's account of a night in early November at Moscow's Sheremetyevo airport, where a carpet protocol was in force for the whole night. Her 1 a.m. flight was pushed back repeatedly, to 6:30, 10:00 and finally 10:40 a.m. Planes that had begun taxiing were held on the tarmac for hours and then returned to the terminal, including a flight to Vietnam whose passengers sat on the runway from 9 p.m. to midnight. Fellow passengers included people who had been trying to reach Surgut for days and a man unable to fly to Vladivostok for a week. The account is a single personal testimony, illustrated with photos from four Moscow airports on carpet-protocol days, and gives no figures on the overall scale of the disruption. It does illustrate how the airspace closures Russia imposes during drone raids create a recurring civilian and economic cost far from the front line.

He called the talks encouraging, but no agreement on the additional aircraft has been finalised. The intended role builds on Aero Vodochody's 'Drone Hunter' concept: an electro-optical targeting system plus launchers for 70 mm laser-guided rockets, developed with Thales Belgium, with gun pods as an option. This configuration is not yet in operational service. The Skyfox is a modernised L-39 with a US Williams FJ44-4M turbofan, a glass cockpit and five hardpoints. TWZ gives a top speed of about 560 mph (about 900 km/h) and a ceiling of 38,000 ft. Aero Vodochody puts operating costs at about $1,700–2,300 per flight hour, against about $22,000 for an F-16-class fighter. A 70 mm guided rocket of the APKWS II type costs roughly $20,000–25,000, against about $1 million for an AIM-120 and about $500,000 for an AIM-9X. The jet is meant to take pressure off Ukraine's F-16s, MiG-29s, Su-27s and Mirages, more than half of whose F-16s were reportedly stuck in maintenance amid shortages of AIM-9 and AIM-120 missiles. It is aimed especially at Russia's jet-powered Geran-3/4/5 drones, with the Geran-4 reportedly reaching about 280–310 mph and 26,000 ft. Justin Bronk of RUSI called the L-39NG very cheap and simple to operate compared with frontline fighters, with speed and endurance suited to intercepting jet drones. His main caveat is the lack of an onboard radar, which Ukraine's ground radar network can partly offset. Many Ukrainian pilots and technicians already know the L-39. Aero Vodochody builds about 12 Skyfox a year (capacity 18), and its schedule is reportedly booked through Q2 2027, which could delay deliveries. Russian outlet Topwar reported the same Czech claims; its readers questioned the radar-less concept and the delivery backlog.
