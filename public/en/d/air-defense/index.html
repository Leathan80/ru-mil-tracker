Russian state-linked entities have spent about 17 billion rubles on anti-drone systems since 2022, Novaya Gazeta Europe finds

2026-10-29

Russian state-owned and state-linked organisations have spent about 17 billion rubles on counter-drone systems since the start of the full-scale invasion of Ukraine. That is the finding of a Novaya Gazeta Europe analysis of public procurement records, republished by Meduza on 29 October 2026. The biggest buyer is Rosseti, the state operator of Russia's power transmission grid, which together with its subsidiaries spent more than 3 billion rubles. Moscow's water utility Mosvodokanal spent 1.7 billion rubles and energy company Inter RAO 1.3 billion. Sheremetyevo Airport and the State Air Traffic Management Corporation are also among the largest buyers, but no amounts are given for them. According to the analysis, the top priority is protecting infrastructure that supplies electricity, heat and water to homes, and airports come second. Hundreds of millions of rubles are going to airport protection even far from Ukraine: Yuzhno-Sakhalinsk airport alone spent about 300 million rubles. The report does not say which types of systems were bought. The spending fits a wider push to make infrastructure operators responsible for their own defence against Ukrainian long-range drone strikes. In late August, President Putin signed a decree allowing the state to impose temporary management on critical infrastructure whose owners fail to protect it against security threats, including drone attacks. Rosimushchestvo, the federal property agency, or another body named by the government would then take over.

HUR says Russia is building a new jet-drone launch site 3.5 km from Millerovo airfield in Rostov Oblast, able to handle up to 100 launches a day

2026-10-28

Ukraine's military intelligence agency HUR said on 28 October, citing satellite imagery, that Russia is building a new attack-drone site near Millerovo airfield. The airfield is at Dolotinka in Rostov Oblast, about 20 km east of Ukraine's Luhansk Oblast. According to HUR, the site's layout points to the preparation and launch of jet-powered attack drones, likely Geran-4 and Geran-5 and their variants. HUR said the site showed no significant signs of construction in late August 2026, and that launch positions and supporting infrastructure have been built since then. It attributes the fast pace to the reuse of technical solutions Russia has already developed. HUR estimates that, once finished, the site could handle up to 100 drone launches a day. It adds that the real tempo will depend on the supply of finished drones, fuel and warheads, the number of trained crews, equipment readiness and the missions Russia assigns. The site is 3.5 km from the airfield, so according to HUR it can benefit from the air defences already protecting the airfield and use the airfield's roads, utilities, logistics and technical support. HUR describes the construction as possibly part of Russian preparations to step up attacks on Ukraine, particularly during the winter. HUR says the new site would add to Russia's network of at least 12 drone launch sites. It also says Russia is upgrading its jet drones: an updated Geran-4 reportedly has additional autonomous navigation systems and a new warhead designed to hit bridges and power transmission towers. HUR puts the range at up to 770 km for Geran-4 and 950 km for Geran-5, which it says makes them a threat to Ukraine's neighbours as well. These are claims by Ukrainian intelligence.

Ukraine's General Staff says it shot down nearly 60% of Russian jet-powered Geran-4 drones over the past three months

2026-10-27

In an update on its Telegram channel on 27 October 2026, Ukraine's General Staff said the shoot-down rate of Russia's jet-powered Geran drones is steadily rising. Over the past three months Ukrainian air defence has intercepted nearly 60% of the Geran-4 jet drones launched, and in some individual attacks the rate reached 80%. Russia has increasingly been using these faster jet-powered variants alongside the propeller-driven Shahed/Geran-2 type. Their speed makes them harder to counter. The General Staff gave no interception rate for the more advanced Geran-5 jet drone. According to the General Staff, several means are proving effective. Tactical aviation (F-16s, French Mirage fighters and MiG-29s) is used to hunt the jet drones. These mobile fire groups carry machine guns and also MANPADS, which the General Staff says troops have used successfully against jet drones. Ukraine is testing cheaper interceptor-drone models, some of which have reportedly already downed dozens of jet-powered Gerans. No models or exact figures were given. The stated aim is to raise interception rates while cutting the cost of defence, because Ukraine is short of guided surface-to-air missiles and wants to keep them for targets that require them. Caveat: all figures come from Ukraine's General Staff, a party to the conflict. They have not been independently verified, and the absolute number of Geran-4 launches behind the percentages was not disclosed.

2026-10-25

Russia has built more than a dozen new towers for air-defence systems around Vladimir Putin's residence at Valdai (Novgorod region) in October, bringing the number of air-defence positions in the area to about 50, or by some counts more than 50. The finding comes from Radio Liberty's (RFE/RL) Russian Service, based on satellite imagery analysed jointly with an OSINT researcher who posts on X as @jembobineuse (jembob); Radio Liberty journalist Mark Krutov mapped the positions. Ukrainska Pravda and Militarnyi report 16 new towers, Meduza's summary of the same investigation 'at least 15'. They were built within a 50 km radius, mainly south of the residence on the side facing Ukraine, forming an additional outer ring. The journalists assume they are meant for Pantsir-S1 gun-missile systems or Tor surface-to-air missile systems. No new positions have yet been found in the northern part of the outer ring; these may follow in the coming months. The new ring runs past Khotilovo military airfield, which is used for Putin's flights and is linked to the Valdai residence by a dedicated railway line; one tower there was built as early as March 2026. Several more positions sit on the shore of the Vyshny Volochyok Reservoir, around Lisiy Island, where according to an earlier Radio Liberty investigation the Russian military built a laboratory complex that may be linked to a biological weapons programme; construction of two towers near that facility began in early September, around the time that investigation was published. Meduza adds the wider picture from the OSINT analyst's count: Russia now has about 177 towers carrying short-range air-defence systems, of which about 98 are in and around Moscow and about 50 near Valdai. Fewer than 100,000 people live within 50 km of Valdai, against more than 20 million in the capital region. The build-up has been gradual: the first air-defence system near the residence appeared in January 2023 and a second in July 2024; by April 2026 satellite imagery showed at least 27 units around it, after seven Pantsir towers were added in March. Russian authorities claimed that more than 90 Ukrainian drones tried to attack the residence in late December 2025; Ukraine denies this, and according to Meduza the CIA concluded that the target of the Ukrainian drone attack in Novgorod region was an unspecified military facility not near the residence. Militarnyi also points to the parallel expansion around Moscow: about 24 new Pantsir towers were reported in July, and at least seven more in June after a Ukrainian drone attack in May. The findings rest on open-source satellite analysis and have not been confirmed by Russian authorities; the exact counts differ slightly between reports.
