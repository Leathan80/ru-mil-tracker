Ukraine's General Staff has approved a new procedure for the use of radars, electronic warfare (EW) equipment and other gear by the air-defence groups that Ukrainian companies may set up to protect themselves against Russian air attacks. Applications to use such equipment, and requests for radio-frequency assignments, will now be processed within five working days. Once approved, companies receive the status of special users of the radio spectrum, allowing them to operate the equipment under the rules that apply to the military. According to the article, the private groups do not act independently: they work with the Ukrainian Air Force, are connected to the unified air-threat detection system, and the military command decides whether a target may be engaged. ArmyInform recalls that as of 14 October almost 100 companies had joined the project, forming about 50 groups, six of which had flown combat tasks and destroyed more than 60 Russian aerial targets. The experimental project began in November 2025; in March 2026 the government allowed companies to receive surplus air-defence weapons and ammunition from the military. The scheme reflects the scale of the Russian drone threat to Ukrainian industrial sites. Single source: ArmyInform is a Ukrainian (MoD/defence) outlet; claims are not independently verified.

2026-10-30

Serhii "Flash" Beskrestnov, Ukraine's presidential adviser on defence technology, told journalists in Kyiv on 29 October that Russia's jet-powered Geran-5 drones have reached about 1,000 km/h during maneuvers, Euromaidan Press reports. Ukraine's military intelligence (HUR) puts the Geran-5's cruise speed at 450–600 km/h, while according to Beskrestnov the earlier Geran-3 flew at 280–300 km/h. From 1 to 26 September only 55% of Russia's jet-powered drones were shot down; on 27 October the General Staff said nearly 60% of jet-powered Geran-4s had been downed over the past three months and pointed to a shortage of guided anti-aircraft missiles (no Geran-5 figure was given). On 29 October a jet-powered drone hit Dnipro railway station; the death toll has since risen to 15. According to Beskrestnov, Ukrainian firms have already shown jet-powered interceptor drones but are struggling with control, guidance, automatic target lock and scaling up production. Some foreign interceptors are being designed for the Geran-5's cruise speed, such as the Vespid Mk 1 from US firm CHAOS Industries, with a claimed top speed of 540 km/h.

US approves possible $2.1bn sale of 500 Patriot PAC-3 MSE interceptors to Ukraine, paid for by Canada, Germany and Norway, as NATO's top commander says PURL deliveries face no delays

2026-10-30

On 30 October the US State Department approved a possible sale to Ukraine of 500 Patriot PAC-3 Missile Segment Enhancement (MSE) interceptors, with spare parts and logistical support, at an estimated $2.1 billion, the Kyiv Independent, Meduza and the Associated Press report. Canada, Germany and Norway would finance the purchase through the US JUMPSTART programme and no US government funds are involved; additional costs would be met from Ukrainian funds backed by the EU's Ukraine Support Loan. The approval is a step towards a sale, not a signed contract: Congress normally has 30 days to review it and the State Department gave no delivery timeline. Zelensky welcomed the decision but warned that deliveries could take several years, while Ukraine needs the missiles this winter to protect its energy infrastructure; he urged the US and partners to supply interceptors from existing stocks now and replenish them later through the new contracts. The same day NATO's Supreme Allied Commander Europe, US Gen. Alexus Grynkewich, told reporters in Riga that US deliveries to Ukraine were ongoing, with one in the past week, that no items in European-funded PURL packages had been delayed and that every third-party transfer requested of the US government had been approved, according to Bloomberg as cited by Ukrainska Pravda and the Kyiv Independent. He declined to give numbers or missile types. That account conflicts with Kyiv's: Zelensky says Ukraine has received an average of only 12 Patriot interceptors a month from the US in 2026 and had waited nearly a month for Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth to sign off on allied-funded Patriot and NASAMS transfers (see the earlier entry on that hold). Secretary of State Marco Rubio said on 20 October that nothing was 'being deliberately denied to Ukraine'. Euromaidan Press, citing a NATO source quoted by Radio Svoboda on 29 October, reports that the US is holding back PAC-3 interceptors, the Patriot variant built mainly against ballistic missiles, while older PAC-2s, used chiefly against aircraft and cruise missiles, keep arriving via PURL; the source said the matter is being discussed at a high political level. Zelensky said on 27 October that Russia had fired more than 100 ballistic missiles at Kyiv and Kyiv Oblast in two weeks and that Ukraine above all lacks Patriot missiles, and on 29 October that intelligence points to new ballistic attacks being prepared. The PAC-3 hold claim rests on a single anonymous source and is not independently verified; the delivery figures come from Ukrainian officials, a party to the war, and are contradicted by Grynkewich.

2026-10-29

Ukraine has completed its share of Freyja, the multinational anti-ballistic system it is building with European partners, President Zelenskyy said on 17 October, Defense News reports. Ukrainian firm Fire Point is building the FP-7.X interceptor and launcher, while partners including Kongsberg, Diehl Defence and Hensoldt supply the sensors, seeker, fire control and command centre. Separately, during a visit to the Netherlands on 28 October, Zelenskyy announced a joint Ukrainian–Dutch air-defence project called 'Bliksem', without giving details. Euromaidan Press notes that if it is the same as the Bliksem EXO consortium formed this summer by Destinus (Dutch-led), MBDA, Safran, Airbus and Thales, the goal is a European exo-atmospheric interceptor in the class of THAAD, SM-3 and Arrow 3, free of US export restrictions, with a space test of the kill vehicle planned for 2027. That link has not been confirmed, and such systems historically take a decade or more to field. The context is Russia's sustained ballistic campaign. According to Zelenskyy, Russia fired more than 100 ballistic missiles at Kyiv and the surrounding region in two weeks and launched nearly 3,500 jet-powered drones in September. Ukrainian military intelligence estimates Russian Iskander production at 55–60 a month, while Ukraine says it received only 24 Patriot interceptors in three months against a need of about 350 a month. Neither project offers near-term relief against Russia's short-range ballistic missiles. Bliksem is explicitly aimed at heavier threats such as Oreshnik.

Russian state-linked entities have spent about 17 billion rubles on anti-drone systems since 2022, Novaya Gazeta Europe finds

2026-10-29

Russian state-owned and state-linked organisations have spent about 17 billion rubles on counter-drone systems since the start of the full-scale invasion of Ukraine. That is the finding of a Novaya Gazeta Europe analysis of public procurement records, republished by Meduza on 29 October 2026. The biggest buyer is Rosseti, the state operator of Russia's power transmission grid, which together with its subsidiaries spent more than 3 billion rubles. The report does not say which types of systems were bought. The spending fits a wider push to make infrastructure operators responsible for their own defence against Ukrainian long-range drone strikes. In late August, President Putin signed a decree allowing the state to impose temporary management on critical infrastructure whose owners fail to protect it against security threats, including drone attacks. Rosimushchestvo, the federal property agency, or another body named by the government would then take over.

HUR says Russia is building a new jet-drone launch site 3.5 km from Millerovo airfield in Rostov Oblast, able to handle up to 100 launches a day

2026-10-28

Ukraine's military intelligence agency HUR said on 28 October, citing satellite imagery, that Russia is building a new attack-drone site near Millerovo airfield. The airfield is at Dolotinka in Rostov Oblast, about 20 km east of Ukraine's Luhansk Oblast. According to HUR, the site's layout points to the preparation and launch of jet-powered attack drones, likely Geran-4 and Geran-5 and their variants. HUR said the site showed no significant signs of construction in late August 2026, and that launch positions and supporting infrastructure have been built since then. It attributes the fast pace to the reuse of technical solutions Russia has already developed. HUR estimates that, once finished, the site could handle up to 100 drone launches a day. It adds that the real tempo will depend on the supply of finished drones, fuel and warheads, the number of trained crews, equipment readiness and the missions Russia assigns. The site is 3.5 km from the airfield, so according to HUR it can benefit from the air defences already protecting the airfield and use the airfield's roads, utilities, logistics and technical support. Critical data centres are to be split into three categories by the consequences of an attack; category K1 covers strategic facilities whose damage could disrupt key government functions for an hour or more. For these, the recommendations call for underground or bunker placement. They also call for reducing data centres' visibility from the air and combining 'physical reinforcement' with electronic-warfare equipment and counter-air systems, and apply to facilities being designed, built or already operating, regardless of owner. The move follows a Ukrainian campaign against Russian data centres that began in October in response to Russian strikes on data centres in Kyiv. Within a week, Ukrainian drones hit three of Yandex's six data centres — in Sasovo (Ryazan region), Kaluga and Vladimir — and later a Sberbank data centre in the Saratov region. IT-market sources cited by Kommersant estimate that replacing equipment knocked out at Yandex will take up to six months. Separately, Yandex said it has moved all three of its supercomputers — Lyapunov, Galushkin and Chervonenkis — out of their previous locations to new computing sites, and that they were not damaged in the drone attacks. Two of them were housed at Sasovo; Chervonenkis ranked 19th in the world TOP500 list in 2021. A source cited by Forbes Russia said the three machines are independent nodes rather than one supercluster, so they can be placed separately. The claim that they were undamaged comes from Yandex itself and has not been independently verified.

Ukrainian maker PUF unveils Ban-One interceptor drone with a claimed peak speed of 370 km/h, marketed against Shahed-type drones

2026-10-26

Ukrainian manufacturer PUF (Persha Universalna Firma) has launched Ban-One, a high-speed interceptor drone for countering UAVs, according to a piece in Militarnyi's 'special projects' section (a format that appears to present manufacturer material). Speed was the design priority: the drone engages targets at 190–345 km/h and reaches a peak of 370 km/h. Its electronic speed controllers are mounted externally to keep it cool at sustained high RPM, and it uses an 8S3P battery pack for high current output. PUF says Ban-One is designed to destroy rotary- and fixed-wing UAVs, 'successfully hunts' Shahed-type drones and remains effective against targets at altitudes of several kilometres. The fiberglass, modular airframe (nicknamed 'banana' for its shape and yellow colour) takes analog or digital video, day/low-light or thermal cameras, and interchangeable lower sections for warheads of different sizes, with a maximum payload of 400 g. Analog video runs on 5.8 and 6.7 GHz, digital on 5.8 GHz, and the control link can use 500–1050 MHz or 1.9–2.6 GHz. The system includes an in-house ground control station that also works with digital and fiber-optic drones, and PUF can adapt the drone to a customer's ground station. Ban-One has been NATO-codified with a NATO Stock Number, can be ordered from the manufacturer and will soon be listed on Brave1 Market; range, altitude and endurance data are only available on request. All performance claims come from the manufacturer and have not been independently verified.
