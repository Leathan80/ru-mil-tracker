A long analysis on Topwar (Voennoye Obozreniye) weighs the strike campaigns both sides waged against each other's infrastructure from May to October 2026. On the Russian side it lists strikes on Ukrainian ports, steel and energy: according to the article, Ukraine's agriculture minister said in July that shipping in Ukrainian ports had stopped, the Financial Times reported that the three biggest steel plants (up to 90% of steel output) had halted, metal exports fell 48% in August, and the economy shrank 2.4% in September, with transport value added down 20% and grain exports down 54% to 574,000 tonnes. He discusses the recurring debate over why the Dnipro bridges and the Beskyd tunnel have not been destroyed without taking a position, notes a reported strike on Kyiv's Southern Bridge on 27 October with unclear effect, and argues that Ukrainian society is tired but not ready to abandon its political course. He cites Putin's figure of about 6,500 km² taken in 2026 up to 16 October. The piece is unusually critical for a Russian outlet, for instance on who is responsible for defending data centres. Serhii "Flash" Beskrestnov, Ukraine's presidential adviser on defence technology, told journalists in Kyiv on 29 October that Russia's jet-powered Geran-5 drones have reached about 1,000 km/h during maneuvers, Euromaidan Press reports. On 29 October a jet-powered drone hit Dnipro railway station; the death toll has since risen to 15. According to Beskrestnov, Ukrainian firms have already shown jet-powered interceptor drones but are struggling with control, guidance, automatic target lock and scaling up production. US approves possible $2.1bn sale of 500 Patriot PAC-3 MSE interceptors to Ukraine, paid for by Canada, Germany and Norway, but deliveries reportedly not before 2030; NATO's top commander says PURL deliveries face no delays

2026-10-30

On 30 October the US State Department approved a possible sale to Ukraine of 500 Patriot PAC-3 Missile Segment Enhancement (MSE) interceptors, with spare parts and logistical support, at an estimated $2.1 billion, the Kyiv Independent, Meduza and the Associated Press report. Canada, Germany and Norway would finance the purchase through the US JUMPSTART programme and no US government funds are involved; additional costs would be met from Ukrainian funds backed by the EU's Ukraine Support Loan. The approval is a step towards a sale, not a signed contract: Congress normally has 30 days to review it and the State Department gave no delivery timeline. Radio Free Europe/Radio Liberty's Washington correspondent Alex Raufoglu, citing sources familiar with the matter, reported that deliveries are not expected before 2030, so the deal does little for Ukraine's acute interceptor shortage this winter (via Ukrainska Pravda). Zelensky welcomed the decision but wrote that it had been 'stipulated' that deliveries may begin 'only in a few years', while Ukraine needs the missiles now: he said Russia had carried out more than 30 strikes on key parts of the power grid in the last two weeks of October alone, that Kyiv had asked Washington for a winter support package of 100 Patriot interceptors a month, and that the US and partners should supply PAC-3s from existing stocks in winter 2026-2027 and replenish them later from the production already contracted and paid for. US Senator Richard Blumenthal called the delivery timeline a 'disappointing catch' and urged immediate aid. The same day NATO's Supreme Allied Commander Europe, US Gen. Alexus Grynkewich, told reporters in Riga that US deliveries to Ukraine were ongoing, with one in the past week, that no items in European-funded PURL packages had been delayed and that every third-party transfer requested of the US government had been approved, according to Bloomberg as cited by Ukrainska Pravda and the Kyiv Independent. He declined to give numbers or missile types. That account conflicts with Kyiv's: Zelensky says Ukraine has received an average of only 12 Patriot interceptors a month from the US in 2026 and had waited nearly a month for Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth to sign off on allied-funded Patriot and NASAMS transfers (see the earlier entry on that hold). Secretary of State Marco Rubio said on 20 October that nothing was 'being deliberately denied to Ukraine'. Euromaidan Press, citing a NATO source quoted by Radio Svoboda on 29 October, reports that the US is holding back PAC-3 interceptors, the Patriot variant built mainly against ballistic missiles, while older PAC-2s, used chiefly against aircraft and cruise missiles, keep arriving via PURL; the source said the matter is being discussed at a high political level. Zelensky said on 27 October that Russia had fired more than 100 ballistic missiles at Kyiv and Kyiv Oblast in two weeks and that Ukraine above all lacks Patriot missiles, and on 29 October that intelligence points to new ballistic attacks being prepared. The PAC-3 hold claim rests on a single anonymous source and is not independently verified; the delivery figures come from Ukrainian officials, a party to the war, and are contradicted by Grynkewich.

Russia hits Kyiv with ballistic missiles in two waves on 26–27 October, striking a university campus and again the Pivdennyi (Southern) Bridge; only two missiles reported downed

2026-10-26

Russia struck Kyiv in two ballistic missile waves a few hours apart. The first came on the evening of 26 October. At 21:41 Ukraine's Air Force warned of a ballistic threat from the Bryansk area, and monitoring channels reported missiles approaching through Chernihiv Oblast. Within minutes they hit the left-bank Desnianskyi district: a university campus and its student hall of residence, Kyoto Park, residential buildings and the area near a metro station. Blast damage also reached medical facilities in Dniprovskyi district. Zelensky said there was no military facility nearby. He also said Russia launched ballistic missiles again while emergency services were still working at the scene. An ambulance crew that had arrived was hit and two medics were injured. The second wave came before dawn on 27 October. It hit an administrative building in Podilskyi district and, at about 05:04, the Pivdennyi (Southern) Bridge and the metro infrastructure on it. Euromaidan Press notes that the same bridge's metro infrastructure had already been damaged on 22 October. According to the Air Force, Kyiv was the main target of an overnight attack with Iskander-M/S-400 ballistic missiles (the Air Force reports the two as one category) and about 280 drones: Shahed (69 of them jet-powered), Gerbera and Parodiya decoys. Air defence destroyed or jammed only two of the ballistic missiles. It did the same to 255 drones, 56 of them jet-powered. The Air Force recorded hits at 25 locations and falling debris at 16. Zelensky put the night's total at 16 ballistic missiles and more than 280 drones. Strikes also hit at least ten other oblasts, mainly energy, logistics and urban infrastructure. He called the attack 'a serious escalation in Russia's operation against our logistics.' Police closed the Pivdennyi Bridge to all traffic. Green-line metro trains stopped crossing it and now run only between Syrets and Vydubychi. In Kyiv, one person was killed (a 46-year-old woman) and about 15 were injured, many of them students. Prosecutors counted at least 12 damaged apartment blocks. In his evening address on 27 October, Zelensky said Russia had launched more than 100 ballistic missiles at Kyiv and Kyiv Oblast since 14 October. He said Ukraine does not have enough Patriot missiles. He blamed 'artificial and prolonged delays' in deliveries of Patriot and NASAMS interceptor packages and asked for the means to destroy ballistic missile launchers. Euromaidan Press writes that Patriot is the only system in Ukraine that reliably stops the Iskander-M, and that the interceptor package has waited almost a month for a US signature. The attack and interception figures come from the Ukrainian Air Force and the president. They have not been independently verified.
