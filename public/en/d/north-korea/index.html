North Korea shows a new flying-wing UAV for the first time; Militarnyi says it resembles the nEUROn and X-47B stealth drone designs

2026-10-30

North Korea has shown a new unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) with a delta-shaped flying-wing design for the first time. It appeared at the opening of the 'Unmanned Aeronautical Technology Development – 2026' military exhibition, attended by Kim Jong Un and senior military leaders. The photos were released by the North Korean state news agency KCNA. According to Militarnyi, the drone looks like Western stealth drone designs, in particular the European nEUROn (Dassault) and the American X-47B (Northrop Grumman). It has a smooth blended-wing body, no vertical tail and an air intake on top, a layout typically used to reduce radar signature. Its technical specifications, role and development status are unknown, and the assessment rests on photographs only. The exhibition also showed conventional reconnaissance UAVs and kamikaze attack drones. Militarnyi notes that North Korea earlier showed large-scale production of attack drones resembling the Israeli Harop loitering munition, which it first revealed in August 2024. The outlet also writes that North Korea is rearming in partnership with China and Russia and is building lessons from the Russian-Ukrainian war into the Korean People's Army's training and combat programmes. The article does not say whether this specific UAV was developed with foreign help.

Sources: Militarnyi

«Балістика та понад сотня дронів: чим росія била по Україні вночі» (ArmyInform: 'Ballistic missiles and over a hundred drones: what Russia struck Ukraine with overnight')

2026-10-30SINGLE SOURCE

According to the Ukrainian Air Force, as relayed by ArmyInform, Russia attacked Ukraine on the night of 30 October with ballistic missiles of the Iskander-M/S-400/KN-23 types, four Kh-59/69 guided air-launched missiles and 113 Shahed-type strike drones, Gerbera drones and Parodiya-type decoy drones. Of the 113 Shaheds, 83 were jet-powered. The number of ballistic missiles was not stated. The main axes of attack were the Kyiv, Poltava and Dnipro regions. The notable element is the share of jet-powered Shaheds: roughly three quarters of the Shahed-type drones launched that night were jet variants, against 40 of 112 the night before. The mention of North Korean KN-23 missiles alongside Iskander-M and S-400 shows these weapons remain part of the ballistic strike mix. Single source: this is a claim by ArmyInform, a Ukrainian state/military outlet (relaying the Ukrainian Air Force), not independently verified.

CSIS/Bush Institute report: about 12,000 North Korean workers assemble Shahed/Geran drones at Russia's Alabuga zone, with Pyongyang taking up to 90% of their pay

2026-10-21

Militarnyi, citing a joint report by the Washington-based Center for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS) and the George W. Bush Institute ('Human Rights Abuses in Drone Production at Yelabuga', published 14 October 2026), reports that North Korea has sent about 12,000 workers to Russia to assemble Shahed-type strike drones (Geran-2) in the Alabuga Special Economic Zone in Tatarstan. According to the report, they earn about $2.50 per hour, and the North Korean state takes 70–90% of their wages. The researchers point to systematic withholding of wages from North Korean workers and describe the arrangement as forced labour organised by Pyongyang. The report identifies three labour pools at Alabuga: Russian youth, foreign recruits from other countries, and North Koreans. Alabuga College recruits young Russians under the label of 'work placements'. Shifts reportedly last up to 12 hours, pay depends on strict production quotas, and workers are threatened with fines if they tell their families what the plant produces. The researchers suspect coercive labour practices across all three groups. Satellite imagery from September shows new production halls and warehouses under construction at the zone, which Militarnyi links to the recruitment of foreign labour. The 12,000 figure matches the number Ukraine's military intelligence (HUR) gave in November 2025, when it said Moscow planned to bring in about 12,000 North Koreans for Alabuga via the North Korean firm Jihyang Technology Trade Company. Reporting on the CSIS paper indicates that the headcount rests largely on Ukrainian intelligence and has not been independently confirmed. Bloomberg reported in September a higher figure of about 25,000 North Koreans at Russian drone plants, many entering on student visas; the two numbers may count different things. The tracker has not previously covered North Korean labour at Alabuga, but has covered the site itself: plans to expand Alabuga-Volokno's carbon-fibre output that could raise Geran production by up to 78% (e-20260920-05), and Alabuga-built engines and GNSS antennas found in Geran-4 drones (e-20261001-02, e-20261012-04). Workers from North Korea would ease a labour constraint on that expansion.

Taliban and North Korean military officials hold talks on the sidelines of Russia's Tsentr-2026 exercise

2026-10-07

Militarnyi reports that Afghan Taliban and North Korean military officials held talks during Russia's Tsentr-2026 (Center-2026) strategic exercise, held from 28 September to 3 October 2026. Nasibullah Madani, commander of the Taliban's Joint Combat Training Command, met North Korean military attaché Ro Ki Chol at an arms exhibition alongside the drills. The information comes from Afghanistan's Ministry of Defense and Safir TV; a photo released by the Afghan ministry shows the two men together. According to the Afghan ministry, the talks covered bilateral relations, the exchange of military experience, professional training and opportunities for cooperation. No concrete details or agreements were disclosed. The Taliban delegation also held talks with military officials from China, India, Vietnam, Colombia, Uzbekistan and Tajikistan, and toured displays of heavy weapons, drones, long-range missiles and robotic systems. Russia hosted the exercise and invited both delegations as observers. Militarnyi notes that Moscow has deepened its military ties with the Taliban, having signed a military-technical cooperation agreement in May 2026 focused on restoring existing Afghan equipment and setting up maintenance training centres. NK News, citing a Russian Defence Ministry announcement and Afghan ministry photographs, adds that the meeting took place on the sidelines at the Chebarkul training ground, that Russia's Defence Ministry organised the exercise and weapons expo, that North Korea was among 33 observer states, and that eight further countries sent contingents to take part, with roughly 47,000 troops involved. NK News also says the discussions are not specified beyond 'talks on the sidelines'.

Zelenskyy says Russia has already begun additional mobilisation as the Kremlin denies it and Putin enlarges the army again; notaries add paperwork hurdles for draft-age men

2026-09-28

In his evening address on 28 September, after a briefing by Ukrainian Defence Intelligence (HUR), President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said that Russia has "in effect" already begun additional mobilisation, increasing the number of people it recruits through various means, and plans to involve more foreigners; he urged the governments of the countries concerned to counter this. He said more than 8,000 North Korean military personnel are on Russian territory and a further 10,000 are being selected for training and transfer, and that Russia pays Pyongyang partly with technology, including support for North Korean production of Shahed-type drones. The same day, Putin signed a decree raising the authorised strength of the armed forces to 2,441,630, including 1,550,500 military personnel: an increase of 15,500 and, according to Euromaidan Press citing Euronews, the fourth such increase this year (47,860 in total since March). These are claims by a party to the war; no figures were given for the new mobilisation. The Kremlin denies any new call-up. Euromaidan Press notes that Putin called reports of a coming mobilisation "complete nonsense" and "an information attack on Russia" in early September (RUSI attributes the "nonsense" dismissal to Security Council deputy chair Dmitry Medvedev), and recalls that officials gave similar assurances days before the 2022 call-up. According to the Kyiv Independent, a further call-up had been expected after United Russia's victory in the 21 September Duma elections; Ukrainian intelligence says Russia's General Staff has prepared a scenario to mobilise around 300,000 people in each of 2026 and 2027, on top of the roughly 400,000 recruits a year it needs merely to replenish losses. A RUSI commentary (Zoe Neiman, 29 September) argues that Russia has not avoided mobilisation but turned it into a permanent, regionalised process: the 2022 decree was never rescinded, conscription has been year-round since 1 January 2026, and coercion to sign contracts has been documented in at least 17 regions. Signs of pressure also show up in everyday administration. Meduza, citing the rights groups School for Conscripts and the Movement of Conscientious Objectors, reports that notaries in Moscow and the Samara region refuse to certify powers of attorney authorising others to represent draft-age men at enlistment offices unless the men show, via the Gosuslugi portal, that no summons has been issued to them in the summons register (fully operational since May 2025). Some notaries also refuse to include powers for deregistration from the military register or representation at a medical board. The Federal Notary Chamber says it issued no such instruction; the rights groups say notaries have no legal authority to demand these records. Background from Meduza: the 2022 partial mobilisation called up over 300,000 men, at least 20,000 of whom have died according to casualty trackers; it follows two of them four years on, one still at the front and one a deserter who sought asylum in Spain.

2026-09-28SINGLE SOURCE

Defense Express reports new details on North Korea's Hwasong-11Ma-1, which Pyongyang first presented in October 2025 and then waited almost a year to launch. It was first tested on 20 September 2026 over 908 km, with the warhead-glider covering most of the distance. Citing Janes estimates, the article gives a body length of 9.6 m, a diameter of 1.1 m and a launch mass of 9.8 tons; it is carried on a six-axle mobile launcher holding one missile. The outlet calls it a "classical" hypersonic missile because of its warhead-glider configuration, as opposed to a conventional ballistic missile. It notes that North Korea already fields the Hwasong-16B, a medium-range missile with a hypersonic glider known since 2024. The central question posed is whether Pyongyang will transfer this missile to Russia; the author describes it as essentially a hypersonic Iskander variant. The article does not report a confirmed transfer. Caveat: this is single-source reporting from Defense Express (Ukrainian defence media); the claims have not been independently verified.

South Korea now withdraws part of its embassy staff from Kyiv, after recalling its ambassador in the dispute over Zelenskyy's disclosure of the transfer of two North Korean POWs

2026-09-23

South Korea is escalating its diplomatic protest against Ukraine: after recalling its ambassador in early October, Seoul will also withdraw part of its diplomatic staff from the embassy in Kyiv, Meduza (citing Yonhap) and Ukrainska Pravda reported on 20 October. According to Meduza, the Foreign Ministry said Kyiv had not taken "positive steps" to resolve the dispute, and also cited ongoing Russian air strikes as complicating the protection of its diplomatic personnel. How many staff will leave, and when, has not been made public; there is no indication that the embassy is closing. The dispute stems from Zelenskyy's address to the UN General Assembly on 23 September, in which he revealed that Ukraine had handed two North Korean prisoners of war, captured while fighting on Russia's side, to South Korea. According to Seoul, both sides had agreed not to disclose the transfer, and Ukraine had initiated that agreement; Zelenskyy's communications adviser Dmytro Lytvyn denied any breach. On 8 October the Foreign Ministry announced the recall of Ambassador Park Hee-chang (Korea Times gives his name as Park Ki-chang) as a form of protest. Seoul demands an official, public apology; Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha apologised only in diplomatic consultations, which Seoul considers insufficient, and President Lee Jae-myung had threatened further measures if Kyiv did not acknowledge a breach. In the same UN speech Zelenskyy said one of the two prisoners had tried to kill himself to avoid capture, and that Russia uses citizens of 47 countries in its war. With the staff cut, a dispute over two POWs has become a second-step diplomatic downgrade, while North Korean troops and munitions remain a factor in Russia's war effort.

Unknown North Korean KN-23 Variant Can Reach 60–70 km Altitude, Posing New Challenge for Patriot Systems

2026-09-22SINGLE SOURCE

Defense Express analyst Ivan Kyrychevskyi, citing Janes, reports that North Korea launched two KN-23 ballistic missiles on 20 September 2026. The first flew 440 km and reached an altitude of about 60 km; the second flew 590 km and peaked at about 70 km. Janes assessed that this may be a new KN-23 variant, possibly with a changed flight trajectory or warhead. According to Defense Express, earlier KN-23 tests typically reached ranges of up to 690 km at maximum altitudes of roughly 37–60 km, while a test on 12 August 2026 went as high as about 90 km. The outlet interprets the current experiments with flight altitude as an attempt to make the missile harder for Patriot systems to intercept. That matters for Ukraine because Russia has fired North Korean KN-23s at Ukrainian targets, and Patriot is Ukraine's main defence against ballistic missiles. Defense Express also argues that the KN-23 relies heavily on Russian technology and documentation, calling North Korea's defence industry effectively a 'branch' of Russia's military-industrial complex, and refers to a 2024 RUSI study on Russian deliveries of materials, components and blueprints for KN-23 production. The link to Patriot evasion is the outlet's own analysis, not a stated North Korean aim. This is a single-source report from Defense Express (a Ukrainian defence outlet, i.e. a source from a warring party, drawing on Janes), not independently corroborated.

Report: up to 25,000 North Koreans may be building military drones for Russia on student visas

2026-09-17

According to a report by the Multilateral Sanctions Monitoring Team (MSMT), published on September 16, 2026, Russia and North Korea have built an extensive system to evade UN sanctions on North Korean labor migration. The report's estimates range from 15,000 to 30,000 North Korean workers employed illegally in Russia in 2025; the majority of them — possibly up to 25,000 people — are believed to have been working on military drone production, particularly at the Alabuga industrial site in Tatarstan, which produces Shahed/Geran attack drones among other systems. The workers reportedly enter Russia through a purpose-built student visa scheme that formally registers them as students rather than employees, circumventing the work permits that would otherwise be required. The report also describes the use of tourist visas, changes to Russian law allowing student-visa holders to earn income, and the bribing of Russian universities to sustain the arrangement. Each worker is said to generate roughly $7,500 a year — up to five times what comparable work pays in China — with most of the wages handed over to the North Korean state. North Korea's overseas labor program is estimated to have generated between $450 million and $800 million in 2025, revenue the sanctions monitors say helps fund Pyongyang's nuclear and ballistic missile programs. UN Security Council resolutions have banned member states from employing North Korean laborers since 2017; if the findings hold, Russia and the workers involved are in ongoing violation of those obligations. The MSMT — an 11-country group including the US, South Korea, and Japan — is the sole source for these figures, which are the monitoring group's own estimates and have not been independently verified.

Sources: meduza-en · kyivindep

Ukrainian Drone Takes Out Rare North Korean Type-75 Rocket Launcher

2026-09-11SINGLE SOURCE

Ukraine's 40th Brigade destroyed a North Korean-supplied Type-75 rocket launcher with a strike drone, according to Defense Express, which cites video released by the brigade. The Type-75 is a 107mm, 12-tube multiple rocket launcher — a North Korean copy of the Chinese Type 63, itself derived from the Soviet BM-14 design of the 1960s. Each unguided rocket weighs about 18 kg with roughly 1.5 kg of explosive in the warhead, effective against area targets but entirely dependent on staying concealed to survive on a battlefield saturated with reconnaissance drones. The system is described as "rare" because, despite Russia's growing reliance on foreign-supplied equipment, North Korean artillery systems still appear infrequently in documented frontline footage. This is not the first confirmed Type-75 kill — Defense Express and other outlets reported similar incidents dating back to mid-2025 — but each new sighting reconfirms that this dated North Korean hardware is actively deployed at the front. The find fits a longer-running pattern of North Korean support to Russia: beyond these rocket systems, Pyongyang has previously supplied millions of artillery shells, deployed KN-23-type ballistic missiles against Ukrainian targets, and since late 2024 has sent its own troops to reinforce Russian forces in the Kursk region. Note: this report rests on a single Ukrainian source (Defense Express) and video evidence supplied by the Ukrainian side; it is not independently verified.

Sources: Defense Express
