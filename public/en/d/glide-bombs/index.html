Ukraine says an intercepted Russian document plans up to 1,000 missiles, drones and bombs against 60 Ukrainian infrastructure targets this winter

2026-10-09

Ukraine says it has obtained a Russian plan for a winter strike campaign against its critical infrastructure. Euromaidan Press, citing The New York Times (2 October) and RFE/RL's Schemes project (8 October), reports that the Russian-language document is dated August and lists 60 key targets to be hit with up to 1,000 weapons. These reportedly include up to 770 Molniya and Geran drones, Dan-T/Banderol missile-drones, 170 FAB guided aerial bombs and 75 Kinzhal and Iskander-M ballistic missiles. The stated aim is to cut electricity, heating, water and sewage services in Kyiv, Kharkiv and Odesa. Twelve other cities are named as priorities, among them Dnipro, Zaporizhzhia, Lviv and Vinnytsia. According to sources cited by the NYT, Energy Minister Denys Shmyhal presented the document as an intercepted Russian plan. The reported sequence begins with substations near the western border, through which Ukraine imports electricity from Europe. Hydroelectric plants would follow, then facilities whose loss would force a shutdown of Ukraine's three operating nuclear power plants. Substations, thermal plants and boiler houses are also listed. Water pumping, treatment and sewage pumping stations receive particular attention; the NYT notes that Russia has not previously attacked such facilities on this scale. Oleh Popenko of the Ukrainian Union of Utility Services Consumers says sewage systems are much harder to protect than district heating, and that only stronger air defence can realistically protect Kyiv's combined heat and power plants. ISW separately says jet-powered Gerans break through Ukrainian air defences more reliably and partly free Russia to concentrate its missiles, and it expects the same pattern in the winter energy campaign. The document's authenticity has not been verified: the NYT could not confirm it, and it is not known how it was intercepted. A Ukrainian defence-industry representative who reviewed it told Schemes it reads more like a calculation than a finalised operations plan. The claim comes as Russia steps up strikes on the grid: blackouts have returned to Kyiv, and Russian strikes recently hit warehouses, a store and a farm, killing three people.

UAC delivers second 2026 batch of Su-34 fighter-bombers; number of aircraft not disclosed

2026-10-07

Rostec announced on 7 October that its subsidiary United Aircraft Corporation (UAC) has handed a new batch of Su-34 fighter-bombers (NATO: Fullback) to the Russian Ministry of Defence under the state defence order. According to Defence Blog, it is the second Su-34 batch delivered in 2026. Rostec says the aircraft completed the full cycle of ground and flight factory tests and have gone to their permanent bases. Rostec did not say how many aircraft were in the batch. Defence analyst Guy Plopsky, quoted by Defence Blog, says the photos and video released by UAC show at least two jets and possibly four. The sources refer only to the Su-34 and do not mention the upgraded Su-34M. Rostec quoted an unnamed Su-34 crew commander as saying the aircraft can strike ground, surface and air targets, including in bad weather and against active air defences. He said its large internal fuel tanks and fuel-efficient engines allow non-stop flights of up to 10 hours. Rostec added that the Su-34 is still being upgraded based on its combat use in Ukraine and that new batches are already in production. The type is built only at the Chkalov aircraft plant in Novosibirsk. Defence Blog says the first 2026 batch was delivered on 9 July, together with Su-30SM2 fighters, after a final 2025 handover on 10 December. Rostec announced seven Su-34 deliveries in 2025. By the start of 2026 the Russian air force had received 212 Su-34s, including 18 in 2025 and seven prototype and pre-production aircraft. Russia has used the Su-34 heavily in Ukraine to drop guided glide bombs on positions and cities from standoff range, and it is among the Russian aircraft lost most often in the war. Caveat: the delivery claims come from Russian state sources (Rostec/UAC, TASS) and have not been independently verified.

Sources: TASS · Defence Blog

SBU drones hit Su-35 and Su-34 at Khanskaya air base in Adygea; satellite imagery shows destruction

2026-10-04

Drones of the Security Service of Ukraine's (SBU) Alpha special operations unit struck the Khanskaya military airfield in Russia's Republic of Adygea overnight on 4 October 2026, the SBU said. According to the agency, a Su-35 multirole fighter and a Su-34 fighter-bomber were hit, as well as an ammunition depot on the airfield. The SBU put the distance from Ukrainian territory at about 500 km, stressed that both aircraft carry guided glide bombs (KAB) used massively against the front line and Ukrainian cities, and estimated their export value at around $100 million (Su-35) and $50 million (Su-34). The airfield lies about 6 km northwest of Maykop; according to Militarnyi, drone alerts in the area ran from 01:42 to 05:34 local time. Militarnyi reports that satellite imagery published by the AviaVector community, taken at 08:28 on 4 October, shows signs of destruction on the aircraft taxiways, and describes both jets as destroyed; a satellite image published by ExileNova+ shows the ammunition depot was hit. The Kyiv Independent, writing earlier the same day, noted that the extent of the damage could not be independently verified. Russia's Ministry of Defence claimed its air defences intercepted 559 Ukrainian drones between 20:00 on 3 October and 08:00 on 4 October, including over Adygea; that state claim has not been verified either. Context: Khanskaya was already hit in October 2024, when Ukraine's General Staff said Su-34, Su-27 and Su-35 aircraft used in the war were based there. The strike fits a sustained Ukrainian long-range drone campaign against Russian airfields and KAB carriers; the Kyiv Independent points to the 16 September hit on a Su-24 at Saky in occupied Crimea.

Засіб проти КАБів: ефективніше збивати не бомби, а літаки, які їх запускають

2026-09-21SINGLE SOURCE

ArmyInform, the information agency of Ukraine's Ministry of Defence, reports comments by Colonel Artem Skuratovskyi, head of the air defence department of Ukraine's 7th Rapid Response Corps, made on Radio NV on 21 September 2026. He argued that the most effective answer to Russian guided glide bombs (KABs) is not to intercept the bombs but to shoot down the aircraft that release them. According to Skuratovskyi, Russia uses KABs intensively against infantry positions, and in one month it directed about 120 KABs at the Sumy direction alone. In his view the situation will only improve once Ukrainian aviation can hit Russian aircraft at ranges comparable to the KAB release distance. For that, Ukraine needs long-range air-to-air missiles such as Meteor (for the Gripen), AIM-120 (for the F-16) and MICA (for the Mirage). The argument reflects a long-standing Ukrainian view: glide bombs are released far outside the reach of most ground-based air defence, and the bombs themselves are hard and costly to intercept, so the carrier aircraft is the more practical target. The article gives no new data on intercepts or aircraft losses. This is a single-source report from ArmyInform (Ukrainian MoD media, i.e. a party to the conflict), not independently corroborated.

Russia's glide bomb strikes hit new monthly record in August 2026

2026-09-10

According to figures from Ukraine's General Staff, compiled and circulated via the OSINT monitor Oko Gora, Russia dropped a record 8,766 glide bombs on Ukrainian positions and cities in August 2026 — the highest monthly total since the start of the full-scale invasion. That works out to an average of roughly 283 bombs a day. The figure continues an upward trend that has run almost without interruption since early 2025 (when monthly totals were closer to 3,500), and it surpasses the previous highs of June (8,266) and July (over 8,300). Most of these weapons are Soviet-era FAB free-fall bombs fitted with a UMPK kit (pop-out wings plus satellite guidance), letting Russian strike aircraft — chiefly Su-34s — hit targets tens of kilometres away without approaching Ukrainian air defences. That standoff range has continued to grow in recent years through improved kits. Ukrainian countermeasures cited include attempts to intercept Russian carrier aircraft before release using F-16s (armed with AIM-120 AMRAAM), ground-based GPS/EW jamming that can degrade UMPK guidance accuracy, and the anticipated arrival of Swedish JAS 39 Gripen jets carrying Meteor air-to-air missiles. Ukraine's Ministry of Defence describes countering Russian tactical aviation as "one of Ukraine's key challenges."

Ukraine unveils indigenous Shershen SAM with R-120K missile and Ai-Petri anti-glide-bomb EW system at MSPO 2026

2026-09-09

At the MSPO 2026 defence exhibition in Kielce, Poland, Ukrainian company Radionix presented its modular "Shershen" (Hornet) air-defence system, built around the Perun command-and-control vehicle, the Athena surveillance/tracking radar (detection range roughly 85 km) and four Borei launchers per battery. A more detailed report from The War Zone (TWZ, 10 September 2026) confirms and sharpens the picture: the unit on display was the second operational prototype and is described as "close to its production configuration." The design's core feature is a deliberately open architecture — the manufacturer states the system is "deliberately open... to use different missiles from different manufacturers" — letting the launcher fire both Soviet air-to-air missiles (R-27/AA-10, R-73/AA-11, R-60) and Western types (AIM-9 Sidewinder, AIM-132 ASRAAM), alongside the new, Ukrainian-developed R-120K. Manufacturer-claimed performance: engagement range up to 25 km, altitude up to 10,000 m, and up to six radar-guided missiles engaged simultaneously. TWZ situates Shershen within Ukraine's broader pattern of "FrankenSAM" improvisation — earlier examples include Buk launchers paired with Sea Sparrow missiles, Osa systems adapted to fire R-73s, and the UK-Danish containerized Gravehawk system — but argues Shershen is the first to systematize this approach, building calibre-agnostic launching in as a core design principle rather than an emergency workaround. TWZ is explicit about the caveats, however: advertised performance figures "have not been independently verified," the R-120K's motor is still under development so its "eventual performance and production status remain uncertain," and actual performance "will vary widely depending on the type of interceptor" fired. Separately, at the same exhibition, company Storkway showed the "Ai-Petri" electronic-warfare system (named after Crimea's highest peak), which jams both drone control links and the guidance of Russian glide bombs (KAB/UMPK). It operates across 300 MHz-6 GHz, scans a full 360 degrees, has a terrain-dependent range of 20-30 km, and draws 250 W in detection mode rising to about 2,000 W while jamming; the mobile pickup-mounted variant can be set up by a two-person crew in roughly 10 minutes. Unlike Shershen, Ai-Petri is not an exhibition concept: development began in 2014, tactical versions have been fielded since 2024, and the manufacturer claims around 400 systems delivered. As with Shershen, its claimed effectiveness against glide bombs remains independently unverified.

Russia hammers Kyiv and other Ukrainian cities with drones, missiles and glide bombs, 8-10 September 2026

2026-09-09

Russia continued its intensive bombardment of Ukrainian cities through a second consecutive night, extending the heavy combined salvo of 8-9 September into the night of 9-10 September. On 8-9 September, Russian forces struck with Iskander-M ballistic missiles, S8000 "Banderol" missiles and roughly 196 Shahed-type attack drones (including jet-powered variants), alongside Gerbera drones and Parodiya decoys; Ukraine's Air Force reported 165 drones shot down or suppressed, with impacts at 23 locations. On the night of 9-10 September, the barrage continued at a slightly smaller but still substantial scale: 149 Shahed (about half jet-powered), Gerbera and Parodiya decoy drones were launched from Millerovo, Kursk and Primorsko-Akhtarsk in Russia as well as occupied Donetsk Oblast and Hvardiiske in Crimea, with air defence reporting 128 downed or jammed by 08:00 and hits recorded at 13 locations plus debris at nine more. The pattern fits the sustained, round-the-clock pressure on Kyiv flagged since 27 August. In Kyiv on 8-9 September, a drone struck a 24-storey apartment block in Darnytskyi district overnight, injuring five, while a second, daytime strike hit a nine-storey building in Dniprovskyi district, killing a 70-year-old woman. Mykolaiv was hit by a combined missile-and-drone attack that killed an elderly woman and injured four, while Russian forces dropped five FAB-250 glide bombs with UMPK kits on a residential area of Kramatorsk. The following night and day (9-10 September) brought further damage: a large-scale drone attack on Kryvyi Rih hit five locations, damaging residential buildings, schools and businesses and leaving two people in surgery; Kyiv Oblast's Obukhiv, Bucha and Fastiv districts were struck on the morning of 10 September, killing one person and injuring two more. In Sumy Oblast, two women died of wounds from an 8 September drone strike on the Kyiv-Sumy commuter train, and a drone hit the Starokozache vehicle checkpoint on the Ukraine-Moldova border, killing two. The 9-10 September night added an international dimension: a Russian drone attack on the tugboat Tedy in the Black Sea killed two Azerbaijani crew members and injured two others, according to Azerbaijan's foreign ministry. Across both nights, the common thread remains deliberate, continuous pressure on urban civilian life: high-rise towers, commuter trains, border crossings and now Black Sea shipping are all being drawn into the target set. Casualty and volume figures are Ukrainian and Azerbaijani statements and vary slightly between outlets.

Germany's Cobra 600 Jet Drone Completes First Flight, Armed With IRIS-T Missile, Creators Hope It Can Shoot Down russian Su-34 KAB Carriers

2026-08-22SINGLE SOURCE

SINGLE SOURCE — Ukrainian publication (Defense Express), not independently corroborated. German defense contractor Diehl Defence, working with startup POLARIS Raumflugzeuge, has developed the Cobra 600, a reusable jet-powered interceptor drone with a takeoff mass of 600 kilograms. The first test flight took place in the second half of August 2026 over the Baltic Sea, covering roughly 20 kilometers; the flight carried no payload or missile simulator. Speed, range, and cost figures have not yet been disclosed by the manufacturer. The drone is designed to carry and launch the IRIS-T air-to-air missile, functioning as a kind of 'missile taxi' meant to extend the reach of ground-based air defense systems. Developers hope that its small size, low observability, and sufficient flight range will allow it to intercept Russian Su-34 fighter-bombers carrying KAB glide bombs, one of the principal threats to Ukrainian troops and cities. Defense Express itself stresses that this capability remains purely hypothetical for now: the initial flight involved neither a missile nor a payload simulator, and further tests with IRIS-T simulators and eventually the live missile are planned for coming months. Operational readiness and actual effectiveness against Su-34s are therefore entirely unproven at this stage.
