Точные попадания «Егеря» (Krasnaya Zvezda: 'Yeger's' precise hits)

2026-10-08SINGLE SOURCE

Krasnaya Zvezda profiles Lieutenant Dmitry, callsign 'Yeger' ('Huntsman'), commander of the mortar platoon of the 'Shtorm' assault detachment of the Northern Fleet's separate Guards Kirkenes naval infantry brigade (based at Sputnik), now fighting with the 'Tsentr' (Centre) grouping on the Dobropillia axis. According to the article, the platoon previously supported assaults on the Kherson axis, near Krynky and the Antonivskyi bridge. A graduate of the Mikhailovsky Military Artillery Academy, he says the month-long lieutenants' course at the brigade, run by instructors with combat experience, covered much that the academy lacked: extensive shotgun shooting at aerial targets, and updated artillery tactics, radio communications and tactical medicine. The tactical points the article makes: placing several guns side by side at standard intervals is no longer possible because of losses, so work at the line of contact is done 'strictly gun by gun', with the emphasis on mobility and accuracy. Firing positions are first scouted from the air and then on the ground. The mortars operate only 3-4 km from the Ukrainian forward defence line; since no vehicle (not even a quad bike) can get that close unseen, crews carry the disassembled mortar (over 200 kg) and its ammunition in by hand. Gun pads, ammunition store and counter-battery shelter are built well apart so that one hit does not knock out the rest. Fire is observed and corrected through a quadcopter's live video feed, and after the initial fire mission the platoon fires on targets passed by the assault groups. The officer was wounded the previous autumn when his motorcycle set off a magnetic-fuzed mine scattered a few hours earlier by a Ukrainian 'Baba Yaga' heavy drone on a trail he was scouting. Single source: this is a report from Krasnaya Zvezda, the newspaper of the Russian Ministry of Defence, that has not been independently verified. Much of the piece is a heroic portrait; the claims about effectiveness are unverified.

China's military is preparing for Ukraine-style drone war

2026-10-08

Defence Blog reports on a CSIS commentary published on 7 October by Lilly Min-Chen Lee (Taiwan's Research Institute for Democracy, Society and Emerging Technology) and Kateryna Bondar (CSIS Wadhwani AI Center), based on more than 90 Chinese-language sources from 45 outlets (July 2022 – August 2026), including PLA Daily and journals of China's state missile, shipbuilding and aviation conglomerates. The authors conclude that China studies the Russia-Ukraine war mainly from the perspective of a likely target, and that Chinese analysts typically write up a new battlefield technique within about four months of its first use. Nearly half the sources come from defense-industrial publications that feed lessons directly into requirements and procurement. On Russian practice, National Defense University analysts cited Russian forces striking detected targets within three to five minutes, with Orlan-10 reconnaissance drones cueing Lancet and Kub loitering munitions. PLA Daily flagged Russia's jam-resistant fiber-optic drones in May 2024, within weeks of their debut, and by April 2025 a People's Armed Police unit in Yunnan was training with FPV drones and researching fiber-optic models. On the defensive side, a Naval University of Engineering team counted more than 10 large Russian naval vessels damaged by Ukrainian strikes and concluded that sea-drone attacks must be met by counter-sea-drone equipment working as a system; PLA drills against unmanned boats followed in August 2025, April 2026 and July–August 2026. After Ukraine's Operation Spider's Web (1 June 2025), a PLA brigade exercised in October 2025 against a drone team of more than 20 operators, using RF detection gear with a catalog of over 800 commercial drone models. The study also notes rapid fielding of Chinese counter-drone weapons (Tianqiong, the Hurricane 3000 microwave weapon and the LW-60 laser at Zhuhai 2024; lasers and microwave weapons at the September 2025 parade), three new military universities created in May 2025 with degree programs in unmanned-systems command and control, and Chinese skepticism about swarms, which China National Defense News argued in October 2024 had underdelivered in Ukraine. Relevance for this tracker is indirect: the item concerns lessons a third country draws from Russian and Ukrainian drone warfare.

Investigation: Russia has been rebuilding Facility 1335 in the Urals, the bunker linked to the 'Dead Hand' (Perimeter) nuclear command system

2026-09-26

A joint investigation by the Danish outlet Danwatch, The Sunday Times and the Dossier Center reports that Russia's Ministry of Defence has been carrying out a large modernisation of Object 1335 (codename 'Grot') since 2018. The facility is an underground complex inside Mount Kosvinsky Kamen near the village of Kytlym in the Sverdlovsk region of the Urals. According to the reporting it serves as a command post of the Strategic Missile Forces and is linked to Perimeter (Perimetr), the Soviet-era backup command system known in the West as 'Dead Hand'. Perimeter was built in the mid-1980s to make sure a retaliatory nuclear strike could still be launched if the leadership were killed or communications cut off. Militarnyi describes the complex as having at least 24 rooms, with the main and emergency exits about 2.5 km apart. The evidence consists of two parts. The first is Russian defence procurement records: the investigators searched about 2.5 million records from 2018 to 2024 and found thousands of tenders naming Object 1335. These cover more than 22,000 tonnes of reinforcing steel, about 56,500 tonnes of cement, 600 km of electrical cable, 182 ventilation units, 5 MW of refrigeration capacity (bought in 2019), 14,000 lamps, heavy machinery, rock-drilling equipment, blast-resistant equipment and filters against radioactive, chemical and biological contamination. The records also list Western-made components such as Rockwool insulation and Danfoss and Belimo fittings. The second is satellite imagery, which shows extensive earthworks at the mountain's entrances and work on about six new access points. A Danish construction professor, Per Goltermann, called the quantities an 'enormous' construction project. These records and images establish that there has been a large construction and hardening effort at Object 1335. The link to a revived and more automated Perimeter comes from interpretation and from Russian testimony. Retired Major General Viktor Khalin, who says he worked on developing Perimeter in 2005–2010, wrote in a Russian veterans' magazine in January 2026 that the system was reactivated in 2023 and can now work 'not only in an automated mode but also in a fully automatic mode'. Western experts such as Jeffrey Lewis and Andy Weber think reactivation is the most likely explanation. Tom Røseth describes the digging and reinforcement as a defensive measure to protect a retaliatory capability. Experts stress that claims about fully autonomous nuclear launch must be qualified, because Perimeter's operational details remain classified. The Kremlin did not respond to the investigators.
