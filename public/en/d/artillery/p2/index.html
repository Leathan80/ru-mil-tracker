Ukrainian Border Guards Destroy Russian Giatsint-B Howitzer with FPV Drones

2026-08-19SINGLE SOURCE

SINGLE SOURCE — Defense Express (Ukrainian defense media). According to Defense Express, drone operators from the Apex unit, part of the Forpost Border Guard Brigade, destroyed a Russian 2A36 Giatsint-B howitzer on the South Slobozhanske axis on 19 August. The gun was first detected while providing fire support to Russian troops in the area, after which the operators conducted a precision strike using FPV drones. Per the source, the system was effectively reduced to scrap by the drone hits, and footage of the strike was released by Ukraine's State Border Guard Service. The Giatsint-B (2A36) is a Soviet-designed, towed 152mm gun capable of engaging targets at significant range, belonging to an older generation of Russian field artillery that Russia continues to deploy alongside newer platforms for ground-support and fire missions. Being towed rather than self-propelled, it lacks integral armor protection, leaving it vulnerable to drone strikes once located. The destruction fits a broader, recurring pattern in which Ukrainian units — including border guard formations such as Apex/Forpost — use relatively cheap FPV drones to knock out costly Russian artillery systems.

Sources: Defense Express

Russian Grad MLRS Pulled Back from Front Line Amid Ukrainian Drone Threat

2026-07-28SINGLE SOURCE

Ukrainian drone pressure is pushing Russian forces to reposition heavy artillery, including BM-21 Grad multiple rocket launchers, much further behind the front line. According to Hektor, chief sergeant of the interceptor UAV battery of Ukraine's 115th Brigade, in his sector Russian self-propelled artillery is now typically positioned 25-30 kilometers behind the line of contact, a standoff distance he attributes directly to the effectiveness of Ukrainian unmanned systems against exposed platforms. Hektor said BM-21 Grad launchers specifically are now rarely employed in his sector because they are actively hunted by Ukraine's drone battalion. In their place, Russian forces rely primarily on the longer-range Msta-S 152mm self-propelled howitzer, occasionally supplemented by the Tornado MLRS. This entry is based on a single Ukrainian-sourced analytical piece (Defense Express), built entirely on the account of one named Ukrainian drone-unit NCO describing one sector of the front, with no independent corroboration, loss figures, or production/replacement data — it should be read as an anecdotal snapshot, not a verified broader trend.

Sources: Defense Express

Azov Destroys Russia's New 'Sarma' MLRS in First Confirmed Strike, Moscow's Claimed HIMARS Counterpart

2026-07-28SINGLE SOURCE

Troops of Ukraine's 1st National Guard Corps (Azov) achieved the first confirmed strike on a Russian 'Sarma' multiple launch rocket system (MLRS) on 22 July, during the repulsion of a large-scale Russian mechanized assault on the Dobropillia axis in Donetsk region. Footage analyzed by Militarnyi shows a fixed-wing attack drone destroying the launcher after it had been deployed to support the assault; the vehicle's white-painted cab suggests an attempt to disguise it as a civilian truck. The Sarma is a 300mm MLRS mounted on a KamAZ-63501 chassis, fitted with six launch tubes. Russia initially claimed a range of 20-120 km, later inflating this to 200 km, though analysts consider roughly 120 km — using 9M544 or 9M549 guided rockets — more credible. Russian sources market the system as a HIMARS equivalent, but analysts call the comparison highly questionable since the Sarma, unlike the American system, lacks multi-caliber capability. The system is essentially a rebrand of the 9A52-4 Kama, an MLRS prototype that first appeared in 2007 and was originally marketed for export, attracting no foreign buyers. It was only re-unveiled for export in February 2026 at Saudi Arabia's World Defense Show. This strike is therefore not just the first confirmed destruction of the system, but effectively the first real-world evidence that it has actually entered Russian operational service. This report is based on a single Ukrainian source (Defense Express) and has not yet been independently corroborated.

Ukraine Destroys Msta-B, Giatsint-B and BM-21 Grad Systems, FPV Drone Cache Near Dobropillia

2026-07-27SINGLE SOURCE

According to Defense Express (22 July 2026), Ukraine's Phoenix border guard unit reportedly destroyed a cluster of Russian artillery and rocket systems on the Dobropillia axis in Donetsk Oblast: a 2A65 Msta-B 152mm self-propelled howitzer, a 2A36 Giatsint-B 152mm towed field gun, and a BM-21 Grad multiple rocket launcher — the latter reportedly engaged while on the move. This falls within the same sector where a separate, broader Russian offensive near Dobropillia was also reported this cycle (tracked in the verified stream). In addition, Ukrainian drone operators reportedly located and struck a stockpile of Russian FPV drones being prepared for combat use, and the State Border Guard Service reported Russian personnel casualties in the area. The systems were characterized as high-value assets used by Russian forces to support offensive operations in the sector. This item is based on a single Ukrainian source (Defense Express, citing the State Border Guard Service) and has not been independently confirmed. Numbers, precise locations and claimed results should be treated as an unverified Ukrainian claim.

Ukraine's 44th Artillery Brigade Claims Destruction of Three Russian Msta-B Howitzers, Strike on Troop Position

2026-07-22SINGLE SOURCE

According to a video release by Ukraine's 44th Separate Artillery Brigade, the unit claims to have destroyed three Russian 2A65 Msta-B 152mm towed howitzers and struck a Russian troop position during combat operations on the Huliaipole and Orikhiv axes in the Zaporizhzhia sector. The footage was reportedly released around 20 July 2026, per Defense Express. Per the brigade's account, drone operators first identified the concealed Russian artillery positions and relayed precise targeting data to artillery crews, who then engaged the howitzers — a combined reconnaissance-strike method pairing drone spotting with indirect artillery fire, rather than a direct drone strike. The brigade further states that Russian forces in the area are relying heavily on artillery while massing personnel in preparation for further combat operations, which it says explains the reported strike on an assembled Russian troop position; no casualty figures for that strike are given, and the video itself is not embedded in available reporting. This is a one-sided Ukrainian-sourced claim based solely on footage released by the brigade itself; there is no independent (e.g., Russian or third-party) confirmation of the destruction of the three howitzers or of the troop-position strike, so the information should be treated as single-source and unverified.
