«Тренога против «Града»: зачем российской пехоте «карманная артиллерия»» (Topwar: Tripod versus Grad: why Russian infantry needs 'pocket artillery')

2026-10-09SINGLE SOURCE

Topwar (Voennoe Obozrenie) author Kirill Ryabov describes the Kozerog ('Capricorn'), a light launcher for standard 122 mm Grad rockets made by the Yekaterinburg company BRT, whose founders reportedly have combat experience. The first version was developed in 2023 and went into serial production shortly afterwards. The base model is a single tube on a tripod carriage with folding legs. It weighs about 270 kg without ammunition, is about 2.5–3 m long, can be traversed by hand roughly 20° either side of its axis, and can be fitted with wheels or mounted on a vehicle. Customers have ordered multi-tube versions with up to 9 rails, and a 16-round package is in design. The Kozerog-1M variant adds a mount for the PG-1M panoramic sight, and BRT is reportedly working on a version for 220 mm Uragan rockets. Citing RIA Novosti (7 October), the article says the manufacturer claims about 300 launchers of all variants have been delivered to combat units, including motorised rifle units. That figure comes from the producer alone and some publications give lower numbers. The idea is to trade firepower for survivability. A BM-21 fires 40 rockets per salvo and is easy to spot, while a Kozerog fires one round from a trench, a log-reinforced dug-in position or a building window, and the crew can then leave. The launcher accepts the full range of 122 mm Grad ammunition, with ranges up to about 20 km. The developer also says it can re-fire rounds that misfired in a full MLRS. The author contrasts it with the Soviet Grad-P 'Partizan' (9P132) of the 1960s, which needed a shortened rocket with about 11 km range. The Kozerog brings the standard round to a light mount. The author also lists weaknesses: a small ammunition load, dependence on external transport, no disclosed night or low-visibility capability, and vulnerability to counter-battery fire and FPV drones once a fixed position has been used for a long time. Heavier multi-tube and 220 mm versions would, in his view, leave the portable class altogether. He presents the system's niche as area fire beyond most drones' reach, not precise strikes. Single source: this is a Russian pro-war outlet (Topwar) report that has not been independently verified.

Точные попадания «Егеря» (Krasnaya Zvezda: 'Yeger's' precise hits)

2026-10-08SINGLE SOURCE

Krasnaya Zvezda profiles Lieutenant Dmitry, callsign 'Yeger' ('Huntsman'), commander of the mortar platoon of the 'Shtorm' assault detachment of the Northern Fleet's separate Guards Kirkenes naval infantry brigade (based at Sputnik), now fighting with the 'Tsentr' (Centre) grouping on the Dobropillia axis. According to the article, the platoon previously supported assaults on the Kherson axis, near Krynky and the Antonivskyi bridge. A graduate of the Mikhailovsky Military Artillery Academy, he says the month-long lieutenants' course at the brigade, run by instructors with combat experience, covered much that the academy lacked: extensive shotgun shooting at aerial targets, and updated artillery tactics, radio communications and tactical medicine. The tactical points the article makes: placing several guns side by side at standard intervals is no longer possible because of losses, so work at the line of contact is done 'strictly gun by gun', with the emphasis on mobility and accuracy. Firing positions are first scouted from the air and then on the ground. The mortars operate only 3-4 km from the Ukrainian forward defence line; since no vehicle (not even a quad bike) can get that close unseen, crews carry the disassembled mortar (over 200 kg) and its ammunition in by hand. Gun pads, ammunition store and counter-battery shelter are built well apart so that one hit does not knock out the rest. Fire is observed and corrected through a quadcopter's live video feed, and after the initial fire mission the platoon fires on targets passed by the assault groups. The officer was wounded the previous autumn when his motorcycle set off a magnetic-fuzed mine scattered a few hours earlier by a Ukrainian 'Baba Yaga' heavy drone on a trail he was scouting. Single source: this is a report from Krasnaya Zvezda, the newspaper of the Russian Ministry of Defence, that has not been independently verified. Much of the piece is a heroic portrait; the claims about effectiveness are unverified.

Russia unveils Izdeliye 347, a truck-mounted ground launcher for a modified LMUR (Izdeliye 305) helicopter missile, at Tsentr-2026

2026-10-03

At the strategic exercise Tsentr-2026, Russia presented a new ground-based missile system, Izdeliye 347 ("Product 347"). It fires a modified LMUR light multipurpose guided missile, which is normally carried by attack helicopters. According to the Russian Ministry of Defence (via RIA Novosti and TASS), the system can strike stationary and moving targets at up to 20 km, and its main feature is that an operator can guide the missile onto the target in the final phase of flight. During the main stage on 2 October at the Chebarkul range (Chelyabinsk region), crews used it against mock artillery positions found by aerial reconnaissance. The ministry did not say how many missiles a launcher carries, who builds it, or when it could enter service. The two sources describe the carrier differently. Judging by the video, Militarnyi identifies an armoured 6×6 Ural truck carrying a two-missile launcher and a communications mast in the cargo bed. Defence Blog says the ministry did not name the chassis and describes it as an unarmoured truck. Militarnyi adds that in the demonstration one missile hit an "enemy artillery position" at 13.9 km, while another missed its target in an open field by a few metres. This is a Russian MoD presentation, and the performance claims have not been independently verified. The LMUR (also Izdeliye 305 and Kh-39) is made by KBM in Kolomna, part of Rostec's High Precision Systems holding. It is carried by Mi-28NM and Ka-52M attack helicopters and Mi-8MNP-2 helicopters, and has been used against Ukraine since at least 2022. The basic missile weighs 105 kg, has a 25 kg high-explosive fragmentation warhead, flies at up to 230 m/s, and has a stated range of 14.5 km. It flies to coordinates using inertial navigation with satellite correction, then sends thermal-seeker video to the operator, who selects the target and guides the missile in, so the target does not have to be visible at launch. Militarnyi thinks the system is probably based on the UR-305UD modification with a 25 km range, reported in June 2025. Defence Blog compares the concept to Israel's Spike NLOS and recalls an earlier project, Baikal, to put the LMUR on a tracked armoured vehicle similar to the Khrizantema-S. What happened to Baikal was never made public.

Krasnaya Zvezda profiles a soldier with the call sign 'Doc' from the anti-tank battalion of the 238th Guards Artillery Brigade (Southern group of forces), a construction worker mobilised in 2022 who was first trained as a medic. The paper uses his career to illustrate its argument that the war has thinned out combat formations: tasks once handled by a platoon or section are now carried out by pairs of infantrymen, covered mainly by drones rather than artillery and tanks, and the copter-type drone is now displacing the anti-tank guided missile just as ATGMs once displaced artillery in the anti-tank role. As a 9K111 Fagot ATGM operator, 'Doc' reportedly destroyed a Ukrainian tank in March 2023 while repelling an attack on Novobakhmutivka on the Avdiivka axis. In 2024 the brigade command, intending to form an FPV-drone battery, sent several soldiers, including 'Doc', on a months-long drone-operator course at a training ground, followed by on-the-job practice with experienced crews in other units. In 2025 his crew set up a position in the ruins of Kurakhove; the article describes a strike on a cellar where Russian and Ukrainian infantry occupied separate entrances, with the operator ordered to hit only the Ukrainian one. On the tactical side, the article states that drone crews are the enemy's priority target and describes how Ukrainian drones hunted the crew across two positions, and how a Ukrainian fibre-optic FPV drone in 'waiting' (ambush) mode had been placed at the crew's former launch point before Russian troops on a quad bike shot it up. Countermeasures cited are camouflage, field engineering of positions, obstacles such as branches and nets across drone approach paths, and constantly changing take-off/landing sites and positions. The unit also trains continuously in a dedicated simulator room and in live flights, including on new antennas. 'Doc' now operates on the Druzhkivka direction; on one recent sortie series he reportedly destroyed a 122 mm D-30 howitzer and a depot holding drones, ammunition and provisions, and shot down seven 'Baba Yaga' heavy hexacopters, and he is said to have occasionally downed Ukrainian FPV drones in the air. The piece is a personnel profile with propaganda framing; the claimed kills are unverified, but it confirms the formation of an FPV battery within an artillery brigade's anti-tank battalion and the retraining of ATGM crews as drone operators. Caveat: this is single-source reporting from Krasnaya Zvezda (Russian MoD daily, state media); the claims have not been independently verified.

Latvia Chooses Czech Morana Over Swedish Archer Self-Propelled Howitzer

2026-09-18

Latvia's Cabinet of Ministers has decided to procure approximately 18 Morana 155mm wheeled self-propelled howitzers from Czech manufacturer Excalibur Army, reversing its earlier plan to acquire Sweden's Archer system from BAE Systems Bofors. The selection process started with market research in May 2024, and Latvia had signed a letter of intent with Sweden in June 2025 for 18 Archer units. Further assessment found that the high cost and long delivery timelines of the Swedish system did not align with available resources and defense plans. Latvia's Ministry of Defence cited cost, delivery timelines, operational capabilities, and the level of involvement of the local defense industry as the deciding factors. The Morana is the latest generation of Czech wheeled self-propelled artillery, following the earlier Dita and Dana models. It is built on a modified four-axle Tatra 815-7 (8x8) chassis with an armored crew cabin, mounting a fully automated 155mm/52-caliber turret. The system is operated by a crew of three, carries 45 rounds in the turret, and has a maximum range exceeding 40 kilometers depending on ammunition type. No specific contract value or delivery timeline was disclosed alongside the announcement. The decision fits a broader pattern of artillery modernization along NATO's eastern flank, where states are increasingly favoring faster delivery and lower cost over established Western European suppliers. For a tracker focused on Russian military developments, this is chiefly relevant as an indicator of how Baltic states are strengthening conventional fire support in response to the Russian threat, though it remains a procurement decision rather than a direct battlefield development.

Ukrainian Drone Takes Out Rare North Korean Type-75 Rocket Launcher

2026-09-11SINGLE SOURCE

Ukraine's 40th Brigade destroyed a North Korean-supplied Type-75 rocket launcher with a strike drone, according to Defense Express, which cites video released by the brigade. The Type-75 is a 107mm, 12-tube multiple rocket launcher — a North Korean copy of the Chinese Type 63, itself derived from the Soviet BM-14 design of the 1960s. Each unguided rocket weighs about 18 kg with roughly 1.5 kg of explosive in the warhead, effective against area targets but entirely dependent on staying concealed to survive on a battlefield saturated with reconnaissance drones. The system is described as "rare" because, despite Russia's growing reliance on foreign-supplied equipment, North Korean artillery systems still appear infrequently in documented frontline footage. This is not the first confirmed Type-75 kill — Defense Express and other outlets reported similar incidents dating back to mid-2025 — but each new sighting reconfirms that this dated North Korean hardware is actively deployed at the front. The find fits a longer-running pattern of North Korean support to Russia: beyond these rocket systems, Pyongyang has previously supplied millions of artillery shells, deployed KN-23-type ballistic missiles against Ukrainian targets, and since late 2024 has sent its own troops to reinforce Russian forces in the Kursk region. Russian artillery brigades stand up organic counter-battery UAV subunits, Izvestia reports

2026-09-04SINGLE SOURCE

The pro-military Russian outlet Topwar (author Kirill Ryabov), on 7 September 2026, relays a 4 September 2026 Izvestia report citing unnamed sources that artillery brigades of the Russian Ground Forces have stood up their own organic UAV subunits dedicated to counter-battery warfare (RU: “подразделения БПЛА контрбатарейной борьбы”). The subunits are said to be already operational at the front. Each fields two drone types — a long-endurance reconnaissance/loitering type for surveillance and target detection, and a strike type (loitering munition) able to engage located targets itself — supplemented by ground-based radio-technical reconnaissance (RTR) and electronic-warfare systems to locate and jam enemy command and communication links. Primary targets are enemy tube and rocket artillery, counter-battery radars and EW complexes; a secondary task is detecting and suppressing enemy UAVs, especially FPV and artillery-spotting drones. The subunit passes target designation directly to the brigade's own artillery. The stated purpose is to compress the counter-battery cycle by removing the delays that arise when drone support has to be requested from outside units — decisive against “roving gun” tactics where a piece fires for seconds to a few minutes and then displaces. The article frames this as the latest step in a long arc: artillery–UAV cooperation became widespread after February 2022, with the Unmanned Systems Troops initiated by Defence Minister Belousov in December 2024 and declared structurally complete on 12 November 2025. Izvestia names no specific drone models, no number of subunits formed, and does not say which brigades hold them, and there is no open information on any force-wide rollout.
