The author argues that if Russia could mass-produce the 2S38, it could pose a significant threat to all types of drones used by Ukraine's armed forces. He adds that there is still no information that Russia is ready to start serial production. Ukrainian battlefield system ComBat Vision releases artillery-aiming training plugin for poorly prepared new gunners and plans a plugin store

2026-10-21

The developers of ComBat Vision, a Ukrainian battlefield reconnaissance and coordination system, have released a plugin that turns artillery fire calculations into a visual training tool, Euromaidan Press reports. The team built it around what it describes as a rapid fall in the quality of specialists entering the military, saying artillery soldiers are sometimes "yesterday's TikTokers or 'tanks' players". The plugin draws a round's flight path from the weapon's firing tables and the dispersion ellipse for that gun and munition, warns of obstacles on the calculated trajectory and simulates the shell after firing with an added training error. It then shows real or simulated bursts, averages the correction over a series of hits and lets the user enter corrections, including by voice. Ballistics come from MyGun, Yaroslav Sherstiuk's app for automated artillery fire calculations. ComBat Vision itself coordinates troops and shares object data, integrating drones, ground robots and security cameras to fix targets in space. The developers say they will open a plugin store within a week, offering the artillery feature and others such as repeater-coverage calculations and remote control of minefields. Euromaidan Press notes these are announced plans, not demonstrated results. For the Russia picture the item matters mainly as an indicator of the manpower-quality pressure on Ukrainian artillery that Russia's attritional approach seeks to exploit.

«Poland Receives New Batch of HOMAR-K Ammunition with 290-km CTM-290 Ballistic Missiles» (Defense Express)

2026-10-20SINGLE SOURCE

According to Defense Express, the Polish Armament Agency has confirmed delivery of another batch of ammunition for Poland's HOMAR-K multiple rocket launchers. The batch includes CTM-290 ballistic missiles and CGR-080 guided rockets. HOMAR-K is Poland's version of the South Korean K239 Chunmoo. It is mounted on Polish Jelcz trucks and uses the Topaz fire-control system. The CTM-290 is a 600 mm ballistic missile with a stated range of 80–290 km, satellite-aided inertial guidance and a reported CEP of up to 9 m. The CGR-080 is a 239 mm guided rocket with a range of up to 80 km and a warhead of about 90 kg, broadly comparable in role to the US GMLRS. Each launcher carries two pods, and each pod holds either one CTM-290 or six CGR-080s. Defense Express notes that the CTM-290 was fired from a HOMAR-K in April 2024, but from a launcher in South Korea. The new deliveries indicate that Poland now holds the missiles in its own inventory. The outlet presents the CTM-290 as an alternative to US-supplied ATACMS, whose deliveries it says have reportedly been suspended while the US replenishes its stocks. It also stresses that HOMAR-K gives Poland a precision-strike capability that does not depend on American ammunition. Poland signed a framework agreement in 2022, received its first launchers in 2023 and has since contracted domestic production of 10,000 rockets and missiles. A factory is under construction, and the first Polish-made CTM-290s are expected in 2028. For this tracker, the relevance lies on NATO's eastern flank: a 290 km reach from Polish territory covers the Kaliningrad exclave and western Belarus, which adds to the deep-strike options opposite Russian and Belarusian forces. Defense Express does not say how many missiles were delivered. Single source: this is a report by the Ukrainian defence outlet Defense Express, based on a Polish Armament Agency statement, that has not been independently verified.

«Зенитная артиллерия возвращается: справятся ли пушки с реактивными беспилотниками?» (Topwar: 'Anti-aircraft artillery is back: can guns cope with jet-powered drones?')

2026-10-19SINGLE SOURCE

In an analysis for Topwar (Voennoe Obozrenie), author Anatoly Blinov examines whether gun-based air defence can counter jet-powered one-way attack drones such as Russia's Geran-5. Citing Ukrainian military intelligence (HUR), he puts the Geran-5's cruise speed at 450–600 km/h (versus roughly 170–180 km/h for the piston-engined Geran-2), with a range of 950 km and a ceiling of 6 km; combat sightings were at 200 m to 3 km. At 600 km/h a drone covers about 167 m per second and crosses 3 km in some 18 seconds, which leaves gunners a very short window to acquire, track and engage. The article's systems are mostly Western ones in Ukrainian service or in development: the Rheinmetall Skynex with 35 mm Revolver Gun Mk3 and programmable AHEAD ammunition (152 tungsten sub-projectiles per round), the Gepard, the Swedish TRIDON Mk2 40 mm with 3P programmable rounds (purchased for Ukraine by Sweden and Denmark in February 2026), the Italian 76 mm DART guided round, and BAE Systems' 155 mm Hypervelocity Projectile for the US MDACS gun programme. The only Russian system discussed in detail is Rostec's 30 mm ZAK-30 Tsitadel, presented in May 2026 with radar, electro-optical sensors and airburst rounds and reportedly combat-tested. Blinov contrasts claimed successes with failures. A Stern report citing an internal Ukrainian document claimed that during a 1 April 2026 attack in western Ukraine, two Skynex units (8 guns) suffered hydraulic, loading and tracking failures and let one drone through; Rheinmetall disputed this. Ukraine's Western Air Command, by contrast, claimed a Skynex battery downed 34 Shaheds and two Kh-101 cruise missiles. He also cites Ukrainian Air Force spokesman Yuriy Ihnat on robotic machine-gun turrets with machine vision against Geran-5 (limited mainly by range), and an 8 October engagement in Rivne Oblast in which machine guns fired first and an Igla MANPADS finished off a jet Geran. For the US MDACS he notes a slipped schedule (assessment now in 2030) and a historical cost reference of about $85,000 per guided round. The author concludes that automated 30–40 mm guns are viable for point defence of individual sites against jet drones, and that programmable and guided rounds can extend reach, but only with precise sensors and expensive ammunition. Magazine depth, reload time and saturation by mass raids remain constraints, and guns must be weighed against EW, interceptor drones and decoys: artillery will hold the 'close sky' over key objects, not the whole airspace. This is a single source: Topwar, a Russian pro-government military portal; its figures and claims have not been independently verified.

«Тренога против «Града»: зачем российской пехоте «карманная артиллерия»» (Topwar: Tripod versus Grad: why Russian infantry needs 'pocket artillery')

2026-10-09SINGLE SOURCE

Topwar (Voennoe Obozrenie) author Kirill Ryabov describes the Kozerog ('Capricorn'), a light launcher for standard 122 mm Grad rockets made by the Yekaterinburg company BRT, whose founders reportedly have combat experience. The first version was developed in 2023 and went into serial production shortly afterwards. The base model is a single tube on a tripod carriage with folding legs. It weighs about 270 kg without ammunition, is about 2.5–3 m long, can be traversed by hand roughly 20° either side of its axis, and can be fitted with wheels or mounted on a vehicle. Customers have ordered multi-tube versions with up to 9 rails, and a 16-round package is in design. The Kozerog-1M variant adds a mount for the PG-1M panoramic sight, and BRT is reportedly working on a version for 220 mm Uragan rockets. Citing RIA Novosti (7 October), the article says the manufacturer claims about 300 launchers of all variants have been delivered to combat units, including motorised rifle units. That figure comes from the producer alone and some publications give lower numbers. The idea is to trade firepower for survivability. A BM-21 fires 40 rockets per salvo and is easy to spot, while a Kozerog fires one round from a trench, a log-reinforced dug-in position or a building window, and the crew can then leave. The launcher accepts the full range of 122 mm Grad ammunition, with ranges up to about 20 km. The developer also says it can re-fire rounds that misfired in a full MLRS. The author contrasts it with the Soviet Grad-P 'Partizan' (9P132) of the 1960s, which needed a shortened rocket with about 11 km range. The Kozerog brings the standard round to a light mount. The author also lists weaknesses: a small ammunition load, dependence on external transport, no disclosed night or low-visibility capability, and vulnerability to counter-battery fire and FPV drones once a fixed position has been used for a long time. Heavier multi-tube and 220 mm versions would, in his view, leave the portable class altogether. He presents the system's niche as area fire beyond most drones' reach, not precise strikes. Single source: this is a Russian pro-war outlet (Topwar) report that has not been independently verified.
