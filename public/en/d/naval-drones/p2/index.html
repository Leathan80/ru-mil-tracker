Ukraine Hits russian Buk-M3 System, Nebo-SVU Radar, Drone Base, and 205th Vessel

2026-07-31SINGLE SOURCE

According to Ukrainian sources (Defense Express, citing statements from Ukraine's Unmanned Systems Forces / "Madyar's Birds"), Ukrainian units carried out a coordinated multi-domain strike operation reported on 31 July 2026. In Donetsk region (Snizhne), operators from the "Phoenix" border-guard unit struck a Buk-M3 surface-to-air missile system while it was being transported on a heavy trailer. In Russia's Rostov Oblast (Russkiy Kolodets), operators from the 9th Kairos Battalion (414th Separate "Birds of Madyar" Brigade) hit a Nebo-SVU radar station and a mobile Sopka-2 radar complex. In occupied Crimea, a naval drone base concealed under radar-transparent shelters was struck, while Phoenix operators also hit a strike-drone launch site at the occupied Donetsk airfield. The 412th Nemesis Brigade additionally reported hitting a P-18 early-warning radar in Zaporizhzhia region. The military value attributed to each target: the Buk-M3 is described as one of the newer elements of Russia's layered air-defense network (fielded since 2016), able to engage targets out to roughly 70 km range and 35 km altitude, with open-source cost estimates of $40-50 million per launcher. The Nebo-SVU is a VHF-band AESA radar valued for long-range early warning and counter-stealth detection, including against drones and cruise missiles. The Crimea facility is described as housing/staging unmanned surface vessels that threaten Ukrainian naval-drone operations in the Black Sea. Separately, Ukraine claims that since Operation MoLoChKa began on 6 July it has struck 205 vessels in total (130 in the Sea of Azov, 75 in the Black Sea) — the "205th vessel" in the headline refers to this cumulative tally rather than a single named ship. This is a claim from a Ukrainian outlet (Defense Express, relaying Ukrainian Unmanned Systems Forces/Madyar statements) and has not been independently verified; the report cites only Ukrainian military sources, with no Russian acknowledgement or independent confirmation mentioned.

Magura Naval Drone Gets Second U.S. Assembly Line, This Time Officially, While Red Cat's Copy Is Already in Serial Production

2026-07-27SINGLE SOURCE

Ukrainian company UForce, developer of the Magura naval drone, has announced a partnership with American boatbuilder ReconCraft to establish an assembly line for the Magura in the United States (Oregon and South Carolina). According to UForce USA head Sean Plankey, this marks the first time the Ukrainian government has officially approved production of the naval drone on American soil, with an eventual goal of producing thousands of Magura drones per year. The Magura V7 measures 7.6 meters, carries a payload of over 770 kilograms, has a range of 560 kilometers, and costs under $500,000. This official licensing arrangement stands in sharp contrast to the situation surrounding American company Red Cat, which has already had its own, unauthorized version of the Magura V7 in serial production since May-June 2025, presented as an independent, in-house development with American control systems and NDAA-compliant components. Notably, despite lacking a license, Red Cat appears to be further along in production than UForce. The development illustrates both the growing, formally sanctioned US-Ukraine defense-industrial cooperation around uncrewed naval systems, and the underlying tension over intellectual property. This summary is based on a single source (Defense Express) only and could not be independently verified.

Russian Corvette Soobrazitelny Encounters Danish USV Near Bornholm During Shadow Fleet Escort

2026-07-23SINGLE SOURCE

On 21 July 2026, the Russian Project 20380 corvette Soobrazitelny reportedly closed to within about 200 meters of the Danish uncrewed surface vessel (USV) Stormborn X-Wave roughly 30 km southeast of Bornholm in the Baltic Sea, according to Ukrainian outlet Defense Express, citing Danish broadcaster TV 2 and the Stormborn organization. The X-Wave was conducting a routine test of its radar and hydroacoustic sensor suite when the corvette approached with its AIS transponder switched off, then launched a reconnaissance drone that circled the USV at close range to inspect it before the ship continued on its way. According to the report, the USV's onboard AI managed to identify the corvette's hull number (P531), crew visible on deck, an embarked Ka-27 helicopter, and tracked the Russian drone by radar, though it reportedly sometimes misclassified the drone as the corvette itself. The collected data was said to be passed to Danish naval surveillance authorities, who declined to comment publicly. Days after the encounter, the same corvette was reportedly observed escorting Russian "shadow fleet" tankers along the European coast, a role Defense Express links to protecting the tankers against possible Ukrainian drone strikes — illustrating a dual maritime role attributed to Russian escort vessels in the Baltic: shadowing NATO-linked surveillance assets while safeguarding sanctioned oil shipments. SINGLE SOURCE — this item is based on one source, the Ukrainian defense outlet Defense Express, itself citing Danish broadcaster TV 2 and the Stormborn organization, and has not been independently corroborated by official Danish, NATO, or Russian statements.

Two Unmanned Boats of Ukrainian Armed Forces Destroyed in the Black Sea

2026-07-20SINGLE SOURCE

SINGLE SOURCE — Russian state media (TASS). The Russian Ministry of Defense claims that the Black Sea Fleet destroyed two unmanned (drone) boats belonging to the Ukrainian Armed Forces in the Black Sea. TASS reports this based on the ministry's daily loss summary, without further detail on the exact location, timing, or method by which the boats were allegedly neutralized. The claim is presented as part of a broader daily rundown in which the ministry also states it intercepted two Neptune-MD long-range missiles, eight guided aerial bombs, a US-made HIMARS munition, and 678 fixed-wing UAVs. This report fits a recurring pattern of Russian claims about intercepting Ukrainian naval drones, which have long been used against Russian naval vessels and, more recently, against targets linked to Russia's "shadow fleet." The Defense Ministry typically publishes such destruction claims daily as part of cumulative loss figures, and TASS, as a state outlet, relays these figures without independent verification. There is no Ukrainian confirmation and no independent verification of this incident available. Both Russia and Ukraine routinely publish mutual destruction and interception claims in this conflict that are rarely independently verifiable; this entry should therefore be read strictly as a representation of what the Russian Ministry of Defense states, not as a verified fact.
