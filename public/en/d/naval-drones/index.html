Topwar reports that the Russian company Tekhno-D plans to increase production of its AURO LED searchlights, which are used to detect drones and uncrewed surface vessels. The Centre for Unmanned Systems and Technologies (TsBST), where the manufacturer is a resident company, announced the plans on 26 November. They are also used to find surface targets, including uncrewed boats, in the dark. The searchlights can work on their own or as part of security systems together with thermal imagers and electro-optical devices. They are remotely controlled and have a marine-grade aluminium housing with an anti-corrosion coating. Author Aleksei Volodin presents this as old practice made new. In the Second World War, searchlights helped anti-aircraft crews find enemy aircraft, and he notes that few would have expected the practice to return five years ago. The report gives no production volumes, prices or users. Caveat: single-source reporting (Topwar, Russian pro-government military site, relaying TASS/TsBST); not independently verified.

Ukrainian naval drones sink Russian corvette Merkury in Libyan port of Benghazi

2026-11-22

On 22 November 2026 the Russian Navy corvette Merkury (pennant 734), a Steregushchiy-class (Project 20380) ship commissioned in 2023 and armed with Kh-35 anti-ship missiles, was struck while moored at the naval base in Benghazi, eastern Libya. It caught fire and capsized; satellite imagery from Vantor and Planet shows only the bow above water. Radio Liberty, reviewing footage, concluded that torpedoes, Kh-35 missiles and gun ammunition on board detonated. On 23 November President Zelensky and the Security Service of Ukraine (SBU) claimed the operation: according to them, seven Ukrainian-made naval drones were launched from a 'mothership', destroyed a Ka-27 helicopter on board, and also hit the sanctioned dry-cargo/ro-ro ship MV Angara (IMO 9179842). The SBU says the Merkury escorted the Angara on the 'Syrian Express' and 'Libyan Express' supply routes. Militarnyi adds that Ukrainian intelligence links the Angara to shipments of North Korean ammunition and that C4ADS lists it among the key transporters of Russia's Africa Corps. A Ukrainian military source told the Wall Street Journal that a single drone boat hit the Angara, which was damaged but stayed afloat. Early reports named the second ship as the ro-ro Ascalon; TWZ says this was probably a confusion with the similar Angara. How and from where the drones were launched remains disputed. TWZ calls the SBU statement the first official confirmation that Ukraine uses a vessel to deliver naval drones, but notes it could also be misdirection to cover Ukrainian activity inside Libya. The Associated Press and Libyan officials say the drones came from a base run by Ukrainian forces in western Libya; WSJ sources say Ukrainian forces based in Libya tracked Russian ships for months and launched the drones from a vessel in the Mediterranean. Albania rejected an online claim that the boats came from its territory. TWZ, citing analyst H.I. Sutton, identifies the drone as a variant of the Sargan 3000 and notes that Benghazi, unlike Russia's home ports, had no booms or other barriers against naval drones. Casualty reports still differ: Reuters cites ten lightly injured, while Al Jazeera's sources speak of an unspecified number of Russians and Libyans killed; there is no official toll. The Russia-backed eastern Libyan government called the strike a violation of its sovereignty, asked for an international investigation into Ukrainian personnel in western Libya, and opened a joint inquiry with Russia. Ukraine's Foreign Ministry said on 24 November that the strike was self-defence under Article 51 of the UN Charter, that warships and cargo ships carrying military equipment are legitimate targets anywhere, and that Russia is putting Libya at risk. Moscow has stayed largely silent: Kremlin spokesman Peskov referred questions to the Defence Ministry, TASS did not report his remarks and the main national TV channels did not mention the ship in their newscasts, according to Meduza. A second Russian convoy of the sanctioned ships Lady D, Lady R and Maia-1, escorted by the frigate Admiral Golovko, had been crawling off Tunisia since 19 November and, according to the OSINT researchers SONARROW and GeoInsider, had turned back by 24 November, although it slowed before the strike. Militarnyi links Benghazi to the drone strikes on Sparta II and Ambal in the Gulf of Finland on 21 November and argues that Russia's sea logistics to Kaliningrad and Africa, including the 'Syrian/Libyan Express', now face a campaign far beyond the Black Sea. Western reactions are divided. Italy expressed its concern to Kyiv, while French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot told BFMTV on 25 November that Ukraine had exercised its absolute right to self-defence, saying that Ukrainians pounded day and night by Russian drones and missiles are defending themselves, and accused Putin of constantly harassing Europeans to undermine their unity. On the same day Libya reported that two Libyan soldiers had died of their injuries, and the analytical group Exilenova+ found an obituary for the first known Russian fatality: Captain 3rd Rank Oleksandr Tsulin (42), the Merkury's deputy commander for personnel, as Militarnyi gives his name. The obituary dates his death to 21 November, whereas the explosion occurred overnight into 22 November; the circumstances have not been disclosed and there is still no official overall casualty toll.
