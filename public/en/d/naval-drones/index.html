China's military is preparing for Ukraine-style drone war

2026-10-08

Defence Blog reports on a CSIS commentary published on 7 October by Lilly Min-Chen Lee (Taiwan's Research Institute for Democracy, Society and Emerging Technology) and Kateryna Bondar (CSIS Wadhwani AI Center), based on more than 90 Chinese-language sources from 45 outlets (July 2022 – August 2026), including PLA Daily and journals of China's state missile, shipbuilding and aviation conglomerates. The authors conclude that China studies the Russia-Ukraine war mainly from the perspective of a likely target, and that Chinese analysts typically write up a new battlefield technique within about four months of its first use. Nearly half the sources come from defense-industrial publications that feed lessons directly into requirements and procurement. On Russian practice, National Defense University analysts cited Russian forces striking detected targets within three to five minutes, with Orlan-10 reconnaissance drones cueing Lancet and Kub loitering munitions. PLA Daily flagged Russia's jam-resistant fiber-optic drones in May 2024, within weeks of their debut, and by April 2025 a People's Armed Police unit in Yunnan was training with FPV drones and researching fiber-optic models. On the defensive side, a Naval University of Engineering team counted more than 10 large Russian naval vessels damaged by Ukrainian strikes and concluded that sea-drone attacks must be met by counter-sea-drone equipment working as a system; PLA drills against unmanned boats followed in August 2025, April 2026 and July–August 2026. After Ukraine's Operation Spider's Web (1 June 2025), a PLA brigade exercised in October 2025 against a drone team of more than 20 operators, using RF detection gear with a catalog of over 800 commercial drone models. The study also notes rapid fielding of Chinese counter-drone weapons (Tianqiong, the Hurricane 3000 microwave weapon and the LW-60 laser at Zhuhai 2024; lasers and microwave weapons at the September 2025 parade), three new military universities created in May 2025 with degree programs in unmanned-systems command and control, and Chinese skepticism about swarms, which China National Defense News argued in October 2024 had underdelivered in Ukraine. Relevance for this tracker is indirect: the item concerns lessons a third country draws from Russian and Ukrainian drone warfare.

Ukrainian Navy says a Sargan 3000 naval drone with a turret shot down a Russian Shahed at sea

2026-10-07

According to the Ukrainian Navy, which released combat footage on its Telegram channel on 7 October, a Sargan 3000 uncrewed surface vessel (USV) shot down a Russian Shahed-type attack drone at sea. The Navy said the Shahed was operating on a mesh network and was hit by fire from a turret aboard the USV; the video shows several bursts of machine-gun fire, after which the drone falls into the sea and explodes. Militarnyi identifies the weapon as the Protector 12.7 mm remote weapon station (RWS) with electro-optical sights — according to Naval News analyst H.I. Sutton, cited by Euromaidan Press, a Kongsberg-made system — and freeze-frames show the Shahed through the Protector's camera. Ukrainska Pravda, Militarnyi and Euromaidan Press give no exact location, date or operating unit. Euromaidan Press notes that Ukrainian sea drones had previously downed Russian aircraft with missiles, but this is the first such kill it describes with a machine gun. It places the engagement in the context of Russian Shahed waves against Odesa and Russia's use, since autumn 2025, of mesh radio networks in which control signals are relayed from drone to drone to ground antennas, allowing operators to re-route Shaheds in flight to evade air defences. Sutton has also written that Russian attack USVs work closely with Shaheds, which can relay the boats' mesh signals. Per Militarnyi, the Sargan 3000 was adopted by the Ukrainian Navy in April 2026 for missions far from the coast, with a combat radius of up to 1,600 km, a speed of 40–55 knots and a payload of up to 450 kg. In September a Sargan 3000 with the same RWS destroyed a Russian USV, described as the first battle between uncrewed naval platforms, and in July one was used to sink a Russian second-rank border patrol ship near Novorossiysk. The reports present the USV as a mobile, armed platform for coastal and air defence over water; the claim comes from the Ukrainian Navy and has not been independently confirmed.

First-ever reported drone-on-drone naval battle: Ukraine's Sargan-3000 destroys Russian unmanned boat in Black Sea

2026-09-12

On September 12, 2026, Ukraine's Navy announced that one of its uncrewed surface vessels (USVs), the Sargan-3000, had for the first time in history destroyed another uncrewed vessel in combat — a Russian unmanned boat in the Black Sea. The Sargan-3000 is a Ukrainian multipurpose naval strike drone that entered service in April 2026 after passing combat trials; it can reach speeds of 40-55 knots, has an operational range of up to 1,600 km, and can carry a payload of up to 450 kg. It is fitted with a remotely operated weapon station (a Protector RWS) armed with a 12.7mm machine gun. According to the Navy, units of Defense Intelligence (HUR) first detected the Russian target, after which the Sargan-3000 closed in and engaged it with machine-gun fire. An 81-second video released by the Navy on Telegram shows the engagement from several angles — the Ukrainian drone's onboard camera, the weapon station's gun sight, and footage from an aerial platform — and depicts the Russian boat being raked with sustained fire until pieces broke off and it sank stern-first. The type and armament of the Russian vessel were not specified; The War Zone, reviewing the footage, reports the target appeared to be unarmed. The Ukrainian Navy explicitly described the incident as "the first naval battle in history between unmanned boats." It did not disclose an exact date, location, or the class of the Russian craft, presenting the operation as a joint result of the Navy and Defense Intelligence. The event is being widely covered as a milestone: until now, Ukrainian naval drones had mainly targeted crewed Russian warships (as part of the Black Sea fleet campaign under way since 2023) or transiting targets such as combat aircraft; this would mark the first confirmed instance of two uncrewed vessels actually fighting each other. Both the Kyiv Independent and The War Zone stress, however, that Ukraine's claim has not been independently verified and rests entirely on the video and Navy statement Kyiv itself released; there is no known Russian confirmation or denial. The War Zone additionally notes it could find no earlier case of one drone boat destroying another, which lends plausibility to the "first-ever" claim without conclusively proving it.

Ukrainian naval drone dodges Russian helicopter gunfire to strike shadow-fleet tanker off Sochi

2026-09-12

On Saturday, September 12, 2026, Major General Robert "Madyar" Brovdi, commander of Ukraine's Unmanned Systems Forces (USF), released video footage of a strike on the oil tanker "Armada Leader" near Sochi in the Black Sea. The vessel sails under the Russian flag and is sanctioned by Ukraine, the EU, Switzerland and Canada as part of Russia's so-called shadow fleet, used to circumvent export restrictions on Russian oil. Both Militarnyi and Defense Express reported the incident based on the same USF footage. The video shows a Russian helicopter pursuing the Ukrainian attack drone and opening fire with an onboard machine gun as the drone held its course toward the ship. The drone operator evaded the incoming fire without breaking off the attack, ultimately striking the tanker in the area of its bridge/wheelhouse. Defense Express notes the vessel had already sustained damage from prior strikes. The episode is a clear illustration of a recurring pattern in drone-versus-helicopter engagements: manned escort helicopters generally lack the precision weaponry and reaction speed to reliably down a small, agile drone platform with unguided machine-gun fire, so evasive maneuvering alone is often enough for the strike to succeed. The attack forms part of the USF's wider MoLoChka campaign against Russia's shadow fleet: according to Brovdi, the force has struck 285 vessels over ten weeks (215 in July, 54 in August, 16 in the first eleven days of September) — a tempo that keeps ratcheting up pressure on Russian oil exports and resupply to occupied Crimea.

О способности БПЛА «отменить» надводные корабли

2026-08-30SINGLE SOURCE

An opinion piece on the Russian military portal Topwar (by Andrey Kolobov) argues that unmanned systems — both aerial UAVs and uncrewed surface vessels (USVs) — will not make the crewed surface fleet obsolete. English gloss of the title: 'On the ability of UAVs to "cancel" surface ships.' The author concedes that Ukrainian USVs scored notable successes against Russia's Black Sea Fleet and that the cost asymmetry is real, but concludes that drones 'have not become, and will not become, the ultimate weapon of naval warfare.' He rests the case on two core limitations. First, speed: cheap strike drones of the Shahed-136 class cruise at only 150-180 km/h and take nearly two hours to cover 350 km, versus minutes for missiles, so a large drone salvo gives the enemy long warning. Second, guidance: affordable drones use GNSS plus inertial navigation and cannot track a moving ship; doing so requires expensive satellite-internet infrastructure (Starlink/OneWeb) and real-time targeting. Citing Soviet Cold War experience tracking US carrier strike groups, he argues the hard problem is, and remains, finding a moving squadron at all. He frames the change in naval warfare as evolutionary rather than revolutionary. Fixed caveat: this is a single-source Russian opinion/analysis article (Topwar), not an official statement and not independently verified. It reflects an ongoing debate in Russian military circles about the implications of the Black Sea Fleet losses, but the arguments and figures are the author's own.
