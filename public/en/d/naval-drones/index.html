The aircraft crossed Bulgaria from the west near Shumen, passed over the Varna area, made several turns over the western Black Sea east of Varna and then circled off Burgas, near the maritime border with Türkiye, at times at about 2,740 m. According to ItaMilRadar it was the first such low-level US Navy P-8A flight over this part of the Black Sea in several months. The P-8A, based on the Boeing 737, is built for anti-submarine warfare and surface surveillance: its radar can pick out small boats, it carries electro-optical/infrared sensors and sonobuoys, and flying low makes it easier to identify small objects such as uncrewed surface vessels. The flight follows Bulgarian Prime Minister Rumen Radev's request to allies to help identify where the drones came from and to strengthen air and sea surveillance off the coast. What struck the Chelsea 3 on 13 October is still disputed: the crew spoke of three drones and Bulgarian reports of aerial drones, while Russian Telegram channels published images of hull damage they attributed to a drone boat. Drones have struck at least eight merchant vessels near Romania and Bulgaria since 5 October, according to Euromaidan Press; Reuters counts at least five cargo ships hit in ten days, with 23 sailors dead or missing. The San Marino-flagged chemical tanker Wine Trader was struck early on 15 October about a mile outside Romanian waters, and seven of its 12 crew were still missing on 16 October. In an analysis for Euromaidan Press, David Axe frames the campaign as Russia's answer to Ukraine's drone war on Russian Black Sea shipping, which has cut Russian Black Sea wheat exports by about 75% year on year, and says Russia now also targets Ukraine's Danube grain ports of Izmail, Reni and Kiliia. The P-8A sortie is the first visible US surveillance step off Bulgaria since the strikes began; its sensor mix fits a search for both aerial and surface drones and their launch points. 2026-10-08

Defence Blog reports on a CSIS commentary published on 7 October by Lilly Min-Chen Lee (Taiwan's Research Institute for Democracy, Society and Emerging Technology) and Kateryna Bondar (CSIS Wadhwani AI Center), based on more than 90 Chinese-language sources from 45 outlets (July 2022 – August 2026), including PLA Daily and journals of China's state missile, shipbuilding and aviation conglomerates. The authors conclude that China studies the Russia-Ukraine war mainly from the perspective of a likely target, and that Chinese analysts typically write up a new battlefield technique within about four months of its first use. Nearly half the sources come from defense-industrial publications that feed lessons directly into requirements and procurement. On Russian practice, National Defense University analysts cited Russian forces striking detected targets within three to five minutes, with Orlan-10 reconnaissance drones cueing Lancet and Kub loitering munitions. On the defensive side, a Naval University of Engineering team counted more than 10 large Russian naval vessels damaged by Ukrainian strikes and concluded that sea-drone attacks must be met by counter-sea-drone equipment working as a system; PLA drills against unmanned boats followed in August 2025, April 2026 and July–August 2026. After Ukraine's Operation Spider's Web (1 June 2025), a PLA brigade exercised in October 2025 against a drone team of more than 20 operators, using RF detection gear with a catalog of over 800 commercial drone models. The study also notes rapid fielding of Chinese counter-drone weapons (Tianqiong, the Hurricane 3000 microwave weapon and the LW-60 laser at Zhuhai 2024; lasers and microwave weapons at the September 2025 parade), three new military universities created in May 2025 with degree programs in unmanned-systems command and control, and Chinese skepticism about swarms, which China National Defense News argued in October 2024 had underdelivered in Ukraine. Relevance for this tracker is indirect: the item concerns lessons a third country draws from Russian and Ukrainian drone warfare.

First-ever reported drone-on-drone naval battle: Ukraine's Sargan-3000 destroys Russian unmanned boat in Black Sea

Citing Soviet Cold War experience tracking US carrier strike groups, he argues the hard problem is, and remains, finding a moving squadron at all.
