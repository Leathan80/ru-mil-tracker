Reuters: Russia plans record 17.1 trillion rubles defence spending in 2027, 27% above the earlier budget, while the deficit and borrowing rise

2026-09-28

Reuters, citing Russian government documents it reviewed, reports that Russia plans to spend 17.1 trillion rubles (about $202.6 billion) on defence in 2027 under a new draft budget. That is the highest figure since the full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022. The budget adopted in 2025 had set the 2027 figure at 13.5 trillion rubles, so the increase is 3.6 trillion rubles, or 27%. Total national defence spending over the three-year budget period is put at about 50 trillion rubles. Meduza, which relays the Reuters report, adds that the 2026 budget set defence at 12.93 trillion rubles, and that Bloomberg reported in June that authorities planned to raise war spending this year by another 4-5 trillion rubles. The same documents show the strain on public finances. The 2026 deficit estimate was raised to 3.2% of GDP from 1.6%; 2027 borrowing is projected to rise 43% to 7.7 trillion rubles, and state debt to reach 21.7% of GDP, above a level of 20% seen as safe. Oil and gas revenue for 2026 was cut to 7.6 trillion rubles from 8.9 trillion, and a new windfall tax on metals and mining companies is planned to raise about 200 billion rubles a year. Actual 2026 defence spending remains classified. Meduza reports (30 September) that the draft was submitted to the State Duma that day after a Finance Ministry presentation on 24 September. It projects deficits of 5.5 trillion rubles in 2027, 5.1 trillion in 2028 and 5.3 trillion in 2029, nearly 16 trillion combined, about 2% of GDP a year. The Finance Ministry proposes a higher tax rate on passive income and a 22% VAT on cross-border e-commerce purchases. Meduza says the government's announcement did not mention the military increase or social cuts, while reporting indicates social welfare spending falls 7%, healthcare 6.8% and education 6%. Caveat: the figures come from draft documents seen by Reuters (relayed via Meduza and Reuters syndication), not from an official publication. The Reuters text available to us did not break the figure down into national defence versus security or give a share of total spending, and the draft may change in parliament.

Russia adapts to Ukraine's middle-strike drone campaign with mobile fire groups, Rubicon interceptors and Starlink jammers

2026-09-23

According to a Kyiv Independent field report from the southern front, Russian forces have adapted to Ukraine's expanded "middle strike" campaign. That campaign uses drones against targets from roughly 35 km (the end of FPV range) out to about 300 km behind the line. Within weeks of the campaign being scaled up in the south, Russia changed logistics routes and fielded new countermeasures. Columns carrying high-value cargo are now often escorted by mobile fire groups, some riding on the vehicles and some posted at intervals along key routes such as the E58 highway to Crimea. Russia's elite Rubicon drone unit has also published dozens of videos of its interceptor teams downing Starlink-equipped Hornet drones and other middle-strike drones. The commander of Ukraine's 422nd Unmanned Systems Regiment, Mykola "Tyson" Kolesnyk, said these measures now bring down "a certain percentage" of Ukrainian drones but have not critically changed his unit's work. He also said that moving Rubicon's interceptor teams to the rear to guard the highways has let more Ukrainian fixed-wing drones cross the front uncontested. The report also describes Russian efforts to take away Ukraine's Starlink advantage. Starlink was cut off for Russian forces in early February 2026 after mandatory terminal registration through Ukraine's Diia app was introduced. Since then, Russian fixed-wing drones, from the tactical Molniya to the long-range Geran class, have increasingly been fitted with decentralized mesh radio modems to keep their links and video feeds under Ukrainian EW. Russia has announced its own satellite internet system, Rassvet, but its rollout has suffered setbacks, including a satellite that reportedly fell from the sky and others that did not reach their intended orbits. Russia is also testing several Starlink jammers. The most prominent is the Peresvet-M, which according to Ukrainian presidential adviser Serhii "Flash" Beskretnov floods the terminal's uplink channel. Beskretnov said it can deny Starlink over about 20 km² and has been deployed to protect key targets in Crimea. Kolesnyk said his regiment, working with the SBU's Alpha centre, destroyed a first Russian Starlink jammer on 14 June 2026 and has since helped destroy at least five such systems. He described them as bulky and vulnerable because of their large power needs. About two weeks before the interview, according to Kolesnyk, Russia began running the jammers from the electricity grid instead of generators. He said the unit felt their effect only once and that it is still unclear whether Russia will scale the jammers up or whether they are mainly an information operation. The regiment says it struck a substation in occupied Zaporizhzhia Oblast that powered radars, EW equipment and Starlink jammers, and that Russian air defence downed two of the four Zozulia drones sent. All these claims come from a Ukrainian unit that is a party to the conflict and have not been independently confirmed.
