China imposes export licensing on drone components, including jet engines used in jet-powered Gerans, but leaves CRPA antennas, navigation modules and Geran-4 servos uncontrolled

2026-11-19

China has introduced export controls on a range of drone components and related technologies. They took effect on 18 November 2026. According to Militarnyi, the legal basis is Decision (Announcement) No. 49, adopted on 19 October 2026 by three bodies: China's Ministry of Commerce, the General Administration of Customs and the Equipment Development Department of the Central Military Commission. This is not an export ban. Chinese exporters must obtain a licence before shipping the listed items abroad. The listed items are: jet engines with at least 400 newtons of thrust; other UAV engines with more than 16 kW of power output; radio modules with an output power of 30 dBm or more, or able to control more than 10 drones; high-power brushless electric motors and their electronic speed controllers. Militarnyi reports that autopilots, certain categories of UAVs and the technologies used to develop and produce them are also covered. The sources give no thresholds for the motors, speed controllers or autopilots. The measures were made public on the Ukrainian side by Vladyslav Vlasiuk, the Ukrainian President's Commissioner for Sanctions Policy. He thanked China for the controls but said they cover only part of the components that matter for Russian weapons production, calling them "a start". He said Ukraine had given partners detailed information on these components and their Chinese manufacturers, and that he expects strict enforcement. Both outlets say the engine category includes the engines used in Russia's jet-powered Geran (Shahed-type) variants. According to both sources, several Chinese parts found in Russian drones remain outside the controls. These are controlled reception pattern antennas (CRPAs) with Chinese-made circuit boards, used on the Geran-2; navigation modules; HARXON antennas; cameras on stabilised gimbals; and servos used on the Geran-4. Neither source discusses the effect on Russian FPV drone production. Euromaidan Press's own assessment is that the controls may make it harder for Russia to buy some components directly from Chinese suppliers. It adds that the real impact depends on how strictly Beijing enforces the licensing requirement, and on whether Russian buyers can keep sourcing through intermediaries or other suppliers. It is still unclear how licences will be granted in practice, whether shipments to Russia will actually be refused, and how quickly any effect would show up in Geran production.
