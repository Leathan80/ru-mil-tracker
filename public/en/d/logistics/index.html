Ukraine makes anti-drone road nets a national standard in defensive-lines strategy through 2028, in response to Russian FPV hunting of logistics

2026-10-16

According to Ukraine's Defence Ministry, the Ukrainian government has approved a Strategy for the Engineering Equipment of Defensive Lines through 2028. One of its priorities is stronger anti-drone protection of logistics routes. The ministry says the strategy turns the existing, largely ad hoc road-protection efforts into a single national standard, with deadlines, assigned responsibilities and control mechanisms. The ministry drafted the document on its own initiative, based on combat experience. Funding is to come from state and local budgets and other sources not prohibited by law. The background is the Russian use of FPV drones against supply lines: according to Euromaidan Press, Russian FPV drones attack almost any moving target within 15–30 km of the front. Roads are therefore being covered with overhead netting, wire-mesh canopies and reinforced barriers meant to stop drones before they reach vehicles. Citing an earlier report, Euromaidan Press says more than 1,066 km of such protection had been built by mid-July 2026; the target is 4,000 km by the end of 2026. Besides road protection, the strategy covers building and maintaining fortifications, engineering obstacles, modern designs, materials and camouflage, a reserve of construction materials, and digital record-keeping. A single digital geographic information platform, building on systems including the DELTA situational-awareness system, is to hold a 'passport', photos and location and condition data for each structure, so that work can be verified and repairs and resources allocated. The ministry says proven solutions will be scaled up while also accounting for new threats and changes in Russian tactics. For the tracker, the move confirms that Russian FPV interdiction of the logistics zone has become persistent enough that Ukraine is answering it with permanent, nationally standardised infrastructure.

The Trump administration is discussing a partial easing of sanctions on so-called shadow fleet tankers that have carried oil from Russia, Iran and Venezuela, Reuters reported citing a US official on 14 October; Ukrainska Pravda, Meduza and Euromaidan Press relayed the report. The talks centre on letting "legal oil" - crude or products not sanctioned by Washington, so not Russian or Iranian oil - move on previously blacklisted vessels. Industry sources added that insurers must still comply with EU and UK rules, which are not expected to loosen. The White House did not respond to Reuters; Reuters noted that high fuel prices are a political risk for Trump ahead of the 3 November midterms. The move follows a 9 October Treasury licence allowing trade in Russian-origin diesel until 7 April 2027, which Zelensky reportedly called weak and Democratic senators Shaheen, Schumer and Warren called a betrayal of Ukraine; Republican senator Thom Tillis questioned its legality. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov called the diesel deal mutually beneficial. Ukrainian intelligence: Kh-47M2 Kinzhal shares ~90% of its components with the Iskander-M's 9M723, and 58 Russian firms make it

2026-10-12

On 12 October Ukraine's Defence Intelligence (HUR/DIU) published a breakdown of the Kh-47M2 Kinzhal and its production chain, including an interactive 3D model on its War&Sanctions portal, as reported by Militarnyi and Euromaidan Press. HUR gives the missile the Russian industrial designation 9-S-7760 and describes it as a single-stage, solid-fuel aeroballistic missile. According to HUR, about 90% of its structural components and systems are the same as, or very similar to, those of the 9M723 ballistic missile fired by the Iskander-M system. The Kinzhal-specific features listed are a tail aerodynamic fairing that separates after the motor ignites, a radar homing seeker, an onboard active jamming (EW) station and a decoy-release system meant to complicate interception. HUR also states that the active radar seeker advertised for the Iskander "exists only on paper" and has not been documented in strikes on Ukraine. According to HUR, the Kinzhal can carry either a penetrating high-explosive warhead in a thick-walled steel casing, designed to break through floors, concrete and fortified structures, or a nuclear warhead. Russia is reportedly developing a high-explosive fragmentation warhead. HUR links this to possible accuracy problems, since a penetrator only works with very precise guidance. HUR further says the Russian MoD sharply cut its 2026 state order for Kinzhal and moved production capacity, components and raw materials to 9M723 production for Iskander-M. It gives a second reason: the MiG-31I/MiG-31K carrier fleet is limited, and Russian industry no longer builds the MiG-31. HUR counts 58 Russian enterprises in the Kinzhal production network and says 22 of them are not sanctioned by any member of the sanctions coalition. Neither outlet names individual companies. Euromaidan Press recalls that Ukraine's sanctions commissioner Vladyslav Vlasiuk said in September that US-made Texas Instruments processors had again been found in a newer Kinzhal version. It also notes that Ukraine's MoD says Patriot PAC-3 can intercept the Kinzhal. The release appeared on the same day the EU sanctioned 1,569 individuals and entities tied to Russia's defence industry. All of these claims come from Ukrainian official sources, a party to the conflict, and have not been independently verified. The Euromaidan Press page is labelled as advertorial content.
