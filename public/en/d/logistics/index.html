Russia intensifies glide-bomb, drone and MLRS strikes on Izium, likely to disrupt Ukrainian logistics for the Borova and Lyman directions (ISW)

Russian forces attacked Izium in Kharkiv Oblast at least seven times between 29 October and 4 November, Euromaidan Press reports, citing the ISW's 5 November assessment. Ukrainian firm UGV Laboratory unveils updated Hyena family of small logistics and combat ground robots, citing Russian drone saturation over supply routes

2026-11-06

Ukrainian company UGV Laboratory presented an updated line of small unmanned ground vehicles (UGVs) in its Hyena family at the Iron Halloween Party 2026 event, a company representative told Ukrainska Pravda. The line comprises the upgraded Hyena Red, the mass-produced Hyena S logistics platform and a working prototype of the Hyena Cobalt combat robot, all designed to be usable "out of the box" without field modifications. The representative explained the demand directly by the Russian drone threat: "Due to the sky being oversaturated with enemy UAVs and the presence of adjusters, the logistics of medium-sized vehicles were extremely complicated," adding that some units moved significantly more cargo with small Hyenas thanks to continuous operation on the routes. The Hyena S carries up to 20 kg at up to 16 km/h over a range of 12 km, has GPS and night cameras, and can be converted into an observation post with extra batteries for long-duration video transmission; it is already in full use with Ukrainian units. The flagship Hyena Red now has a Starlink terminal as standard for digital control, with the analogue radio link retained as a backup in case of jamming; it carries a warhead of under 30 kg over about 30 km at 16 km/h, has mounting points for standard munitions, improvised explosives or TM-62 anti-tank mines, and the supply set has grown from 5 to 10 vehicles. The baseline Hyena combat vehicle takes a warhead under 26 kg over up to 20 km and has a self-destruct timer adjustable from 30 to 60 hours. The Hyena Cobalt prototype carries two Saab AT4 disposable anti-tank launchers, fires two remote shots and then detonates near the target in kamikaze mode. Hyena Red is completing combat trials in a near-final pre-production form before mass production; for Cobalt the developers are still working out regulatory procedures before delivering it combat-ready with AT4s. All performance figures are manufacturer claims and have not been independently verified.

Former Russia-to-Europe gas pipelines have become an underground front: both armies use them to infiltrate behind enemy lines and bypass the drone kill zone

2026-11-02

Meduza summarises October reports by The Atlantic (Simon Shuster) and The New York Times (Maria Varenikova) on fighting inside disused gas pipelines beneath the front line. Both armies move through the steel pipes, which once carried Siberian gas to Ukraine and the rest of Europe, to cross a drone-dominated kill zone tens of kilometres wide and to surface behind enemy lines. Ihor Obolensky, commander of the 2nd Khartiia Corps of Ukraine's National Guard, says Russian commanders adopted the tactic first and Ukrainian assault troops are now catching up. Maps obtained from the state energy company showed at least 57 steel pipes under the combat zone, most 1-2 metres in diameter; Kyiv ended gas transit for good only in early 2025. The reference case is Russia's 'Operation Pipe' in Kursk Oblast: in early March 2025, between 100 and several hundred soldiers crawled several kilometres through a section of the 1,420 mm Urengoy-Pomary-Uzhhorod trunk line and emerged behind Ukrainian lines, helping Russia retake Sudzha, although Meduza notes Kremlin propaganda exaggerated its effect. Obolensky says the surprise attacks mostly came in bad weather, when drones struggled to spot soldiers surfacing, and that Russian troops kept appearing several kilometres behind Ukrainian positions. Similar tactics followed inside Ukraine, including at Pokrovsk. At Kupiansk in 2025, Russian forces crossed under the Oskil River from Lyman Pershyi to Radkivka through the local 1,020 mm Shebelynka-Ostrogozhsk pipeline and then used it to supply their forward troops; the entrance at a compressor station near Radkivka is now held by Ukraine. Khartiia soldiers reportedly spend weeks underground, moving on modified coal-mining carts powered by electric scooter motors; the NYT describes an underground logistics hub hundreds of metres long linked to the pipe network. Fighting in the pipes involves high-calibre rifles and grenades, ricochets, blast overpressure, gas masks and oxygen tanks, and sandbag barriers that must be breached under fire. Obolensky says Khartiia regained about 14 km of pipeline in north-eastern Ukraine in October in an operation code-named Veles, which he calls the first successful counterattack under such conditions, but that most of the network remains under Russian control. He warns that control of the Donbas pipeline network could give Russia a decisive advantage in an expected winter offensive. These are Ukrainian commander accounts relayed by Western media and have not been independently verified.

Sources: Meduza
