Several Russian milblogger channels comment on the Ukrainian strike on the Crimean Bridge of 16-17 November. WarGonzo, in a post by a reserve sailor written for the project, says photos confirm that enemy missiles and drones brought down one of the bridge arches and the roadway beneath it. It calls this unpleasant but not fatal, says the bridge will be restored, and dismisses Ukrainian claims that the collapse would block Azov Sea shipping and grain, coal and other exports from Rostov-on-Don, Azov, Taganrog and Mariupol: in its view the debris would be lifted by crane onto barges within one or two working days, the sunken wreckage cannot be large enough to catch ship hulls, and the Kerch Strait fairway will be cleared quickly, hindered only by repair work or new attacks. Fighterbomber gives a more critical account. In a second post Fighterbomber rejects the 'excuses' of Ukrainian or American origin about the guidance: it argues that the 'communication system' by which a drone passes an aim point to missiles is laser designation, which needs an operator with a live link to the drone (Starlink/Starshield), that footage from a missile flying at a railway bridge shows an ordinary thermal seeker, and that a 'home-made' relay on a drone may have carried the Starlink signal; without Starlink the bridge would still stand. This is the channel's own reading, not a confirmed technical finding. Kavtarov and S. Sakhanov. The authors argue that linear formations have become extremely vulnerable now that enemy drones dominate the battlefield, and propose a 'qualitatively new' defensive method they call the 'dispersed echeloned bastion': abandoning a continuous front line in favour of a network of autonomous combat modules ('bastions') spread across the front and in depth. Each bastion is built around mobile groups equipped with uncrewed ground vehicles, FPV interceptor drones and EW assets, tied together by a single situational-awareness picture from Zala and Orlan reconnaissance drones. An attacker who wedges into such a defence does not break through it, the authors write, but comes under crossfire from prepared, concealed and frequently changing module positions. A follow-up column by Business Online military commentator Nikita Yurchenko (15 November) quotes more of the same Voyennaya Mysl article (titled "On the direction of development of the employment of the Unmanned Systems Forces of the Russian Armed Forces", by Colonel V.V. For the bastion defence they claim 2–3 times fewer losses to drone strikes, 60–80% higher survivability against FPV hits than a classic trench system, bastions spaced 500–2,000 m apart and ground robots changing position every 15–30 minutes, and they set out a five-phase drone-led attack (reconnaissance and relay placement, robotic engineer reconnaissance and breaching, strikes on enemy drone control points, air and EW cover by interceptor drones, then infantry clearance with UGVs under smoke and drone resupply/evacuation).
