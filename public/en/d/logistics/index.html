Viacheslav Klymov, co-owner of the Nova group, told the Kyiv International Economic Forum that Nova Post (Nova Poshta), Ukraine's largest private postal and courier company with more than 15,800 branches and over 34,000 employees, has set up its own air defence capability and has already destroyed Russian Shahed-type drones targeting its facilities. He called Nova Post "one of the few companies" to have implemented the project, but declined to say how many drones were downed or what equipment the unit uses. Nova Post offices and distribution centres have frequently been hit by Russian strikes, including a warehouse in Sviatopetrivske near Kyiv on 28 August 2026. The legal basis is an experimental Defence Ministry programme launched in November 2025. Under it, private companies may form air defence groups that come under Air Force command and are integrated into the Air Force's unified command-and-control system and the national air defence network. In March 2026 the Cabinet widened the terms to better protect critical infrastructure. The Air Force Command may now temporarily transfer air defence equipment and ammunition that combat units are not using, decided case by case. Prime Minister Yuliia Svyrydenko stressed that all such decisions remain under military control. According to Euromaidan Press, the units use interceptor drones and remotely operated heavy machine guns. On 30 March 2026, Defence Minister Mykhailo Fedorov announced the programme's first result: a private unit shot down several Shahed and Zala drones in Kharkiv Oblast. Euromaidan Press reports that nearly 100 companies have joined the programme and about 50 air defence groups have been formed. Crews from six companies are flying combat missions, and the groups had downed more than 60 aerial targets, mostly drones, by 14 October, according to the Defence Ministry. These are Ukrainian government figures and have not been independently verified. Not every company is taking part. Pharmaceutical firm Darnytsia, hit by Russian strikes three times in September, studied the option but decided against it, and is focusing on attack monitoring, shelters and safety procedures instead. The private units point to the reach of Russia's Shahed campaign against Ukraine's commercial logistics and civilian infrastructure, which Kyiv Independent says has included supermarkets, telecom and data centres, petrol stations and medical facilities since late summer, along with jet-powered drones flying day and night.
