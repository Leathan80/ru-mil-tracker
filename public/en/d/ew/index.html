Ukraine's Pluton detection system upgraded to detect the mesh-network modems of Russian Geran, Gerbera and Molniya drones

2026-11-10

The Ukrainian company INFOZAHYST told Militarnyi that its Pluton drone-detection ESM system has been upgraded to detect the mesh modems used in Russian Geran-, Gerbera- and Molniya-type drones. Monitoring covers the main known frequencies of 1.5 GHz and 3.4 GHz, and the developer says the upgrade allows timely detection of threats at distances of up to 70 km. A new software version also allows remote monitoring and partial control of any number of Pluton systems over a dedicated VPN. Mesh modems are one of the most notable recent upgrades to Russian drones: each drone acts as both receiver and relay, which extends control range, lets operators correct a drone's flight path almost until impact, and makes jamming much harder because the network reroutes when a node drops out. Euromaidan Press notes that a jam-resistant link still emits and can therefore be detected. It also recalls earlier reporting that the mesh modem found in downed Russian drones is an openly sold Chinese part (Frontelligence Insight, August 2026), and that Ukraine's HUR in December 2025 described a Geran-2 variant relaying video and commands via a Chinese Xingkay Tech XK-F358 modem. Pluton scans 500 to 6,000 MHz (extendable to 24 GHz according to Militarnyi), already detects FPV, Supercam, Zala, Lancet and Orlan drones, estimates distance and time of arrival at a protected site, and can work with the Plastun-RP-3000M direction finder. Digital FPV video detection and control of counter-UAS assets are planned for the next update. All performance figures are developer claims; it is not stated whether the 70 km range applies specifically to mesh modems, and there is no independent test data or information on deployment numbers.

Ukrainian expert says Iskander-M seekers let Russia hit Kyiv's Dnipro bridges despite GNSS jamming

2026-11-09

Serhii "Flash" Beskrestnov, a Ukrainian radio-technology expert and presidential adviser, wrote on Telegram on 9 November that Russian 9M723 Iskander-M ballistic missiles keep hitting bridges very accurately even though satellite navigation is suppressed across the whole of Ukraine during air-raid alerts. Under such jamming, he argued, an Iskander would struggle to achieve metre-level accuracy, and its inertial system alone cannot deliver that precision. According to Beskrestnov, the missiles therefore carry optical homing heads or more expensive radar-guided ones: as the warhead descends, a radar scans the terrain for a few seconds and a correction is fed into the missile's guidance system. Beskrestnov pointed to War&Sanctions, the database of Ukraine's Defence Intelligence (HUR/DIU), which lists both seekers for the 9M723 Iskander-M: the 9E436 optical correlation homing head, made by Russia's TsNIIAG with the NII Televideniya institute involved, and the 9B918 active radar homing head, made by NPP Radar MMS. Euromaidan Press notes that the database confirms these components exist but does not say how they perform under jamming. This is the technical explanation of one expert; there is no official Ukrainian or Russian confirmation of which seeker was used in the bridge strikes. The context is a Russian campaign against Kyiv's Dnipro crossings, the first such attacks since the full-scale invasion. Drones first hit the Southern Bridge on 1 October and again the next day; days later a strike cratered the Northern Bridge. Overnight on 27 October a ballistic attack damaged the Southern Bridge, closing traffic and suspending metro trains between Osokorky and Chervonyi Khutir, according to Mayor Vitali Klitschko. On 7 November six ballistic missiles targeted the Northern Bridge and four hit it, President Zelenskyy said; it was closed in both directions. Euromaidan Press concludes that if jamming cannot divert these missiles, the bridges depend on interceptors and repairs; Infrastructure Minister Mykola Kalashnyk said on 30 October that Ukraine plans 21 alternative river crossings, the first in Kyiv due in early 2027.

Ukrainian Mangal active protection system, which automatically fires nets at incoming FPV drones, is heading for serial production

2026-11-05

Ukrainian company Phantom Defense is preparing Mangal for serial production. Mangal is an active protection system that automatically detects FPV drones and catches them with a net as they approach military vehicles. A Militarnyi journalist watched a demonstration at a test range. Mangal grew out of the Karakurt net launchers, which were first mounted on drones to intercept other drones; Euromaidan Press notes that Phantom Defense bought Karakurt's developer in October. According to the developer, the 100th Separate Mechanized Brigade was the first to put a manually fired net launcher on a ground robot, and operator-fired versions have been at the front since March 2026. Each module covers a sector of about 90 degrees, so several are needed for all-round coverage; eight were fitted to a Stryker in one test. A module contains its own microcomputer, a control unit, day and thermal cameras, and net launchers. It computes the drone's trajectory and chooses when to fire and which launcher to use. Developer figures: detection at about 40 m against trees or cluttered terrain and over 60 m against the sky, reaction time up to 0.2 seconds, and roughly UAH 2,000 per shot (about $44 according to Euromaidan Press), including a 6x6 m net and pyrotechnic cartridge. About 1,000 test firings were carried out. Endurance is at least 12 hours, up to 36 hours in some tests, and an external battery can be connected. The unit can be detached and set up near cover if a vehicle has to be abandoned. It has been tested on Strykers, other armoured vehicles, pickups, ground robots and boats, including boats under way. Phantom Defense presents Mangal as a second layer behind electronic warfare: if jamming fails, especially against fiber-optic FPVs, the net engages at close range. According to the company, ten systems have gone to Ukrainian units for combat trials and have repelled two FPV attacks, and a Mangal 2.0 based on front-line feedback is in development. All performance figures are company claims and have not been independently verified. Euromaidan Press frames the system against road netting, which according to the Ukrainian Defence Ministry covered more than 1,640 km of front-line roads this year, but which does not move with the vehicle.

Sources: Militarnyi · Euromaidan Press

Large-scale GPS jamming mapped along NATO's eastern flank, from the Baltic to the Black Sea

2026-11-04

Large-scale interference with GPS satellite navigation has been recorded along almost the entire eastern flank of NATO, from the Baltic Sea to the Black Sea, Militarnyi reports. The finding comes from the OSINT project ItaMilRadar, which published a map of navigation interference as of 3 November 2026 based on data from the AirNav Radar flight-tracking service. The heaviest interference was concentrated along NATO members' borders with Russia, Belarus and Ukraine: near Russia's Kaliningrad region, in Lithuania, eastern Poland, Romania and Bulgaria, and over the western Black Sea. Less intense interference was seen over Belarus, western Russia, occupied Crimea and the Caucasus. Citing Defence Blog, Militarnyi notes that the geographic pattern partly overlaps with areas crossed by the flight paths of Russian attack drones. According to Militarnyi, Russian EW systems in the Kaliningrad region conduct GPS jamming and spoofing that affect the routes of Ukrainian attack drones: fake navigation signals can make a drone miscalculate its position and stray from its planned route towards the Baltic states and Finland. The map is a snapshot built from aircraft navigation data; it does not by itself attribute each interference zone to a specific emitter. The report places the snapshot in a longer pattern of Russian navigation interference in the Baltic region. In May the Finnish Border Guard reported an increase in Russian GPS jamming in the Baltic, notably in the Archipelago Sea and the Åland Sea. In August Denmark recorded a record number and duration of satellite-navigation disruptions; the crew of the cargo vessel Saturn reported losing GPS for about 24 hours while sailing between Gdańsk and southern Gotland.

Ukraine carries out first known aerial casualty evacuation by heavy-lift drone: Heavy Shot Ayudag-6 extracts wounded soldier after 491 days in the grey zone near Kupiansk; Zelensky has since made him a Hero of Ukraine

2026-10-23

On 4 October 2026 Ukrainian forces evacuated a wounded infantryman from the grey zone on the Kupiansk front by heavy drone. Ukrainska Pravda and Reuters (republished by Meduza) both call it the first known operation of its kind. The soldier is Kostiantyn "Magura" Vlasenko of the 2nd Mechanised Battalion, 30th Separate Mechanised Brigade. He had held a position on the left bank of the Oskil River since 2 June 2025. On 14 February 2026 a Russian drone-dropped munition wounded him in both legs. He lost one foot and amputated the other himself. For months he could not be reached: the area was mined, Russian infantry had infiltrated around his position, and Russian FPV and reconnaissance drones attacked anything that moved. A ground robot sent to fetch him was destroyed on the way (Reuters says two), and infantry groups also failed. Drones resupplied him every four to five days. The aircraft was the Heavy Shot Ayudag-6 from Gurzuf Defence, a modular heavy-lift logistics drone with a cargo winch. Ukrainska Pravda gives its payload as up to 130 kg. Reuters reports roughly 90 kg over up to 25 km, so the sources disagree. The drone was adapted over about six months with help from the 92nd Separate Assault Brigade's unmanned ground systems company. The day before the flight it delivered a mountaineering harness (a Petzl Pitagor evacuation triangle) and a phone with instruction videos, so the soldier could clip himself to the winch cable. Reuters puts the cable height at about 70 m. The drone flew at first light without fog, at a time when Russian drone activity had been assessed as lowest. It used Starlink for control, and Ukrainska Pravda says no jamming was detected. Several measures protected it: Vampire heavy bomber drones flew decoy sorties in neighbouring sectors to draw Russian anti-aircraft FPV operators (one Vampire was shot down), an Autel quadcopter watched overhead, a Lucky Strike interceptor drone stood ready to ram Russian UAVs, and 10th Army Corps artillery fired on identified Russian drone-launch points. Units from the 14th and 43rd Mechanised Brigades also took part, with EW support from the 3rd Army Corps. The 16 km route needed a battery swap at a pre-positioned ground team. The soldier was then handed to a Peacemaker unmanned ground vehicle, which drove him another 8 km to a vehicle and a stabilisation point. Reuters gives different figures: a 15 km distance and a 15-minute flight. Ukrainska Pravda says the drone was airborne for under 1.5 hours in total. Vlasenko is now in rehabilitation in Kharkiv. The operation matters for tactics because ground casualty evacuation in the drone-saturated 'kill zone' has become nearly impossible, and the 'golden hour' is now rarely achievable. The developer itself warns against treating this as a new routine. Gurzuf co-founder Pavlo Feldblium calls aerial evacuation a last resort that needs unique, multi-unit planning (about 15 personnel, two months of planning, seven days of pre-flight preparation). Without decoys, escorts and counter-battery fire, he says, the slow drone with its underslung load would have been shot down by the first Russian interceptor. Gurzuf will nevertheless fit its mass-produced Ayudag-6 drones with evacuation mounting kits and is developing variants with higher payloads. Update (31 October): by a decree of 30 October, President Zelensky awarded Vlasenko the title Hero of Ukraine, Ukrainska Pravda reports, citing the presidential website. Private First Class Vasyl Prokopyvniuk, who according to the report led the mission, received the Medal for Saving a Life. The President's Office said all other participants will also receive state awards and that the nominations have already been prepared. The details come from Ukrainian military participants and the developer, and from Vlasenko's own account. They have not been independently verified.
