Downed Geran-4 drones carry a 32-element, likely Alabuga-made anti-jam GNSS antenna, while Geran-4 and Geran-5 also get camera-based terrain navigation

2026-10-10

A 32-element CRPA (Controlled Radiation Pattern Antenna) has been found in a Geran-4 strike drone shot down over Ukraine, Militarnyi reports, citing an analysis by The Insider of photos and videos of wreckage; the photos, taken in September 2026, are credited to Serhii "Flash" Beskrestnov. The unit is effectively two 16-element antennas in one housing: 16 elements work in the GPS L1 band (about 1,575 MHz) and 16 in the L2 band (about 1,227 MHz). A CRPA protects satellite-navigation reception against jamming; the more elements, the more separate jamming sources it can null. Cheaper L1-only versions are the norm on drones and missiles, so a dual-band 32-element design marks a step up in jam resistance. The antenna closely resembles products of the Chinese firm Harxon but shows signs of local manufacture, including a rough metal casing stamped "ALB". Militarnyi assesses it was likely made at the Alabuga plant in Tatarstan, where Geran drones are produced, and that Russia is moving towards at least kit (screwdriver) assembly of such boards there. Harxon was earlier reported as Alabuga's main supplier of multi-element antennas; it was subsequently sanctioned and says it dismissed staff who had contact with Russia. Ukraine had already seized a 32-element CRPA on a Russian weapon system in July. The sources do not describe the antenna fit of the Geran-2 or Geran-3. Separately, Beskrestnov, an adviser to the Ukrainian president on defence technology, wrote on Telegram that Russia is fitting drones with image-based navigation: an onboard camera compares what it sees with stored terrain imagery, which in basic versions can be public satellite or ground imagery such as Google's. Because this does not rely on satellite signals, it is hard to jam. According to him, Russian forces already use it on Molniya, V2U and Lancet drones, and similar systems were recently found on the Geran-4 and Geran-5, which use a flight controller called "Susanin". He expects wider use in 2027, including on medium- and long-range strike drones and cheap cruise missiles, which would push Ukraine towards physical interception rather than jamming.
