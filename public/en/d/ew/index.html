Ukraine's CODE380 develops portable D·Vision SDR analyzer that detects drone signals and displays analog drone video

2026-10-19

Ukrainian company CODE380 has developed the D·Vision SDR, a portable radio-frequency analyzer that scans the airwaves for drone signals and lets the operator view video transmitted over analog channels, Militarnyi reports, citing the Ukrainian tech outlet DOU and the manufacturer. Unlike conventional analog video receivers, the device uses software-defined radio to search for and identify characteristic drone signals across a wide frequency range. It detects PAL and NTSC analog video as well as compatible digital signals from DJI Mavic drones, but it can only play back analog video: DJI digital video is detected, not decoded. According to the manufacturer, analog video can be received and viewed at up to 10 km at 540–6,000 MHz and at 3.5–5 km above 6,000 MHz; DJI Mavic digital signals are detected at up to 2.5 km. The unit has two SMA antenna inputs, a built-in screen, microSD recording, a waterfall display of spectrum activity, an EW-system testing function and USB remote control. Battery life is up to three or six hours depending on the version; it weighs about 290 g without antennas and measures 130 × 80 × 22 mm. Development took about a year and a half in total, and the developers say it was field-tested with Ukrainian service members, including on combat missions. These performance figures are manufacturer claims and have not been independently verified. Militarnyi places the device in a wider Ukrainian trend of portable electronic-reconnaissance detectors for early warning of enemy drones, such as BABAI's Vydun 7.5 (analog video up to 7.5 GHz), the SENSE-4 (up to 8.2 GHz) and the Chuyka 3.0 extended to the 6–8.8 GHz band.
