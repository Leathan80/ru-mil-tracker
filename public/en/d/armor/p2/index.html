Ukraine's KhKBM says it built ~600 BTR-4s under fire and unveils the self-funded Pegas IFV at MSPO 2026

2026-09-09

At the MSPO defence exhibition in Kielce, Poland, the Kharkiv Morozov Machine Building Design Bureau (KhKBM/KMDB) said it has built roughly 600 BTR-4 8x8 armoured personnel carriers since the start of 2022, having relocated production out of Kharkiv beyond Russian strike range and worked through repeated attacks on Ukraine's defence industry. Company representatives put annual capacity at about 200 vehicles and said the BTR-4E is roughly 80% Ukrainian-made by component count. The 600 figure is a company statement and is not independently confirmed. The resilience story has a flip side: KhKBM also said the state is not currently ordering BTR-4Es. The last existing state order was completed in mid-2026, and on-hand component stocks are enough for only another 100-200 hulls. The plant has kept producing under fire, but faces an order drought that threatens continuity. KhKBM separately unveiled the Pegas, a new tracked IFV of about 35 tonnes that it has been developing on its own initiative since 2024 using company funds, with no state financing. Intended to replace Soviet BMP-1/BMP-2 vehicles, it is designed for STANAG 4569 Level 4 all-round protection and Level 6 across the frontal arc, with planned armament via the Topaz module (30 mm cannon, coaxial 7.62 mm, two ATGMs, 30 mm automatic grenade launcher) plus planned anti-UAV electronic warfare. A mock-up was displayed at MSPO and a first prototype is reported to be about half assembled. All specifications are design intent; nothing is combat-proven.

Sources: Defence Blog · Ukrainska Pravda (EN)

Ukrainian Abrams reportedly survives 20 FPV drone hits with improvised cage-and-hedgehog armour

2026-09-09SINGLE SOURCE

Defense Express reports that two Ukrainian video materials appeared in open access at roughly the same time, both dealing with improvised anti-drone protection fitted to M1A1 Abrams tanks in Ukrainian service. In an interview by the outlet Hromadske with crews of the 425th Separate Assault Regiment "Skelia," the tankers describe an episode in which one of their Abrams withstood hits from as many as 20 enemy FPV drones. That vehicle was said to be additionally covered with cage ("mangal") armour combined with reactive/active-protection blocks. The account is presented as evidence both of the platform's baseline survivability and of the quality of the field-improvised add-on protection. The second video, attributed to the Armia Media portal, shows a tank crew of the 225th Separate Assault Regiment operating an Abrams fitted with "hedgehog"-type protection — outward-projecting welded steel spikes. According to the crew, this design is particularly effective against fiber-optic-guided FPV drones: the trailing control cable catches and tears on the steel spikes, so the drone loses control and falls before it can strike. The report notes that the spike array is welded on ergonomically, still allows access to the turret, and does not appear to restrict the tank's mobility. Surrounding context in the piece states that a single tank sortie now requires around two weeks of planning and weather timed to limit Russian strike-drone employment. This is a single Ukrainian-source account (Defense Express, relaying Ukrainian unit videos) and has not been independently confirmed; the "20 drone hits" figure and the claimed performance of the improvised armour rest on the crews' own statements. Defense Express is a Ukrainian outlet with a pronounced editorial slant.

Почему танки перестали доминировать на поле боя? (Rybar video essay)

2026-09-05SINGLE SOURCEMILBLOGGER

Rybar published a short video essay (2:21) from its #ИсторияПодвига series, forwarded from the affiliated channel Тактикарь, asking why the tank no longer dominates the battlefield. Rybar first sketches the classic role: since the early twentieth century the tank was the "steel fist" of armies — it broke through defensive lines, supported infantry and served as an assault gun. Tanks entered the "SVO" in that role, with examples such as a T-80BV crew destroying Ukrainian armour and the "carousel" tactic used against Ukrainian infantry. Then, Rybar argues, everything changed because of "small, cheap products made from commercial components" — FPV drones — which he calls the most serious challenge to the tank since it first appeared on the battlefield. The factors cited are: mass-producible, very cheap FPV drones and anti-tank guided missiles that make any visible vehicle vulnerable; the loss of combined-arms cover (infantry, air defence, electronic warfare) so that tanks can no longer advance under protection; and a cost asymmetry in which a drone worth a few hundred dollars disables a vehicle worth millions. The consequence: both armies no longer use the tank for the breakthrough but as mobile artillery and standoff fire support — firing from cover, then withdrawing quickly. As countermeasures Rybar points to physical protection (cope cages, rubber and chain skirts, and more recently the heavier "hedgehog" cage armour), vehicle-mounted jammers against FPV control links, active protection systems, and above all adapted tactics that minimise exposure; he implicitly concedes that no single measure removes the drone threat. Caveat: this is a single Russian milblogger source (Rybar), a personal/editorial voice and not an MoD position; milbloggers contradict each other and the MoD. The item connects thematically to entry e-20260905-02 (hedgehog cage armour and Ukraine's decision to park its Abrams tanks).

Krasnaya Zvezda profile «The Fiddler and his symphony of battle»: 1st Guards Tank Army assault-group training

2026-09-01SINGLE SOURCE

SINGLE SOURCE — Krasnaya Zvezda, the Russian Defence Ministry's official daily (a state/propaganda outlet). A front-line profile built around an instructor with the call sign «Skripach» (Fiddler). The peg is a multi-day combat-coordination course (боевое слаживание) for assault groups at a polygon in the rear area of the «special military operation» zone. The article identifies the trainees as personnel of the assault subunits of the 12th Guards Tank Regiment, 4th Guards Tank Division (Kantemirovskaya), 1st Guards Tank Army, «Zapad» grouping of forces. The soldiers practised seizing strongpoints and small-arms fighting in built-up terrain across more than 20 assault episodes; the training ground is described as a maze of buildings with breached walls and multi-level firing points, with new injects every hour (enemy armour, loss of communications). Skripach is a senior sergeant and machine-gunner who has fought since 2022 and now trains new intakes between rotations. A comrade («Zira», a rifleman-instructor) says the optimal urban assault team is no more than four men — machine-gunner, rifleman, grenadier and commander, each with his own sector. Entering a building is called the most dangerous phase: approach at an angle, throw a grenade, then an instant rush inside while the machine-gunner suppresses the adjacent floors. Training also covers self-emplaced smoke, mock underground passages and tactical medicine (self-applied tourniquet, shift the weapon to the good hand, keep moving and firing). As a final check, the assault groups took three notional strongpoints in sequence, with success defined as an assault time no longer than eight minutes; the article says all groups passed. There are no claims of specific battlefield results, locations or dates — it is primarily a morale and human-interest story. The one piece of durable OSINT value is the ORBAT detail (12th Guards Tank Regiment / 4th Guards Tank Division / 1st Guards Tank Army under «Zapad») and the picture of tank-division personnel trained and fielded as dismounted urban assault infantry — consistent with the well-documented Russian pattern. Uncorroborated by independent reporting.

Lightness and Maneuverability Against Drones: US Army Orders Thousands of ISV Armored Vehicles

2026-08-23SINGLE SOURCE

SINGLE SOURCE — Russian publication (Topwar.ru) on US procurement policy. The US Army has sharply expanded its ISV order from GM Defense: from an original 649 to 11,582 units (nearly 18-fold). The ISV carries nine soldiers and prioritizes lightness/agility over armor; meant to complement, not replace, the Humvee/JLTV. Rationale per the article: lessons from Ukraine — units remaining visible/stationary too long can be detected by drones and quickly destroyed. US planners reportedly aim for rapid repositioning/dispersal to outpace enemy targeting cycles. One ISV variant uses a Toyota Hilux chassis (TAG Dynamics), indicating reliance on civilian platforms. CAVEAT: Russian publication on US equipment policy; no independent confirmation of exact figures, contract terms, or rationale — treat as a claim relayed by Topwar, not verified fact.

Sources: topwar

UK Pulls Challenger 2 Tanks From Estonia, Replacing Armored Group With Drone-Focused Mobile Anti-Armour Force After Ukrainian Operators Destroyed Two Battalions in One Day at Hedgehog 2025

2026-07-20SINGLE SOURCE

According to Ukrainian defense outlet Defense Express, the United Kingdom is withdrawing its Challenger 2 tanks from Estonia and replacing the existing armored battlegroup with a new "Mobile Anti-Armour Force" (MAAF). The British contingent in the country would reportedly grow from roughly 800 to 1,200 personnel. The new force emphasizes mobility, survivability, and drone integration, and would be equipped with high-mobility gear, modern weapons, and advanced unmanned systems tailored to Estonia's operational environment. The restructuring is directly tied by the source to Ukrainian combat experience and to the NATO Hedgehog 2025 exercise held in May, which involved about 16,000 troops from twelve NATO countries plus Ukraine. During that exercise, Ukrainian drone operators — reportedly around ten personnel from the 412th Separate Unmanned Systems Brigade "Nemesis" — allegedly managed to "destroy" two notional adversary battalions in roughly half a day, mock-destroying 17 armored vehicles and conducting about 30 strikes on other targets. Defense Express frames this within a broader shift toward a more drone-centric British doctrine, describing an eventual unmanned force structure that would be roughly 80% drone-based across land, naval, and air domains. SINGLE SOURCE — this is a report from Defense Express, a Ukrainian outlet, apparently relying on unnamed or anonymous sources. There is no official confirmation from the UK Ministry of Defence of this restructuring, the personnel figures, or the stated rationale. The "two battalions destroyed in one day" claim from Hedgehog 2025 is likewise not independently verified and should be treated as an unconfirmed claim rather than established fact.
