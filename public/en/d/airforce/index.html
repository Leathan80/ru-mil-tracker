RFE/RL investigation: Serbian firm Grindex exported about €27.5m in machine tools to Russia, some reaching Iskander and military aircraft producers

2026-11-10

A joint investigation by Radio Liberty's (RFE/RL) Balkan and Ukrainian services, reported by Ukrainska Pravda, found that the Serbian metalworking-equipment maker Grindex, based in Kikinda, exported roughly €27.5 million in machine tools to Russia after the start of the full-scale invasion. Ukraine's Defence Intelligence (HUR) reportedly has information that the shipments ran from at least March 2022 to March 2025, with the equipment registered to Russian intermediaries before reaching sanctioned military plants. A main buyer was Alfa Machinery Group (AMG), sanctioned by the US in 2023 and the EU in late 2024. Grindex signed a deal with AMG in December 2022 and delivered a €778,000 machine after the US listing; it ended up at the Rumyantsev Machine-Building Association, part of Rostec, which makes assemblies for combat aircraft. A second machine bought in 2023 for over US$384,000 went in spring 2025 to the Hydraulics plant in Ufa, which makes parts for military aircraft fuel and hydraulic systems. Sanctioned intermediaries Finval Energo (about €12 million) and Unimatic MSK (nearly €980,000) also received Grindex tools. Via the unsanctioned firm Krona, a Serbian grinding machine went in 2024 to the Machine-Building Design Bureau (KBM), maker of the Iskander-M short-range ballistic missile system; another machine imported in 2025 through a Moscow front man was leased to the Perm instrument-making company that builds navigation and stabilisation systems for MiG-29 and Su-27 fighters. Serbia's Ministry of Trade told the journalists it had no information on Grindex products and had issued no dual-use export permits to the company; Serbian customs had carried out no inspections of its exports. Serbia has not joined Western sanctions against Russia. The findings rest on the investigation and Ukrainian intelligence information; Grindex's response is not mentioned in the report. Leaked documents published by InformNapalm in 2025 had already pointed to Russian dependence on Grindex grinding machines for Su-57 and PAK DA engine components.

«New Tu-22M3s, Tu-160s Are Not Really Being Built, Tu-214 Deliveries Average Just One Year: So What Is Kazan Aircraft Plant Doing?» (Defense Express) — Ukrainian analysis says Kazan Aircraft Plant has delivered one Tu-22M3M, up to five Tu-160M2s and three Tu-214s since 2022, far below Russian targets

2026-11-09SINGLE SOURCE

Defense Express (author Ivan Kyrychevskiy, a serviceman of the 413th Raid Regiment of Ukraine's Unmanned Systems Forces) examines the output of the Kazan Aircraft Plant (KAPO), the Russian plant known for the Tu-22M3 and Tu-160 strategic bombers and the Tu-214, including military variants. Citing Russian specialist sources, it says KAPO can in theory build up to three Tu-214s a year with a production cycle of at least 14 months, but has delivered only three Tu-214s to customers since 2022. One of them had stood unfinished and rusting behind the factory fence since the late 2000s. The often-quoted 'one aircraft a year' is, according to the outlet, partly a statistical artefact: the same Tu-214 was counted as manufactured in 2025 and as delivered in 2026. Two more airframes are at various stages of completion. Plans from 2023 called for 70 Tu-214s by 2030; actual output is about ten times lower, yet the targets have not been substantially revised. Russia now plans four Tu-214 deliveries in 2026 (including aircraft for security agencies), eight in 2027 and twelve in 2028, while acknowledging that even at maximum capacity KAPO cannot deliver more than four a year. On strategic aviation, the plant has delivered only one upgraded Tu-22M3M to the Russian MoD since the full-scale invasion began (reportedly fitted with an in-flight refuelling probe the standard Tu-22M3 lacks), and reportedly up to five Tu-160M2s assembled from airframe sections stockpiled in the Soviet era. Defense Express concludes that genuinely new bomber production is virtually nonexistent, despite KAPO management's claims of a 'large-scale modernisation' of the plant. It attributes the gap less to theft than to a lack of organisational capability, and recalls that in January 2026 Tupolev's mounting problems led only to a change of management. Single source: an analysis by the Ukrainian defence outlet Defense Express, partly based on unnamed Russian specialist sources; the delivery figures are not independently confirmed.

Ukrainian F-16 pilot downs nine Russian strike drones in one sortie using only the M61A1 Vulcan cannon

2026-11-06

A Ukrainian F-16 pilot shot down nine Russian strike drones in a single sortie using only the aircraft's 20mm M61A1 Vulcan cannon, according to the Ukrainian Air Force's 107th Separate Aviation Wing, which relayed its commander's remarks at the International Fighter Conference in Munich. Days earlier the same pilot had downed eight drones in one flight, firing 400 rounds and landing with 100 left; on the record flight he returned with 80 rounds still in the belt. "The tactic is simple: just get into range and fire the cannon," the wing commander said. These are claims by the Ukrainian side and have not been independently verified. According to the figures cited by Euromaidan Press, Ukraine's F-16s have downed about 4,700 Russian strike drones over the course of the war, roughly 1,100 of them in October alone, most of them with the cannon. The gun is partly a workaround for scarcity: Ukraine's F-16s are so short of air-to-air missiles that pilots increasingly engage drones with the cannon and save missiles for harder targets. The method has drawbacks: falling 20mm rounds behave like grenades on impact and can endanger civilians over a wide area. The approach depends on foreign ammunition supplies. Denmark has sent more than 50,000 cannon rounds, and Spain, Belgium and Poland have also supplied 20mm rounds, with a larger package expected. The 107th is the first of the NATO-style "wings" Ukraine is forming as it restructures its air force on the alliance model by 2035.

US Air Force issues request for information to industry on mass-producing the ERAM cruise missile for Ukraine, seeking over 1,000 within 15 months

2026-11-03

The US Air Force has published a Request for Information (RFI) asking defence companies to describe their ability to supply the Extended Range Attack Munition (ERAM) for Ukraine, Defense News reports. Responses are due on 19 November. The RFI calls for the first of more than 1,000 missiles to be delivered within three months of contract award and all deliveries to be completed within 15 months. The Air Force describes ERAM as a next-generation cruise missile "designed to provide affordable mass to the fight" with a precision-guided, stand-off capability against high-value fixed targets. According to the RFI, ERAM is a 500-pound-class air-launched weapon with a range of more than 250 nautical miles (about 460 km), a speed above Mach 0.6 and terminal accuracy within 10 metres even under jamming such as degraded GPS, with blast-fragmentation effects and limited penetration. The Air Force awarded prototype contracts to two small firms, Zone 5 Technologies and CoAspire, in 2024, and a live warhead test took place at Eglin Air Force Base in early 2026. In August 2025 the Trump administration announced an $825 million sale of 3,350 ERAMs to Ukraine, according to CNN. Ukrainian media suggested in June 2026 that Ukraine had used the AGM-188A "Rusty Dagger", an ERAM version, against the Voronezh Semiconductor Plant, which makes components for Russian missiles; this has not been officially confirmed. Defense News notes it remains unclear whether Ukraine already launches ERAM from its F-16s, and how much the US military itself will adopt the weapon after the Iran war depleted stocks of expensive missiles. Update 5 November: according to Defence Blog, the request for information (notice FA865927W0002, issued by the Air Force Life Cycle Management Center's armament directorate at Eglin AFB) seeks more than 1,000 ERAMs, with first deliveries within three months of award and completion within 15 months. Defence Blog calculates that this means more than 80 missiles a month, against roughly 42 a month under the existing programme as reported earlier by the Kyiv Post. Bidders must show transfer alignment with the F-16 Block 10/15/20 Mid-Life Update, the European standard of the jets Ukraine received from Denmark, the Netherlands, Norway and Belgium. The notice also asks that active seekers can be integrated, naming dual-band infrared and anti-radiation seekers as examples, but does not say whether missiles in this buy would carry them. The required performance remains a 500-lb-class weapon with a range of at least 463 km, a speed of at least Mach 0.6 and a 10 m CEP, including under GPS jamming. No contract value was given.

Russian glide-bomb use fell 11% in October to 8,016 after Ukrainian Neptune strikes on Su-34s at Borisoglebsk and Marinovka

2026-11-03

Russian forces dropped 8,016 guided aerial bombs on Ukrainian positions in October, 11% fewer than in September, according to the monitoring project Oko Gora, as reported by Defence Blog. That is an average of 259 bombs a day and the first monthly decline after a steady climb through the summer; working back from the 11% drop, September's record was roughly 9,000. Ukrainian Ministry of Defence figures cited by the outlet show 8,266 glide bombs in June, more than 8,300 in July and 8,766 in August. In the night before 15 October Ukraine hit Borisoglebsk air base in Voronezh region, about 370 km from the front; President Zelensky said six Su-34M bombers were struck and four destroyed, and Ukraine's navy said Neptune missiles were used. On 29 October the navy said it destroyed two more Su-34s with Neptune missiles at Marinovka air base in Volgograd region. These Ukrainian claims have not been independently confirmed. Defence Blog stresses that the October figures alone do not prove the airfield strikes caused the decline: monthly totals also move with the tempo of ground fighting, and Oko Gora's summary is not broken down by aircraft type or sector. Even after the fall, the October rate of more than 250 bombs a day remained close to the summer levels of June and July.
