The War Zone reports that Ukraine's F-16 fleet is in a readiness crisis. Of the roughly 40-50 F-16s Ukraine has received so far (out of 110 promised by Belgium, Denmark, the Netherlands and Norway), more than half are reportedly stuck in maintenance limbo, and five losses have been confirmed. TWZ builds on Wall Street Journal reporting that phase inspections at the maintenance hub in Belgium can take around 40 weeks, against 2-6 weeks typical in the US. TWZ credits F-16s with some 2,500 drones and 650 cruise missiles destroyed (the Air Force commander's own figures are covered in an earlier entry, e-20260930-10). The consequence is felt against Russia's jet-powered Geran drones, which reach roughly 375 mph and which slower interceptor drones struggle to catch; TWZ says Russia launched about 2,000 such drones in September versus 350 in June. Analyst Michael Kofman is quoted calling this a growing issue precisely because F-16s are important in intercepting Geran-4/5. Relief is slow: three more F-16s from Belgium are promised by end-2026, 16 Gripen C/D from Sweden in early 2027, Gripen E deliveries from early 2029, plus limited Mirage 2000-5F deliveries. Poland switches to sensor-based identification for shooting down aerial objects during Russian strikes on Ukraine, as Sweden returns Gripens to guard the Rzeszów aid hub

2026-09-30

Poland put simplified procedures for detecting and shooting down foreign objects in its airspace into force on 1 October, Defence Minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz announced, according to Ukrainska Pravda citing European Pravda and Gazeta Prawna. Deputy Defence Minister Cezary Tomczyk explained on 28 September that the change mainly concerns how Polish pilots on airspace-protection missions during Russian strikes on Ukraine identify targets: the peacetime requirement to identify a target visually before engaging it is replaced by dynamic, sensor-based identification. The change follows a series of Russian strikes close to the Polish border. On 17 September a Russian jet-powered strike drone came down about 4 km inside Ukraine near the Dorohusk-Yahodyn border crossing in Volyn Oblast during an attack on western Ukraine; Prime Minister Donald Tusk spoke of 'a very powerful explosion' and said the target was probably a petrol station. Tusk called it an exceptional day for security, after similar incidents with Russian drones near Polish territory on 15-16 September. In parallel, Swedish Defence Minister Pål Jonson announced on 30 September that Sweden will rejoin NATO's integrated air and missile defence over Poland from October with JAS 39 Gripen fighters, chiefly to guard the logistics hub around Rzeszów-Jasionka in south-eastern Poland, through which much Western aid for Ukraine passes. Most aircraft will operate from bases in Sweden with only a small forward presence in Poland; the number of jets and the duration were not disclosed. Jonson cited 'repeated violations of NATO airspace' as the reason for high readiness on the eastern flank. Swedish Gripens guarded the same hub before, from May 2025 (six Gripen C and about 120 personnel of the Blekinge Wing), after a first deployment of six Gripens to Malbork in March 2025.
