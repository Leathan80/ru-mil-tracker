RAF reportedly moves aircraft into hangars and hardened shelters at night over drone threat

2026-11-25

Britain's Royal Air Force is moving aircraft into hangars and hardened aircraft shelters at night to protect them from possible drone attacks, The Times and Sky News reported on 25 November 2026. According to the reports, RAF personnel 'stationed across the country' are putting aircraft under cover at night, and troops have been tasked with erecting Kevlar anti-drone nets, like those used in Ukraine, around jets that have to stay in the open. Neither outlet named its sources and the Ministry of Defence did not immediately comment, so the measure has not been officially confirmed. Russian milbloggers (Fighterbomber, Dva Mayora) quickly picked up the reports. Defence Blog notes that only a handful of British bases have hardened shelters, and that lawmakers called this month for more after US B-1 bombers were withdrawn from RAF Fairford. The reports follow weeks of security alerts at bases in England used by the US Air Force. On 13 November USAF Security Forces at RAF Lakenheath responded to 'suspected unauthorized drone activity' without damage or injuries; a former British Army pilot later said three light aircraft he was flying with may have triggered the alert. At RAF Fairford five men were arrested in September after suspicious activity around the base; counter-terrorism police called Iranian involvement 'highly likely'. In November 2024 groups of up to 20 drones appeared over RAF Mildenhall, Lakenheath and Feltwell for several nights. The reports do not attribute the drone threat to a specific state, though they coincide with a warning by the Defence Ministry and MI5, issued on 24 November, of an increased Russian sabotage threat against companies that directly support Ukraine's military. If confirmed, nightly sheltering and netting would show the RAF adopting passive protection measures learned from Ukraine for its home bases.
