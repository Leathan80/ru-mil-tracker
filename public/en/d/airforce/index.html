Czechia discusses up to 16 L-39NG Skyfox jets for Ukraine as low-cost hunters of Russian drones

2026-11-12

Czechia could supply Ukraine with up to 16 Aero L-39NG Skyfox light jets, The War Zone reports. Four were previously agreed for transfer and twelve more are under discussion. Czech Deputy Prime Minister and Industry and Trade Minister Karel Havlíček raised the expanded deal on a visit to Kyiv on 11 November, where he met Ukrainian Prime Minister Serhiy Koretskyi. He called the talks encouraging, but no agreement on the additional aircraft has been finalised. Ukraine had earlier sought Czech L-159 ALCA light combat jets; President Petr Pavel supported transferring up to four, but Prime Minister Andrej Babiš's government refused, saying the Czech military needed them. The intended role builds on Aero Vodochody's 'Drone Hunter' concept: an electro-optical targeting system plus launchers for 70 mm laser-guided rockets, developed with Thales Belgium, with gun pods as an option. This configuration is not yet in operational service. The Skyfox is a modernised L-39 with a US Williams FJ44-4M turbofan, a glass cockpit and five hardpoints. TWZ gives a top speed of about 560 mph (about 900 km/h) and a ceiling of 38,000 ft. Aero Vodochody puts operating costs at about $1,700–2,300 per flight hour, against about $22,000 for an F-16-class fighter. A 70 mm guided rocket of the APKWS II type costs roughly $20,000–25,000, against about $1 million for an AIM-120 and about $500,000 for an AIM-9X. The jet is meant to take pressure off Ukraine's F-16s, MiG-29s, Su-27s and Mirages, more than half of whose F-16s were reportedly stuck in maintenance amid shortages of AIM-9 and AIM-120 missiles. It is aimed especially at Russia's jet-powered Geran-3/4/5 drones, with the Geran-4 reportedly reaching about 280–310 mph and 26,000 ft. Justin Bronk of RUSI called the L-39NG very cheap and simple to operate compared with frontline fighters, with speed and endurance suited to intercepting jet drones. His main caveat is the lack of an onboard radar, which Ukraine's ground radar network can partly offset. Many Ukrainian pilots and technicians already know the L-39. Aero Vodochody builds about 12 Skyfox a year (capacity 18), and its schedule is reportedly booked through Q2 2027, which could delay deliveries. Russian outlet Topwar reported the same Czech claims; its readers questioned the radar-less concept and the delivery backlog.

A joint investigation by Radio Liberty's (RFE/RL) Balkan and Ukrainian services, reported by Ukrainska Pravda, found that the Serbian metalworking-equipment maker Grindex, based in Kikinda, exported roughly €27.5 million in machine tools to Russia after the start of the full-scale invasion. Ukraine's Defence Intelligence (HUR) reportedly has information that the shipments ran from at least March 2022 to March 2025, with the equipment registered to Russian intermediaries before reaching sanctioned military plants. A main buyer was Alfa Machinery Group (AMG), sanctioned by the US in 2023 and the EU in late 2024. Grindex signed a deal with AMG in December 2022 and delivered a €778,000 machine after the US listing; it ended up at the Rumyantsev Machine-Building Association, part of Rostec, which makes assemblies for combat aircraft. A second machine bought in 2023 for over US$384,000 went in spring 2025 to the Hydraulics plant in Ufa, which makes parts for military aircraft fuel and hydraulic systems. Sanctioned intermediaries Finval Energo (about €12 million) and Unimatic MSK (nearly €980,000) also received Grindex tools. Via the unsanctioned firm Krona, a Serbian grinding machine went in 2024 to the Machine-Building Design Bureau (KBM), maker of the Iskander-M short-range ballistic missile system; another machine imported in 2025 through a Moscow front man was leased to the Perm instrument-making company that builds navigation and stabilisation systems for MiG-29 and Su-27 fighters. Serbia's Ministry of Trade told the journalists it had no information on Grindex products and had issued no dual-use export permits to the company; Serbian customs had carried out no inspections of its exports. Serbia has not joined Western sanctions against Russia. The findings rest on the investigation and Ukrainian intelligence information; Grindex's response is not mentioned in the report. Leaked documents published by InformNapalm in 2025 had already pointed to Russian dependence on Grindex grinding machines for Su-57 and PAK DA engine components.

2026-11-09SINGLE SOURCE

Defense Express (author Ivan Kyrychevskiy, a serviceman of the 413th Raid Regiment of Ukraine's Unmanned Systems Forces) examines the output of the Kazan Aircraft Plant (KAPO), the Russian plant known for the Tu-22M3 and Tu-160 strategic bombers and the Tu-214, including military variants. Citing Russian specialist sources, it says KAPO can in theory build up to three Tu-214s a year with a production cycle of at least 14 months, but has delivered only three Tu-214s to customers since 2022. One of them had stood unfinished and rusting behind the factory fence since the late 2000s. The often-quoted 'one aircraft a year' is, according to the outlet, partly a statistical artefact: the same Tu-214 was counted as manufactured in 2025 and as delivered in 2026. Russia now plans four Tu-214 deliveries in 2026 (including aircraft for security agencies), eight in 2027 and twelve in 2028, while acknowledging that even at maximum capacity KAPO cannot deliver more than four a year. On strategic aviation, the plant has delivered only one upgraded Tu-22M3M to the Russian MoD since the full-scale invasion began (reportedly fitted with an in-flight refuelling probe the standard Tu-22M3 lacks), and reportedly up to five Tu-160M2s assembled from airframe sections stockpiled in the Soviet era. Defense Express concludes that genuinely new bomber production is virtually nonexistent, despite KAPO management's claims of a 'large-scale modernisation' of the plant. It attributes the gap less to theft than to a lack of organisational capability, and recalls that in January 2026 Tupolev's mounting problems led only to a change of management. Ukrainian F-16 pilot downs nine Russian strike drones in one sortie using only the M61A1 Vulcan cannon

2026-11-06

A Ukrainian F-16 pilot shot down nine Russian strike drones in a single sortie using only the aircraft's 20mm M61A1 Vulcan cannon, according to the Ukrainian Air Force's 107th Separate Aviation Wing, which relayed its commander's remarks at the International Fighter Conference in Munich. Days earlier the same pilot had downed eight drones in one flight, firing 400 rounds and landing with 100 left; on the record flight he returned with 80 rounds still in the belt. According to the figures cited by Euromaidan Press, Ukraine's F-16s have downed about 4,700 Russian strike drones over the course of the war, roughly 1,100 of them in October alone, most of them with the cannon. The method has drawbacks: falling 20mm rounds behave like grenades on impact and can endanger civilians over a wide area. The approach depends on foreign ammunition supplies. Denmark has sent more than 50,000 cannon rounds, and Spain, Belgium and Poland have also supplied 20mm rounds, with a larger package expected. The 107th is the first of the NATO-style "wings" Ukraine is forming as it restructures its air force on the alliance model by 2035.

US Air Force issues request for information to industry on mass-producing the ERAM cruise missile for Ukraine, seeking over 1,000 within 15 months

2026-11-03

The US Air Force has published a Request for Information (RFI) asking defence companies to describe their ability to supply the Extended Range Attack Munition (ERAM) for Ukraine, Defense News reports. Responses are due on 19 November. The RFI calls for the first of more than 1,000 missiles to be delivered within three months of contract award and all deliveries to be completed within 15 months. The Air Force describes ERAM as a next-generation cruise missile "designed to provide affordable mass to the fight" with a precision-guided, stand-off capability against high-value fixed targets. According to the RFI, ERAM is a 500-pound-class air-launched weapon with a range of more than 250 nautical miles (about 460 km), a speed above Mach 0.6 and terminal accuracy within 10 metres even under jamming such as degraded GPS, with blast-fragmentation effects and limited penetration. The Air Force awarded prototype contracts to two small firms, Zone 5 Technologies and CoAspire, in 2024, and a live warhead test took place at Eglin Air Force Base in early 2026. In August 2025 the Trump administration announced an $825 million sale of 3,350 ERAMs to Ukraine, according to CNN. Ukrainian media suggested in June 2026 that Ukraine had used the AGM-188A "Rusty Dagger", an ERAM version, against the Voronezh Semiconductor Plant, which makes components for Russian missiles; this has not been officially confirmed.
