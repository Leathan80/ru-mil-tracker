Quantum Wiy Industries unveils upgraded serial SPYS 'Shahed hunter' interceptor: ~500 km/h on trials, 500 g warhead, radar-cued auto-intercept

2026-11-16

Ukrainian company Quantum Wiy Industries, which makes the SPYS and Strila interceptors, has presented an updated serial version of its high-speed SPYS interceptor drone, a company representative told Militarnyi. The design was finalised and industrialised using feedback from combat crews who had tested the first prototypes against jet-powered targets. The main change is the aerodynamics: narrower wings, a reshaped nose section, redesigned air intakes and a relocated antenna that had caused significant drag. The airframe also changed from black to white plastic, because black drones lying in the summer sun heated up to around 80 °C. It is still 3D-printed so the design can be changed quickly. Take-off weight stays at 4.6 kg. The warhead grew from 300 g to 500 g and is now a codified, factory-made blast-fragmentation charge with a standard fuze, replacing improvised warheads that troops had fitted themselves. Its job is to damage the target's airframe so it loses speed, or to set off the target's own warhead. Range rose from 33 to 45 km. Stable top speed is above 450 km/h, and according to the company it reached up to 500 km/h on the test range. Stronger power electronics make the drone reusable: if it finds no target, it can return, recharge and fly again. New functions include automatic take-off and automatic routing to the target area. Integration with radars and the Sky Map system lets the onboard software calculate a likely intercept point from the target's speed and track, with no operator input during the cruise phase. Against satellite-navigation jamming, a radio-beacon system locates the drone by triangulation and keeps the ground-station antenna pointed at it. The company puts local content at 70–80%, including a Ukrainian thermal camera, and plans a future software update for automatic head-on target lock and interception. These performance figures are the manufacturer's own and have not been independently verified.

Ukrainian drone attack damages the Crimean Bridge and closes it to road traffic for hours; satellite imagery shows a deformed arch, Kremlin calls the damage minor

2026-11-16

Ukrainian drones struck the Crimean Bridge, which links Krasnodar Krai with the occupied Crimean Peninsula, early on 16 November 2026, Meduza and The War Zone report. Traffic had been closed around 02:00 Moscow time; according to TWZ a one-way attack drone hit one of the arches of the navigable road span at about 05:38 while Russian air defences were engaging drones near the bridge, and Sergei Aksyonov, the Russian-installed head of Crimea, spoke of a second attack on the bridge within 24 hours. Video reportedly of the strike shows a straight-winged, propeller-driven drone with twin booms and an inverted-V tail, a design TWZ links to several Ukrainian strike drones. The Telegram channel Mash, which is linked to the Russian security services, said the warhead shifted the arch on the Crimean side by about 15 degrees; this has not been independently verified. Russian state TV briefly showed visible deformation of the road span's superstructure before removing the footage, and Vantor and Exilenova+ satellite imagery shows a damaged, deformed arch. How badly the structure is compromised is unknown, and Ukraine has not commented on the strike. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov spoke of minor damage and said no air defence system can fully protect against massive strikes; Deputy Prime Minister Marat Khusnullin said a repair timeline would be known soon. Rail traffic continued with delays, while road traffic stayed closed for more than 12 hours, leaving about 2,000 vehicles queued on both sides. By the afternoon Russian media reported a partial reopening with alternating one-way traffic; TWZ notes that the accompanying video avoided the damaged arch and showed no vehicles, and suggests the reopening may be a show of normality rather than proof that the structure is sound. Air Force reserve officer and defence-industry executive Anatolii Khrapchynskyi told Radio NV (via Euromaidan Press) that repeated strikes are meant to wear down Russian air defences, which spend expensive S-400 and Pantsir interceptors on each attack, and that pressure on Moscow is forcing Russia to pull air defences back, leaving Crimea more exposed; he gave no figures, and this is analysis rather than verified data. The strike coincided with what the Russian Ministry of Defence called a record night: 1,336 Ukrainian drones downed overnight (previous record 1,254 on 29 October) and 2,049 drones, four Flamingo missiles and seven guided aerial bombs in 24 hours; Moscow mayor Sergei Sobyanin said about 600 drones targeted the capital region. These are Russian official figures and the number of drones actually launched is unknown. The bridge was previously hit in October 2022 (truck bomb, later acknowledged by the SBU), in July 2023 (Sea Baby naval drones) and in June 2025, when the SBU said it detonated more than 1.1 tonnes of TNT equivalent underwater against the supports.
