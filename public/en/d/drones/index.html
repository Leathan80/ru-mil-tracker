Ukrainian study finds indirect signs some pro-Kremlin bot networks slowed after drone strikes on Yandex data centres

2026-11-13

The Ukrainian NGO CAT-UA (Communication Analysis Team – Ukraine) has found indirect evidence that some pro-Kremlin bot networks slowed after Ukraine's October drone strikes on data centres of Russian tech company Yandex, Euromaidan Press reports. The study, published on 5 November, used AI to analyse 15,000 randomly sampled posts and comments per topic from eight platforms, tracking five themes of official Russian propaganda between 13 September and 12 October. Activity fell in three: criticism of Ukraine's military enlistment offices and claims of a utilities collapse in Kyiv on 11 October, and calls for a "peace deal" on 8 October. The claim has been disputed for a month. A Cloudflare Radar chart of Yandex bot traffic falling to near zero, shared as proof that troll farms went silent, actually measured automated web requests from Yandex's network, Ukrainian outlet Texty explained. US firm Graphika reported on 13 October no notable change in the AfD, MAGA and Reform UK communities, with the clearest declines in Russian-language Telegram channels but no established cause. Ukraine's Center for Countering Disinformation reported sharp drops on 8 and 12 October, recovering within days, and none after the partial strike on Yandex's Kaluga site on 9 October; Zakharchenko called its methods not fully transparent.

According to the developer, StarGate is a purpose-built casualty-evacuation platform with a payload of up to 150 kg, a top speed of up to 105 km/h, and an evacuation module based on certified medical stretchers. These are manufacturer claims. In a separate case reported by Euromaidan Press, the National Guard's 14th Brigade 'Chervona Kalyna' described a two-day rescue on the Dobropillia axis in autumn 2026, based on a video the brigade posted on 10 November. A soldier, call sign Yapokha, was wounded while moving up to a position and needed a transfusion. The battalion formed a heavy-bomber drone crew to drop blood at his position, and the brigade's head of intensive care guided the transfusion by video link. In total he received more than a litre of blood over repeated deliveries. Once he was stable, a heavy drone dropped a monowheel at the position, which the group used for about two kilometres. They then switched to a ground robot for another 14 km before an armoured group and a medevac vehicle took over. The soldier has had surgery and is alive, his battalion commander said. Russia reportedly loses three aircraft and seven aircrew while repelling Ukrainian drone attack on St Petersburg region — friendly fire suspected

2026-11-11

Russia reportedly lost three aircraft and seven aircrew on the night of 11 November 2026 while repelling a large Ukrainian drone attack on St Petersburg and the Leningrad region. The first signal came from the Fighterbomber Telegram channel (run by Ilya Tumanov), which wrote that the defence of the skies over St Petersburg had cost Russia 'very, very, very' dearly and bade farewell to 'brothers', without details. The channel Voevoda Veshchaet then reported that 'three experienced crews' — seven people — had been killed. Based on this and other Russian channels (@helifriends, Na Predele), Militarnyi assesses the losses were most likely two Ka-52 attack helicopters and one Mi-8; according to Euromaidan Press, one aviation channel's memorial post instead showed a Ka-52, an Mi-8 and a MiG-31 interceptor. Russian authorities have not confirmed any aircraft losses; officially they reported only three port workers injured at Ust-Luga. The cause is unknown. According to Meduza, some Russian pro-war bloggers suggest Russia's own air defence shot the helicopters down while engaging the drones over the St Petersburg suburbs; 'friendly fire' is one of the most common causes of manned aircraft losses in this war, and The Insider counts at least six Ka-52s lost to friendly fire since 2022. Euromaidan Press (David Axe) notes that the crew deaths suggest the aircraft were hit in the air rather than on the ground, and weighs fratricide against the less likely possibility that Ukrainian attack drones targeted aircraft in flight. Attack helicopters are a widely used Russian tool against long-range drones because they can cover approach routes that ground-based air defence cannot reach. The same raid set the oil terminal at Ust-Luga, about 100 km west of St Petersburg, on fire (fire hotspots visible in NASA FIRMS data). TASS called it the largest attack on the Leningrad region this year, and governor Alexander Drozdenko claimed 150 drones were destroyed over the region; these official figures have not been independently verified. Euromaidan Press places the night's losses in the context of a costly October for Russian aviation, when 13 fighters and helicopters were reportedly lost.
