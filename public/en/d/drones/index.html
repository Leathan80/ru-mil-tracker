2026-10-25SINGLE SOURCE

On the night of 24–25 October Russia attacked Ukraine with 153 Shahed-type strike drones, 92 of them jet-powered, together with S8000 "Banderol/Dan-T", Gerbera and "Parodiya" decoy drones, ArmyInform reports, citing the Ukrainian Air Force. ArmyInform also mentions a mobile fire group soldier who downed a jet-powered Shahed-type drone with a MANPADS over Odesa oblast. This is a single source: ArmyInform, the news agency of the Ukrainian Ministry of Defence, relaying Ukrainian Air Force figures; the claims have not been independently verified.

Russia's campaign against Kyiv's 'power ring': the capital's own CHP generation is out and strikes on 330/750 kV substations raise the prospect of 8-20 hour daily blackouts this winter

2026-10-24

In an analysis for Ukrainska Pravda (24 October 2026, translated from Ekonomichna Pravda), energy journalist Mykola Topalov describes how Russia is systematically attacking Kyiv and Kyiv Oblast with two aims: to destroy the city's combined heat and power (CHP) plants and to break the so-called 'power ring'. That ring is the system of high-voltage lines and 330 kV substations linking the capital to the national grid from several directions, with the 750 kV Kyivska substation as a key node through which power from the Rivne and Khmelnytskyi nuclear plants reaches the city. Kyiv normally uses 1-1.2 GW (12-15% of national demand), rising to 1.8-2 GW in winter. Before the large-scale winter attacks of 2025, local generation from CHP-5, CHP-6 and Darnytsia CHP (CHP-4) supplied up to about 1.3 GW, according to an energy-company source. Those plants can no longer generate electricity, yet all three were hit again on the night of 13-14 October. The article explains the tactical logic. Russia does not need to destroy the whole ring: damaging a few critical nodes that carry a large share of the flow is enough. Power may then exist in the system but cannot be delivered to Kyiv in sufficient volume, forcing dispatchers into emergency or scheduled cuts. The ring's key components have been under constant attack for more than a month. Repairs are slowed by repeat strikes, long air-raid alerts, risks to repair crews, and government plans to cut the share of energy workers exempt from mobilisation. Replacing a large transformer takes much longer if no spare is held in reserve. The article does not give numbers of missiles or drones per strike. It stresses that protection of these sites against ballistic missiles depends mainly on air defence, that the engineering protection built at most substations cannot guarantee equipment survives a direct hit, and that Patriot interceptors are in short supply. The article sets out three winter scenarios. If repairs keep pace and new strikes cause no critical losses, outages would be about 8-10 hours a day. With continued attacks and lingering damage during moderate cold, they would be 12-16 hours. The worst case, regular large-scale strikes on key substations during severe frost, would mean up to 20 hours a day. Energy-sector sources nevertheless do not expect Russia to 'plunge Kyiv into darkness' entirely. Critical infrastructure (water pumps, heating, transport, hospitals, communications) needs about 200 MW. According to Ekonomichna Pravda's sources, 150 MW of distributed generation is already running, rising to 180-185 MW by the end of 2026, and private plants could add another 55-60 MW before winter, about half of which are ready. The scenarios are the outlet's assessment based on unnamed energy-sector sources.

Sources: Ukrainska Pravda (EN)

War on the Rocks: deception against automated sensing and decision chains, drawing on Russo-Ukrainian combat lessons

2026-10-23

In an analysis for War on the Rocks, Édouard R. Maurin (call sign FIDES) argues that concealment alone no longer protects manoeuvre on a battlefield made transparent by sensors and drones. Maurin is a French veteran who served in 2024–2025 with an international volunteer section attached to a Ukrainian special-purpose battalion near Pokrovsk. He also runs Fog Defence, a company that develops decoys, which is a commercial interest in the subject. His argument is that deception should target the whole automated chain of sensing, sensor fusion, classification and decision, not only the human commander. He wants it judged on profitability: does the adversary spend more engaging a decoy than the decoy costs? He also wants it nested from section to corps level under one signature-management system. He adds that US doctrine (JP 3-13.4, FM 3-13.4) still assumes a human decision-maker. Most of the evidence comes from Russia and Ukraine. Maurin describes a fake radio call at Kotlyne on 5 January 2025, transmitted while Russian forces were listening, after which two soldiers left a bunker about two minutes later; he watched this himself through a drone's thermal camera. He also mentions feigned holding of terrain at Pokrovsk while survivors were evacuated. On the Russian side he cites Gerbera foam and wooden Parodiya decoy drones fitted with Luneburg lenses to mimic the radar signature of a Shahed/Geran. According to media reports, these decoys make up about half of the swarms, and Alabuga reportedly keeps their cost below $10,000. He contrasts Western interceptors costing hundreds of thousands to millions of dollars with Ukraine's Sting interceptor (about $2,500). He also quotes Unmanned Systems Forces commander Robert 'Magyar' Brovdi, who puts a mass-produced kamikaze drone at about $400. The article also describes Operation Vivaldi (September 2026). There, Ukraine's Third Army Corps first degraded Russian EW and drone-pilot teams and then used feints and fake signatures to retake the Nove settlement and its heights. According to deputy commander Maksym Zhorin, an EW 'protective dome' disrupted or suppressed about 20,000 Russian drones. Corps commander Andriy Biletsky called deception a key factor in the success. Maurin recommends company-level signature fleets, battalion-level signature management cells, and procurement of cheap active decoys. He states explicitly that the effect on automated classifiers is his hypothesis and has not been observed. Much of the Russo-Ukrainian evidence rests on media and unit reports and has not been independently verified.
