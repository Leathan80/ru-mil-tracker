««Очі» для приватної ППО: Генштаб спростив правила використання радарів і РЕБ» (ArmyInform: ''Eyes' for private air defence: General Staff simplifies rules for using radars and EW')

2026-10-30SINGLE SOURCE

Ukraine's General Staff has approved a new procedure for the use of radars, electronic warfare (EW) equipment and other gear by the air-defence groups that Ukrainian companies may set up to protect themselves against Russian air attacks. Applications to use such equipment, and requests for radio-frequency assignments, will now be processed within five working days. Once approved, companies receive the status of special users of the radio spectrum, allowing them to operate the equipment under the rules that apply to the military. According to the article, the private groups do not act independently: they work with the Ukrainian Air Force, are connected to the unified air-threat detection system, and the military command decides whether a target may be engaged. Only certified equipment listed in official registers may be used, and groups must report on their results. ArmyInform recalls that as of 14 October almost 100 companies had joined the project, forming about 50 groups, six of which had flown combat tasks and destroyed more than 60 Russian aerial targets. The experimental project began in November 2025; in March 2026 the government allowed companies to receive surplus air-defence weapons and ammunition from the military. The scheme reflects the scale of the Russian drone threat to Ukrainian industrial sites. Single source: ArmyInform is a Ukrainian (MoD/defence) outlet; claims are not independently verified.

Ukraine's 3rd Assault Brigade says it destroyed a Russian Mi-8 helicopter in occupied Luhansk Oblast with two US-made Hornet one-way attack drones after it landed

2026-10-30

Ukraine's 3rd Separate Assault Brigade says a drone crew from the SKYFOM strike-wing platoon of its unmanned systems battalion destroyed a Russian Mi-8 transport helicopter in occupied Luhansk Oblast. The crew spotted the helicopter during a raid on Russian supply lines and launched a Hornet drone to intercept it in the air. According to the brigade, the Russian crew changed course and the helicopter outran the drone, since its cruising speed was higher. Once it landed, a Hornet struck it near the fuel tanks and a second Hornet finished it off. The brigade says the helicopter burned out completely. Sources told Ukrainska Pravda the strike happened on 30 October. The brigade did not say exactly where in the region it took place. Russia has not commented, and the claim has not been independently verified beyond the brigade's video. The Hornet is a fixed-wing one-way attack drone built by the US company Perennial Autonomy. Ukrainian units use it against Russian supply routes deep behind the front. Defence Blog reports that 1st Azov Corps has flown it more than 50 km beyond the front line. The case fits a wider pattern. Ukrainian drone units increasingly target Russian helicopters, but usually catch them on the ground or while hovering, because most strike drones cannot match a helicopter in level flight. Earlier cases include a Ka-52 and a Mi-8 destroyed at a forward airfield in Oryol region (February), a Mi-28 and a Mi-8 hit at an airstrip in Voronezh region more than 150 km from the front (April), a Mi-28 that the Rarog unit says it brought down (July), and a Mi-8AMTSh destroyed at a helipad near Anapa (September, according to Russian sources).

Sources: Defence Blog · Ukrainska Pravda (EN)

It argues that the priority is to keep up the tempo of strikes and steadily weaken the bridges so they become harder to repair. The existence and effect of the cutting-munition Geran variants are not independently confirmed. Zelensky's drone adviser says Russia's jet-powered Geran-5 has reached 1,000 km/h while maneuvering and that Chinese engineers help develop Russia's Geran drones

2026-10-30

Serhii "Flash" Beskrestnov, Ukraine's presidential adviser on defence technology, told journalists in Kyiv on 29 October that Russia's jet-powered Geran-5 drones have reached about 1,000 km/h during maneuvers, Euromaidan Press reports. Ukraine's military intelligence (HUR) puts the Geran-5's cruise speed at 450–600 km/h, while according to Beskrestnov the earlier Geran-3 flew at 280–300 km/h. He also said Chinese engineers help develop Russia's Geran drones: the Alabuga drone plant reportedly gives them tasks such as work on the control module, and key components in the Shahed-type drones are Chinese. Ukraine's sanctions commissioner Vladyslav Vlasiuk added that China will require licences for Geran engines from 18 November, stressing that licensing is not a ban. These claims come from Ukrainian officials and have not been independently confirmed. The faster drones are a growing problem for Ukrainian air defence. From 1 to 26 September only 55% of Russia's jet-powered drones were shot down; on 27 October the General Staff said nearly 60% of jet-powered Geran-4s had been downed over the past three months and pointed to a shortage of guided anti-aircraft missiles (no Geran-5 figure was given). On 29 October a jet-powered drone hit Dnipro railway station; the death toll has since risen to 15. According to Beskrestnov, Ukrainian firms have already shown jet-powered interceptor drones but are struggling with control, guidance, automatic target lock and scaling up production. Some foreign interceptors are being designed for the Geran-5's cruise speed, such as the Vespid Mk 1 from US firm CHAOS Industries, with a claimed top speed of 540 km/h.
