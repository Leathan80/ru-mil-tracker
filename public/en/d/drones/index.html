2026-11-13SINGLE SOURCE

The Kazan-based paper Business Online, in its weekly review of the war, quotes an article from the September 2026 issue of the Russian General Staff journal Voyennaya Mysl (Military Thought) by V. Kavtarov and S. Sakhanov. The authors argue that linear formations have become extremely vulnerable now that enemy drones dominate the battlefield, and propose a 'qualitatively new' defensive method they call the 'dispersed echeloned bastion': abandoning a continuous front line in favour of a network of autonomous combat modules ('bastions') spread across the front and in depth. Each bastion is built around mobile groups equipped with uncrewed ground vehicles, FPV interceptor drones and EW assets, tied together by a single situational-awareness picture from Zala and Orlan reconnaissance drones. An attacker who wedges into such a defence does not break through it, the authors write, but comes under crossfire from prepared, concealed and frequently changing module positions. The paper frames this against the current front: deputy defence minister Colonel General Valery Solodchuk has said the 'kill zone' has grown to about 30 km, within which supplies are delivered only by drones and robots and rations have been made lighter with dehydrators and vacuum packers. Business Online concludes that rapid advances inside that 30 km belt are impossible under current conditions and expects Russian forces to shift to new ways of sustaining the front-line zone this winter. In the same review, citing the Telegram channel Voyennaya Khronika and the US think tank FPRI, the paper says Russia's drone units are moving from specialised, highly autonomous formations towards established structures integrated into the combined-arms armies, with unmanned systems brigades and more regiment- and battalion-level drone units. Czechia discusses up to 16 L-39NG Skyfox jets for Ukraine as low-cost hunters of Russian drones

2026-11-12

Czechia could supply Ukraine with up to 16 Aero L-39NG Skyfox light jets, The War Zone reports. Four were previously agreed for transfer and twelve more are under discussion. Czech Deputy Prime Minister and Industry and Trade Minister Karel Havlíček raised the expanded deal on a visit to Kyiv on 11 November, where he met Ukrainian Prime Minister Serhiy Koretskyi. He called the talks encouraging, but no agreement on the additional aircraft has been finalised. Ukraine had earlier sought Czech L-159 ALCA light combat jets; President Petr Pavel supported transferring up to four, but Prime Minister Andrej Babiš's government refused, saying the Czech military needed them. The intended role builds on Aero Vodochody's 'Drone Hunter' concept: an electro-optical targeting system plus launchers for 70 mm laser-guided rockets, developed with Thales Belgium, with gun pods as an option. This configuration is not yet in operational service. The Skyfox is a modernised L-39 with a US Williams FJ44-4M turbofan, a glass cockpit and five hardpoints. TWZ gives a top speed of about 560 mph (about 900 km/h) and a ceiling of 38,000 ft. Aero Vodochody puts operating costs at about $1,700–2,300 per flight hour, against about $22,000 for an F-16-class fighter. A 70 mm guided rocket of the APKWS II type costs roughly $20,000–25,000, against about $1 million for an AIM-120 and about $500,000 for an AIM-9X. The jet is meant to take pressure off Ukraine's F-16s, MiG-29s, Su-27s and Mirages, more than half of whose F-16s were reportedly stuck in maintenance amid shortages of AIM-9 and AIM-120 missiles. It is aimed especially at Russia's jet-powered Geran-3/4/5 drones, with the Geran-4 reportedly reaching about 280–310 mph and 26,000 ft. Justin Bronk of RUSI called the L-39NG very cheap and simple to operate compared with frontline fighters, with speed and endurance suited to intercepting jet drones. His main caveat is the lack of an onboard radar, which Ukraine's ground radar network can partly offset. Many Ukrainian pilots and technicians already know the L-39. Aero Vodochody builds about 12 Skyfox a year (capacity 18), and its schedule is reportedly booked through Q2 2027, which could delay deliveries. Russian outlet Topwar reported the same Czech claims; its readers questioned the radar-less concept and the delivery backlog.
