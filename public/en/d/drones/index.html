Drones damage Mius bridge on R-280 road to occupied Crimea, closing the road; substation also hit in Rostov Oblast

2026-11-08

Drones damaged the bridge over the Mius River at kilometre 97 of the R-280 in Neklinovsky district, Rostov Oblast, on the morning of 8 November. Governor Yuri Slyusar said the attack and falling debris hit the bridge deck and two crawler cranes and caused no casualties. Traffic first moved in alternating directions; he later announced a full closure of the section. The same morning, Slyusar said, a drone damaged a transformer at the Sulin substation in Millerovsky district, cutting power to several villages. It is the only energy site in Rostov Oblast that the sources name. The R-280 links Rostov-on-Don with occupied Mariupol, Berdiansk, Melitopol and Crimea. According to Militarnyi, it is part of the Azov transport ring and Russian forces use it to move equipment, ammunition and fuel into occupied Ukrainian territory. Euromaidan Press writes that Russia's army in occupied southern Ukraine depends on this land route and puts the bridge about 155 km from the front line. Russia began widening the crossing to four lanes in 2025; the road agency Rosavtodor put the bridge's readiness at 50% in June. The Kyiv Independent describes the road as part of the so-called "highway of death" for military cargo and a target of Ukraine's "middle strike" campaign, launched in spring 2026. Militarnyi cites earlier effects elsewhere on the land route: truck restrictions in occupied Kherson in May and damaged bridges near Chonhar in June. The damage and closure details come from a Russian governor and have not been independently confirmed. The Ukrainian monitoring channel Exilenova-Plus published footage and said an FP-2 drone hit the target; the Kyiv Independent could not verify this. An OSINT researcher published photos of the damaged bridge and construction equipment. Euromaidan Press reported no comment from Ukraine. The sources do not say how severe the damage is, how long the closure will last, or what effect it will have on Russian supply.

Ukraine claims 16,130 Russian losses in one week, the highest weekly total of the war by Kyiv's count

2026-11-08

Ukraine's General Staff reports that Russia lost 16,130 troops from 1 to 7 November, which by Kyiv's count is the highest weekly total since the full-scale invasion began. This is an official Ukrainian claim and has not been independently verified. 'Losses' in this count means killed and wounded combined; deaths are not listed separately. The average is more than 2,300 a day, which the General Staff compares to one average Russian motorized rifle regiment per day. By the same count, the total since February 2022 stands at about 1,606,400 killed and wounded. For 1 November the General Staff gave 2,500 losses, the highest daily figure of the war; the previous record was 2,250 on 21 October. October was also a record month at 54,400 losses. German Defence Minister Pistorius gave a lower figure in an interview with the Berliner Morgenpost: 30,000 to 40,000 Russian soldiers a month. The Ukrainian military blames Russian commanders who keep sending infantry into frontal assaults. On 7 November the General Staff counted 199 combat engagements, with the heaviest pressure near Kostiantynivka (23 attacks); Ukrainian figures put Russian use that day at 7,557 kamikaze drones and 235 guided bombs. Robert Brovdi, commander of Ukraine's Unmanned Systems Forces, claims his drone operators killed or wounded 751 Russian troops on 5 November, a unit record, and reads this as an unprecedented buildup of Russian manpower at the front. That claim has not been independently confirmed either.

2026-11-07SINGLE SOURCE

Topwar (author Oleg Myndar, citing the U.S. Army as its source) reports that the US Army tested the FD-1 interceptor drone in Poland by launching it from the SGT Stout air-defence vehicle, which is built on the Stryker chassis. According to the outlet, the trials took place on 2–6 November at the Ustka range as part of the Audacious Fury test programme, and were carried out by a battery of the 5th Battalion, 4th Air Defense Artillery Regiment. The stated aim is a cheaper way to counter mass drone attacks while keeping air-defence missiles for more complex and expensive targets; besides the launches, the unit is said to be developing employment procedures, training and operator certification, with the programme expected to extend to other US units in Europe. The FD-1, developed by Mountain Horse Solutions, is described as a fast FPV quadcopter flown via its onboard camera. Topwar gives claimed figures of over 265 km/h in the terminal phase, a range of more than 20 km with an external antenna and about 6 km in the basic configuration, and launch preparation of under two minutes. A 'Pixel-Lock' function lets the operator mark a target, after which the drone continues its approach on the image without direct control; the outlet notes that intercept performance depends on target parameters, link stability and crew training. The SGT Stout (formerly M-SHORAD) already carries Stinger missiles, a 30 mm cannon, a machine gun and radar and electro-optical sensors; the FD-1 is being evaluated as an additional tool for its crew against small drones. Topwar adds that procurement is running in parallel with testing: on 8 October the US Army ordered a further 385 FD-1 drones, following an earlier contract for 300. Ukraine's defence forces struck two Russian ammunition storage points in occupied Zaporizhzhia Oblast, plus a drone control post and a fuel and lubricants depot in occupied Donetsk Oblast, on 5 November and overnight on 5–6 November, according to the General Staff of the Armed Forces of Ukraine, as reported by Ukrainska Pravda and the Kyiv Independent. According to the General Staff, the ammunition depots were near Melitopol and near the settlement of Khliborobne in Zaporizhzhia Oblast. A UAV ground control point of a Russian unit was hit near Chasiv Yar and a fuel and lubricants depot of a Russian unit near Luhanske, both in Donetsk Oblast. The General Staff said destroying ammunition storage sites reduces Russian forces' firepower and complicates logistical support for their units. These are claims by a party to the war; the General Staff did not say what weapons were used or give damage details, and the results have not been independently verified. The Kyiv Independent notes that Ukrainian forces regularly strike military targets in Russian-occupied territory.

Sources: Ukrainska Pravda · Kyiv Independent

Ukrainian drone attack sets off a large fire near the Afipsky oil refinery in Krasnodar Krai

2026-11-06

On the night of 6 November Ukrainian drones attacked Russia's Krasnodar Krai, setting off a large fire near the Afipsky oil refinery, Militarnyi reports, citing the monitoring resource Exilenova+. Local residents reported numerous explosions and air-defence activity, and said thick smoke from the industrial area reached the outskirts of Krasnodar; footage of the glow of the fire was posted online. Russian authorities' account of damage is not given in the report, and the extent of damage to the refinery has not been independently confirmed. The refinery lies in the settlement of Afipsky in Seversky district, about 20 km southwest of Krasnodar. According to Militarnyi it has a processing capacity of about 6.25 million tonnes of oil per year and produces diesel fuel, petrol components and other petroleum products. The Kyiv Independent adds that the refinery, one of the largest in the region, was placed under EU sanctions in April over its role in Russia's war against Ukraine. The Afipsky refinery has been targeted by Ukrainian drones several times before; Militarnyi notes a previous strike on 14 July 2026 that also caused a fire on the site.

Sources: Militarnyi

«Сили ППО перехопили за ніч 196 ворожих БПЛА, 70 з яких — реактивні» (ArmyInform: 'Air defence intercepted 196 enemy UAVs overnight, 70 of them jet-powered') — 86 of 227 attacking Shahed-type drones were jet-powered, per the Ukrainian Air Force

2026-11-06SINGLE SOURCE

According to the Ukrainian Air Force, as relayed by ArmyInform, Russia attacked Ukraine during the night of 6 November (from 18:00 on 5 November) with 227 Shahed-type strike drones, Gerbera drones and Parodiya decoy (imitation) drones. Of the Shahed-type drones, 86 were jet-powered. The attack was repelled by aviation, surface-to-air missile units, electronic warfare (EW) units, unmanned systems units and mobile fire groups. By preliminary data as of 08:00, air defence had shot down or suppressed 196 drones of the Shahed, Gerbera and other types, 70 of them jet-powered. The Air Force reported hits by Russian strike assets at 14 locations and falling debris at 8 locations, with about 15 strike drones still in the air and combat work continuing. The jet-powered share — 86 of 227 launched, and 70 of the 196 claimed downed — is notably high. Single source: ArmyInform is the news agency of the Ukrainian Ministry of Defence; the claims have not been independently verified.

Sources: ArmyInform

««Бандероль» на «Иноходце»: против разных целей» (Topwar: 'Banderol on the Inokhodets: against various targets') — Izvestia claims Inokhodets/Orion UAVs strike Ukrainian drone control points, with Banderol cruise missiles in the mix

2026-11-06SINGLE SOURCE

Topwar (author Kirill Ryabov) builds on a 5 November report by the Russian daily Izvestia, which cites unnamed sources familiar with the combat zone. According to Izvestia, heavy Inokhodets (export name Orion) reconnaissance-strike UAVs are being used against Ukraine's drone forces by hunting down the ground sites from which Ukrainian UAVs are based and controlled. As an example, it describes a recent episode in the area of the 'Centre' group of forces: scouts of a motor-rifle formation used an electro-optical station to spot antennas and cable runs of Ukrainian drone systems, located a control post in a concrete shelter, and passed the coordinates to a unit of the Unmanned Systems Forces. An Inokhodets crew then struck it with a guided missile of unspecified type; Izvestia's sources say the fortified post was put out of action together with its duty crew and its communications and control equipment. Topwar stresses that detection and strike were carried out by different units. The weapon used in that episode is not named, but Izvestia points to the use of Banderol air-launched cruise missiles, linking them to the Inokhodets' growing ability to hit distant and fortified targets; a dedicated concrete-piercing warhead for the Banderol is not confirmed. Per Izvestia, the Banderol has a warhead of up to 150 kg, a small turbojet, a speed of up to 650 km/h and a range of 500 km; Ukraine's HUR has estimated its length at about 5 m and wingspan at about 2.2 m. It is described as navigating by autopilot with satellite and inertial guidance against targets with known coordinates; whether terminal homing will be added is unknown. First reports of Banderol launches from the Inokhodets date from spring 2025, and in July 2026 Russian television showed the UAV being prepared for a sortie with a large munition that a serviceman called a 'Banderol'. The Mi-28N attack helicopter has also been named as a prospective carrier. For the platform, Topwar cites the published Orion-E export data: 8 m length, 16 m wingspan, 1,150 kg maximum take-off weight, 250 kg payload, 200 km/h cruise speed, 7,500 m ceiling, at least 24 hours endurance with a standard payload and 250 km line-of-sight radius, with an optional satellite link; the configuration of front-line aircraft may differ. Topwar notes that limited speed and a small munition load remain constraints. Single source: Topwar (Voennoye Obozreniye) is a pro-Kremlin Russian military news site, not an official state source; the claims have not been independently verified.

Ukraine's 34th Marine Brigade claims Russia moved more than 1,000 troops to the Kherson left bank during the Oleshky truce; Zelenskyy alleges a major rotation, and the brigade says its marines are advancing on the left bank

2026-11-06

Ukraine's 34th Separate Marine Brigade claims that Russia used the local truce around occupied Oleshky (five days from 27 October, for the evacuation of civilians) to move more than 1,000 troops to the left bank of the Dnipro in Kherson Oblast. Yaroslav Moroz, head of the brigade's unmanned systems section, told Krym.Realii (Radio Svoboda) that Ukrainian estimates point to several battalions, including several hundred infantrymen waiting in settlements for orders to assault; other personnel bring the total above 1,000. Russian forces are also said to have brought in weapons, artillery systems, UAVs, watercraft, electronic warfare systems and supplies from fuel to food, which the brigade estimates will last at least several months. President Zelenskyy said after a meeting with regional administration heads that the Russian army carried out a major rotation of personnel and equipment under cover of the humanitarian operation and brought additional forces into Oleshky and the surrounding community. He instructed the military leadership to propose ways of better protecting Kherson residents. According to Moroz, Ukrainian forces had expanded the kill zone before the truce, pushed Russian troops back and cut their logistics. He says Russian logistics now sit 20 kilometres closer to the line of contact and that many new UAV take-off and landing sites have appeared, which he expects to increase attacks on Kherson. He also alleges that Russia stored large amounts of equipment and ammunition in Oleshky hospital, knowing Ukraine would not strike a building with patients inside. As of early November the brigade had seen no mass assaults, only isolated movements; Moroz expects them in rain, fog and strong wind, when drones are hampered. Militarnyi reports that small infantry groups were already trying to cross the Dnipro in October, before the truce, and that the first explosions in Kherson came at 23:00, one minute after the truce expired. These are Ukrainian military and presidential claims; the 1,000 figure is one brigade's estimate and has not been independently confirmed. Kyiv Post reports that the Kremlin denies the allegations and accuses Ukraine of violating the truce. The same outlet writes that Sky News footage appeared to support the Ukrainian account and that Russian milbloggers acknowledged rotations and the movement of armoured vehicles, ammunition and fuel; ombudsman Lubinets said some of the filmed vehicles were used only for demining. Separately, Col. Alim Kerimov, chief of staff of the same brigade, said marines are advancing on several axes in the island area and on the left bank and rotating positions there. Militarnyi's headline says they have successfully landed in a settlement, but Kerimov's quoted words are vaguer ("we will be — let's put it this way — in a certain settlement on the left bank") and he gave neither the location nor the depth of the advance. This too is unconfirmed.

Sources: Kyiv Post · Militarnyi · Militarnyi · Ukrainska Pravda (EN)
