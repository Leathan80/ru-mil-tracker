Ukrainian Mangal active protection system, which automatically fires nets at incoming FPV drones, is heading for serial production

2026-11-05

Ukrainian company Phantom Defense is preparing Mangal for serial production. Mangal is an active protection system that automatically detects FPV drones and catches them with a net as they approach military vehicles. A Militarnyi journalist watched a demonstration at a test range. Mangal grew out of the Karakurt net launchers, which were first mounted on drones to intercept other drones; Euromaidan Press notes that Phantom Defense bought Karakurt's developer in October. According to the developer, the 100th Separate Mechanized Brigade was the first to put a manually fired net launcher on a ground robot, and operator-fired versions have been at the front since March 2026. Each module covers a sector of about 90 degrees, so several are needed for all-round coverage; eight were fitted to a Stryker in one test. A module contains its own microcomputer, a control unit, day and thermal cameras, and net launchers. It computes the drone's trajectory and chooses when to fire and which launcher to use. Developer figures: detection at about 40 m against trees or cluttered terrain and over 60 m against the sky, reaction time up to 0.2 seconds, and roughly UAH 2,000 per shot (about $44 according to Euromaidan Press), including a 6x6 m net and pyrotechnic cartridge.
