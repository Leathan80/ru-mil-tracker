В режиме свободной охоты (Krasnaya Zvezda: In free-hunt mode)

2026-10-08SINGLE SOURCE

Krasnaya Zvezda profiles 'Altai', deputy commander of the FPV drone group of the 58th Separate Guards Special Purpose Battalion, which now fights on the Druzhkivka axis. The article opens by saying that the main weight of the fighting has shifted from the front edge to the near rear, where drones dominate and target the opposing sides' lines of communication; the line of contact has become almost a demarcation line held by small forward groups. It describes the workflow: a technician launches the FPV drone from a concealed spot and leaves, while the operator flies it from a camouflaged dugout far from the launch site. In one case Ukrainian EW replaced his video feed with an image (normally, he says, Ukrainian jammers show white noise). According to 'Altai', a 2014 volunteer who started with drones in 2018, the battalion's FPV group was formed from 2024 onwards. That meant building a workshop and finding skilled technicians, on whom 'the main part of drone work rests'. The group fought at Bakhmut (Artyomovsk) towards Chasiv Yar, also supplying forward units by air, and in 2025 on the Kostiantynivka axis. It now strikes vehicles, infantry and Ukrainian unmanned ground vehicles in and around Druzhkivka. Its tactics: strikes on pre-identified targets, 'free hunting', and landing drones in ambush. Depending on conditions it flies radio-controlled or fibre-optic FPVs, and repairing broken cables or antennas can take half a day. He describes flying a drone under an anti-drone net over a road and aborting an attack at the last moment after spotting an 'Evacuation' sign on a minibus carrying civilians. In earlier years his quadcopter corrected SPG-9 and AGS-17 fire. Single source: this is a report from Krasnaya Zvezda, the newspaper of the Russian Ministry of Defence, that has not been independently verified. The claim about the territorial situation around Kostiantynivka and Druzhkivka reflects the Russian MoD's own account.

Точные попадания «Егеря» (Krasnaya Zvezda: 'Yeger's' precise hits)

2026-10-08SINGLE SOURCE

Krasnaya Zvezda profiles Lieutenant Dmitry, callsign 'Yeger' ('Huntsman'), commander of the mortar platoon of the 'Shtorm' assault detachment of the Northern Fleet's separate Guards Kirkenes naval infantry brigade (based at Sputnik), now fighting with the 'Tsentr' (Centre) grouping on the Dobropillia axis. According to the article, the platoon previously supported assaults on the Kherson axis, near Krynky and the Antonivskyi bridge. A graduate of the Mikhailovsky Military Artillery Academy, he says the month-long lieutenants' course at the brigade, run by instructors with combat experience, covered much that the academy lacked: extensive shotgun shooting at aerial targets, and updated artillery tactics, radio communications and tactical medicine. The tactical points the article makes: placing several guns side by side at standard intervals is no longer possible because of losses, so work at the line of contact is done 'strictly gun by gun', with the emphasis on mobility and accuracy. Firing positions are first scouted from the air and then on the ground. The mortars operate only 3-4 km from the Ukrainian forward defence line; since no vehicle (not even a quad bike) can get that close unseen, crews carry the disassembled mortar (over 200 kg) and its ammunition in by hand. Gun pads, ammunition store and counter-battery shelter are built well apart so that one hit does not knock out the rest. Fire is observed and corrected through a quadcopter's live video feed, and after the initial fire mission the platoon fires on targets passed by the assault groups. The officer was wounded the previous autumn when his motorcycle set off a magnetic-fuzed mine scattered a few hours earlier by a Ukrainian 'Baba Yaga' heavy drone on a trail he was scouting. Single source: this is a report from Krasnaya Zvezda, the newspaper of the Russian Ministry of Defence, that has not been independently verified. Much of the piece is a heroic portrait; the claims about effectiveness are unverified.

Снаряд в самое сердце вражеской разведки (Krasnaya Zvezda: A shell into the very heart of enemy reconnaissance)

2026-10-08SINGLE SOURCE

Krasnaya Zvezda reports that a 152 mm 2S19 Msta-S self-propelled howitzer crew of the 275th Self-Propelled Artillery Regiment (4th Guards Tank Division, 1st Guards Tank Army, 'Zapad' grouping), commanded by a soldier with callsign 'Penza', destroyed a Ukrainian UAV control point in a treeline with its first shot. The crew then immediately changed position, moved the vehicle into cover and camouflaged it, and replenished ammunition. The article calls this shoot-and-relocate pattern 'a vital necessity' given the density of Ukrainian reconnaissance and strike assets over the battlefield. The gun commander highlights the Msta-S's mobility and range as letting the crew reach the enemy at distances it considers safe. No location beyond the 'Zapad' grouping is given. Ukrainian firm Phantom Defense says its counter-drone EW systems were tested at Fort Carson in the Pentagon's Drone Dominance programme

2026-10-08

Ukrainian defence tech company Phantom Defense says its electronic warfare (EW) systems were tested in the United States during the second stage ("Gauntlet II") of the Pentagon's Drone Dominance programme, at Fort Carson in Colorado. According to the company's press service, speaking to Ukrainska Pravda, Phantom Defense was one of three manufacturers engaged by the Pentagon to build a counter-UAV environment against which the competing drones were tested. The company deployed its Specter PRO dome and directional EW system, intended to protect strongpoints, brigade headquarters and critical infrastructure, plus its compact Specter L, X and XL systems. It also demonstrated Streamhunter, a tool that intercepts drone video feeds. All elements were tied into a C2 system for detection, classification, engagement and confirmation of suppression. Phantom Defense says its systems suppressed the tested drones, worked in various weather conditions and recreated the EW environment drone operators face on the front line; it says it has been invited to further Drone Dominance events. These claims come from the company itself. According to the report, the Drone Dominance programme has a budget of US$1.1 billion and tested solutions from 25 manufacturers in its initial phase; after the first phase around 30,000 drones are to be bought from 11 companies for US$150 million. The US military earlier placed an additional order for 1,320 F10 FPV drones from Ukrainian firm F-Drones under the same programme. The item is relevant mainly as an indicator that Ukrainian counter-drone EW, developed against Russian drones, is being used to reproduce front-line conditions in US testing.

Dutch firm Intelic's AI-based Nexus C2 software links multi-vendor sensors and interceptor drones, now being rolled out with Ukrainian brigades

2026-10-08

Defense News, carrying a Reuters report, describes Amsterdam-based software company Intelic and its Nexus command-and-control system, which links radars, sensors, cameras and interceptor drones from different vendors. Intelic has a €30 million (about $33.5 million) contract with the Dutch Defence Ministry to upgrade air defences. In a test in late September at a disused Dutch airbase, Nexus used radar and AI to detect and verify a mock target, a reconnaissance drone confirmed it with image recognition, and an interceptor drone was launched. A human made the final launch decision, although the process could run fully autonomously. Intelic has an office in Kyiv and says Nexus is being rolled out on the Ukrainian battlefield, supplementing Ukraine's own battlefield management system Delta. According to the company, Ukrainian brigades often field drones from different vendors and Nexus lets them connect these in one system; an Intelic engineer called it the "connecting tissue" between drone systems. The report puts this in the context of Russian air attacks: it says Russia uses high-altitude, jet-propelled attack drones and, citing Ukrainian Prime Minister Sergii Koretskyi, that in the first three weeks of September Russia launched 2,100 such drones and 136 missiles at Ukraine, killing 288 people. It also cites Kiel Institute figures of about €1.4 billion in Dutch funding for Ukraine's drone programme since 2022, total Dutch military aid above €12.5 billion including nearly 1 million drones, and European drone aid rising from €400 million (2022) to €1.2 billion (2025) and €1.6 billion in the first four months of 2026.

Ukraine strikes Russia's largest oil refinery at Omsk for the second time, using upgraded FP-1 drones flying over 2,500 km

2026-10-08

Ukraine's General Staff confirmed that Ukrainian forces struck the Omsk oil refinery in south-western Siberia on the afternoon of 8 October. The plant, owned by Gazprom (Gazprom Neft), is Russia's largest refinery, with an annual crude processing capacity of more than 21 million tonnes and, according to the General Staff, a refining depth of about 99%. It produces fuels, lubricants and petrochemical products and, Kyiv says, supplies the Russian military. Omsk lies roughly 2,500 km from Ukrainian-controlled territory. The General Staff presented the strike as self-defence under Article 51 of the UN Charter, aimed at reducing Russia's military-economic potential. Ukrainian drone maker Fire Point said the attack was carried out with upgraded versions of its FP-1 long-range attack drones, which flew more than 2,500 km to the target; it reported that "a hit on the target was recorded, followed by a fire". The independent Russian outlet Meduza, citing Astra, reported a column of black smoke over the plant, and Ukrainian monitoring channels shared local videos of explosions and fire around the refinery. Omsk Oblast governor Vitaly Khotsenko confirmed a drone attack on the region, saying one drone "reached the northern industrial hub" and the others were shot down, but he did not name the refinery or give damage details. The extent of the damage has not been independently established. This was the second Ukrainian strike on the Omsk refinery. On 6 July 2026 an attack hit the ELOU-AVT-11 primary distillation unit (design capacity 8.4 million tonnes per year), according to the General Staff; sources told Reuters that a crude distillation unit accounting for 38% of the plant's capacity was damaged, and the refinery temporarily halted operations.

Drone strike shuts down Yandex data center on grounds of sanctioned Sasta machine-tool plant in Sasovo (Ryazan region)

2026-10-08

Overnight on 8 October 2026 a drone attack caused a fire in Sasovo, Ryazan region (about 580 km from Ukraine), hitting the grounds of the Sasta machine-tool plant, where Yandex has occupied part of the former premises since 2014. Yandex said the Sasovo data center had completely stopped operating; there were no casualties. According to Meduza, it houses two of Yandex's three neural-network supercomputers, "Lyapunov" and "Chervonenkis" (the company's most powerful). Outages were reported for Yandex Cloud, Disk, Documents and Mail and for customers such as Cian, Avito, MegaFon, T-Bank and Russian Railways; Yandex shares fell more than 3%. Governor Pavel Malkov claimed two drones were downed and an enterprise roof caught fire. Ukraine has not officially claimed the strike, and it is unclear whether the plant or the data center was the target. The military link runs through Sasta: per Euromaidan Press (citing Exilenova+, Militarnyi and Astra) it makes computer-controlled machine tools, including five-axis machining centers for precision parts, has supplied the Tactical Missiles Corporation (KTRV) and Roscosmos, is linked by Militarnyi to Iskander missile supply chains, and claims a place on Russia's defense-industry register. It has been sanctioned by the US (Nov 2023), UK (Feb 2024) and Ukraine (Apr 2025). The strike follows Russia's campaign since 23 September against Kyiv data centers of at least eight companies, which the Russian MoD called a "forced digital detox"; Meduza calls the Yandex strike a logical Ukrainian response. On vulnerability, Meduza notes that data centers' generators, redundant links and fire suppression proved defenseless against drones, except facilities in former Cold War shelters. Digital-rights expert Sarkis Darbinyan says a single regional strike cannot take down a major service because traffic reroutes; Russia has close to 200 data centers, with the main ones concentrated and well protected around Moscow. Strikes on regional facilities (Rostelecom, Sber, VK, Selectel) would have local effects for hours to a day, while hitting the MSK-IX exchange could slow the internet nationwide temporarily without shutting it down.

China's military is preparing for Ukraine-style drone war

2026-10-08

Defence Blog reports on a CSIS commentary published on 7 October by Lilly Min-Chen Lee (Taiwan's Research Institute for Democracy, Society and Emerging Technology) and Kateryna Bondar (CSIS Wadhwani AI Center), based on more than 90 Chinese-language sources from 45 outlets (July 2022 – August 2026), including PLA Daily and journals of China's state missile, shipbuilding and aviation conglomerates.
