Ukrenergo says Russia has shifted from pinpoint strikes to 'carpet' cluster-warhead attacks on secondary substation equipment to drag out repairs, while Zelensky calls Russian tactics 'unchanged'

2026-11-25

Speaking at the European Business Association's Energy Day 2026 on 24 November, Ukrenergo board member Ivan Yuryk said Russia has changed how it attacks Ukraine's transmission grid. According to Yuryk, Russia is moving away from pinpoint attacks on main equipment towards 'carpet' strikes over large areas, often with cluster-warhead missiles that burst over substation grounds to damage as much equipment as possible. Without repeat strikes, he said, crews can repair much of the damage within two to three hours; the new method aims to lengthen restoration. Large autotransformers are now fairly well protected, so the strikes target secondary equipment such as switches, relays, and current and voltage transformers, which take a long time to replace. Yuryk named two bottlenecks: few manufacturers in Ukraine or abroad make high-voltage equipment, so orders take a long time to arrive, and Ukraine lacks contractors for physically protecting and restoring energy sites. Ukrenergo has received more than €1 billion in donor aid since early 2022, mainly spent on its emergency equipment reserve. Euromaidan Press notes that cluster-warhead missiles have hit substations since at least August 2024, and that researchers found a cluster warhead on downed Kalibr cruise missiles for the first time in spring 2026. Scheduled outages already run four to 18 hours a day in parts of Ukraine. In his evening address on 25 November, President Zelensky put it differently: 'The Russians' tactics are unchanged: they are striking power lines, grids and substations specifically to complicate the transmission of electricity.' He named Dnipro, Dnipropetrovsk Oblast and Zaporizhzhia as the hardest-hit areas and said restoration equipment is available everywhere. The two statements pull against each other on equipment availability, but are not strictly incompatible: Ukrenergo describes a change in method, Zelensky continuity in targets and purpose. Both are claims by Ukrainian officials and have not been independently verified.

Germany plans first Bundeswehr drone regiment from 2027, with Ukrainian systems including the Flamingo cruise missile in its deep-strike arsenal

2026-11-25

Internal German government documents obtained by POLITICO, and reported by Militarnyi, show the German Army plans to begin forming the Bundeswehr's first dedicated drone regiment in 2027. It would start with about 350 personnel and grow to about 600 by 2029. The planned structure has six companies: one for reconnaissance and strikes close to German forces, one for protection against enemy drones, two operating deeper behind enemy lines, a Deep Strike company for the most distant targets, and an in-house unit that tests new technology and brings it into service quickly. Berlin estimates that buying thousands of drones for the regiment will cost about €150 million. The German Army would not confirm the details; it said the final structure is still being worked out and that the unit will keep adapting to changes on the battlefield. The planned arsenal mixes Ukrainian, German and US systems; according to the documents, seven of the nine are already in operational use in Ukraine. For close range: the German Quantum Systems Vector reconnaissance UAV, the Bumblebee small strike drone, the Termit unmanned ground vehicle with a heavy machine gun, and the Merops counter-drone system with an interceptor drone, originally developed for Ukraine against Shaheds. For greater depth: the Ukrainian Raybird reconnaissance UAV and the Hornet strike drone, of which Militarnyi says Ukraine used more than 10,000 in strikes in three months this summer. Bumblebee, Merops and Hornet are made by the US company Perennial Autonomy. The Deep Strike company would get an unnamed long-range reconnaissance UAV with a reported range of up to 2,000 km and Fire Point's Ukrainian Flamingo (FP-5) cruise missile, which the German document credits with a range of more than 2,000 km. Defence Minister Boris Pistorius said supporting Ukraine "has long ceased to be a one-way street", because the Bundeswehr is drawing on Ukraine's experience with unmanned systems. In ArmyInform (Ukraine's Defence Ministry outlet), analyst Valentyn Badrak links the regiment to new agreements: in Berlin, Ukrainian Defence Minister Yevhenii Khmara and Pistorius signed an implementation agreement for a joint defence-production project giving Ukrainian manufacturers access to German software and the Bundeswehr access to combat-tested Ukrainian systems, and Germany is the first partner country to get access to Ukraine's DELTA battle-management ecosystem. Badrak's view that Ukraine is being integrated into Europe's defence system is his own commentary, and the equipment list comes from internal documents that the Bundeswehr has not confirmed.
