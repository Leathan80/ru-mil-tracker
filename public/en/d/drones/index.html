A new software version also allows remote monitoring and partial control of any number of Pluton systems over a dedicated VPN. Mesh modems are one of the most notable recent upgrades to Russian drones: each drone acts as both receiver and relay, which extends control range, lets operators correct a drone's flight path almost until impact, and makes jamming much harder because the network reroutes when a node drops out. Euromaidan Press notes that a jam-resistant link still emits and can therefore be detected. Pluton scans 500 to 6,000 MHz (extendable to 24 GHz according to Militarnyi), already detects FPV, Supercam, Zala, Lancet and Orlan drones, estimates distance and time of arrival at a protected site, and can work with the Plastun-RP-3000M direction finder. Digital FPV video detection and control of counter-UAS assets are planned for the next update. All performance figures are developer claims; it is not stated whether the 70 km range applies specifically to mesh modems, and there is no independent test data or information on deployment numbers.

War on the Rocks analysis by ex-PM Honcharuk: Ukraine's Unmanned Systems Forces compete in a 'Time Domain' of adaptation speed, a model Russia has copied

2026-11-10

In an opinion-driven analysis for War on the Rocks, Oleksiy Honcharuk (Ukraine's prime minister in 2019-2020, former adviser to the first commander of the Unmanned Systems Forces, chair of the Aerorozvidka supervisory board and, since 2025, chairman of the defence-tech firm UFORCE) argues that the decisive dimension of the war is how fast each side adapts. He calls this the 'Time Domain': a contest over the interval between a new capability reaching the front and the enemy's working counter, which can now shrink to weeks. Ukraine created the Unmanned Systems Forces against opposition from career generals; he states that with about 2.5% of personnel the branch accounted for roughly a third of Russian losses in the past year. On Russia, Honcharuk credits it with being first to field jam-resistant fibre-optic drones at scale, around Kursk, through Rubicon, a defence-ministry centre that links developers, staff officers and operators in one loop; that lead lasted months, until Ukraine had more than 20 certified fibre-optic models from 11 producers by April 2025. He writes that Putin ordered a dedicated branch in December 2024 and that Russia's Unmanned Systems Forces were operational by late 2025, which he reads as Moscow copying a form that works. He presents Ukraine's 412th Brigade 'Nemesis' and Lasar's Group as 'Combat Hubs' that fuse combat, analysis, R&D and production on daily, six-weekly and six-monthly cycles; the Nemesis figures (over 520 targets destroyed for 107 airframes lost in seven months) come from the project's own records, not public data. He urges Western militaries to track adaptation time in planning, buy adaptation cycles rather than platforms, train against unpredictable opposition and create a small dedicated unmanned branch, and suggests a visible adaptation capacity could deter, while warning that a rapidly closing window can also provoke an aggressor.
