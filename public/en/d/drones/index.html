«БПЛА «Молния-2» стал носителем лёгких коптеров-камикадзе» (Topwar: 'The Molniya-2 UAV has become a carrier of light kamikaze copters') — Russian fixed-wing strike drone used as a mothership for one or two FPV drones, with field-modified delivery out to a claimed 70 km

2026-11-19SINGLE SOURCE

Topwar (Voennoye Obozreniye) pulls together earlier Russian reports on the Molniya-2 fixed-wing strike drone used as a carrier for smaller FPV drones. The site notes the configuration is more than a year old: a Molniya-2 with two FPV copters under its wings was shown at the Dronnitsa-2025 event. On 15 September 2026 the TV channel Zvezda reported Centre group troops using a fixed-wing UAV plus light copter, with both able to strike on their own. On 21 September RIA Novosti quoted a deputy battalion commander of the 49th Combined Arms Army's unmanned systems unit (Dnepr group), call sign 'Donskoy', as saying a field-modified Molniya delivered an FPV drone out to 70 km; Topwar stresses this is a single combat case, not a new standard range. On 22 September the Russian MoD reported a Molniya-2 with an extra FPV drone on the Dnipropetrovsk axis: the crew made sequential strikes with both drones and used the copter to watch the effect. On 16 November Izvestia described the tactic: the Molniya-2 brings the FPV drone to the target area, then one drone strikes while the other keeps watching or attacks another target. Topwar gives these approximate published figures for the Molniya-2 (individual variants, may differ when modified or carrying external loads): wingspan about 1.5 m, take-off mass about 10 kg, payload up to 6 kg, speed about 80 km/h, range up to 60 km, endurance up to 40 minutes. The airframe uses metal tubes with plywood and foam parts, a straight wing and tail; unlike the single-motor Molniya-1, the Molniya-2 has two wing-mounted electric motors, a warhead, a forward camera and remote control. Both one-copter and two-copter layouts have been shown; it is unclear which is standard. The carried copter takes up part of the 6 kg payload, so the carrier cannot be assumed to keep a full-weight warhead of its own. How the copter is controlled after release (for example via relays) has not been disclosed. Topwar is cautious: there is no open data on how widespread the carrier version is, whether there is a standard set-up, or whether the two-drone scheme is cheaper per task; two drones do not mean two successful strikes, and many modifications are made by the troops themselves rather than as a serial system. Compared with the earlier ArmyInform entry (a Ukrainian brigade hitting a Molniya carrying two FPV drones, one reconnaissance and one strike), this piece adds the Russian-side sourcing, the claimed 70 km delivery case and the Molniya-2 specifications. Ukrainian lawmaker: Russia is testing a jet-powered Geran-6 that could reach 1,000 km/h, and missiles, not just interceptor drones, must be the answer

2026-11-18

Russia is running experimental trials of a jet-powered "Geran-6" attack drone that could reach 1,000 km/h and carry a large warhead, according to Ukrainian intelligence data relayed by Fedir Venislavsky, a member of the Verkhovna Rada's Committee on National Security, Defence and Intelligence, in an interview with RBC-Ukraine. Euromaidan Press reports the interview. At that speed the drone moves like a cruise missile. Venislavsky also said Russia is scaling up its jet-powered drones as fast as it can and that they already fly at 500 to 1,000 km/h. For comparison, a 2025 analysis by the Ukrainian defence-tech firm VGI-9 put the first Shaheds at around 200 kg and speeds just over 185 km/h. According to Ukrainian military intelligence, Russia produces up to 500 Geran-4 and Geran-5 drones a month. Venislavsky said speed is the core problem: a drone flying at about 800 km/h forces any interceptor to fly at least as fast, and destroying the target at such speeds is extremely hard. Ukraine still downs jet Gerans, including a Geran-5 clocked at 425 km/h, but only a share of them: about 60% of jet-powered Geran-4s, against more than 90% of the slower piston-engine Shaheds. He called air-defence missiles the most effective tool against such targets, whether fired from aircraft or from ground-based systems. Interceptor drones are showing good results, he added, but are not yet used at scale because the technology is so demanding. His answer is to strengthen air defence, build more surface-to-air and air-to-air missiles and work with partners on their supply. The Geran-6 claim comes from a Ukrainian lawmaker citing intelligence data. It has not been independently confirmed, and the article gives no detail on test dates, engine or range. Russian sources had already mentioned a Geran-6 (Topwar, October), and Ukrainian presidential adviser Serhii Beskrestnov said at the end of October that the Geran-5 had reached about 1,000 km/h while manoeuvring.

Quantum Wiy Industries unveils upgraded serial SPYS 'Shahed hunter' interceptor: ~500 km/h on trials, 500 g warhead, radar-cued auto-intercept

2026-11-16

Ukrainian company Quantum Wiy Industries, which makes the SPYS and Strila interceptors, has presented an updated serial version of its high-speed SPYS interceptor drone, a company representative told Militarnyi. The design was finalised and industrialised using feedback from combat crews who had tested the first prototypes against jet-powered targets. The main change is the aerodynamics: narrower wings, a reshaped nose section, redesigned air intakes and a relocated antenna that had caused significant drag. The airframe also changed from black to white plastic, because black drones lying in the summer sun heated up to around 80 °C. It is still 3D-printed so the design can be changed quickly. Take-off weight stays at 4.6 kg. The warhead grew from 300 g to 500 g and is now a codified, factory-made blast-fragmentation charge with a standard fuze, replacing improvised warheads that troops had fitted themselves. Its job is to damage the target's airframe so it loses speed, or to set off the target's own warhead. Range rose from 33 to 45 km. Stable top speed is above 450 km/h, and according to the company it reached up to 500 km/h on the test range. Stronger power electronics make the drone reusable: if it finds no target, it can return, recharge and fly again. New functions include automatic take-off and automatic routing to the target area. Integration with radars and the Sky Map system lets the onboard software calculate a likely intercept point from the target's speed and track, with no operator input during the cruise phase. Against satellite-navigation jamming, a radio-beacon system locates the drone by triangulation and keeps the ground-station antenna pointed at it. The company puts local content at 70–80%, including a Ukrainian thermal camera, and plans a future software update for automatic head-on target lock and interception. These performance figures are the manufacturer's own and have not been independently verified.
