Finland's Patria to Partner With Ukraine's General Cherry on Drone Production

2026-08-24SINGLE SOURCE

On 24 August 2026, Defense News reported that Finnish defense manufacturer Patria has signed a letter of intent with Ukrainian drone maker General Cherry to jointly develop unmanned systems and establish drone production in Finland. General Cherry, co-founded by Stanislav Hryshyn, makes one-way FPV strike drones and interceptor drones among other products, and says its systems are fielded by more than 200 Ukrainian military units. Patria is represented in the article by Mikko Leino, executive vice president for defense and weapon systems, who emphasized combining General Cherry's expertise in compact unmanned systems with Patria's technological and defense-industrial experience. No details on funding, production quantities, or a production start date were disclosed; Patria did not immediately respond to Defense News' questions about which drone models would be built. The deal fits a broader trend of European partnerships with Ukrainian drone manufacturers aimed at relocating or duplicating production capacity in Europe — Defense News notes similar German plans and UK-based initiatives involving Ukrainian firms Skyeton and Ukrspecsystems in the same context. The announcement fell on the same Independence Day as the UK's Storm Shadow/SCALP announcement and the EU funding decision, but concerns a different country (Finland), a different weapons segment (small FPV/interceptor drones rather than long-range missiles), and a standalone bilateral corporate initiative with no direct link to the Coalition of the Willing meeting. Given this substantive distance, it is treated here as a separate development.

The author frames the French companies as desperately "muscling into a niche they shouldn't be in" with flawed technology — a recurring Topwar theme that dismisses Western counter-drone innovations while implicitly asserting Russian expertise or superiority in drone warfare.
