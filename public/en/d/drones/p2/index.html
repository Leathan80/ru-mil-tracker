Bars Missile-Drone Struck Moscow First, Now Adapted to Shoot Down Jet Shaheds at 2,000 Units Per Month

2026-10-05SINGLE SOURCE

Defense Express looks at President Zelensky's statement that Ukraine's Bars missile-drone is being used as an interceptor against Russian jet-powered Shaheds. Until then, Bars was known as a strike weapon used against Moscow, with production reportedly running at about 2,000 units a month. The outlet stresses several times that its analysis is only preliminary assumptions, to be added to when more information becomes available. Defense Express notes that three warhead options have previously been declared for Bars: 22 kg, 60 kg and 105 kg. A smaller warhead gives more range and better manoeuvrability, while a larger one gives more destructive power at a shorter range. Defense Express argues that any judgement of cost-effectiveness, including how many Bars are needed per downed Geran, should also take into account the real and psychological damage that Russian jet drone strikes cause. The outlet lists several open questions: how mobile the Bars launchers are, and how operators get target designation to engage jet Shaheds. The article gives no new interception figures. Germany to set up its first drone regiment in 2027, modeled on Ukraine war lessons

2026-10-05

According to Defense News, German Defense Minister Boris Pistorius announced that Germany will set up its first dedicated drone regiment, with formation beginning in 2027. The unit would start with 300-350 troops and grow to as many as 600, with full operational capability targeted for 2029. It is to be organized in five to six companies with specialized functions. The regiment is to consolidate reconnaissance systems, deep-strike platforms (with ranges of up to 500 kilometers, subject to the availability of commercial equipment), unmanned ground vehicles, electronic warfare capabilities and counter-drone defenses, plus an internal trials company meant to speed up testing. It is to be equipped with loitering munitions that are expected to reach troops in 2027. The regiment will be a divisional asset of the 10th Panzer Division, Germany's high-readiness NATO 'Division 2025' formation, which also oversees Panzerbrigade 45 in Lithuania. German Army Chief Lt. Gen. Christian Freuding described a dual-track approach: dedicated formations alongside the institutionalization of drone use across light infantry, reconnaissance and armor units, starting at the end of this year. Officials credited 'direct operational exchanges with Ukrainian frontline units' for shaping live-fire trials of aerial drones and unmanned ground systems, with ground robotics getting equal emphasis. The article, as summarized, does not state a specific Russian threat rationale. Open questions include the permanent basing location and contracts for specific long-range platforms.

Bloomberg: NATO's SACEUR Grynkewich drafts a 'Theory of Victory' against a Russian attack on the eastern flank, built on low-cost drones, minimally crewed systems and AI targeting

2026-10-05

Bloomberg has obtained an unclassified version of a document titled 'Theory of Victory'. It was drafted by NATO's Supreme Allied Commander Europe, General Alexus Grynkewich, and sets out how the Alliance would quickly defeat a Russian attack on its eastern flank. Grynkewich is due to present it in Poland on 12 October. Officials and diplomats familiar with the matter say it is meant to explain how NATO would respond to an invasion of its territory. It should also show how NATO will combine conventional weapons with capabilities that have emerged since 2022, and push allies to invest more in defence and preparedness. The strategy stresses that NATO is defensive, that unintended provocations must be avoided and that hybrid threats require a collective response. Showing readiness is meant to convince an adversary that victory is impossible. If NATO is attacked anyway, it would try to stop the invasion with ground forces and other assets under the NATO Eastern Flank Deterrence Initiative. That concept relies on low-cost drones, minimally crewed systems and AI-powered targeting. Grynkewich's spokesperson, Colonel Martin O'Donnell, declined to discuss details. He said SACEUR's theory of victory is simply to maintain NATO's advantage, and that conditions must be set now to deter aggression and, if necessary, win if attacked. The details come from Bloomberg via European Pravda and Ukrainska Pravda; the document itself has not been published.

An interceptor apparently derived from the Bars missile-drone is covered in a companion entry. Ukraine is testing five interceptor-drone models and plans about 400 units of one of them by the end of October. Taken together, the announcements show Ukraine trying to build a cheaper, mass-producible interception layer below Patriot and IRIS-T, against jet-powered Shahed/Geran variants that conventional interceptor drones struggle to catch. Whether the small missiles and Nika work will only become clear after testing and deployment from late October.

Russian Mi-8 lost over Voronezh region, three crew killed; reportedly shot down by friendly fire during Ukrainian drone raid

2026-10-01

A Russian military Mi-8 helicopter crashed on 1 October in a field in Anninsky district, about 80 km from Voronezh and roughly 200 km from the Ukrainian border. All three crew were killed: the pilot, the navigator and the gunner. The pro-Kremlin Telegram channel Mash reported the crash and the deaths that evening but gave no cause. Russian officials have not given a cause or acknowledged friendly fire. The Telegram channel Dossier Shpiona, cited by Ukraine's UNITED24 Media, reported that a Russian Su-35 fighter shot the helicopter down while responding to a large Ukrainian drone attack, after its crew mistook the Mi-8 for an enemy drone. Defence Blog reported the same, citing unnamed sources, after first attributing the shot to a ground-based air defence crew, which shows how uncertain the cause still is. Ilya Tumanov of the milblogger channel Fighterbomber, known for his ties to Russian military aviation, hinted at friendly fire without saying so directly, calling the cause 'a classic one' that technology and 'friend or foe' systems are unlikely ever to remove. The friendly-fire account has not been confirmed independently or officially. If confirmed, the loss would add to a series of Russian helicopter losses to friendly fire during Ukrainian drone raids. Defence Blog lists a Ka-52 hit by a Verba MANPADS from a BARS reserve fire group in Voronezh region on 2 July, a probable shootdown near Millerovo on 3 March, a Ka-52M possibly downed by a Russian fighter in late 2025, and a Ka-29 shot down near Anapa in June 2024. Mi-8s are increasingly used to hunt Ukrainian long-range drones at low altitude, which puts them in the same airspace as fighters and ground air defences during raids.
