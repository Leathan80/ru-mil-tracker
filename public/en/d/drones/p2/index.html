Large-scale GPS jamming mapped along NATO's eastern flank, from the Baltic to the Black Sea

2026-11-04

Large-scale interference with GPS satellite navigation has been recorded along almost the entire eastern flank of NATO, from the Baltic Sea to the Black Sea, Militarnyi reports. The finding comes from the OSINT project ItaMilRadar, which published a map of navigation interference as of 3 November 2026 based on data from the AirNav Radar flight-tracking service. The heaviest interference was concentrated along NATO members' borders with Russia, Belarus and Ukraine: near Russia's Kaliningrad region, in Lithuania, eastern Poland, Romania and Bulgaria, and over the western Black Sea. Less intense interference was seen over Belarus, western Russia, occupied Crimea and the Caucasus. Citing Defence Blog, Militarnyi notes that the geographic pattern partly overlaps with areas crossed by the flight paths of Russian attack drones. According to Militarnyi, Russian EW systems in the Kaliningrad region conduct GPS jamming and spoofing that affect the routes of Ukrainian attack drones: fake navigation signals can make a drone miscalculate its position and stray from its planned route towards the Baltic states and Finland. The map is a snapshot built from aircraft navigation data; it does not by itself attribute each interference zone to a specific emitter. The report places the snapshot in a longer pattern of Russian navigation interference in the Baltic region. In May the Finnish Border Guard reported an increase in Russian GPS jamming in the Baltic, notably in the Archipelago Sea and the Åland Sea. In August Denmark recorded a record number and duration of satellite-navigation disruptions; the crew of the cargo vessel Saturn reported losing GPS for about 24 hours while sailing between Gdańsk and southern Gotland.

The decision follows a series of stray-drone incidents on NATO's northern and eastern flank. Between late March and late May, Finland, Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania reported more than a dozen drones crossing into their airspace, according to Reuters; Ukraine said Russian electronic warfare had diverted its long-range drones aimed at Russian Baltic oil ports. On 19 May a Romanian NATO fighter shot down a suspected Ukrainian drone over Estonia, and on 15 May Finland briefly closed Helsinki airport and told 1.8 million residents to stay indoors.

The complete system costs between US$5,000 and US$7,000 depending on the communications fit: either the company's own SkyLoki analogue radio link, described as jam-resistant, or a Starlink terminal with an LTE-modem control system. The company says it can be repaired in the field with a cordless drill, a screwdriver and a rivet gun. An air-defence configuration carries three RP7-Zenit interceptor drones (0.4 kg warhead, up to 250 km/h or 320 km/h in boost mode, 20 minutes endurance) linked to the SineLink system. As a bomber it can carry 15 kg out to 60 km and return; as a one-way attack drone with a 15 kg warhead its range is 100–125 km. A lighter Chomga Light carries 5 kg over up to 80 km. The developers rejected using the Chomga as a permanent relay because of its vulnerability at low speed, opting instead for a tethered balloon repeater (RP7-15-RLY SkyLoki Ultra+) with up to 10 hours of endurance. According to the manufacturer, the aircraft is ready for deployment and talks are under way with military units on combat trials; SkyLoki modules were earlier tested against EW together with units of the 10th Army Corps. The Russian milblogger channel WarGonzo reported on 2 November that Russian security forces had prevented a series of attacks involving a batch of booby-trapped FPV goggles that Ukrainian special services allegedly tried to send to Russian servicemen in the 'special military operation' zone. According to the post, sappers confirmed that the devices contained explosives, which were meant to detonate when the goggles were switched on. WarGonzo writes that law enforcement established that the shipment was arranged through fake 'pseudo-patriotic' accounts used by Ukrainian agents to contact Russian volunteer organisations that supply troops. It adds that this is not the first such method, recalling that the FSB has previously defused booby-trapped power banks, speakers, car parts, shoe insoles, gift figurines and even church utensils intended for sabotage. The post does not say where or when the goggles were found, how many there were, or which agency made the discovery beyond the general reference to security forces; it is a milblogger version of a claim attributed to the FSB. Single source: WarGonzo is a personal/editorial pro-war Telegram channel, not a Russian MoD position; milbloggers can contradict each other and the MoD. The underlying security-service claim has not been independently verified.
