According to ArmyInform, Ukraine's Ministry of Defence, together with the Ministry of Digital Transformation and the Brave1 defence cluster, is launching new grant competitions for domestic developers. Ukrainian drones hit Rosneft's Novokuibyshevsk refinery in Samara Oblast; Fire Point says its FP-1s struck a diesel hydrotreating unit hours after WSJ reported a pause in refinery strikes

2026-10-30

Early on 30 October, Ukrainian forces hit the Rosneft-owned Novokuibyshevsk oil refinery in Samara Oblast with FP-1 long-range drones, the Ukrainian drone maker Fire Point said. Fire Point said one of the units hit hydrotreats diesel fuel. Residents reported explosions and a fire. Exilenova+ counted at least 11 separate fires, and Astra geolocated the blaze to the refinery. The published footage shows several smoke columns but not which units burned. Ukraine's General Staff has not commented. The refinery lies about 910 km from the front, can process 8.8 million tonnes of oil a year, is a main jet-fuel producer, and according to Astra supplies jet fuel to the Russian military. The strike came hours after the Wall Street Journal reported that Ukraine had unofficially paused refinery strikes under pressure from President Trump until after the US midterm elections on 3 November. Kyiv has not announced a pause or said whether one is over. According to Euromaidan Press, this was the first fire at a Russian refinery since 15 October (Ryazan) and the first reported strike on the Russian oil sector since a Druzhba pipeline pumping station in Tambov Oblast was hit on 20 October. Reuters, as cited by The Moscow Times, reported that Russian refinery utilisation was 25% higher at the end of October than at the start of the month. Russia's MoD claimed it downed 1,043 Ukrainian drones overnight over 19 regions. Moscow Mayor Sobyanin said 862 drones flew towards the Moscow region, 170 of them downed on approach to the capital. Astra also reported a presumed hit on a building of the Konservsushprod army canned-food plant in Starodub, Bryansk Oblast, based on footage its analyst had not verified. These figures and claims have not been independently verified.

Meduza, drawing on an interview his wife gave to iStories, describes "Nikolai" (a pseudonym), an IT specialist from Chelyabinsk who wrote software for government agencies. In summer 2026 he signed a contract with the Russian Defence Ministry that, according to an appendix, assigned him to the drone forces as a drone operator with training for that role. Instead he was sent to military unit 95378 in Samara region, home of the 1437th Motorized Rifle Regiment (15th Separate Guards Motorized Rifle Brigade). For several months he received neither drone nor weapons training and worked as a company clerk and on housekeeping duties. In September he called his wife from a borrowed phone, said he was being sent on a combat mission and said goodbye. Command later notified the family that he had gone missing on 28 September 2026 'while performing tasks during the special military operation'. His fate and location are unknown. His wife found an Ozon order in his email for five laptops worth 120,000 rubles, placed while he was at the front. She believes someone in his chain of command withdrew about 1.5 million rubles from his bank card. She publicly accuses regiment commander Colonel Vladimir Teslo (31, Hero of Russia since 2024) of treating soldiers as a source of money and then getting rid of them. She also says her husband described extortion in the unit and punishments such as being put in a pit or sent on assaults. Relatives of other servicemen in the regiment have posted similar complaints on social media. These are her claims; Meduza does not confirm them, and no official response is reported. Meduza presents the case as part of a broader pattern. Since late 2025 the Defence Ministry has recruited students and others into the drone forces with promises of high pay, one-year contracts and service away from the front. In practice the contracts are open-ended, and recruiters cannot guarantee any service conditions. The article gives no figures on how many similar cases exist. It also notes that the 15th Brigade is suspected of abuses against civilians in Kyiv region and of torture and extrajudicial killings, citing Radio Svoboda and Verstka.

«Белорусы показали, как правильно делать мангалы» (WarGonzo: 'The Belarusians showed how to make cope cages properly')

2026-10-27SINGLE SOURCEMILBLOGGER

WarGonzo reports that Belarus's State Military-Industrial Committee has presented a modular lattice anti-drone protection kit ('mangal', cope cage) for the 122 mm BM-21B 'BelGrad' multiple rocket launcher. The work was done by the 2566th Radio-Electronic Armament Repair Plant, the enterprise that carried out the deep modernisation of Soviet Grad systems. The structure covers the cab, the ammunition racks and the launcher, and according to the developer can be fitted in the field by the crew if needed. The BelGrad is a Belarusian modernisation of the Soviet Grad: it uses a MAZ-6317 chassis instead of the Ural truck, and its carried ammunition load is increased from 40 to 100 rockets, while the launcher retains 40 tubes for 122 mm rockets. WarGonzo contrasts this with the Russian army, where artillery and tank crews often have to improvise protective cages from whatever is at hand, and hopes the Belarusian example will push Russian manufacturers to turn front-line improvisation into a standard factory solution. This is a single source from a Russian milblogger: a personal/editorial voice, not an MoD position; milbloggers can contradict each other and the MoD. Not independently verified.
