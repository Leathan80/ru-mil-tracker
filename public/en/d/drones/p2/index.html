NATO's top commander Grynkewich outlines a 'theory of victory' against a Russian attack: a drone and robot zone on the eastern flank, long-range strikes on Kaliningrad, the St Petersburg area and Belarus, then a counteroffensive

2026-10-12

General Alexus Grynkewich, NATO's Supreme Allied Commander Europe (SACEUR), set out what he called a 'theory of victory' for the eastern flank at a security conference in Warsaw on 12 October. Meduza bases its account largely on reporting by the Wall Street Journal and Reuters. Grynkewich did not name Russia, but he said any attack on an ally would be 'utterly futile' and that NATO would 'break the warfighting system' used against it, including everything that 'supports, sustains, and controls' the attacking force. According to the reporting, the concept has three layers. The first is an 'autonomous zone', or drone wall, on the eastern flank, where drones and ground robots would slow an advance without heavy NATO casualties. The second is long-range missile strikes on Russian command posts and air defences in Kaliningrad, near St Petersburg and in Belarus, to contest control of the air so that the US Army's V Corps and other allied ground forces can counterattack. The third is air strikes on Russian reinforcements and rear-area targets. A cloud-based network for sharing classified targeting data between allies would link the system; parts of it were reportedly tested during the US attack on Iran. The NATO command-post exercise Steadfast Duel, with more than 20,000 personnel, simulated one million NATO troops repelling an invasion by an army of similar size. Bild reported a comparable Baltic plan in August, which included disabling Russian missile systems in Kaliningrad. Meduza also sets out the Russian response. In September, Russia's embassy in Ireland claimed NATO was preparing an air and naval blockade of Kaliningrad and warned that Russia was ready to use 'any weapon', including nuclear weapons. A Russian note to NATO, obtained by Reuters, accused the alliance of a 'dangerous and reckless course'. Putin said a direct attack on Russian territory would put all of Russia's weapons on the table. NATO says its exercises do not threaten Russian territory. For Russia-watchers, the plan's significance is that it treats Kaliningrad, the Leningrad Military District area and Belarus as early targets for strikes against command and air defence. Moscow's messaging presents exactly that as grounds for nuclear escalation.
