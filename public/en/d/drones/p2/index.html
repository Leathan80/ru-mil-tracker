Upgraded Ukrainian Zirka interceptor with Raspberry Pi machine vision downs a Russian jet-powered Geran-5 at about 400 km/h and 3,500 m

2026-11-01

An upgraded Zirka interceptor drone destroyed a Russian jet-powered Geran-5 flying at about 400 km/h at an altitude of 3,500 metres, according to the developer, which published the interception video on 31 October, Euromaidan Press reports. The mission was flown by the crew 'Retro' of the Darknode battalion, part of the 412th Nemesis brigade of the Unmanned Systems Forces. The specifications of the upgraded version have not been disclosed. Zirka was built by NOCTIS to a brief drawn up with Darknode; Vyriy Industries holds a 30% stake in NOCTIS. At its debut in June, Zirka was listed with a top speed above 340 km/h, a 6 km ceiling, a 30 km radius and a unit price of up to $2,000. In late July it downed a jet-powered Geran-4, reportedly by meeting the target head-on or from the side rather than chasing it from behind. The drone carries a Raspberry Pi 5 running a machine-vision model trained to recognise Shahed-type drones; it detects targets at up to 1 km, and once the operator confirms a target the system tracks it and guides the interceptor in, leaving the operator mainly to manage closing speed. NOCTIS claims this makes Zirka up to 60% more effective against manoeuvring targets than manual piloting. The article places the kill in a wider problem: according to President Zelensky in October, Ukraine shoots down 50-60% of jet-powered Shaheds against 85-92% of propeller-driven ones. Ukraine's military intelligence (HUR) estimates Russia builds about 3,000 jet drones a month and puts the Geran-5's cruise speed at 450-600 km/h; radio specialist Serhii Beskrestnov says it has reached 1,000 km/h while manoeuvring and that Ukrainian jet interceptors still struggle with guidance, automatic target lock and mass production. The 1020th anti-aircraft regiment intercepted a Geran-5 at 425 km/h in September, and Wild Hornets says its STING-family interceptors have destroyed about ten Geran-4s. The kill claim and performance figures come from the developer and Ukrainian sources and have not been independently verified.

An RAF Protector RG1 remotely piloted aircraft, the British version of General Atomics' MQ-9B SkyGuardian, has flown its first operational missions in UK airspace, the UK Ministry of Defence said, according to Defence Blog. The unarmed drone provided the Royal Navy with more than 10 hours of surveillance over the English Channel while the Type 45 destroyer HMS Dauntless and the offshore patrol vessel HMS Tyne monitored the Russian frigate Neustrashimy and accompanying merchant ships through the Channel between 1 and 14 October. It flew alongside military and civilian air traffic. The ministry did not say how many sorties were flown, which sensors were used or how much of the frigate's transit was covered. Protector is flown from RAF Waddington by 31 and XIII squadrons. It received a military type certificate on 29 April 2025 allowing flight without geographic restrictions in UK airspace and entered service in June 2025; the MQ-9A Reaper it replaced never flew in UK airspace. The UK is buying 16 aircraft. The RAF lists endurance at more than 30 hours and a ceiling of up to 40,000 feet, with full-motion video, infrared, synthetic aperture radar and ground target tracking among its sensors. In June 2026 a Protector flew from Waddington to RAF Akrotiri in Cyprus through European civil airspace (more than 2,000 nautical miles in about 13.5 hours). The Channel mission was one of several Royal Navy operations against Russian ships between late September and mid-October. From 11 to 14 October HMS Tyne and a Wildcat helicopter followed the Ropucha-class landing ship Aleksandr Shabalin, the cargo ship MV Energy-2 and the tug Proton through the Channel towards the Baltic.
