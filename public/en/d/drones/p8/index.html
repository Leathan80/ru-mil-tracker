Ukraine scales up counter-measures against jet-powered Shaheds: small air defence missiles due in October, a new Nika missile heading into testing, Spys interceptor test and German funding for tens of thousands of interceptor drones

2026-10-04

During German Chancellor Friedrich Merz's visit to Kyiv on 4 October, President Zelensky said Ukraine will start using small air defence missiles against Russian jet-powered drones in the second half of October. According to Militarnyi, he said there are several types, that they are not yet in use, and that production could be scaled up quickly if the interception results are good. Militarnyi lists the Ukrainian systems around this effort: the short-range DRAGON (currently firing R-73 and AIM-9 missiles, with the small ST-100 missile in development), Desna and Rif with RK-2PZ missiles, and Osa-AKM with the new RK-19. The Brave1 grant programme has funded low-cost air defence missiles with grants of UAH 100–150 million per developer. At the same event Zelensky announced a new compact missile called Nika, built specifically to shoot down jet-powered Shaheds (Geran-4 and Geran-5). Euromaidan Press, citing Suspilne, reports that Nika has not yet been tested; according to Zelensky, both the Ukrainian and German sides reacted positively, but he would only say the results 'could be positive'. No developer, specifications or cost have been published. Zelensky also said the new Spys interceptor had passed a successful test (Ukrainska Pravda). Spys, made by WIY Drones, was built to counter jet-powered UAVs, and a first batch of 50 went to units for combat testing in August. An interceptor apparently derived from the Bars missile-drone is covered in a companion entry. Germany announced a package worth more than €1.3 billion ($1.46 billion), according to the Kyiv Independent. Of this, €1 billion is military aid: financing for interceptor drones against Russian jet-powered weapons, 'thousands' of IRIS-T missiles and additional IRIS-T fire units. The remaining roughly €350 million goes to energy aid, including generators and repairs. Quantum Systems will receive German financing to produce tens of thousands of interceptors in Ukraine. According to the company's press service, cited by Ukrainska Pravda, these are drones from Quantum WIY Industries, a joint venture of Quantum Systems and Ukraine's WIY Drones. Quantum Systems also signed a memorandum with Fire Point on joint counter-air unmanned systems. Zelensky put the total value of the agreements with Germany at more than €8.5 billion and also mentioned expanding Patriot missile production in Germany. The scale of the threat explains the urgency. Zelensky said Russia launched more than 1,900 attack drones in a single week, about 800 of them jet-powered (Kyiv Independent). Euromaidan Press cites a Ukrainian military intelligence estimate that Russian plants produce about 3,000 jet-powered Geran-4 and Geran-5 drones a month. At the press conference Zelensky said Ukraine shoots down 85–92% of propeller-driven Shaheds but only about 50–60% of the jet-powered variants, with F-16s helping to close the gap. Ukraine is testing five interceptor-drone models and plans about 400 units of one of them by the end of October. Taken together, the announcements show Ukraine trying to build a cheaper, mass-producible interception layer below Patriot and IRIS-T, against jet-powered Shahed/Geran variants that conventional interceptor drones struggle to catch. Whether the small missiles and Nika work will only become clear after testing and deployment from late October.

Russia upgrades the Geran-4 jet drone with a shaped-charge cutting warhead and 'whisker' contact fuze against power lines and bridges; HUR details a US-made magnetometer, 'Susanin' optical navigation and an Alabuga-built engine

2026-09-30

According to Serhii "Flash" Beskrestnov, adviser to the Ukrainian President on defence technology, Russia on 30 September used for the first time a special shaped-charge cutting payload (KRSN) on a jet-powered strike drone, designed to destroy high-voltage transmission towers and steel bridge structures. Militarnyi, citing photos published by Defense Express, identified the carrier as the Geran-4: the warhead combines a main charge of about 40 kg with four supplementary linear cutting charges in a revolver-cylinder layout, which focus the blast along a line to slice through pylon legs, cables or bridge girders rather than causing broad blast damage. This also explains the 'whiskered Shahed' that volunteer Serhii Sternenko showed on X on 1 October (reported by Defence Blog). According to Beskrestnov, the rods on its nose are a simple mechanical contact fuze: a metal rod inside a metal tube closes the firing circuit on contact, so a close pass over a power line is enough to set off the charges, whereas a standard impact fuze needs a solid surface and may not fire when a drone clips a cable or the open lattice of a tower. On 5 October Ukraine's Defence Intelligence (HUR) published details and photos, via its War&Sanctions portal, of the upgraded Geran-4, which it says Russia began deploying in autumn 2026 (Ukrainska Pravda). HUR confirms that the KRSN warhead is meant to cut bridge structures and overhead power-line supports with a shaped-charge jet. The upgraded drone also carries a new MOKh module built around a high-precision magnetic induction sensor (magnetometer), the PNI5GA made by the US company PNI Sensor; working together with the SADRA inertial navigation module, it reduces gyroscope drift and improves the accuracy of autonomous navigation. On top of satellite and inertial navigation, Russia has added the 'Susanin' optoelectronic correlation system, which compares live images from an optical sensor with a reference image stored in memory to keep the drone on its route from launch to target. According to HUR, the identical system is fitted to the Geran-5 in its K5 modification, which confirms earlier Telegram-based reporting. HUR also says the Chinese Telefly turbojet has been replaced by a localised Russian-made equivalent produced at Alabuga, while the remaining electronics and components are unchanged from earlier Geran versions. Militarnyi describes the Geran-4 as a development of the Geran-3: about 3 m wingspan, 3.5 m long, 450 kg take-off weight, a 160 kgf turbojet, a cruise speed of 350-500 km/h, a range of 850 km, an altitude band of 100-5,000 m and a warhead of more than 50 kg. Ukrainska Pravda notes that Russia used five times as many jet drones in July as in June and says the new warhead may signal that Russia plans to step up attacks on transmission lines. According to Defence Blog, such lines have so far been hit less often than power plants and substations because towers and cables are small and hard to hit. HUR's emphasis on bridges follows Russian jet-drone strikes on Kyiv's Dnipro bridges in early October. The claims come from Ukrainian intelligence, official-adjacent, volunteer and media sources and have not been independently confirmed.
