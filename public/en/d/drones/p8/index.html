Vluchnyk unveils Zorelit v5 interceptor drone, claims 416 km/h against jet-powered Gerans

2026-09-21

Ukrainian company Vluchnyk (CEO Stepan Radiboh) has completed internal testing of the Zorelit v5 (also marketed as "Starship"), an interceptor drone purpose-built to down jet-powered Russian attack UAVs. Per Radiboh, the system "has completed all testing and is ready for military use"; Vluchnyk says it is open to direct contracts with military units and, once the drone receives official codification (a NATO Stock Number), will also list it in Ukraine's e-Points system, through which units earn points for confirmed strikes on Russian personnel and equipment. The manufacturer states a top speed of 416 km/h and a range of up to 47 km on a single charge; at a cruising speed of around 200 km/h, endurance is roughly 17 minutes. The warhead weighs 300-600 g. An optional AI-based system provides automatic target detection/tracking with terminal guidance in the final approach, and the drone can be integrated with the SkyMap system for automatic navigation to the target area. Vluchnyk says it is designed to intercept Russian reconnaissance UAVs, Gerbera decoys, Geran-2, and the faster, jet-powered Geran-3 and Geran-4 variants. Zorelit v5 was developed from the earlier Zorelit v4, which the company says has already seen combat use, entered service, and received a NATO Stock Number. Price is listed at UAH 89,500 (about US$2,000). The unveiling adds to a growing roster of Ukrainian interceptor drones aimed at jet-powered Shahed/Geran threats (including the previously tracked STING S and an SBU counterintelligence-developed interceptor mentioned in the same reporting) — this is not a first-of-kind capability. Note: Zorelit v4's combat record and NATO Stock Number are company self-reports, not independently verified by any outlet, and no combat kill claim yet exists for Zorelit v5 itself — only completed internal testing.

Not Just More Drones: How Russia Is Changing Its Air Attacks With Jet-Powered Shaheds

2026-09-21SINGLE SOURCE

Russia has launched roughly 7,700 jet-powered ("reactive") Shahed/Geran drones since introducing the variant, Ukrainian Air Force spokesperson Colonel Yuriy Ihnat said. In September 2026 Russia fired an estimated 1,800-1,900 jet-powered units, down from about 2,800 in July, but drones are now airborne nearly around the clock. Ihnat stressed the change is not just about growing volume but about employment: strike packages increasingly alternate or combine jet-powered Shaheds with conventional propeller-driven ones and other weapons rather than relying on a single type. In early September, Russia reportedly launched several waves of large numbers of classic (propeller) Shaheds toward Ukraine's western regions via northern routes, preceded by heavy waves of jet-powered drones — a sequenced combination rather than simultaneous mixed use. Weather reportedly influences which drone variant Russia deploys on a given night, since conditions affect Ukrainian detection and engagement. Ukrainian forces report intercepting roughly 60% of jet-powered UAVs at various flight stages, with aviation playing a significant role, but Ihnat cautioned that not all current Ukrainian air-defense systems can effectively engage the faster jet-powered variant, and available interceptor missiles remain insufficient in quantity. Ihnat said Ukraine needs manufacturers to deliver cheaper, more effective interception systems at scale, and faster warning from Western partners on missile launches to improve response time. This is a single-source report from a Ukrainian military-affiliated outlet, not independently corroborated, and the Ukrainian side has an interest in emphasizing the evolving and growing nature of the threat it is defending against.

No tanks, lone soldiers: Russia shifts to small-group infiltration tactics on the northeastern front

2026-09-20

Ukrainian Joint Forces Task Force spokesperson Viktor Trehubov said on 20 September that Russian assault tactics have shifted markedly over the past year on the northeastern front: rather than attacking in columns supported by tanks and armored vehicles, Russian troops now advance in small groups of a few soldiers, or even alone, trying to cross the "kill zone" separating rear positions from the front line undetected. According to Trehubov, small groups or individual soldiers who make it through the kill zone take cover and wait for others to catch up before regrouping into larger assault elements — reflecting Russia scaling down each assault wave because of the difficulty of crossing a kill zone that has expanded to roughly 20 km. Armor has been all but pulled from frontline assault roles: tanks have "moved not just to the back seat, but to the third row," used mainly when Russia can mass enough drone cover to escort them through, or when poor weather grounds Ukrainian drones. Ukrainian mechanized brigades told the Kyiv Independent this spring that their own tank crews rarely deploy under current drone-saturated conditions — indicating this is a mutual adaptation to dense drone ISR/strike coverage on both sides, not a Russian-only phenomenon. Ukrainian military media separately reporting on the Kupyansk sector specifically describe a similar pattern — infantry infiltrating in small groups through remaining forest cover, spreading pressure across multiple directions rather than one concentrated mechanized thrust, with heavy equipment relegated to fire support, mine-clearing and logistics, and Ukrainian drone reconnaissance credited with detecting groups before they regroup. Note: this Kupyansk-specific framing comes from separate Ukrainian military-affiliated reporting, not from the verified kyivindependent source itself, which describes the tactical shift at the broader "northeastern front" level without naming Kupyansk explicitly.

Alongside the already-documented licensed production at Alabuga (Tatarstan, $1.75 billion, finalized around October 2022) and the evolution into jet-powered Geran-4/5 variants — with a production target of thousands of additional Geran drones this month and jet-powered models expected to make up roughly 80% of attacks by November — the article reports a new element: a covert program codenamed C430L, begun in 2023, in which Russian specialists are helping Iran develop ramjet propulsion for supersonic and hypersonic cruise missiles. A second new element is that Russia is supplying Iran with upgraded munitions, satellite imagery, and targeting data that has been used against American forces in the Middle East — a concrete intelligence-sharing component layered on top of the drone cooperation. All of this sits within a 20-year strategic treaty signed in 2025 covering joint military exercises, intelligence-sharing, and defense-industrial cooperation. The article also notes at least 10 new construction sites at Alabuga as of early September 2026. The Institute for the Study of War warns this constitutes a 'complete technological feedback loop' giving Iran upgraded capabilities to threaten US land and naval assets, bases, command centers, and logistics hubs.
