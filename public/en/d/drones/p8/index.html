ISW assesses Russia's mass drone strikes aim to force Ukraine's capitulation by spring 2027

2026-09-28

The Institute for the Study of War (ISW) assesses that Russia is running a large-scale long-range strike campaign, built on jet-powered drones and ballistic missiles, with the aim of forcing Ukraine to capitulate by spring 2027, as reported by Euromaidan Press. ISW calls this Putin's theory of victory: launching cheap Geran (Shahed-type) jet-powered drones in such numbers that enough of them get through Ukrainian defences. The figures cited: Russian use of jet-powered drones rose eightfold between June and August; about 7,700 have been used since their first launch; more than 2,730 Shahed drones were launched in September alone, of which Ukraine intercepted or suppressed over 1,500. On 26 September Zelensky put the interception rate for jet-powered Shaheds at 55%. ISW says the approach relies on Ukraine's shortage of Patriot interceptors against ballistic missiles, the degradation of the power grid and Ukraine's limited air defence resources. On 25 September Putin said Ukraine 'must feel' Russia's 'retaliatory' strikes, which ISW reads as a sign of intensification over the winter. Possible disruptions: Presidential Office head Kyrylo Budanov indicated that mass deployment of Ukrainian interceptor drones could begin within months, though it is uncertain whether it comes in time for the winter escalation. ISW also assesses that Putin may postpone large-scale forced mobilisation until the results of the drone campaign are clear, despite groundwork already being laid. Note that the capitulation timeline is an ISW assessment, not a stated Russian aim, and the figures on launches and interceptions come from Ukrainian and ISW reporting.

russia Equips New Geran-5 Drones With Kh-101-Style Navigation: Why Darkness Could Be Problem

2026-09-28SINGLE SOURCE

According to Defense Express, Russia has upgraded its Geran-5 jet-powered drones with a new guidance module called Susanin, a Digital Scene Matching Area Correlation (DSMAC) system that navigates by comparing preloaded reference satellite imagery with images from an onboard camera. This lets the drone keep navigating where satellite navigation (GNSS) is jammed or unavailable, making it more resistant to electronic warfare. Defense Express stresses that the technology is not new: comparable systems have flown on the Russian Kh-101 cruise missile and the American Tomahawk for years. It also notes that Ukraine uses similar scene-matching on its own deep-strike drones. The outlet points to a key limitation: accuracy depends on lighting, season and even the time of day, since the sun angle changes shadow patterns. At night the system performs considerably worse, particularly without extra illumination or specialised image processing. This creates a tension, the outlet argues, because Russia mostly launches Kh-101 missiles at night, when Ukrainian mobile fire groups find targets harder to detect. A night-launched Geran-5 relying on Susanin could thus lose navigation accuracy, although this is Defense Express's assessment and not an observed result. Caveat: this is single-source reporting from Defense Express (Ukrainian defence outlet); the claims have not been independently verified.

Sources: Defense Express

In the night of 2–3 September 2026, incendiary devices were thrown from a moving car onto a construction site in Munich's Berg am Laim district, directly next to the headquarters of technology and defence company Rohde & Schwarz and defence firm Helsing, which supplies drones to Ukraine's armed forces. Only one device ignited, near containers; a second was defused. Nobody was hurt and damage was limited. German intelligence reportedly attributes such incidents to the GRU's Department of Special Tasks, a unit said to have been set up in February 2025 to coordinate assassinations and sabotage abroad and to infiltrate Western companies and universities. If confirmed, Munich would be the second suspected Russian attack in Germany within about a month, after explosive drones were found in August at Leipzig Airport near Ukrainian cargo aircraft.
