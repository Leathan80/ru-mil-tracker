Russia has lost about 13 military aircraft since late September, Defence Blog tallies: seven Su-34s destroyed on the ground by Ukrainian strikes, a Tu-95MS and two Su-30SMs in crashes, and a Su-57 burned out at Akhtubinsk

2026-10-31

Defence Blog puts Russia's military aviation losses at about a dozen aircraft in roughly five weeks. It bases the count on the Ukrainian open-source project Oko Gora, which counted 11 Russian fighter aircraft and one helicopter lost in October, plus two Su-34s partially damaged. Adding the Tu-95MS that crashed on 29 September brings the total to about 13 airframes. The listed losses are: on 4 October, SBU drones hit a Su-34 and a Su-35 at Khanskaya air base in Adygea, about 500 km from Ukrainian-held territory, along with an ammunition depot; on the night of 14-15 October, Ukrainian Navy Neptune missiles struck Borisoglebsk air base (Voronezh Oblast), where satellite imagery showed four Su-34s destroyed and two badly damaged; and on 29 October another Neptune strike destroyed two Su-34s at Marinovka air base (Volgograd Oblast). Russia has confirmed only the two training-flight crashes and has not acknowledged losses from Ukrainian strikes. The crashes and other losses: on 29 September a Tu-95MS crashed on take-off near Ukrainka air base in Amur Oblast. Defence Blog says six of the seven crew were killed, although earlier reporting gave a higher death toll among strategic aviation personnel (see the earlier entry). It was the first Tu-95MS lost in a crash since 2015, and Russia no longer builds the type, which launches Kh-101 cruise missiles at Ukraine. On 21 October a Su-30SM crashed on a training flight in Kaliningrad Oblast, with both crew ejecting; Russian and Ukrainian outlets reported a second Su-30SM lost the same day. The Russian Knights aerobatic team later announced the deaths of two pilots, Col. Oleg Yerofeyev and Lt. Col. Vladimir Kochetov, without giving a cause; Ukrainian analysts link them to the second crash, which Russia has not confirmed. On 29 October a Su-57 caught fire during an engine start at the Akhtubinsk flight-test centre (Astrakhan Oblast) and burned out, according to the Ukrainian channel Absolutely Reliable Source, which published satellite imagery of a burn scar. If confirmed it would be the fourth Su-57 lost or damaged this year. On 30 October Ukraine's 3rd Assault Brigade said it destroyed a landed Mi-8 in Luhansk Oblast with two Hornet drones. The Su-34, Russia's main glide-bomb carrier, bore the brunt: seven destroyed in October, or nine counting the two damaged at Borisoglebsk. Ukraine's military intelligence (HUR) said on 22 October that Russia had about 140 Su-34s of all versions in service and that industry delivered 22 new ones between October 2025 and October 2026, per Ukrinform; October's losses thus equal about a third of a year's output. Oryx had visually confirmed 205 Russian aircraft destroyed or damaged since February 2022, with the Su-34 the most-lost type at 48. The individual incidents are covered in earlier entries; the overall tally relies largely on Ukrainian sources and satellite imagery, and several losses are not confirmed by Russia.
