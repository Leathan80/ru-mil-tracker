2026-09-26

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy says Ukraine will share its experience of defending against drones with partners facing Russian hybrid and drone attacks, NATO members and non-members alike, Euromaidan Press reported, citing UNIAN. Asked whether Ukraine would send troops to defend NATO members under attack, Zelenskyy did not answer that question directly. He pointed instead to Ukraine's "Drone Deal" agreements. As a precedent for sharing counter-drone expertise, Zelenskyy cited Ukraine sending specialists to the Middle East to help protect critical infrastructure. He said sharing drone-defence know-how is "precisely part of this exchange" under the Drone Deal framework. The development shows Kyiv using its combat-tested counter-UAS experience as a diplomatic and security asset, while it avoids any commitment of troops.

The system was first reported by the Russian daily Izvestia on 22 September. Instead of radar, Pauk uses a network of small modules with microphones, a processor and a radio link; the modules pick up the characteristic noise of a UAV, recognise it with neural-network algorithms and pass the data to an operator console. The modules come in three housings: a rectangular one for fixed placement, a dart-shaped one that can be dropped from the air, and a spherical 'hedgehog' with spikes for placement in vegetation. The recommended layout is a checkerboard pattern with modules tens of metres apart and overlapping zones, so that long perimeters can be monitored. The author stresses that the claimed performance still has to be confirmed in independent trials and that Pauk has yet to find a customer, so there is no indication that it is in service or in use in Ukraine. The system fits a wider trend of cheap, passive acoustic sensor networks against low-flying drones, which Ukraine has also used at scale.
