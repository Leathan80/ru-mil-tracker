2026-09-27

Oleksandr Syrskyi, whom Militarnyi describes as the former Commander-in-Chief of the Armed Forces of Ukraine, told LB.ua in an interview that Ukrainian active protection systems (APS) for armoured vehicles can already intercept between 60% and 80% of FPV drones. He said Ukraine and Russia are both developing systems to shield armour from drone attack, and that the latest Ukrainian APS developments have proven effective in testing. Syrskyi did not name a specific system or vehicle type. In his view drones still hold the advantage in the contest between attack and defence, but once these measures are "brought to full maturity, the use of armored vehicles will once again become relevant, and the pace of offensive operations will change". On the Russian side, Militarnyi recalls that in July 2026 Russia deployed a T-72B3A tank with the Arena-M APS, modified to counter FPV drones, for the first time. Ukrainian drones destroyed the tank, but it took seven drones to use up the APS's countermeasure ammunition, and the total came to 15–20 FPV drones per vehicle. According to Militarnyi, Russia has started only limited deployment of such systems despite their shortcomings. Scaling up production and improving how they are used could still greatly increase the battlefield value of armour. That would raise the cost of Russia's FPV-centred anti-armour tactics and, in turn, of Ukrainian drone strikes on Russian armour. Militarnyi points to two Ukrainian projects in this area. The company Ukrainian Armor has shown an unmanned module that detects, tracks and destroys drones automatically using shotguns. The company I-SEE has built an AI drone-detection system that combines computer vision with Edge AI and is now developing an autonomous anti-drone APS on top of it. The company says the system has grown from an intelligent detector and tracker into a "universal core" for kinetic interception systems. It is hardware-independent and processes video on board, without latency or an internet connection. The 60–80% figure comes from Syrskyi alone; no test conditions, drone types or systems were given, and the claim has not been independently verified. Fibre-optic FPV drones were not mentioned.

Russia reportedly fields Geran-5 'K5' variant with autonomous optical (scene-matching) navigation and redesigned fuselage

2026-09-27

Russian forces have reportedly begun using a new modification of the jet-powered Geran-5 strike UAV with an autonomous optical navigation system and a modified fuselage. The report comes from the monitoring Telegram channel Colonel GSh ("Polkovnik GSh"), which calls the variant the Geran-5 K5 and published a photo comparing the old and new airframes. According to the channel, the drone has an electro-optical correction system that navigates by matching images of the ground below. It works on the same principle as DSMAC-type (Russian: OEKS) scene-matching systems, which let a weapon correct its course without satellite navigation. The channel also says the K5 is faster than the standard Geran-5 and may therefore be misidentified in flight as other jet-powered strike weapons such as the Dan-T. Militarnyi stresses that Ukrainian sources have not officially confirmed either the K5 designation or the presence of a DSMAC-class system. Russian sources partly corroborate the claim. Russian military correspondent Andrei Filatov (via Gazeta.ru) said that Geran-series drones already use an autonomous optical navigation system called "Susanin", which navigates by terrain imagery and lets the UAV continue on its route without radio beacons. He did not say which Geran modification carries it or describe it as DSMAC. Ukraine hits two Russian missile-component plants in two nights: Almaz-Antey's Iskra in Ulyanovsk (Kh-59, Pantsir parts) and the Azov Optical-Mechanical Plant, which leaked files tie to at least seven missile programs including Kh-101 and Oreshnik

2026-09-25

Ukrainian long-range drones struck JSC NPP 'Iskra Plant' in Ulyanovsk overnight on 24–25 September 2026, as part of a wider drone barrage on Russia. The plant belongs to the Almaz-Antey defence conglomerate and makes semiconductor devices, microwave components and electronics. According to Militarnyi and an identification by the independent Russian outlet ASTRA, it supplies components for Kh-59M2/M2A air-launched guided missiles (via the Yaroslavl Radio Plant) and diodes used in Pantsir-S1 air defence systems. Ulyanovsk governor Alexei Russkikh confirmed the attack, reporting damage to industrial and civilian infrastructure and eight people injured, including a child. On the night of 26 September Ukraine hit the Azov Optical-Mechanical Plant (AOMZ) in Azov, Rostov Oblast, which President Zelensky and the General Staff confirmed. The plant is part of the Tactical Missiles Corporation (KTRV) and makes opto-mechanical and optoelectronic equipment and electronics for artillery, missile and anti-tank weapons. According to the SBU, its Alpha special group and the Navy used Neptune cruise missiles and Ruta, Palianytsia and Bars jet-powered drone-missiles. The SBU said the strike started a fire at the foundry and damaged equipment in five production buildings, including optical production, an assembly section, an electroplating workshop and a printed-circuit-board facility. OSINT and NASA FIRMS fire data indicated a fire near the site. The same night Ukraine also hit the Ilsky oil refinery in Krasnodar Krai. After the strike, the private analytical group Dallas Analytics published internal AOMZ, KTRV and supplier correspondence, state-contract data and cooperation schedules covering April 2023 to March 2025. According to these files, the plant is part of the production chains of at least seven missile programs. For the Kh-101 cruise missile it makes the Otblesk navigation lidar, which fixes the missile's position when satellite navigation is jammed, and the flight-control automation unit, for which no alternative supplier reportedly exists. The files also list guidance elements for the Oreshnik intermediate-range ballistic missile, seekers for the Kh-31P anti-radiation missile, and electronics and seekers for the R-74 air-to-air missile. For the R-37M air-to-air missile the plant makes power, switching and battery-activation units, harnesses and housings. It also makes flight-control automation for the Kh-35U, Kh-59 and Kh-69 missiles, plus seekers for the Kh-38ML missile and the KAB-250L guided bomb. A March 2025 request asked whether the plant could supply 500 sets of electronic components and 1,000 housings a year from 2026, as the Aerospace Forces sought larger deliveries. The two strikes show Ukraine targeting the electronics and seeker supply chain behind Russian missiles and air defence, not only launch platforms and fuel. The Azov documents suggest single points of failure, notably for the Kh-101. The damage claims rest on Russian regional officials, statements from the SBU and the Ukrainian military, and OSINT indicators. The leaked documents come from a Ukrainian-aligned private group and have not been independently authenticated. The extent of production disruption has not been independently verified. According to the analyst, most of the strikes hit the building's facade rather than the roof. As a result, around 80% of the strikes were not captured by the satellite: only two of the 11 confirmed strikes caused visible damage in the imagery. This is the analyst's assessment; the extent of internal damage is not confirmed.
