Senior Pentagon official: US was 'caught flat-footed' by the drone war in Ukraine

2026-11-05

Emil Michael, the US Under Secretary of Defense for Research and Engineering, has acknowledged that the United States was not ready for drone-dominated warfare. Quoted by The Wall Street Journal, as relayed by TASS, he said: "We were caught flat-footed by the drone warfare in Ukraine and Russia. We weren't prepared because we just didn't imagine it." According to the report, the US is now working to equip its Army with autonomous systems that could dominate in conflicts in Europe and the Middle East. Michael also claimed that the US now holds a stockpile of about 500,000 drones, compared with about 1,000 produced under former President Joe Biden, and said that even as the Pentagon embraces AI, the military still relies on intelligence reports and satellite imagery to develop targets. TASS links this to earlier remarks by Travis Metz, acting director of the Pentagon's Defense Innovation Unit, who told Bloomberg that the Department of Defense is "starting from basically zero" in manufacturing cheap, expendable drones. According to Bloomberg, US drone makers face supply chain problems, high component costs and bureaucracy that slows the rapid fielding of new technology, while Washington's restrictions on Chinese components have pushed production costs up. State-source caveat: TASS is a Russian state news agency and has an interest in highlighting Western weaknesses; this entry is based on TASS's rendering. The original Wall Street Journal article (and the occasion on which Michael spoke) could not be located, so the exact wording and context of the quotes, and the 500,000 versus 1,000 figures, have not been independently verified.

The appeal, according to the article, is that it reuses an existing rocket family and a known modular launcher. NATO opens counter-drone marketplace with 16 pre-selected systems from five companies

2026-11-05

NATO opened a Counter-Unmanned Aircraft Systems (C-UAS) marketplace on 5 November 2026. Allies can now buy systems that detect, track or defeat drones directly from a NATO catalogue, with no tender of their own. The NATO Communications and Information Agency (NCIA) ran the competition and chose 16 solutions from five companies: Alta Ares, Axon Enterprise, Northrop Grumman, OSL UK and Quantum Systems. The sources do not name individual systems or say how many solutions each company supplies. The only breakdown given is static, transportable and on-the-move systems. NATO says the selection drew on experience from NATO and national innovation activities and from exercises, and that all chosen solutions have already been deployed in Allied countries and in Ukraine. The NCIA, which also hosts the NATO Counter-UAS Centre of Expertise, manages the multi-award framework contracts, which run for 18 months. NATO plans regular competitions to bring in newer technology and has set aside EUR 3.1 million for further testing of the solutions by nations or NATO commands. The marketplace is part of Drone Edge, the initiative announced at the NATO Summit Defence Industry Forum in Ankara in July 2026. Under it, Allies have pledged to invest more than USD 40 billion in counter-drone capabilities over the next five years. The announcement names no buyers or delivery quantities.

Large-scale GPS jamming mapped along NATO's eastern flank, from the Baltic to the Black Sea

2026-11-04

Large-scale interference with GPS satellite navigation has been recorded along almost the entire eastern flank of NATO, from the Baltic Sea to the Black Sea, Militarnyi reports. The finding comes from the OSINT project ItaMilRadar, which published a map of navigation interference as of 3 November 2026 based on data from the AirNav Radar flight-tracking service. The heaviest interference was concentrated along NATO members' borders with Russia, Belarus and Ukraine: near Russia's Kaliningrad region, in Lithuania, eastern Poland, Romania and Bulgaria, and over the western Black Sea. Less intense interference was seen over Belarus, western Russia, occupied Crimea and the Caucasus. Citing Defence Blog, Militarnyi notes that the geographic pattern partly overlaps with areas crossed by the flight paths of Russian attack drones. According to Militarnyi, Russian EW systems in the Kaliningrad region conduct GPS jamming and spoofing that affect the routes of Ukrainian attack drones: fake navigation signals can make a drone miscalculate its position and stray from its planned route towards the Baltic states and Finland. The map is a snapshot built from aircraft navigation data; it does not by itself attribute each interference zone to a specific emitter. The report places the snapshot in a longer pattern of Russian navigation interference in the Baltic region. In May the Finnish Border Guard reported an increase in Russian GPS jamming in the Baltic, notably in the Archipelago Sea and the Åland Sea. In August Denmark recorded a record number and duration of satellite-navigation disruptions; the crew of the cargo vessel Saturn reported losing GPS for about 24 hours while sailing between Gdańsk and southern Gotland.

2026-11-02SINGLE SOURCE

ArmyInform, citing the Ukrainian General Staff on 2 November, reports that in October 2026 Russia launched a total of 6,473 Shahed-131/136 and Shahed-238 drones (Geran-1/2/3/4/5 in Russian designation), of which Ukrainian forces destroyed 4,859, or roughly 75%. Fighter and army aviation, surface-to-air missile units, mobile fire groups, unmanned-systems operators and electronic warfare were all involved. According to the General Staff, the effectiveness against jet-powered Shahed-type strike drones last month was 66%, against 89% for conventional Shaheds. It calls this a significant result given the scale and growing complexity of the Russian attacks, citing changing routes and flight altitudes and the combination of strike drones with decoys and missile strikes. It names countering ballistic missiles, which Russia used actively in October against civilian infrastructure, as a major remaining challenge, says Ukraine needs more effective means against them, and says it continues to integrate and strengthen a multi-layered air defence system.
