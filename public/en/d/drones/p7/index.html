Ukraine combat experience shapes Redwire's new Penguin Mk3 drone

2026-09-12

US defense company Redwire has delivered more than 250 of its Penguin reconnaissance drones — a Group 3 fixed-wing UAS with roughly 14 hours of endurance and EO/IR sensors for target recognition — to Ukraine since 2022, largely through US aid packages. That deployment generated a large body of operational data, now feeding into a next-generation variant: the Penguin Mk3. The central lesson is that raw specifications — range, endurance, payload — do not determine whether a tactical drone stays useful. Having the right aircraft, in the right configuration, available at the right moment, along with maintenance speed, training quality and operator workload, proved just as decisive. Russia's heavily contested electromagnetic environment also forced Redwire to rethink navigation and communications assumptions: rather than building in one permanent EW-hardening fix, the company adopted a modular architecture that allows rapid adaptation as adversaries roll out new jamming or spoofing techniques. As a result, the Mk3 adds fully automated vertical takeoff and landing, multiple larger payload bays, winter-operating provisions, and deployability by a two-person crew. An undisclosed NATO country has since signed a multi-year contract for the Mk3, valued in the high eight figures and supported through Redwire's European maintenance and engineering operation. The development reflects a broader shift among Western manufacturers away from treating drones as finished products at delivery and toward continuous iteration — a direct response to the Ukrainian reality that drone tactics and countermeasures can become obsolete within weeks or even days.

Pivnenko acknowledged that adoption was initially difficult — problems with cable quality and how the aircraft handled — before crews mastered both reconnaissance and strike missions with the type. The technology's widely reported downsides, not itemized in this particular source but relevant to assessment: the weight and drag of the fiber spool cap effective range (roughly 10-20 km depending on how much fiber is carried), the cable can snag on vegetation, trees, structures and power lines, spent cables litter the terrain, and the drone loses the freedom of maneuver a radio link allows. For the close-range FPV attacks that dominate the front, Ukrainian operators judge these limitations acceptable against the gain in jamming immunity.

Russian marketplace sellers shift to own warehouses after drone strikes as trade ministry downplays damage

2026-09-09

Sellers on the Russian online marketplaces Wildberries and Ozon have shifted heavily toward storing and shipping goods from their own warehouses (the so-called FBS model) rather than relying on the platforms' own fulfillment centers. At Wildberries, the share of sellers using FBS rose to 97.4% by the end of August 2026 — nearly doubling — while at Ozon it grew by 7.8 percentage points to 73.1%. Wildberries itself acknowledges 'growing seller interest' in FBS, but says sellers are mainly trying to combine both models, since the platform's own warehouses still offer faster delivery for goods with steady demand. The shift follows a sustained Ukrainian drone campaign against Russian e-commerce logistics infrastructure that has run since mid-2026, first targeting major Wildberries facilities and later Ozon warehouses as well. By early August, cumulative losses to Wildberries and affected sellers were estimated at 'hundreds of billions of rubles,' and in late August the Russian government approved support measures, including tax-payment deferrals for both sellers and the platforms. Russia's Ministry of Industry and Trade, meanwhile, is downplaying the need for full reconstruction. Deputy Minister Roman Chekushov said not all damaged logistics facilities need to be rebuilt, and that by the end of 2026 the ministry will have clarity on how logistics will be restructured. This points to a deliberate restructuring of the sector rather than straightforward reconstruction — and to a degree of official minimization of the strikes' cumulative toll. Note that this framing comes from a Russian government official; the seller-behavior figures were independently reported by Meduza.
