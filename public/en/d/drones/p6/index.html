Azov Corps says its small UAVs hit more than 100 Russian trains carrying fuel and supplies in occupied Donbas over four months, disabling locomotives and fuel tank cars

2026-10-30

The 1st Corps of Ukraine's National Guard 'Azov' said on social media on 28 October that its fighters had struck more than 100 Russian trains carrying fuel and other supplies for the occupying forces in Donbas, disabling locomotives, fuel tank cars and other equipment, Euromaidan Press reports. Citing Defense Express (29 October), the outlet says the figure covers Azov's operations over the past four months, and that the rail hubs hit had carried Russian military trains to the fighting in Donbas and southern Ukraine. The strikes on diesel locomotives, each weighing over 100 tonnes, were carried out with small UAVs. No specific stations, rail lines or drone types are named. Azov said the enemy 'cannot protect the key railway stations through which the fuel supply chain toward the front runs', and that neither mobile fire groups nor electronic warfare can protect Russian logistics routes. It linked the strikes to Putin's statement on 16 October that seizing all of Donetsk Oblast remains one of Russia's main tasks, saying they help destroy Russia's offensive capabilities. The corps credited donations and the Come Back Alive foundation, which it said supplied 440 Starlink Mini terminals. The campaign began publicly on 2 July, when Azov announced 'Operation Hell' and released its first footage of a strike on a Russian diesel locomotive. The figure of more than 100 trains is Azov's own claim, a party to the war, and has not been independently verified.

Operation Vivaldi: Ukraine's 125th Heavy Mechanized Brigade says it went from 17% to near-full manning in a year as 1,500 AWOL soldiers returned; an analyst argues Vivaldi shows armour still works, while the new 29th Brigade's patchwork tank battalion shows army-wide shortages

2026-10-30

Operation Vivaldi was the counteroffensive of Ukraine's 3rd Army Corps north of Lyman in northern Donetsk Oblast. According to Euromaidan Press, it aimed to disrupt Russian plans to encircle, from the north and south, the fortress-belt cities of Sloviansk, Kramatorsk, Druzhkivka and Kostiantynivka. Over four months the corps cleared 277 km². By its own count it inflicted 8,000 casualties on Russia's 20th and 25th Combined Arms Armies and pushed them back across the Zherebets River. The corps declared the operation finished on 19 October, though elements reportedly keep pushing towards the river's eastern bank (see earlier entries on Vivaldi). Euromaidan Press singles out manning as one reason for its success. The 125th Heavy Mechanized Brigade, one of the corps' five line brigades, is commanded by Maj. Volodymyr Fokin. It says its main units, three infantry and tank battalions that make up about half the brigade, are now filled to 95–100%, or as many as 1,500 infantry and vehicle crew. The brigade says that in October 2025 it was 17% manned and had no tanks, guns, armoured vehicles or drone crews. Fokin said in June that the whole brigade had 'two pickups and one minibus'. The brigade now reports a 430% rise in manning, helped by the return of some 1,500 soldiers who had gone absent without leave. It says it replaced about 90% of commanders in key posts within the first six months. Its drone crews grew by 1,200% for aerial reconnaissance, 950% for FPV, 1,400% for heavy bombers and 600% for fixed-wing drones. It also set up ground-robot units from scratch, along with its own training, air-defence, EW, reconnaissance and medical structures. It now fields US M2 Bradley infantry fighting vehicles, German Leopard tanks (the variant is unclear) and artillery. It claims to have ranked first among heavy mechanized brigades for three consecutive months. Ukrainian military observer Oleksandr Kovalenko argues that Vivaldi refutes the view that drones have made massed armour obsolete. He says NATO exercises have reinforced that view: at Hedgehog in Estonia (May 2025) and Aurora 26 on Gotland, small Ukrainian FPV teams repeatedly beat Alliance armour, in one case notionally knocking out a combat group built around a British brigade. In Vivaldi the corps carried out two mechanized assaults into the Russian rear. These built on the 2024 Kursk operation, which paired armour with intensive EW. Vivaldi added deeper reconnaissance, dedicated action against Russian strike systems, extra passive and active protection on the vehicles, specially trained crews and suppression of Russian surveillance and communications. Corps commander Brig. Gen. Andrii Biletskyi said the operations prove that armoured manoeuvre is still possible on a battlefield dominated by reconnaissance drones, loitering munitions and precision weapons. Kovalenko warns that NATO armies are still stuck near Cold War templates for attack and defence. The 125th is an exception. Euromaidan Press cites analyst Giorgi Revishvili, who wrote in June that none of Ukraine's roughly 150 ground combat brigades was fully manned. The 29th Heavy Mechanized Brigade, stood up last year, is still not ready for action. According to observer Stevius, it is building a single tank battalion, 31 tanks at full strength, from a mix of T-72B, T-72EA, T-72AV and T-64BV tanks. It also has BTR-60 and M113 armoured personnel carriers dating from the 1960s, plus new 2S22 howitzers. Oryx has visually confirmed the loss of nearly 1,500 Ukrainian tanks and about 6,900 other armoured vehicles. UALosses counts nearly 190,000 Ukrainian troops killed, missing or captured since February 2022. Euromaidan Press estimates that many brigades cannot equip all their battalions. It argues that the infantry shortage is the main driver of Ukraine's reliance on drones and ground robots. The manning and performance figures are the brigade's own claims, and the casualty figures come from the 3rd Army Corps, a party to the war. None of them are independently verified.

Zelensky's drone adviser says Russia's jet-powered Geran-5 has reached 1,000 km/h while maneuvering and that Chinese engineers help develop Russia's Geran drones

2026-10-30

Serhii "Flash" Beskrestnov, Ukraine's presidential adviser on defence technology, told journalists in Kyiv on 29 October that Russia's jet-powered Geran-5 drones have reached about 1,000 km/h during maneuvers, Euromaidan Press reports. Ukraine's military intelligence (HUR) puts the Geran-5's cruise speed at 450–600 km/h, while according to Beskrestnov the earlier Geran-3 flew at 280–300 km/h. He also said Chinese engineers help develop Russia's Geran drones: the Alabuga drone plant reportedly gives them tasks such as work on the control module, and key components in the Shahed-type drones are Chinese. Ukraine's sanctions commissioner Vladyslav Vlasiuk added that China will require licences for Geran engines from 18 November, stressing that licensing is not a ban. These claims come from Ukrainian officials and have not been independently confirmed. The faster drones are a growing problem for Ukrainian air defence. From 1 to 26 September only 55% of Russia's jet-powered drones were shot down; on 27 October the General Staff said nearly 60% of jet-powered Geran-4s had been downed over the past three months and pointed to a shortage of guided anti-aircraft missiles (no Geran-5 figure was given). On 29 October a jet-powered drone hit Dnipro railway station; the death toll has since risen to 15. According to Beskrestnov, Ukrainian firms have already shown jet-powered interceptor drones but are struggling with control, guidance, automatic target lock and scaling up production. Some foreign interceptors are being designed for the Geran-5's cruise speed, such as the Vespid Mk 1 from US firm CHAOS Industries, with a claimed top speed of 540 km/h.

Dutch firm Destinus says it has built 1,750 Ruta B1 cruise missiles and now makes nearly 300 a month, more than Europe's annual output before 2022, while small jet engines remain the bottleneck for Ukraine's strike programmes

2026-10-30

Destinus, a Netherlands-headquartered defence company, said on 29 October that it has completed 1,750 Ruta B1 cruise missiles and reached a monthly output of nearly 300, which it told Reuters exceeds Europe's entire annual cruise-missile production before Russia's full-scale invasion, Euromaidan Press reports. The Ruta B1 (Block 1) has a range of about 300 km and runs on Destinus' own T150 turbojet; in June 2026 the company announced its 1,000th T150. Block 2, now in production, carries a 250 kg payload more than 700 km and is stored, transported and launched from the same container; Block 3 is planned with a 2,000 km range, with flight tests scheduled for 2027. Destinus is also working with Rheinmetall on cruise missiles and on rockets for multiple-launch systems, including a planned European equivalent of the GMLRS fired by HIMARS. The article stresses that engines, not airframes, are the constraint on Ukraine's deep-strike programmes. Reuters reported in April 2026 that a looming shortage of mini jet engines could hold back Ukraine's jet-powered strike drones; Czech maker ZofiTech was then producing about 200 engines a month, nearly all for Ukraine, against expected demand of thousands. Destinus says it expects to meet Europe's demand, including Ukraine's, for 1.5-kilonewton-class cruise-missile engines from next year, and CEO Mikhail Kokorich said it already builds such turbojets in series for itself and other manufacturers, but the company has not disclosed its engine output. Zelensky acknowledged the Ruta's combat use in October 2025, and in June 2026 then-Defence Minister Mykhailo Fedorov said the Netherlands was ready to finance about 700 Rutas for Ukraine. Kokorich argued that Europe's challenge is shifting from production to integration: sufficient deterrence would need 'tens of thousands of missiles', plus new units, logistics, storage, maintenance and targeting infrastructure. He said Destinus uses no Chinese components, which makes production slower and costlier, and predicted that cheaper missiles with ranges under 1,000 km will make up most future demand. The production and engine figures are company claims and have not been independently verified.

Militarnyi notes that North Korea earlier showed large-scale production of attack drones resembling the Israeli Harop loitering munition, which it first revealed in August 2024.
