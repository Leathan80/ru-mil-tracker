HIMERA B1 is a Ukrainian-made autonomous radio repeater that extends the range of battlefield mesh radio networks. The episode highlights two things at once: Russia's sustained effort to disrupt Ukrainian battlefield communications through repeated precision strikes on relay infrastructure, and the emphasis Ukrainian manufacturers place on ruggedness and redundancy in this class of equipment as a countermeasure to that campaign.

Ukrainian long-range drone strikes in mid-September 2026 knocked two Rosneft-run refineries out of operation: Syzran and Saratov. Reuters, citing three industry sources, reports that three of Russia's six biggest diesel-producing refineries — Omsk, Kirishi, Volgograd, Perm, NORSI and Taneco, which together account for roughly half of national diesel output — are now sharply curtailing production or fully shut down. Kirishi has ceased operations entirely, while Volgograd and NORSI are running at only about 25% of capacity. The strikes form part of Ukraine's sustained deep-strike campaign against Russian refining capacity. By late August that campaign had already produced a petrol shortfall of roughly one-third inside Russia, with domestic production covering only about 70% of summer consumption (85% once imports are included), according to earlier reporting cited by Meduza and Novaya Gazeta.
