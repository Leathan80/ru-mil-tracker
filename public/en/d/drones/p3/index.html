Denmark rushes 50,000+ rounds of 20 mm F-16 cannon ammunition to Ukraine to shoot down jet-powered Geran/Shahed drones

2026-10-23

Denmark has delivered more than 50,000 rounds of 20 mm ammunition for the internal cannons of Ukraine's F-16s, and Ukrainian pilots are already using them against Russia's jet-powered Shahed-type attack drones, according to an announcement by Ukraine's Ministry of Defence on X on 23 October 2026. The ministry said Denmark sent the rounds on an expedited basis at Ukraine's request, called aviation, and the F-16 in particular, one of the most effective tools against jet-powered Shaheds, and said that destroying them requires sufficient ammunition stocks. Germany has delivered a first batch of F-16 ammunition for the same purpose, and Belgium and Spain have also sent urgent shipments. The F-16's six-barrel M61A1 Vulcan fires about 6,000 rounds per minute. Using the gun lets pilots save far more expensive guided missiles for cruise missiles and other priority targets, and 20 mm rounds are available worldwide in much larger quantities than Patriot interceptors or Western air-to-air missiles. Consumption is high: Bloomberg reports that downing one jet drone can take about 150 rounds and that, at the current intensity of Russian attacks, Ukraine may need around 10,000 rounds a day. At that rate the Danish shipment covers roughly 330 engagements. Russia began fielding jet-powered Geran drones (Russian derivatives of the Iranian Shahed-136) in significant numbers in early 2026. According to the article they carry up to 90 kg of explosives, have a range of up to 1,000 km and reach up to 600 km/h, which makes them much harder to intercept than the propeller versions. Ukrainian Air Force spokesman Yurii Ihnat has said Ukraine downs about 60% of the jet drones, against roughly 90-95% for other types. President Zelenskyy said combat aviation, mainly the F-16, accounts for 55-60% of all intercepts of jet Shaheds. These figures are Ukrainian official claims and have not been independently verified. Ukraine is also testing domestically built interceptor drones designed to catch jet-powered Shaheds.

Russian MoD claims 443 Ukrainian drones downed over 17+ regions overnight 22-23 October; all four Moscow airports shut again in daytime

2026-10-23

Russia's Defence Ministry claims its air defences intercepted and destroyed 443 Ukrainian fixed-wing drones overnight from 22 to 23 October, one night after the attack in which Moscow mayor Sergei Sobyanin claimed 630 drones were downed. According to the ministry, drones were shot down over the Smolensk, Nizhny Novgorod, Belgorod, Bryansk, Kursk, Kaluga, Tula, Oryol, Lipetsk, Tambov, Voronezh, Ryazan, Vladimir, Rostov, Volgograd, Perm and Krasnodar regions, as well as the Moscow region, Tatarstan, Udmurtia, Crimea, the Black Sea and the Sea of Azov. Sobyanin said more than 150 drones (155, according to AeroTime's reading of his statements) flew towards the Moscow region; he said most were destroyed at long range and five were intercepted on approach to the capital. Regional governors gave their own figures: 20 over Smolensk, 19 over Nizhny Novgorod, 12 over Tula, plus interceptions over four districts of Rostov region. All of these numbers are claims by the Russian MoD and Russian officials and have not been independently verified. In the Gorodetsky district of Nizhny Novgorod region, falling debris injured three people, who were taken to hospital, according to preliminary information from governor Gleb Nikitin. Several residential buildings, an educational facility and infrastructure were damaged. Schools in the district switched to remote learning, and traffic over the dam of the Nizhny Novgorod hydroelectric power station at Zavolzhye was temporarily halted because of the drone threat. According to Nikitin, MoD units and the volunteer unit Bars-NN took part in the air defence. The pressure on Moscow went on into the daytime. According to AeroTime, which cites notices from the aviation authority Rosaviatsiya, Vnukovo and Domodedovo were restricted to cleared flights from about 12:55-13:00 Moscow time, and at 14:05 all four Moscow airports (Sheremetyevo, Domodedovo, Vnukovo and Zhukovsky) were closed entirely, citing flight safety. It was the second full closure in two days. Sobyanin reported nine drones destroyed by 13:39. Shortly before 15:00, Sheremetyevo declared an air raid alert and moved passengers, including those on aircraft parked on the apron, into shelters. A claim on social media that drone debris fell on the runway was not verified. Vnukovo and Domodedovo resumed flights under the clearance regime at 16:43.

On 11 September 2026, footage showed a drone diving at the plant. Makhonin then reported attacks on two industrial sites and 36 drones shot down, without naming the targeted facilities. War on the Rocks: deception against automated sensing and decision chains, drawing on Russo-Ukrainian combat lessons

2026-10-23

In an analysis for War on the Rocks, Édouard R. Maurin (call sign FIDES) argues that concealment alone no longer protects manoeuvre on a battlefield made transparent by sensors and drones. Maurin is a French veteran who served in 2024–2025 with an international volunteer section attached to a Ukrainian special-purpose battalion near Pokrovsk. He also runs Fog Defence, a company that develops decoys, which is a commercial interest in the subject. His argument is that deception should target the whole automated chain of sensing, sensor fusion, classification and decision, not only the human commander. He wants it judged on profitability: does the adversary spend more engaging a decoy than the decoy costs? He also wants it nested from section to corps level under one signature-management system. He adds that US doctrine (JP 3-13.4, FM 3-13.4) still assumes a human decision-maker. Most of the evidence comes from Russia and Ukraine. Maurin describes a fake radio call at Kotlyne on 5 January 2025, transmitted while Russian forces were listening, after which two soldiers left a bunker about two minutes later; he watched this himself through a drone's thermal camera. He also mentions feigned holding of terrain at Pokrovsk while survivors were evacuated. On the Russian side he cites Gerbera foam and wooden Parodiya decoy drones fitted with Luneburg lenses to mimic the radar signature of a Shahed/Geran. According to media reports, these decoys make up about half of the swarms, and Alabuga reportedly keeps their cost below $10,000. He contrasts Western interceptors costing hundreds of thousands to millions of dollars with Ukraine's Sting interceptor (about $2,500). He also quotes Unmanned Systems Forces commander Robert 'Magyar' Brovdi, who puts a mass-produced kamikaze drone at about $400. The article also describes Operation Vivaldi (September 2026). There, Ukraine's Third Army Corps first degraded Russian EW and drone-pilot teams and then used feints and fake signatures to retake the Nove settlement and its heights. According to deputy commander Maksym Zhorin, an EW 'protective dome' disrupted or suppressed about 20,000 Russian drones. Corps commander Andriy Biletsky called deception a key factor in the success. Maurin recommends company-level signature fleets, battalion-level signature management cells, and procurement of cheap active decoys. He states explicitly that the effect on automated classifiers is his hypothesis and has not been observed. Much of the Russo-Ukrainian evidence rests on media and unit reports and has not been independently verified.

Sources: War on the Rocks

2026-10-22

Romanian F-16 fighters shot down two jet-powered Geran-family drones over Romania's territorial waters in the Black Sea on 17 October 2026, the Romanian Defence Ministry said. Interim Defence Minister Radu Miruță said the drones flew at about 450 km/h and were likely Geran-4s, which would make them the first of that type Romania has downed. The identification rests on the Romanian assessment; no wreckage analysis has been published. According to Ukraine's military intelligence (HUR), Russia began using the Geran-4 in May 2026 as a response to effective Ukrainian interceptor drones, and the type reaches up to 500 km/h. By June, interceptors were downing about half of the slower Shahed-type drones, according to an estimate by defence adviser Serhii Beskrestnov. In a 20 October op-ed for CEPA, Fabian Hoffmann of the Norwegian Institute for Defence Studies traces the shift to the Geran-3, recovered intact by Ukraine in 2025, whose Chinese JT80 turbojet roughly doubled speed from 185 to 350 km/h. He puts newer models, including the Geran-4, at 600 km/h or more, higher than HUR's figure, and estimates their cost at $150,000–250,000 each against about $50,000 for a propeller-driven long-range drone; in his view Russia buys a significantly higher chance of penetration for that price. Hoffmann, who calls these weapons low-cost cruise missiles, argues that in a direct confrontation European NATO states would be even more vulnerable to swarms of them than Ukraine. Defences must match their speed, for example with jet-powered interceptor drones, but jet engines are scarce because too few European and US suppliers can meet demand, while gun systems and higher-end missile defences are too scarce or too costly. Ukraine already fields Bars jet interceptors; President Zelenskyy said on 4 October they hit about 30% of targets, the highest rate among Ukrainian interceptors, and Defence Minister Yevhenii Khmara called aviation one of the most effective tools against jet drones.
