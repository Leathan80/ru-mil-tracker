Ukrainian PM Koretskyi: Russia could launch up to 6,500 jet-powered Shaheds in November, 12 times the August figure

2026-11-16

In an interview with Radio Liberty's Ukrainian Service, Prime Minister Serhii Koretskyi said Russia could launch up to 6,500 jet-powered Shahed drones against Ukraine in November. That is about twelve times the August figure, which he put at 530 "if I remember correctly". He said the scale of attacks has already grown several times over before winter, and that Russia is also making heavy use of guided aerial bombs, cruise and ballistic missiles, and tens of thousands of fibre-optic FPV drones. Koretskyi said the government, the president and local authorities are protecting energy facilities and making sure critical infrastructure has backup power. Fully protecting power lines and large switchgear is impossible, so power engineers are focusing on fast repairs. He called Russia's strikes on bridges, infrastructure and businesses "pure terror" aimed at making life in large cities unbearable. The November figure is a projection, not a count. Koretskyi's August baseline also differs from earlier Ukrainian statements reported by Ukrainska Pravda: presidential adviser Serhii Beskrestnov reported more than 2,500 jet-powered Shaheds in August (1,500 in July), and Air Force spokesman Yurii Ihnat said the August number exceeded 2,800. The article gives the context of an autumn campaign against the energy grid, including repeated strikes on the Trypillia power plant (30 September and 9 November) and more than 310 strikes on energy facilities in the first days of November.

Investigation: around 50 African women recruited for Shahed/Geran production at Russia's Alabuga plant

2026-11-16

A joint investigation by the Ukrainian NGO Ukrainian Security and Cooperation Center (USCC) and Fox News, reported by Militarnyi and Euromaidan Press, obtained internal documents, including employee passes, from a network that recruits young African women for the Alabuga Special Economic Zone in Tatarstan. Shahed-type (Geran) attack drones are produced there. The documents name about 50 women, ten Russian recruiters and two African intermediaries. Two of the women, from Tunisia and Uganda, are named as alleged recruitment coordinators. According to USCC, the workers come from Uganda, Ethiopia, Zambia, Zimbabwe, Tanzania, Kenya, Gambia and DR Congo, and also from Sri Lanka. Foreign recruits help ease staff shortages in Russia's arms industry. USCC says the network works on three levels: Russian compatriot councils and cultural organisations that promote the programme at events in Africa, local recruiters, and Alabuga's own staff, who arrange employment and logistics. The main vehicle is 'Alabuga Start'. USCC's Solomiia Khoma says recruits are promised a dual education, but those who fail entrance or Russian-language requirements end up in factory work and cannot afford a ticket home. A coordination council in Tunisia signed a memorandum with Alabuga in September 2024. AP reported in 2024 on social-media adverts, long shifts, unpaid wage promises and exposure to hazardous chemicals. Fox News notes that the investigation does not show that Russian diplomats or cultural groups knowingly steered recruits into drone production. Alabuga, Russia's Foreign Ministry and the African governments approached gave no comment in the report. On 28 October 2026, Ghana's labour minister Emmanuel Kwadwo Agyekum signed a memorandum with Alabuga on recruiting Ghanaian industrial workers. He said the zone needs more than 75,000 workers. That same day DR Congo's labour minister signed a five-year memorandum on training and employment, and the Congolese presidency linked a deal made during Tshisekedi's Moscow visit to Alabuga Start. As publicly described, neither deal shows an intent to put workers into arms production. A 2025 Alabuga brochure claims 8,498 foreign-worker quota places from Russia's labour ministry. The UK sanctioned recruiters for Alabuga Start, including ENANGUE HOLDING, on 5 May 2026. September 2026 satellite imagery showed at least ten new production buildings under construction, and Russia is also said to be recruiting North Korean workers. USCC is calling for sanctions on recruitment networks and intermediaries.

In the early hours of 14 November, suspects tried to set fire to and cause an explosion at the facility of Estonian defence company Threod Systems in Viimsi, near Tallinn. Czechia discusses up to 16 L-39NG Skyfox jets for Ukraine as low-cost hunters of Russian drones

Czechia could supply Ukraine with up to 16 Aero L-39NG Skyfox light jets, The War Zone reports. Four were previously agreed for transfer and twelve more are under discussion. This configuration is not yet in operational service. The Skyfox is a modernised L-39 with a US Williams FJ44-4M turbofan, a glass cockpit and five hardpoints. TWZ gives a top speed of about 560 mph (about 900 km/h) and a ceiling of 38,000 ft. Aero Vodochody puts operating costs at about $1,700–2,300 per flight hour, against about $22,000 for an F-16-class fighter. A 70 mm guided rocket of the APKWS II type costs roughly $20,000–25,000, against about $1 million for an AIM-120 and about $500,000 for an AIM-9X. The jet is meant to take pressure off Ukraine's F-16s, MiG-29s, Su-27s and Mirages, more than half of whose F-16s were reportedly stuck in maintenance amid shortages of AIM-9 and AIM-120 missiles. It is aimed especially at Russia's jet-powered Geran-3/4/5 drones, with the Geran-4 reportedly reaching about 280–310 mph and 26,000 ft. Justin Bronk of RUSI called the L-39NG very cheap and simple to operate compared with frontline fighters, with speed and endurance suited to intercepting jet drones. His main caveat is the lack of an onboard radar, which Ukraine's ground radar network can partly offset. Many Ukrainian pilots and technicians already know the L-39. Aero Vodochody builds about 12 Skyfox a year (capacity 18), and its schedule is reportedly booked through Q2 2027, which could delay deliveries. Russian outlet Topwar reported the same Czech claims; its readers questioned the radar-less concept and the delivery backlog.
