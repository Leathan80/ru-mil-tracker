Ukraine fields the Pasika system, which lets one operator fly five drones at once without GPS

2026-11-06

Ukrainian troops are already using Pasika ("Apiary"), a multi-drone control system developed by Sine Engineering, which lets a single operator control five drones at the same time. Ukrainska Pravda reports this based on Brave1, the government-backed Ukrainian defence-technology platform. "All units are managed via a single interface – the operator steps in only during the terminal phase," Brave1 said. Ukrainian Mangal active protection system, which automatically fires nets at incoming FPV drones, is heading for serial production

2026-11-05

Ukrainian company Phantom Defense is preparing Mangal for serial production. Mangal is an active protection system that automatically detects FPV drones and catches them with a net as they approach military vehicles. A Militarnyi journalist watched a demonstration at a test range. Mangal grew out of the Karakurt net launchers, which were first mounted on drones to intercept other drones; Euromaidan Press notes that Phantom Defense bought Karakurt's developer in October. According to the developer, the 100th Separate Mechanized Brigade was the first to put a manually fired net launcher on a ground robot, and operator-fired versions have been at the front since March 2026. Each module covers a sector of about 90 degrees, so several are needed for all-round coverage; eight were fitted to a Stryker in one test. A module contains its own microcomputer, a control unit, day and thermal cameras, and net launchers. It computes the drone's trajectory and chooses when to fire and which launcher to use. Developer figures: detection at about 40 m against trees or cluttered terrain and over 60 m against the sky, reaction time up to 0.2 seconds, and roughly UAH 2,000 per shot (about $44 according to Euromaidan Press), including a 6x6 m net and pyrotechnic cartridge. About 1,000 test firings were carried out. Endurance is at least 12 hours, up to 36 hours in some tests, and an external battery can be connected. The unit can be detached and set up near cover if a vehicle has to be abandoned. It has been tested on Strykers, other armoured vehicles, pickups, ground robots and boats, including boats under way. Phantom Defense presents Mangal as a second layer behind electronic warfare: if jamming fails, especially against fiber-optic FPVs, the net engages at close range. According to the company, ten systems have gone to Ukrainian units for combat trials and have repelled two FPV attacks, and a Mangal 2.0 based on front-line feedback is in development. All performance figures are company claims and have not been independently verified. Euromaidan Press frames the system against road netting, which according to the Ukrainian Defence Ministry covered more than 1,640 km of front-line roads this year, but which does not move with the vehicle.

Ukrainian military engineers dig a nearly 6 km tunnel in Donetsk Oblast to move troops and supplies beneath Russia's drone kill zone

2026-11-03

Ukrainian military engineers have dug an underground passage almost six kilometres long between two settlements in Donetsk Oblast, according to Colonel Dmytro Pishko of the Air Assault Forces Command, speaking to Ukrinform and cited by Euromaidan Press. The settlements are not named. Pishko said the tunnel is used to move personnel, motorbikes and quad bikes, ammunition and supplies, and to evacuate the wounded, all out of sight of Russian drones. No details were given on depth, width, construction time, cost or the number of people involved. The article sets the tunnel against the roughly 30-kilometre-deep strip along the front that soldiers call the "death zone", where constant drone surveillance means movement in the open can draw a strike within minutes. Both armies have increasingly moved troops, vehicles and supply lines underground. Pishko also recalled that in 2023, after Russia destroyed a road bridge towards Lyman, the 81st Separate Airmobile Brigade spent a winter using a PTS-2 amphibious transporter for medical evacuation, saving 30 to 40 minutes per run; he said the unit "made 86 runs this way". The article further cites the Khartiia Corps' October claim that its forces advanced more than 15 kilometres through underground utility tunnels into the Russian rear on the Kupiansk axis in Kharkiv Oblast (Operation Veles). All tunnel details come from Ukrainian military sources and have not been independently verified.

Sources: Euromaidan Press
